# Number Classification

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Number Classification** identifies the number that does not follow the same mathematical property or pattern as the other numbers.

The main idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Find the common mathematical property shared by most numbers}}
$$

Then identify the number that breaks that property.

Example:

**4, 9, 16, 25, 30**

The first four are perfect squares:

$$
4=2^2
$$

$$
9=3^2
$$

$$
16=4^2
$$

$$
25=5^2
$$

But:

$$
30\neq n^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{30}
$$

### Intuition

Do not immediately perform complicated calculations.

Check common properties in a fixed order:

1. Even/odd
2. Prime/composite
3. Divisibility
4. Perfect square
5. Perfect cube
6. Multiples
7. Factors
8. Digit properties
9. Number patterns
10. Mathematical relationships

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Number classification is a type of **odd-one-out reasoning** where numbers are grouped according to a mathematical property.

Example:

**12, 18, 24, 35**

Check divisibility by 6:

$$
12\div6=2
$$

$$
18\div6=3
$$

$$
24\div6=4
$$

But:

$$
35\div6
$$

is not an integer.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{35}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula.

The general rule is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Common Number Property}\rightarrow\text{Find Exception}}
$$

### Common Properties

| Property | Example |
|---|---|
| Even | 2, 4, 6, 8 |
| Odd | 1, 3, 5, 7 |
| Prime | 2, 3, 5, 7 |
| Composite | 4, 6, 8, 9 |
| Perfect square | 4, 9, 16, 25 |
| Perfect cube | 8, 27, 64, 125 |
| Multiple of 3 | 6, 12, 18 |
| Multiple of 5 | 10, 15, 20 |
| Divisible by 2 | 14, 28, 36 |
| Divisible by 7 | 14, 21, 35 |
| Same digit sum | 12, 21, 30 |
| Same digit product | 24, 42, 63 |
| Consecutive pattern | 5, 10, 15, 20 |
| Power relationship | 8, 16, 32, 64 |

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Most numbers should share a clear mathematical property.
2. One number violates that property.
3. Check simple properties before complex ones.
4. A number can have many properties simultaneously.
5. The intended property should distinguish exactly one option.
6. Prime/composite classification is frequently useful.
7. Divisibility tests can save time.
8. Perfect squares and cubes should be recognized instantly.
9. Digit-based properties may appear in advanced questions.
10. If several patterns are possible, use the most natural and specific one.

### Recommended Checking Order

$$
\boxed{
Even/Odd
\rightarrow
Prime
\rightarrow
Divisibility
\rightarrow
Square/Cube
\rightarrow
Factors
\rightarrow
Digits
\rightarrow
Advanced Pattern
}
$$

# 12. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Check parity first**

Look at the last digit.

Even last digit:

$$
0,2,4,6,8
$$

means the number is even.

Odd last digit:

$$
1,3,5,7,9
$$

means the number is odd.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Check divisibility by 3 using digit sum**

If:

$$
\text{Digit Sum}\equiv0\pmod3
$$

then the number is divisible by 3.

Example:

$$
123\rightarrow1+2+3=6
$$

Therefore:

$$
123\div3=41
$$

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Check divisibility by 5**

A number divisible by 5 ends in:

0\text{ or }5

# 13. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Even/Odd

> [!important]
> If all but one number have the same parity, check **Even/Odd** first.

Example:

$$
12,\ 18,\ 24,\ 31
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{31}
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Prime/Composite

> [!important]
> If numbers look irregular, check whether most are prime.

Example:

$$
11,\ 17,\ 23,\ 27
$$

Since 27 is composite:

$$
\boxed{27}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Perfect Square

> [!important]
> If numbers are close to familiar squares, check:

$$
n^2
$$

Example:

$$
16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 45
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{45}
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Perfect Cube

> [!important]
> If numbers are larger and look like powers, check:

$$
n^3
$$

Example:

$$
27,\ 64,\ 125,\ 250
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{250}
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Divisibility

> [!important]
> If numbers appear to share a common factor, test divisibility by:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 11
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Digit Sum

> [!important]
> If the numbers do not share an obvious arithmetic property, calculate their digit sums.

Example:

$$
18\rightarrow9
$$

$$
27\rightarrow9
$$

$$
36\rightarrow9
$$

This reveals a hidden common property.

---

## Pattern 7 — Consecutive Multiples

> [!important]
> If numbers increase regularly, check whether they are consecutive multiples.

Example:

$$
12,\ 18,\ 24,\ 30
$$

These are:

$$
6\times2,\ 6\times3,\ 6\times4,\ 6\times5
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Number of Factors

> [!important]
> If the numbers are small, compare their number of factors.

Example:

$$
6,\ 10,\ 14
$$

each has four factors, while a prime such as 17 has only two.

# 15. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Checking only even/odd

A set may contain both even and odd numbers while sharing a deeper property.

Always continue checking if parity does not isolate one number.

---

### Mistake 2 — Assuming the largest number is the odd one

The largest number is not necessarily the exception.

Classification depends on the **property**, not size.

---

### Mistake 3 — Confusing prime and odd numbers

Not every odd number is prime.

For example:

$$
9
$$

is odd but composite:

$$
9=3\times3
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Forgetting 1 is neither prime nor composite

$$
\boxed{1\text{ is neither prime nor composite}}
$$

This is a common exam trap.

---

### Mistake 5 — Calling every non-square number an exception

A classification question must have a common property shared by the remaining numbers.

Do not choose an option just because it "looks different."

---

### Mistake 6 — Using a weak pattern

If three numbers are perfect squares and one is not, that is usually stronger than a complicated digit pattern that also happens to work.

---

### Mistake 7 — Ignoring answer choices

In difficult questions, options can help reveal whether the intended pattern is:

- square
- cube
- divisibility
- digit-based
- factor-based

Use them for verification.

# 17. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Number Classification

Find the number that does not share the common mathematical property.

### Check in This Order

1. Even/Odd
2. Prime/Composite
3. Divisibility
4. Perfect square
5. Perfect cube
6. Multiples
7. Factors
8. Digit sum
9. Digit product
10. Advanced arithmetic pattern

### Important Facts

$$
1=\text{Neither prime nor composite}
$$

$$
2=\text{Only even prime}
$$

$$
\text{Even}\Rightarrow\text{Last digit }0,2,4,6,8
$$

$$
\text{Divisible by 5}\Rightarrow\text{Last digit }0\text{ or }5
$$

$$
\text{Divisible by 3}\Rightarrow\text{Digit sum divisible by 3}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

**"Check simple properties first, then digits and advanced patterns."**

# One-Line Recognition

**When several numbers are given, find the strongest mathematical property shared by most of them and choose the number that breaks it.**
