# Word Classification

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Word Classification** asks you to identify the word that does not belong to the same logical group as the others.

The basic idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Find the common property shared by most words}}
$$

Then identify the word that does not satisfy that property.

Example:

**Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot**

Apple, Mango, and Banana are fruits.

Carrot is a vegetable.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Carrot}}
$$

### Intuition

Do not ask:

> "Which word looks different?"

Ask:

> **"What common relationship connects three or more words?"**

Then find the word that breaks that relationship.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Classification is also commonly called:

- Odd One Out
- Find the Different Word
- Word Classification

A typical question gives four or five words.

Example:

**A. Dog  
B. Cat  
C. Cow  
D. Rose**

The first three are animals.

Rose is a flower.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rose}}
$$

### Core Principle

If:

$$
A,B,C,D
$$

are given and:

$$
A,B,C\rightarrow\text{Same Category}
$$

but:

$$
D\rightarrow\text{Different Category}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{D=\text{Odd One Out}}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no mathematical formula for word classification.

The general rule is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Common Property} \rightarrow \text{Find Exception}}
$$

### Common Classification Properties

| Property | Example |
|---|---|
| Same category | Apple, Mango, Banana |
| Same profession | Doctor, Teacher, Engineer |
| Same place | School, College, University |
| Same animal type | Lion, Tiger, Leopard |
| Same food type | Rice, Wheat, Corn |
| Same material | Gold, Silver, Copper |
| Same function | Pen, Pencil, Marker |
| Same relationship | Brother, Sister, Father |
| Same geographical type | River, Lake, Ocean |
| Same grammatical type | Run, Walk, Jump |
| Same meaning | Big, Large, Huge |
| Opposite meaning | Hot, Cold, High |
| Same characteristic | Red, Blue, Green |
| Same group | Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday |

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Usually, most words share a common property.
2. One word violates that property.
3. The relationship may be based on meaning, category, function, usage, or association.
4. The words may have more than one possible relationship.
5. Always search for the **strongest common relationship**.
6. Do not choose an answer based only on spelling.
7. Classification can be semantic or structural.
8. Sometimes the odd word is identified through a hierarchy.

### Example

**Rose, Lotus, Lily, Mango**

Rose, Lotus, and Lily are flowers.

Mango is a fruit.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mango}}
$$

# 12. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Find the category**

Look at the words and immediately ask:

> "What category do most of these belong to?"

Example:

**Apple, Orange, Banana, Carrot**

Think:

$$
Fruit,\ Fruit,\ Fruit,\ ?
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Carrot}}
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Group into pairs**

If the category is not immediately obvious, group the words.

Example:

**Doctor, Hospital, Teacher, School**

Pairs:

$$
Doctor\rightarrow Hospital
$$

$$
Teacher\rightarrow School
$$

This may reveal a different relationship.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Check function**

Ask:

> "What are these things used for?"

Example:

**Pen, Pencil, Marker, Chair**

Three are used for writing.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — Check type before spelling**

Do not focus on:

- word length
- first letter
- number of letters

unless the question clearly suggests a spelling-based pattern.

Start with meaning.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5 — Use elimination**

If three options clearly belong to one group, the remaining option is usually the answer.

# 13. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Same Category

> [!important]
> If three or more words belong to the same broad category, choose the word outside that category.

Example:

**Dog, Cat, Horse, Rose**

Think:

$$
Animal,\ Animal,\ Animal,\ Flower
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rose}}
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Same Function

> [!important]
> If several objects perform the same function, identify the object with a different function.

Example:

**Pen, Pencil, Marker, Plate**

Three are writing instruments.

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Plate}}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Same Profession

> [!important]
> If several words represent professions, find the non-profession.

Example:

**Doctor, Engineer, Teacher, Hospital**

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Hospital}}
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Same Meaning

> [!important]
> If three words have similar meanings and one has a different/opposite meaning, choose the different one.

Example:

**Fast, Quick, Rapid, Slow**

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Slow}}
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Same Biological Group

> [!important]
> If three organisms belong to one biological group and one does not, choose the outsider.

Example:

**Lion, Tiger, Leopard, Snake**

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Snake}}
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Same Time Category

> [!important]
> If three words represent days and one represents a month, choose the month.

Example:

**Monday, Wednesday, Friday, March**

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\text{March}}
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Same Material

> [!important]
> If three objects are made from similar material, identify the one made from a different material.

---

## Pattern 8 — Category Level

> [!important]
> Check whether all words belong to the same **level of classification**.

Example:

**Animal, Bird, Mammal, Sparrow**

Sparrow is more specific than the broad categories.

# 15. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Choosing the word that looks different

Visual appearance is not always the basis.

Example:

**Apple, Mango, Banana, Carrot**

Carrot may look different, but the real reason is:

$$
Fruit\ vs.\ Vegetable
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Using an overly broad category

Almost everything can belong to a broad category such as "object."

Choose the **most specific meaningful common category**.

---

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring function

Some words belong to different physical categories but have the same function.

Always check usage if simple categories fail.

---

### Mistake 4 — Ignoring category level

"Bird" and "Sparrow" are related, but they are not at the same classification level.

Check whether the question is testing:

$$
Class\rightarrowClass
$$

or:

$$
Class\rightarrowMember
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Finding multiple possible answers

If more than one answer seems possible, search for the relationship that groups the **maximum number of words with the strongest logical connection**.

---

### Mistake 6 — Overthinking easy questions

If three words are clearly fruits and one is a vehicle, do not search for hidden letter patterns.

# 16. Formula Sheet

## Core Rule

$$
\boxed{\text{Common Property}+\text{Exception}=\text{Classification Answer}}
$$

## Category Method

$$
A,B,C\rightarrow X
$$

$$
D\notin X
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{D=\text{Odd One Out}}
$$

## Classification Priority

$$
\boxed{\text{Category}\rightarrow\text{Function}\rightarrow\text{Meaning}\rightarrow\text{Structure}}
$$

Use the first valid and strongest relationship.

# 17. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Word Classification

Find the word that does not belong to the same logical group.

### Fast Method

1. Read all words.
2. Find the strongest common category.
3. Group the obvious matches.
4. Identify the outsider.
5. Check whether function or meaning gives a stronger relationship.
6. Verify that the other words genuinely belong together.

### Most Common Categories

- Animals
- Fruits
- Vegetables
- Professions
- Tools
- Vehicles
- Places
- Materials
- Body parts
- Food
- Days/months
- Synonyms
- Antonyms
- Biological groups

### Golden Memory Trick

**"Group the common three, then find the outsider."**

# One-Line Recognition

**When four or more words are given, find the strongest property shared by most of them and select the word that breaks that property.**
