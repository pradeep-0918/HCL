# Alphabet Classification

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Alphabet Classification** identifies the letter or group of letters that does not follow the same positional, structural, or logical property as the others.

The basic idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Find the common alphabet property shared by most options}}
$$

Then identify the letter that breaks that property.

Alphabet classification may be based on:

- Alphabet position
- Odd/even position
- Vowel/consonant
- Forward/backward position
- Reverse alphabet pairs
- Position differences
- Consecutive letters
- Fixed gaps
- Letter groups
- Symmetry or visual properties
- Alphabetical order

### Example

**A, E, I, K**

A, E, and I are vowels.

K is a consonant.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{K}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Alphabet classification is a type of **odd-one-out reasoning** involving letters.

A question gives several letters or letter groups.

Your task is to find the one that does not belong to the same logical group.

### Example

**B, D, F, H, K**

Positions:

$$
B=2
$$

$$
D=4
$$

$$
F=6
$$

$$
H=8
$$

The first four have even alphabet positions.

K has:

$$
K=11
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{K}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula.

The general rule is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Common Alphabet Property}\rightarrow\text{Exception}}
$$

### Important Alphabet Properties

| Property | Example |
|---|---|
| Vowel | A, E, I, O, U |
| Consonant | B, C, D, F |
| Even position | B, D, F, H |
| Odd position | A, C, E, G |
| Consecutive letters | A, B, C, D |
| Fixed gap | A, D, G, J |
| Reverse pairs | A-Z, B-Y |
| First half | A-M |
| Second half | N-Z |
| Position multiple | C, F, I, L |
| Alphabetical order | A, C, E, G |
| Reverse order | Z, X, V, T |

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Remember:

$$
A=1,\ B=2,\ldots,Z=26
$$

2. Vowels are:

$$
A,E,I,O,U
$$

3. Remaining English letters are consonants.
4. Even-position letters have positions:

$$
2,4,6,\ldots,26
$$

5. Odd-position letters have positions:

$$
1,3,5,\ldots,25
$$

6. The first half is:

$$
A-M
$$

7. The second half is:

$$
N-Z
$$

8. Reverse alphabet pairs satisfy:

$$
A+Z=27
$$

$$
B+Y=27
$$

9. Many classification questions use fixed positional gaps.
10. For letter groups, compare corresponding positions.

### Question

**A-Z, B-Y, C-X, D-W, E-T**

### Pattern Recognition

For reverse pairs:

$$

$$

$$

$$
2+25=27
$$

$$
3+24=27

$$
4+23=27
$$

$$
5+20=25

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{E-T}

# 11. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Convert letters to positions**

If the pattern is not obvious, immediately write:

$$
A=1,\ldots,Z=26
$$

Then compare the numbers.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Check parity**

Look at whether positions are:

$$
Odd\quad\text{or}\quad Even
$$

Example:

B, D, F, H:

$$
2,4,6,8
$$

All are even.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Check gaps**

Calculate consecutive differences.

Example:

**A, D, G, J**

Positions:

$$
1,4,7,10
$$

Differences:

$$
+3,+3,+3
$$

This is an immediate pattern.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — Check multiples**

If positions look like:

$$
3,6,9,12
$$

think multiples of 3.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5 — Check reverse positions**

For a pair of letters:

$$
P_1+P_2=27
$$

means they are reverse alphabet pairs.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 6 — Check vowels first**

If the options contain several vowels, check:

$$
A,E,I,O,U
$$

before performing calculations.

# 12. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Vowel/Consonant

> [!important]
> If most letters are vowels and one is a consonant, choose the consonant.

Example:

$$
A,E,I,O,T
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{T}
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Odd/Even Position

> [!important]
> Convert letters to positions and check parity.

Example:

$$
B,D,F,H
$$

positions:

$$
2,4,6,8
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Consecutive Letters

> [!important]
> If the letters increase one position at a time, think **Consecutive Sequence**.

Example:

$$
A,B,C,D
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Fixed Gap

> [!important]
> If the difference is constant, identify the gap.

Example:

$$
A,D,G,J
$$

Each gap is:

$$
+3
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Reverse Alphabet

> [!important]
> If letters are paired from opposite ends:

$$
A-Z,\ B-Y,\ C-X
$$

think **Reverse Alphabet**.

---

## Pattern 6 — Multiples of a Position

> [!important]
> If positions are:

$$
3,6,9,12
$$

think:

$$
\text{Multiples of 3}
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — First Half / Second Half

> [!important]
> Check whether letters belong to:

$$
A-M
$$

or:

$$
N-Z
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Letter Group Pattern

> [!important]
> For groups such as:

$$
ABC,\ DEF,\ GHI
$$

check whether letters are consecutive within each group.

# 14. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing letter order with alphabet position

Always remember:

$$
A=1
$$

not $A=0$ for standard aptitude questions.

---

### Mistake 2 — Ignoring the gap

For:

$$
A,D,G,J
$$

the pattern is not simply "letters in increasing order."

It is:

$$
+3,+3,+3
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting vowels

If three options are vowels and one is a consonant, no complex calculation is required.

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing reverse pairs

Correct reverse pairs include:

$$
A-Z
$$

$$
B-Y
$$

$$
C-X
$$

$$
D-W
$$

$$
E-V
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Checking only one property

A letter can be both:

- an odd-position letter
- a consonant
- part of a fixed-gap sequence

Choose the property that clearly explains the majority.

---

### Mistake 6 — Ignoring group structure

For:

$$
ABC,\ DEF,\ GHI
$$

check relationships **inside each group**, not only between groups.

# 15. Formula Sheet

## Alphabet Position

$$
\boxed{A=1,\ldots,Z=26}
$$

## Reverse Position

$$
\boxed{27-n}
$$

## Position Difference

$$
\boxed{d=P_2-P_1}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=2k-1}
$$

## Even Position

$$
\boxed{P=2k}
$$

## Reverse Pair

$$
\boxed{P_1+P_2=27}
$$

## Fixed Gap

$$
\boxed{P_{n+1}-P_n=k}
$$

## Classification Rule

$$
\boxed{\text{Common Property}\rightarrow\text{Exception}}
$$
