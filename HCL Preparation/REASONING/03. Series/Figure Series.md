# Figure Series

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **Figure Series** is a sequence of figures arranged according to a visual pattern. The task is usually to identify the next figure, missing figure, or figure that correctly continues the sequence.

Unlike number or letter series, the pattern is usually hidden in visual properties such as:

- Rotation
- Direction
- Movement
- Addition of elements
- Removal of elements
- Change in shading
- Change in size
- Change in position
- Change in shape
- Number of objects
- Alternating patterns
- Mirror transformation
- Combination of multiple rules

### Core Intuition

Do not try to understand the entire figure at once.

Break it into components:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Shape}
+
\text{Position}
+
\text{Direction}
+
\text{Count}
+
\text{Shading}
+
\text{Rotation}
}
$$

Then compare what changes from one figure to the next.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

A figure series may contain:

$$
F_1,\ F_2,\ F_3,\ F_4,\ ?
$$

Your job is to identify the transformation:

$$
F_1\rightarrow F_2
$$

then:

$$
F_2\rightarrow F_3
$$

and so on.

If the same transformation continues, apply it to the final figure.

### Example

Suppose an arrow points:

$$
\uparrow,\ \rightarrow,\ \downarrow,\ \leftarrow,\ ?
$$

The arrow rotates:

$$
90^\circ
$$

clockwise each time.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\uparrow}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula for every figure series.

However, several mathematical transformations are extremely useful.

## Rotation

Quarter-turn rotation:

$$
\boxed{90^\circ}
$$

Half-turn rotation:

$$
\boxed{180^\circ}
$$

Three-quarter rotation:

$$
\boxed{270^\circ}
$$

Full rotation:

$$
\boxed{360^\circ}
$$

---

## Number of Elements

If the number of objects changes:

$$
n,\ n+1,\ n+2,\ldots
$$

or:

$$
n,\ n-1,\ n-2,\ldots
$$

---

## Position Movement

A figure may move:

$$
\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Right}
$$

or around a fixed path.

---

## Alternation

A figure may follow:

$$
A,\ B,\ A,\ B,\ A,\ ?
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{B}
$$

---

## Mirror Transformation

A figure may be reflected horizontally or vertically.

Horizontal reflection:

$$
\boxed{\text{Left}\leftrightarrow\text{Right}}
$$

Vertical reflection:

$$
\boxed{\text{Top}\leftrightarrow\text{Bottom}}
$$

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Figure series are based on visual transformations.
2. The same rule may repeat in every step.
3. Two or more rules may operate simultaneously.
4. Rotation is one of the most common patterns.
5. Position movement is another common pattern.
6. Objects may be added or removed.
7. Shading may change independently from shape.
8. The direction of an arrow or line may change.
9. The number of components may increase or decrease.
10. Figures may alternate between two states.
11. A figure may rotate and move simultaneously.
12. Mirror images may be involved.
13. Inner and outer shapes may follow separate patterns.
14. The transformation may occur clockwise or anticlockwise.
15. The correct answer must continue the complete visual rule, not just one visible feature.

---

# 5. What to Observe

When looking at a figure series, inspect the following properties in order.

| Property | What to Check |
|---|---|
| Shape | Does the basic shape change? |
| Rotation | Does the figure turn? |
| Direction | Does an arrow/line change direction? |
| Position | Does the object move? |
| Count | Are objects added or removed? |
| Size | Does the object grow or shrink? |
| Shading | Does filled/unfilled status change? |
| Orientation | Does the figure flip? |
| Inner Element | Does the internal symbol change? |
| Outer Element | Does the boundary change? |
| Symmetry | Does symmetry change? |
| Combination | Are multiple transformations occurring? |

> [!tip]
> In difficult questions, compare **one property at a time** rather than trying to recognize the complete image immediately.

$$

$$

### Pattern

Each arrow rotates:

$$
90^\circ
$$

clockwise.

Therefore:

$$
\leftarrow\rightarrow\uparrow
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\uparrow}
$$

$$

anticlockwise.

$$
\boxed{\uparrow}

### Question

An arrow sequence is:

\uparrow,\ \downarrow,\ \uparrow,\ \downarrow,\ ?

\boxed{\uparrow}

objects respectively.

# 11. Rotation Patterns

Rotation is one of the most important Figure Series patterns.

## Pattern 1 — $90^\circ$ Clockwise

$$
\uparrow
\rightarrow
\downarrow
\leftarrow
\uparrow
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — $90^\circ$ Anticlockwise

$$
\uparrow
\leftarrow
\downarrow
\rightarrow
\uparrow
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — $180^\circ$

$$
\uparrow
\downarrow
\uparrow
\downarrow
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — $45^\circ$

A diagonal or pointer may rotate:

$$
45^\circ
$$

at each step.

> [!important]
> If the same shape appears repeatedly but its orientation changes, test rotation before looking for more complicated rules.

---

# 12. Clockwise Movement

## Example 6

Consider a point moving around a square:

$$
\text{Top}\rightarrow\text{Right}\rightarrow\text{Bottom}\rightarrow\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Top}
$$

The point moves:

$$
90^\circ
$$

clockwise around the center.

### Answer

After Left, the next position is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Top}}
$$

---

# 13. Anticlockwise Movement

## Example 7

A dot moves:

$$
\text{Top}\rightarrow\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Bottom}\rightarrow\text{Right}
$$

The movement is anticlockwise.

Therefore, after Right:

$$
\boxed{\text{Top}}
$$

---

# 14. Position Movement

A figure may move through different positions without rotating.

## Example 8

Suppose a circle appears:

$$
\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Right}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Left}
$$

The movement alternates.

### Pattern

$$
L,\ C,\ R,\ C,\ L,\ ?
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C}
$$

---

# 15. Diagonal Movement

## Example 9

A dot moves:

$$
\text{Top-left}
\rightarrow
\text{Center}
\rightarrow
\text{Bottom-right}
\rightarrow
\text{Center}
\rightarrow
\text{Top-left}
$$

The pattern repeats every four steps.

Therefore the next position is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Center}}
$$

---

# 16. Addition of Elements

## Example 10

Suppose a figure contains:

$$
1\rightarrow2\rightarrow3\rightarrow4\rightarrow?
$$

stars.

### Pattern

One star is added each step.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5\text{ stars}}
$$

---

# 17. Removal of Elements

## Example 11

Suppose:

$$
5\rightarrow4\rightarrow3\rightarrow2\rightarrow?
$$

circles are present.

One circle disappears at every step.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1\text{ circle}}
$$

---

# 18. Alternating Figures

## Example 12

Suppose the series is:

$$
A,\ B,\ A,\ B,\ A,\ ?
$$

The pattern is:

$$
A,B,A,B,A,B
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{B}
$$

$$
1,3,5,\ldots
$$

$$
2,4,6,\ldots
$$

# 19. Three-Figure Repetition

## Example 13

Suppose:

$$
A,\ B,\ C,\ A,\ B,\ C,\ ?
$$

The pattern repeats every three positions.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A}
$$

# 20. Shading Pattern

Shading can follow an independent sequence.

Example:

$$
\text{Filled}
\rightarrow
\text{Empty}
\rightarrow
\text{Filled}
\rightarrow
\text{Empty}
\rightarrow
?
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Filled}}
$$

### Common Shading Patterns

- Filled → empty
- Empty → filled
- Half-filled → full
- One shaded section moves
- Shading rotates
- Shading increases
- Shading decreases

---

# 21. Example — Shading + Rotation

Suppose a triangle:

1. points upward and is filled
2. points right and is empty
3. points downward and is filled
4. points left and is empty

Two patterns exist.

### Direction

$$
90^\circ
$$

clockwise.

### Shading

$$
\text{Filled},\text{Empty},\text{Filled},\text{Empty}
$$

Next:

- Direction = Up
- Shading = Filled

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{Upward filled triangle}}
$$

> [!important]
> Difficult figure-series questions often combine two independent rules.

---

# 22. Size Change

## Example 14

Suppose a circle changes:

$$
\text{Small}\rightarrow\text{Medium}\rightarrow\text{Large}\rightarrow\text{Small}
$$

The pattern repeats every three steps.

Therefore, after Large:

$$
\boxed{\text{Small}}
$$

---

# 23. Increasing Size

Suppose:

$$
\text{Small}\rightarrow\text{Medium}\rightarrow\text{Large}\rightarrow?
$$

The size increases at each step.

If the available options contain:

- Small
- Medium
- Large
- Extra Large

then the next figure may be:

$$
\boxed{\text{Extra Large}}
$$

> [!warning]
> Size patterns must be interpreted relative to the figures/options. Do not invent a size level that does not exist.

---

# 24. Inner Element Movement

A common pattern is a fixed outer shape with a changing inner object.

Example:

$$
\boxed{\circ}
$$

with an inner dot moving:

$$
\text{Top}\rightarrow\text{Right}\rightarrow\text{Bottom}\rightarrow\text{Left}
$$

The outer circle stays unchanged.

Only the inner dot moves.

### Next

$$
\boxed{\text{Top}}
$$

> [!important]
> Always check the **inside and outside separately**.

---

# 25. Outer Shape Movement

The inner object may remain fixed while the outer shape changes.

Example:

$$
\text{Circle}\rightarrow\text{Square}\rightarrow\text{Triangle}\rightarrow\text{Circle}
$$

The inner dot remains unchanged.

The outer boundary follows a repeating sequence.

---

# 26. Shape Transformation

## Example 15

Suppose:

$$
\text{Triangle}\rightarrow\text{Square}\rightarrow\text{Pentagon}\rightarrow\text{Hexagon}
$$

Number of sides:

$$
3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6
$$

Next:

$$
7
$$

### Answer

A seven-sided figure.

$$
\boxed{\text{Heptagon}}
$$

> [!important]
> When shapes change systematically, count the number of sides.

---

sides.

$$

lines.

Pattern:

$$
+2

Next:

$$
\boxed{10}

lines.

# 29. Dot Count Pattern

Suppose:

$$

Pattern:

dots.

# 30. Alternating Addition and Removal

## Example 16

Suppose the number of dots is:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 2,\ 3,\ 2,\ ?
$$

Pattern:

$$
+1,-1,+1,-1,+1
$$

Therefore:

$$

$$

dots.

# 31. Rotation + Position Movement

This is an important advanced pattern.

Suppose an arrow:

- rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise
- simultaneously moves one position clockwise around a square

Then each transition has two rules.

### Rule 1

$$
\text{Orientation}\rightarrow+90^\circ
$$

### Rule 2

$$
\text{Position}\rightarrow\text{next location}
$$

> [!important]
> When one property alone cannot explain the sequence, look for **two simultaneous transformations**.

---

# 32. Mirror Image Pattern

## Example 17

Suppose a figure alternates between:

$$
\text{Original}\rightarrow\text{Horizontal Mirror}\rightarrow\text{Original}\rightarrow\text{Horizontal Mirror}
$$

Then the next figure is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Original}}
$$

---

# 33. Vertical Mirror Pattern

A figure may transform:

$$
\text{Top-left}\leftrightarrow\text{Bottom-left}
$$

while maintaining left-right orientation.

This indicates vertical reflection.

> [!important]
> Horizontal and vertical mirror operations should not be confused.

### Horizontal Reflection

$$
\boxed{\text{Left}\leftrightarrow\text{Right}}
$$

### Vertical Reflection

$$
\boxed{\text{Top}\leftrightarrow\text{Bottom}}
$$

---

# 34. Rotation vs Mirror

This is a common exam trap.

### Rotation

The whole figure turns around a point.

### Mirror

The figure is reflected across an axis.

For example:

$$
\rightarrow
$$

after a $180^\circ$ rotation becomes:

$$
\leftarrow
$$

But reflection across a vertical mirror also changes:

$$
\rightarrow\leftrightarrow\leftarrow
$$

Therefore, inspect other internal features to distinguish them.

> [!warning]
> A figure can look similar after rotation and reflection. Check the position of asymmetric internal elements.

---

# 35. Movement of a Shaded Part

Suppose a square has one shaded corner.

The shaded corner moves:

$$
\text{Top-left}
\rightarrow
\text{Top-right}
\rightarrow
\text{Bottom-right}
\rightarrow
\text{Bottom-left}
$$

This is clockwise movement.

Next:

$$
\boxed{\text{Top-left}}
$$

---

# 36. Shaded Part with Rotation

Suppose a triangle contains one shaded side.

The triangle rotates:

$$
90^\circ
$$

and the shaded side rotates with it.

The entire figure therefore maintains its internal relationship.

> [!important]
> If the internal marking moves together with the figure, treat the figure as a single rotating object.

---

# 37. Shaded Part Moving Independently

Suppose the triangle remains fixed but the shaded side changes:

$$
\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Right}\rightarrow\text{Bottom}\rightarrow\text{Left}
$$

Here:

- Outer triangle = fixed
- Shaded side = moving

This is different from whole-figure rotation.

---

# 38. Figure Series Based on Count

Count every visible component.

Example:

$$
\triangle+1\text{ dot}
$$

then:

$$
\triangle+2\text{ dots}
$$

then:

$$
\triangle+3\text{ dots}
$$

Next:

$$
\boxed{\triangle+4\text{ dots}}
$$

### Components to Count

- Dots
- Lines
- Arrows
- Circles
- Triangles
- Squares
- Shaded regions
- Intersections

---

# 39. Figure Series Based on Decreasing Count

Suppose:

$$
6,\ 5,\ 4,\ 3,\ ?
$$

objects.

Pattern:

$$
-1
$$

Next:

$$
\boxed{2}
$$

---

# 40. Figure Series Based on Alternating Count

Suppose:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 3,\ 5,\ 4,\ ?
$$

Differences:

$$
+2,-1,+2,-1,+2
$$

Next:

$$
-1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3}
$$

---

# 41. Figure Series Based on Opposite Movement

Suppose two dots move in opposite directions.

Dot A:

$$
\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Right}
$$

Dot B:

$$
\text{Right}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Left}
$$

The two components move symmetrically.

> [!important]
> When two objects move in opposite directions, track each object separately.

---

# 42. Figure Series Based on Convergence and Divergence

Two objects may move:

$$
\text{Far}\rightarrow\text{Closer}\rightarrow\text{Together}
$$

and then:

$$
\text{Together}\rightarrow\text{Farther apart}
$$

This can create a repeating pattern.

### Recognition

If the distance between two objects changes systematically, track:

$$
\boxed{\text{Distance}}
$$

rather than their absolute positions.

---

# 43. Figure Series Based on Symmetry

A figure may alternate:

$$
\text{Symmetric}
\rightarrow
\text{Asymmetric}
\rightarrow
\text{Symmetric}
\rightarrow
\text{Asymmetric}
$$

Next:

$$
\boxed{\text{Symmetric}}
$$

### Check

- Vertical symmetry
- Horizontal symmetry
- Rotational symmetry

---

# 44. Figure Series Based on Number of Symmetry Axes

A sequence may change:

$$
1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ ?
$$

symmetry axes.

If the pattern increases by one:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

symmetry axes.

---

# 45. Figure Series Based on Rotation Amount

Not every rotation is $90^\circ$.

Example:

$$
0^\circ,\ 45^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 135^\circ,\ ?
$$

Difference:

$$
+45^\circ
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{180^\circ}
$$

---

# 46. Figure Series Based on Increasing Rotation

Example:

$$
0^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 270^\circ,\ ?
$$

This is not automatically a simple sequence.

Always calculate the actual angular change.

> [!warning]
> Do not assume every rotating figure follows $90^\circ$ rotation. The rotation amount itself may change.

---

# 47. Figure Series Based on Direction

Directions can be represented as:

$$
N,\ E,\ S,\ W
$$

A figure may follow:

$$
N\rightarrow E\rightarrow S\rightarrow W
$$

which is clockwise movement.

Another may follow:

$$
N\rightarrow W\rightarrow S\rightarrow E
$$

which is anticlockwise movement.

---

# 48. Figure Series Based on Number of Sides + Rotation

A complex series may combine:

- Shape changes
- Rotation

Example:

$$
\text{Triangle at }0^\circ
$$

$$
\text{Square at }90^\circ
$$

$$
\text{Pentagon at }180^\circ
$$

The number of sides increases by one while orientation changes.

> [!important]
> When the shape itself changes, check both:
>
> 1. What changed?
> 2. How did it change?

---

# 49. Figure Series Based on Addition of Lines

Suppose:

$$
1\text{ line}
\rightarrow
2\text{ lines}
\rightarrow
3\text{ lines}
\rightarrow
4\text{ lines}
$$

Next:

$$
\boxed{5\text{ lines}}
$$

But the **location and orientation** of the new line may also follow a pattern.

Therefore, count alone may not be sufficient.

---

# 50. Figure Series Based on Removing Lines

Suppose:

$$
5\rightarrow4\rightarrow3\rightarrow2
$$

lines.

Next:

$$
\boxed{1}
$$

But check which line disappears.

The removed line may rotate or move according to another rule.

---

# 51. Complex Pattern — Rotation + Addition

Suppose:

1. A triangle has one dot.
2. The triangle rotates $90^\circ$.
3. One dot is added.
4. The triangle rotates another $90^\circ$.
5. One dot is added.

Then the rules are:

$$
\text{Rotation}=90^\circ
$$

and:

$$
\text{Dots}=+1
$$

Next figure must satisfy **both**.

---

# 52. Complex Pattern — Rotation + Shading

Suppose:

1. Shape rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise.
2. Shading alternates filled/empty.

Then:

$$
\text{Orientation}: +90^\circ
$$

and:

$$
\text{Shading}: A,B,A,B
$$

The next figure must continue both rules.

---

# 53. Complex Pattern — Position + Count

Suppose a dot moves:

$$
\text{Left}\rightarrow\text{Center}\rightarrow\text{Right}
$$

while the number of dots increases:

$$
1\rightarrow2\rightarrow3
$$

The next figure should have:

- Dot position = next position according to movement
- Dot count = $4$

> [!important]
> Multi-rule questions are solved by creating a separate rule for each changing property.

---

# 54. Complex Pattern — Inner and Outer Rules

Suppose:

- Outer shape rotates clockwise.
- Inner dot moves anticlockwise.

Then two independent movements exist.

### Outer

$$
+90^\circ
$$

clockwise.

### Inner

$$
-90^\circ
$$

anticlockwise.

> [!important]
> In difficult figure series, always ask:
>
> **"Does the inner element follow the outer element, or does it have its own rule?"**

---

# 55. How to Solve Figure Series

## Step 1 — Compare Figure 1 and Figure 2

Do not look at all figures initially.

Ask:

> What changed?

---

## Step 2 — Identify the Changing Property

Check:

- Shape
- Rotation
- Direction
- Position
- Count
- Shading
- Size
- Inner element

---

## Step 3 — Check Whether the Same Change Repeats

Compare:

$$
F_1\rightarrow F_2
$$

and:

$$
F_2\rightarrow F_3
$$

If the transformation is identical, continue it.

---

## Step 4 — Separate Independent Properties

Example:

| Property | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Rotation | $+90^\circ$ |
| Dots | $+1$ |
| Shading | Alternates |
| Position | Clockwise |

---

## Step 5 — Check Alternation

Try:

$$
A,B,A,B
$$

or:

$$
A,B,C,A,B,C
$$

---

## Step 6 — Check Rotation

Test:

$$
45^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 180^\circ
$$

and direction:

- Clockwise
- Anticlockwise

---

## Step 7 — Count Elements

Count:

- Dots
- Lines
- Shapes
- Arrows
- Shaded regions

---

## Step 8 — Check Position

Track where the element moves.

---

## Step 9 — Check Inner Elements

The outer figure may remain constant while the inner element changes.

---

## Step 10 — Verify the Complete Transformation

The correct option must satisfy all important changes.

---

# 56. Pattern Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Same Shape, Different Direction

> [!important]
> If the shape remains the same but orientation changes, think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rotation}}
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Same Shape, Opposite Orientation

> [!important]
> If the figure repeatedly flips left-right, think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mirror}}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Object Count Changes

> [!important]
> If the number of dots/lines/shapes changes, count them.

Think:

$$
+1,\ -1,\ +2,\ -2
$$

or another numerical pattern.

---

## Pattern 4 — Every Other Figure Is Similar

> [!important]
> If:

$$
A,B,A,B,A
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Alternation}}
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Three-Figure Repetition

> [!important]
> If:

$$
A,B,C,A,B,C
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Cycle of 3}}
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Inner Object Changes

> [!important]
> If the outer boundary stays constant, inspect the inner object separately.

---

## Pattern 7 — Shading Alternates

> [!important]
> If filled and empty figures alternate:

$$
F,E,F,E
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Alternating shading}}
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Position Moves Around a Shape

> [!important]
> If an object moves around the corners:

$$
TL\rightarrow TR\rightarrow BR\rightarrow BL
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{\text{Clockwise movement}}
$$

---

## Pattern 9 — Two Things Change

> [!important]
> If both orientation and count change, create two separate rules.

---

## Pattern 10 — Figure Looks Almost Identical

> [!important]
> Check small details:

- Dot
- Line
- Shading
- Direction
- Position
- Number of sides

These often contain the actual pattern.

---

# 57. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Compare consecutive figures**

Start with:

$$
F_1\rightarrow F_2
$$

Do not immediately compare $F_1$ with $F_4$.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Count before interpreting**

If the figures contain multiple objects, count them first.

For example:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5
$$

may reveal the pattern immediately.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Track one component at a time**

Choose one component:

$$
\boxed{\text{Arrow}}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{\text{Dot}}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{\text{Shading}}
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — Use clockwise order**

For objects around a square:

$$
TL\rightarrow TR\rightarrow BR\rightarrow BL
$$

This makes movement recognition faster.

# 59. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Looking only at the shape

Two figures may have the same shape but different:

- Direction
- Shading
- Position
- Internal marks

Always check all components.

---

### Mistake 2 — Assuming every change is rotation

A figure that appears reversed may actually be mirrored.

Check asymmetric details.

---

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring the inner object

The outer shape may be fixed while the inner symbol follows the actual sequence.

---

### Mistake 4 — Counting but ignoring position

If:

$$
1,2,3,4
$$

dots appear, also check **where** the new dot is added.

---

### Mistake 5 — Missing alternating patterns

If no single transformation works, check:

$$
F_1,F_3,F_5
$$

and:

$$
F_2,F_4,F_6
$$

---

### Mistake 6 — Assuming only one rule

Advanced questions often use:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rotation}+\text{Shading}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{\text{Movement}+\text{Count}}
$$

---

### Mistake 7 — Ignoring direction

For arrows and asymmetric figures, direction is often the key.

---

### Mistake 8 — Overcomplicating a simple pattern

If every figure rotates $90^\circ$, do not search for a complicated mathematical rule.

---

### Mistake 9 — Choosing an option based on one property

An option may have the correct rotation but the wrong shading.

The correct answer must satisfy the complete pattern.

---

### Mistake 10 — Not comparing consecutive transformations

Always verify:

$$
F_1\rightarrow F_2
$$

$$
F_2\rightarrow F_3
$$

$$
F_3\rightarrow F_4
$$
