# Missing Number Series

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Missing Number Series** is a number sequence in which one or more terms are missing. The task is to identify the hidden pattern and calculate the missing value.

Example:

$$
3,\ 6,\ 9,\ ?,\ 15
$$

The pattern is:

$$
+3
$$

Therefore:

$$
9+3=12
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{12}
$$

The important skill is not simply calculating the missing number.

It is recognizing **which pattern controls the entire sequence**.

### Core Strategy

$$
\boxed{
Observe
\rightarrow
Find\ Pattern
\rightarrow
Verify\ Both\ Sides
\rightarrow
Calculate\ Missing\ Term
}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

A missing number series may contain:

- One missing term
- Multiple missing terms
- Missing terms at the beginning
- Missing terms in the middle
- Missing terms at the end
- Alternating patterns
- Difference patterns
- Ratio patterns
- Power patterns
- Mixed operations
- Recursive relationships

### Example

$$
5,\ 10,\ ?,\ 20,\ 25
$$

The difference is:

$$
+5
$$

Therefore:

$$
10+5=15
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{15}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula for every missing-number series.

The most important pattern equations are:

## Arithmetic Pattern

$$
\boxed{a_n=a_{n-1}+d}
$$

## Geometric Pattern

$$
\boxed{a_n=a_{n-1}\times r}
$$

## Increasing Difference

$$
\boxed{\Delta a_n=a_{n+1}-a_n}
$$

## Fibonacci-Type Pattern

$$
\boxed{a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}}
$$

## Mixed Operation

$$
\boxed{a_n=a_{n-1}\times k+c}
$$

The goal is to determine which rule explains the known terms and then use it to find the missing term.

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Never calculate the missing number before identifying the pattern.
2. Check the terms immediately before and after the blank.
3. Verify the proposed pattern across the entire sequence.
4. Start with simple patterns.
5. Check differences before complex operations.
6. Check ratios when numbers grow rapidly.
7. If the pattern alternates, separate odd and even positions.
8. Check second differences when first differences change regularly.
9. Check squares and cubes for familiar values.
10. Check recursive relationships when each term depends on previous terms.
11. For multiple blanks, solve the easiest blank first.
12. A correct pattern should explain all available terms.

### Recommended Pattern Priority

$$
\boxed{
Difference
\rightarrow
Ratio
\rightarrow
Second\ Difference
\rightarrow
Squares/Cubes
\rightarrow
Alternate\ Terms
\rightarrow
Mixed\ Operations
\rightarrow
Recursive\ Patterns
}
$$

# 13. Difference Table Method

For difficult series, build a difference table.

### Example

$$
2,\ 5,\ 9,\ 14,\ 20,\ ?
$$

First differences:

$$
3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6
$$

Second differences:

$$
1,\ 1,\ 1
$$

Therefore the next first difference is:

$$
7
$$

Hence:

$$
20+7=27
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{27}
$$

# 19. Pattern Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Constant Difference

> [!important]
> If consecutive terms differ by the same number, use arithmetic progression.

Example:

$$
4,\ 9,\ 14,\ ?,\ 24
$$

Difference:

$$
+5
$$

Missing:

$$
19
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Constant Ratio

> [!important]
> If each term is multiplied by the same number, use geometric progression.

Example:

$$
3,\ 6,\ ?,\ 24
$$

Ratio:

$$
\times2
$$

Answer:

$$
12
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Increasing Difference

> [!important]
> If differences are:

$$
+3,+4,+5,+6
$$

the next difference is:

$$
+7
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Decreasing Difference

> [!important]
> If differences are:

$$
-3,-4,-5,-6
$$

the next difference is:

$$
-7
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Second Difference

> [!important]
> If first differences change regularly, calculate the second differences.

Example:

$$
3,7,13,21
$$

First:

$$
4,6,8
$$

Second:

$$
2,2
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Square

> [!important]
> If terms resemble:

$$
1,4,9,16,25
$$

think:

$$
n^2
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Cube

> [!important]
> If terms resemble:

$$
1,8,27,64
$$

think:

$$
n^3
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Alternate Series

> [!important]
> If one pattern does not fit the whole sequence, separate odd and even positions.

---

## Pattern 9 — Mixed Operation

> [!important]
> If differences and ratios fail, test operations such as:

$$
\times2+1
$$

$$
\times3-2
$$

---

## Pattern 10 — Fibonacci

> [!important]
> If a term appears to depend on the previous two, test:

$$
a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}
$$

---

## Pattern 11 — Increasing Multiplier

> [!important]
> If the numbers grow very quickly, test:

$$
\times2,\times3,\times4,\times5
$$

---

## Pattern 12 — Power + Constant

> [!important]
> If terms are close to squares or cubes, test:

$$
n^2+c
$$

or:

$$
n^3+c
$$

# 20. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Look at both sides of the blank**

For:

$$
5,\ 9,\ ?,\ 17
$$

compare:

$$
9\rightarrow?
$$

and:

$$
?\rightarrow17
$$

If the difference is constant, the missing value is immediately determined.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Difference first**

For most moderate-growth series, calculate consecutive differences before trying complicated operations.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Ratio for fast growth**

If numbers approximately double, triple, or quadruple, test ratios.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — Split alternating terms**

For:

$$
2,5,4,10,6,15,?
$$

analyze:

$$
2,4,6,\ldots
$$

and:

$$
5,10,15,\ldots
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5 — Recognize familiar powers**

Memorize:

$$
1^2\text{ through }15^2
$$

and:

$$
1^3\text{ through }10^3
$$

This makes power-based missing terms much faster.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 6 — Verify after finding the answer**

Never stop after obtaining one possible number.

Substitute it back into the sequence and verify every neighboring relationship.

# 22. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Guessing from the nearest terms

A missing number may depend on the entire sequence, not just the two neighboring numbers.

---

### Mistake 2 — Stopping at the first possible pattern

Always verify:

$$
\boxed{\text{Pattern must fit all known terms}}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring alternating patterns

If the complete sequence does not work, split odd and even positions.

---

### Mistake 4 — Missing second differences

If:

$$
+3,+5,+7,+9
$$

appears, the actual rule may be:

$$
+2
$$

between differences.

---

### Mistake 5 — Using a complicated pattern unnecessarily

Prefer:

$$
+5
$$

over a complicated operation if the simple rule works everywhere.

---

### Mistake 6 — Forgetting squares and cubes

For values such as:

$$
9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36
$$

immediately check perfect squares.

---

### Mistake 7 — Ignoring the position of the blank

A missing term at the beginning may require working backward.

A missing term in the middle may allow verification from both directions.

---

### Mistake 8 — Not verifying the answer

After calculating the missing value, substitute it back into the series.

# 24. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

## Missing Number Series

Find the hidden rule and calculate the missing term.

### Fast Solving Order

$$
\boxed{
Difference
\rightarrow
Ratio
\rightarrow
Second\ Difference
\rightarrow
Squares/Cubes
\rightarrow
Alternating
\rightarrow
Mixed\ Operations
\rightarrow
Recursive
}
$$

### Ask These Questions

1. Is the difference constant?
2. Is the ratio constant?
3. Are the differences increasing?
4. Is there a second-difference pattern?
5. Are the terms squares?
6. Are the terms cubes?
7. Are odd and even positions separate sequences?
8. Is there an alternating operation?
9. Does each term depend on the previous two?
10. Is there a multiplication-plus/minus pattern?

### Golden Rules

$$
\boxed{\text{Find the rule before calculating}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Verify the rule on both sides}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Prefer the simplest valid pattern}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Never force a pattern}}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

**"Difference → Ratio → Difference of Differences → Alternate → Mixed."**

# One-Line Recognition

**When a number in a sequence is missing, identify the simplest rule that explains every known term, then use that rule to calculate and verify the missing value.**
