# Number Analogy

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Number Analogy** finds the mathematical relationship between two numbers and applies the same relationship to another number.

Basic pattern:

$$
A:B::C:D
$$

The goal is to discover the operation or mathematical rule that changes $A$ into $B$, then apply the **same rule** to $C$.

Example:

**4 : 16 :: 7 : ?**

Since:

$$
4^2=16
$$

Apply the same rule:

$$
7^2=49
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{49}
$$

### Intuition

Do not immediately calculate randomly.

Ask:

> "What operation changes the first number into the second?"

Check in this order:

1. Addition/subtraction
2. Multiplication/division
3. Square/cube
4. Multiplication + addition/subtraction
5. Power relationships
6. Digit-based operations
7. Prime/factor relationships

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Number analogy is a reasoning problem where numbers are connected by a mathematical relationship.

### Standard Form

$$
A:B::C:D
$$

This means:

$$
f(A)=B
$$

Therefore:

$$
f(C)=D
$$

### Example

**8 : 24 :: 5 : ?**

Relationship:

$$
8\times3=24
$$

Apply the same relationship:

$$
5\times3=15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{15}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula for number analogy.

The general rule is:

$$
\boxed{f(A)=B}
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{f(C)=D}
$$

### Common Mathematical Relationships

| Pattern | Formula |
|---|---|
| Addition | $B=A+k$ |
| Subtraction | $B=A-k$ |
| Multiplication | $B=A\times k$ |
| Division | $B=A\div k$ |
| Square | $B=A^2$ |
| Cube | $B=A^3$ |
| Square + constant | $B=A^2+k$ |
| Cube + constant | $B=A^3+k$ |
| Multiply + constant | $B=A\times k+c$ |
| Divide + constant | $B=A\div k+c$ |
| Product of consecutive numbers | $B=A(A+1)$ |
| Product of previous/current number | $B=A(A-1)$ |

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. The same mathematical rule must work for both pairs.
2. Prefer the **simplest valid rule**.
3. Always check whether the operation is performed on the entire number or individual digits.
4. Addition and subtraction should usually be checked before complicated operations.
5. Multiplication and division are common in basic questions.
6. Squares and cubes are common when numbers change rapidly.
7. Some questions use two-step operations.
8. Digit-based patterns may appear in advanced questions.
9. Do not assume a pattern after checking only one pair.
10. Verify the rule against the complete analogy.

### Direction Matters

For:

**5 : 20**

the relationship is:

$$
5\times4=20
$$

It does not automatically mean:

$$
20\div4=5
$$

The question asks you to apply the transformation from the **first number to the second number**.

# 9. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Check the difference first**

For:

**12 : 19**

calculate:

$$
19-12=7
$$

If the same constant works naturally, test addition.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Check multiplication/division next**

If the numbers grow significantly, test:

$$
B\div A
$$

Example:

**8 : 40**

$$
40\div8=5
$$

So the rule is likely:

$$
A\times5
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Check squares and cubes**

If the output is much larger than the input, immediately test:

$$
A^2
$$

and:

$$
A^3
$$

Example:

**7 : 49**

Immediately recognize:

$$
7^2=49
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — For two-digit numbers, test digits**

If simple operations fail, check:

- sum of digits
- product of digits
- difference of digits
- reversed number
- square of digits

Example:

**23 : 29**

Since:

$$
23+(2\times3)=29
$$

the relationship is digit-based.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5 — Look for two-step operations**

If direct operations do not work, test:

$$
\times k+c
$$

or:

$$
\times k-c
$$

Example:

**6 : 20**

$$
6\times3+2=20
$$

# 10. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Constant Addition

> [!important]
> If the difference between the numbers is constant, think **Addition/Subtraction**.

Example:

$$
8\rightarrow13
$$

$$
13-8=5
$$

Rule:

$$
A+5
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Constant Multiplication

> [!important]
> If the output is a simple multiple of the input, think **Multiplication**.

Example:

$$
9\rightarrow45
$$

$$
45\div9=5
$$

Rule:

$$
A\times5
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Square

> [!important]
> If the second number equals the square of the first, think **Square**.

$$
A\rightarrow A^2
$$

Example:

$$
8\rightarrow64
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Cube

> [!important]
> If the output is approximately the cube of the input, think **Cube**.

$$
A\rightarrow A^3
$$

Example:

$$
4\rightarrow64
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Square + Constant

> [!important]
> If the output is close to a perfect square, compare it with $A^2$.

Example:

$$
6^2=36
$$

If the output is $39$:

$$
39-36=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
A^2+3
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Cube + Constant

> [!important]
> If the output is close to a perfect cube, compare it with $A^3$.

Example:

$$
5^3=125
$$

If output is $128$:

$$
128-125=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
A^3+3
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Consecutive Product

> [!important]
> If the output is close to $A(A+1)$ or $A(A-1)$, test consecutive multiplication.

Examples:

$$
5\times6=30
$$

$$
7\times8=56
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Digit Sum

> [!important]
> If the numbers are two-digit numbers and simple operations fail, check the sum of digits.

For $24$:

$$
2+4=6
$$

Then test whether:

$$
24+6=30
$$

---

## Pattern 9 — Digit Product

> [!important]
> If the output differs from the original number by the product of its digits, think **Digit Product**.

For $23$:

$$
2\times3=6
$$

$$
23+6=29
$$

# 12. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Finding a rule that works only once

Suppose:

$$
4\rightarrow16
$$

You might think:

$$
\times4
$$

But if the complete analogy is:

**4 : 16 :: 7 : ?**

then $7\times4=28$, while $7^2=49$.

Both may appear plausible from one pair.

### Correct Approach

Use the **simplest intended relationship** and check all available answer choices.

---

### Mistake 2 — Using multiple unnecessary operations

Do not invent:

$$
A\times7+13-4
$$

when a simple rule such as:

$$
A^2
$$

works.

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting digit patterns

For two-digit numbers, if basic arithmetic does not work, check:

$$
\text{Digit Sum}
$$

and:

$$
\text{Digit Product}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing square and cube

Remember:

$$
5^2=25
$$

but:

$$
5^3=125
$$

Check the growth of the number.

---

### Mistake 5 — Reversing the operation

If:

$$
8\rightarrow40
$$

the direct relationship is:

$$
8\times5=40
$$

Do not unnecessarily think:

$$
40\div5=8
$$

The analogy direction is first number → second number.

---

### Mistake 6 — Ignoring answer choices

In difficult aptitude questions, answer choices can help identify the intended pattern.

Use them for **verification**, not random guessing.

# 14. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Number Analogy

$$
A:B::C:D
$$

Find the mathematical transformation:

$$
A\rightarrow B
$$

Then apply it:

$$
C\rightarrow D
$$

### Fast Checking Order

1. Addition
2. Subtraction
3. Multiplication
4. Division
5. Square
6. Cube
7. Two-step operation
8. Consecutive multiplication
9. Digit sum
10. Digit product
11. Other digit-based patterns

### Most Important Rule

> **Find the simplest valid mathematical relationship.**

### Golden Memory Trick

**"First find the operation, then repeat the operation."**

# One-Line Recognition

**When you see `A : B :: C : ?`, determine the mathematical rule that changes A into B and apply exactly the same rule to C.**
