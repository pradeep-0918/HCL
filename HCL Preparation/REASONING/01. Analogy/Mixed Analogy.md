# Mixed Analogy

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Mixed Analogy** combines different types of relationships such as words, numbers, letters, symbols, or multiple transformations in a single analogy problem.

Basic form:

$$
A:B::C:D
$$

The challenge is to identify **what kind of relationship** is being used before applying it.

A mixed analogy may involve:

- Word → Word
- Number → Number
- Letter → Letter
- Word → Number
- Letter → Number
- Number → Letter
- Word → Letter
- Multiple-step transformations
- Arithmetic + alphabet positions
- Meaning + numerical relationship

### Core Strategy

Think in this order:

$$
\boxed{\text{Identify Type}\rightarrow\text{Find Relationship}\rightarrow\text{Apply Relationship}\rightarrow\text{Verify}}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

In ordinary analogy questions, both pairs may belong to the same category.

For example:

**Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : School**

But mixed analogy can combine different forms.

Example:

**A : 1 :: C : ?**

Since:

$$
A=1
$$

and:

$$
C=3
$$

the answer is:

$$
\boxed{3}
$$

Here, the relationship is:

$$
Letter\rightarrow Alphabet\ Position
$$

Another example:

**B : 4 :: D : ?**

If the rule is:

$$
2^2=4
$$

then:

$$
4^2=16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16}
$$

The important skill is recognizing the underlying relationship.

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no single formula for mixed analogy.

The general rule remains:

$$
\boxed{f(A)=B}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{f(C)=D}
$$

### Common Mixed Relationships

| Relationship | Example |
|---|---|
| Letter → Position | A → 1 |
| Position → Letter | 1 → A |
| Letter → Number | C → 3 |
| Number → Letter | 5 → E |
| Word → Number | FIVE → 4 letters |
| Number → Word | 4 → FOUR |
| Word → Function | Pen → Write |
| Number → Square | 5 → 25 |
| Letter → Reverse Letter | A → Z |
| Word → Category | Dog → Animal |
| Symbol → Meaning | $+$ → Addition |
| Multiple transformation | Letter → Position → Operation |

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. First identify the **type of relationship**.
2. Do not assume that both sides use the same representation.
3. Convert letters to positions when needed.
4. Convert words to numerical properties when required.
5. Check whether the relationship is semantic or mathematical.
6. Look for the simplest transformation.
7. Multi-step relationships should be verified carefully.
8. In multiple-choice questions, eliminate options that belong to the wrong relationship type.
9. Never force a mathematical pattern when a word relationship is clearly intended.
10. Never assume a word relationship when the numbers clearly indicate arithmetic.

### Important Principle

> **The representation may change, but the relationship must remain consistent.**

# 11. Complex Mixed Analogy

## Example 19 — Multiple Operations

### Question

**B : 6 :: E : ?**

### Pattern Recognition

B = position 2.

\boxed{15}

# 12. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1 — Identify the data type first**

Ask:

> Is this a word, letter, number, symbol, or combination?

This immediately narrows the possible patterns.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2 — Convert letters to numbers**

When letters and numbers appear together:

$$
A=1,\ldots,Z=26
$$

This often exposes the hidden relationship.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3 — Check simple properties of words**

For word-number questions, quickly test:

- Number of letters
- Number of vowels
- Number of consonants
- Number of repeated letters
- Alphabet-position sum

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4 — Check square/cube after position conversion**

For:

**C : 9**

immediately test:

$$
C=3
$$

$$
3^2=9
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5 — Use the simplest valid relationship**

Try in this order:

1. Direct conversion
2. Addition/subtraction
3. Multiplication/division
4. Square/cube
5. Digit or letter properties
6. Multi-step transformation

# 13. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Letter → Position

> [!important]
> If a letter is paired with a number from 1 to 26, think **Alphabet Position**.

Example:

$$
D\rightarrow4
$$

---

## Pattern 2 — Number → Letter

> [!important]
> If numbers from 1 to 26 are paired with letters, convert the number directly to a letter.

Example:

$$
15\rightarrow O
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Letter → Square

> [!important]
> If a letter is paired with a perfect square, convert the letter to its position and test the square.

Example:

$$
E=5
$$

$$
5^2=25
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Letter → Cube

> [!important]
> If the number looks like a cube, convert the letter to its position and test:

$$
n^3
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Word → Length

> [!important]
> If a word is paired with a small number, check its number of letters first.

Example:

$$
APPLE\rightarrow5
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Word → Vowel Count

> [!important]
> If a word is paired with a small number and word length does not work, count vowels.

$$
A,E,I,O,U
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Reverse Alphabet

> [!important]
> If letters map to their opposite alphabet positions:

$$
A\leftrightarrow Z
$$

$$
B\leftrightarrow Y
$$

use:

$$
27-n
$$

---

## Pattern 8 — Multi-Step Relationship

> [!important]
> If the output cannot be obtained directly, test:

$$
Letter\rightarrow Position\rightarrow Operation
$$

Example:

$$
C\rightarrow3\rightarrow9
$$

# 15. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Assuming every number represents alphabet position

A number such as 25 could mean:

$$
Y
$$

but it could also represent:

$$
5^2
$$

or another relationship.

Always inspect the complete pair.

---

### Mistake 2 — Ignoring word properties

If:

**APPLE : 2**

do not immediately assume a mathematical relationship.

Check:

$$
A,E=2\ vowels
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Using an unnecessarily complicated rule

If:

$$
C=3
$$

and:

$$
C\rightarrow3
$$

there is no reason to introduce additional operations.

---

### Mistake 4 — Assuming one relationship from one pair

A single pair can sometimes support multiple interpretations.

Use:

- answer choices
- question context
- consistency
- simplest valid rule

to determine the intended pattern.

---

### Mistake 5 — Confusing word length and vowel count

Example:

**ORANGE**

Length:

$$
6
$$

Vowels:

$$
3
$$

These are different properties.

---

### Mistake 6 — Forgetting multi-step transformations

For:

**C : 9**

the relationship may be:

$$
C\rightarrow3\rightarrow9
$$

rather than a direct letter-number mapping.
