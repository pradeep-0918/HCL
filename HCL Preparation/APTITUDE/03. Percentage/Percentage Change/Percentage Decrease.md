# Percentage Decrease

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> **Percentage decrease** tells us how much a quantity has decreased relative to its **original value**.

The fundamental formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Decrease}
=
\frac{\text{Decrease}}
{\text{Original Value}}
\times100
}
$$

where:

$$
\text{Decrease}
=
\text{Original Value}-\text{New Value}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Decrease}
=
\frac{\text{Original}-\text{New}}
{\text{Original}}
\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. Golden Rule

> [!important]
> For percentage decrease, the denominator is **always the original value**.

Example:

Original = `100`

New = `80`

Decrease:

$$
100-80=20
$$

Percentage decrease:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 3. Three-Step Method

### Step 1 — Find the decrease

$$
\text{Decrease}
=
\text{Original}-\text{New}
$$

### Step 2 — Divide by original

$$
\frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original}}
$$

### Step 3 — Multiply by 100

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original}}\times100
}
$$

---

# 9. Decrease Multiplier

If a quantity decreases by `x%`, the new value is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}

$$

This is one of the most important aptitude formulas.

# 16. Finding the Decrease Amount

If the original value is `N` and the decrease is `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Decrease}
=
\frac{x}{100}\times N
}
$$

Example:

Find the decrease when `800` decreases by `15%`.

$$
\frac{15}{100}\times800
$$

$$
=\boxed{120}
$$

---

# 17. Finding the New Value

Subtract the decrease:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}-\text{Decrease}
}
$$

Example:

Original:

$$
800
$$

Decrease:

$$
120
$$

Therefore:

$$
800-120
=
\boxed{680}
$$

---

# 18. Direct Formula

Instead of finding the decrease separately:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
N\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

`800` decreases by `15%`:

$$
800(0.85)
=
\boxed{680}
$$

---

# 19. Reverse Decrease Problem

Suppose a number is decreased by `20%` and becomes `400`.

Find the original value.

A `20%` decrease means:

$$
100\%-20\%=80\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Original}\times0.8=400
$$

So:

$$
\text{Original}
=
\frac{400}{0.8}
$$

$$
=\boxed{500}
$$

---

# 20. Reverse Decrease Formula

If final value is `F` after a decrease of `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{F}{1-x/100}
}
$$

Equivalent:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{100F}{100-x}
}
$$

# 26. Important Trap — Wrong Denominator

Original = `100`

New = `80`

Decrease:

$$
20
$$

Correct:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Wrong:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
25\%
$$

Why?

Because `80` is the new value, not the original value.

# 38. Decrease and Fixed Expenditure

A common aptitude pattern:

> Price decreases by `20%`. If expenditure remains constant, what happens to consumption?

Price multiplier:

$$
0.8
$$

Since:

$$
\text{Price}\times\text{Consumption}
=
\text{constant}
$$

new consumption multiplier:

$$
\frac1{0.8}
=
1.25
$$

Therefore consumption increases by:

$$
25\%
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\text{ price decrease}
\rightarrow
25\%\text{ consumption increase}
}
$$

---

# 39. Decrease and Fixed Expenditure — General Formula

If price decreases by `x%`, consumption must increase by:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price decreases by `25%`.

Consumption increase:

$$
\frac{25}{75}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 40. Increase and Fixed Expenditure

If price increases by `x%`, consumption must decrease by:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price increases by `25%`.

Consumption decrease:

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

This is an important reverse-percentage relationship.

# 42. Successive Decrease

If a value decreases by `a%` and then by `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Multiplier}
=
\left(1-\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac b{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 43. Example — 10% Then 20%

Initial:

$$
100
$$

After `10%` decrease:

$$
100\times0.9
=
90
$$

After `20%` decrease:

$$
90\times0.8
=
72
$$

Final:

$$
72
$$

Net decrease:

$$
100-72
=
28
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 44. Successive Decrease Formula

For decreases of `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net Decrease}
=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20-\frac{10\times20}{100}
$$

$$
=30-2
$$

$$
=\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 45. Important Trap — Adding Decreases

Wrong:

$$
20\%+20\%=40\%
$$

Correct:

$$
0.8\times0.8
=
0.64
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{36\%\text{ net decrease}}
$$

---

# 46. Decrease Then Increase

Suppose a value decreases by `20%`, then increases by `20%`.

Multipliers:

$$
0.8\times1.2
=
0.96
$$

Therefore final value is:

$$
96\%
$$

of original.

Net change:

$$
100\%-96\%
=
\boxed{4\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

> [!important]
> Equal percentage decrease and increase do **not** cancel each other.

---

# 47. Why Equal Increase and Decrease Don't Cancel

Start with:

$$
100
$$

Decrease `20%`:

$$
100\rightarrow80
$$

Increase `20%` of `80`:

$$
80\rightarrow96
$$

Final:

$$
96
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

# 49. Reverse Decrease Formula

If A is `x% less than B`, then B is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100\%
}
$$

more than A.

Example:

A is `25% less than B`.

Then:

$$
\frac{25}{75}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

Therefore B is `33⅓% more than A`.

# 52. Advanced Pattern — Same Absolute Decrease

Suppose:

A decreases from `1000` to `800`.

Decrease:

$$
200
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Now another quantity decreases from `500` by the same `200`.

Percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{500}\times100
=
40\%
$$

Therefore:

> [!important]
> **Same absolute decrease can produce different percentage decreases.**

# 53. Percentage Decrease vs Percentage Points

Suppose a rate decreases from:

$$
80\%
$$

to:

$$
60\%
$$

Percentage-point decrease:

$$
80-60
=
\boxed{20\text{ percentage points}}
$$

Percentage decrease relative to the original rate:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\text{ percentage points}
\ne
25\%\text{ decrease}
}
$$

---

# 54. Important Trap — Percentage vs Percentage Points

If an interest rate falls from `10%` to `8%`:

Percentage-point decrease:

$$
10-8
=
\boxed{2\text{ percentage points}}
$$

Percentage decrease:

$$
\frac{2}{10}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 60. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Decrease}
=
\frac{\text{Original}-\text{New}}
{\text{Original}}\times100
}
$$

### Decrease Multiplier

$$
\boxed{
x\%\text{ decrease}
\rightarrow
1-\frac{x}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
20\%\rightarrow0.8
$$

### New Value

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}\times
\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

### Reverse

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{\text{New}}
{1-x/100}
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Decrease = Original − New. Percentage decrease = Decrease ÷ Original × 100.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **Whenever a quantity falls, compare the amount lost with the ORIGINAL quantity—not the new quantity.**
