# Percentage Increase

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> **Percentage increase** tells us how much a quantity has increased **relative to its original value**.

The fundamental formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Increase}
=
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original Value}}
\times100
}
$$

where:

$$
\text{Increase}
=
\text{New Value}-\text{Original Value}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Increase}
=
\frac{\text{New}-\text{Original}}
{\text{Original}}
\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. The Golden Rule

> [!important]
> For percentage increase, the denominator is **always the original value**.

Example:

Original = `100`

New = `120`

Increase:

$$
120-100=20
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 3. Three-Step Method

For any percentage increase problem:

### Step 1 — Find the increase

$$
\text{Increase}=\text{New}-\text{Original}
$$

### Step 2 — Divide by original

$$
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}
$$

### Step 3 — Multiply by 100

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}\times100
}
$$

# 9. Increase Multiplier

If a quantity increases by `x%`, the new value is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}
\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)

$$

This is one of the most important aptitude patterns.

# 14. Increase as a Fraction

A percentage increase can be converted into a fraction.

Example:

`20% increase`

$$
20\%=\frac15
$$

Therefore the increase is:

$$
\frac15
$$

of the original.

New value:

$$
1+\frac15
=
\frac65
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\text{ increase}\rightarrow\times\frac65
}
$$

# 17. Finding the Increase Amount

If the original value is `N` and the increase is `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Increase}
=
\frac{x}{100}\times N
}
$$

Example:

Find the increase when `800` increases by `15%`.

$$
\frac{15}{100}\times800
$$

$$
=\boxed{120}
$$

---

# 18. Finding the New Value

Add the increase to the original:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}
+
\text{Increase}
}
$$

Example:

Original = `800`

Increase = `120`

Therefore:

$$
800+120
=
\boxed{920}
$$

---

# 19. Direct Formula

Instead of calculating the increase separately:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
N\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

`800` increased by `15%`:

$$
800(1.15)
=
\boxed{920}
$$

---

# 20. Reverse Problem

Suppose the new value is known.

Example:

A number is increased by `20%` and becomes `600`.

Find the original value.

Since:

$$
20\%\text{ increase}
\rightarrow
120\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Original}\times1.2=600
$$

So:

$$
\text{Original}
=
\frac{600}{1.2}
$$

$$
=\boxed{500}
$$

---

# 21. Reverse Increase Formula

If final value is `F` after an increase of `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{F}{1+x/100}
}
$$

Equivalent:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{100F}{100+x}
}
$$

---

# 24. Increase in Terms of Original

If:

$$
A\rightarrow B
$$

and A is the original value:

$$
\boxed{
B=A\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac BA=1+\frac{x}{100}
}
$$

# 25. Finding Percentage Increase From Multiplier

If:

$$
\text{New}=1.25\times\text{Original}
$$

then:

$$
1.25=125\%
$$

Therefore increase:

$$
125\%-100\%
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Increase \%}
=
(\text{Multiplier}-1)\times100
}
$$

---

# 27. Percentage Increase Using Ratio

If:

$$
\text{New}:\text{Original}=5:4
$$

then increase:

$$
5-4=1
$$

Relative increase:

$$
\frac14\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

General pattern:

If:

$$
\text{New}:\text{Original}=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Increase \%}
=
\frac{a-b}{b}\times100
}
$$

---

# 28. Important Trap — Wrong Denominator

Original = `80`

New = `100`

Increase:

$$
20
$$

Correct:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Wrong:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Why is it wrong?

Because `100` is the **new value**, not the original value.

> [!important]
> **Percentage increase always uses the original value as denominator.**

---

# 29. Increase vs New Value

If a value increases by `25%`:

The increase is:

$$
25\%
$$

of the original.

The new value is:

$$
100\%+25\%
=
\boxed{125\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
25\%\text{ increase}\ne125\%\text{ increase}
}
$$

The new value is `125% of original`.

---

# 30. Important Interpretation

If:

$$
A=125\%\text{ of B}
$$

then:

$$
A
=
B+25\%\text{ of B}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A\text{ is 25% more than B}
}
$$

# 41. Comparing Two Percentage Increases

Suppose:

- Product A increases from `100` to `120`.
- Product B increases from `200` to `240`.

A:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
20\%
$$

B:

$$
\frac{40}{200}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Both increased by 20\%}
}
$$

Even though their absolute increases are different.

---

# 42. Same Percentage Increase

If two quantities both increase by `x%`, their ratio remains unchanged.

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

Both increase by `20%`.

New A:

$$
2\times1.2=2.4
$$

New B:

$$
3\times1.2=3.6
$$

Ratio:

$$
2.4:3.6
=
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 43. Increase Does Not Mean Same Absolute Amount

Suppose:

A = `100`

B = `500`

Both increase by `20%`.

A increase:

$$
20
$$

B increase:

$$
100
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Same percentage}\ne\text{same absolute increase}
}
$$

# 44. Successive Increase

If a value increases by `a%` and then by `b%`, the changes are applied one after another.

Use multipliers:

$$
\boxed{
\left(1+\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1+\frac b{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 45. Example — 10% Then 20%

Initial:

$$
100
$$

After `10%` increase:

$$
100\times1.1=110
$$

After `20%` increase:

$$
110\times1.2=132
$$

Net increase:

$$
132-100=32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{32\%}
$$

---

# 46. Successive Increase Formula

For increases of `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net Increase}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20+\frac{10\times20}{100}
$$

$$
=30+2
$$

$$
=\boxed{32\%}
$$

---

# 47. Three Successive Increases

For increases of `a%`, `b%`, and `c%`:

Use multiplication:

$$
\boxed{
\left(1+\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1+\frac b{100}\right)
\left(1+\frac c{100}\right)
}
$$

This is safer than trying to memorize a long expanded formula.

---

# 55. Increase and Consumption

If price increases by `20%` and expenditure must remain constant, consumption must decrease.

This is a common aptitude application.

Price multiplier:

$$
1.2
$$

To keep expenditure constant:

$$
\text{New Consumption}
=
\frac{\text{Old Consumption}}{1.2}
$$

This gives a decrease of:

$$
1-\frac1{1.2}
=
\frac16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
16\frac23\%\text{ decrease}
}
$$

This pattern is important for placement aptitude.

# 57. Percentage Increase With Fractions

Suppose:

$$
A=\frac34B
$$

and A increases to B.

Increase:

$$
B-\frac34B
=

$$

# 64. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Increase}
=
\frac{\text{New}-\text{Original}}
{\text{Original}}
\times100
}
$$

### Increase Multiplier

$$
\boxed{
x\%\text{ increase}
\rightarrow
1+\frac{x}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
20\%\rightarrow1.2
$$

### New Value

$$
\boxed{
\text{New}
=
\text{Original}\times
\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

### Reverse

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{\text{New}}
{1+x/100}
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Increase = New − Original. Percentage increase = Increase ÷ Original × 100.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **Whenever a quantity grows, compare the amount of growth with the ORIGINAL quantity—not the new quantity.**
