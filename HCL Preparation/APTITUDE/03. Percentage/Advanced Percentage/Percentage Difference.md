# Percentage Difference

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Percentage difference** is generally used to compare two values when neither value is treated as the definite "true" value.

The standard formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Difference}
=
\frac{|A-B|}
{\frac{A+B}{2}}
\times100
}
$$

Simplified:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Difference}
=
\frac{2|A-B|}{A+B}\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. Difference vs Percentage Difference

Suppose:

$$
A=120
$$

$$
B=100
$$

Absolute difference:

$$
120-100
=
\boxed{20}
$$

Percentage difference:

$$
\frac{20}{(120+100)/2}\times100
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{220}{2}
=
110
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{20}{110}\times100
=
\boxed{18.18\%}
$$

---

# 3. Why Use the Average?

Percentage difference treats both values symmetrically.

For:

$$
A=120,\quad B=100
$$

average:

$$
110
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Difference is measured relative to their average}
}
$$

This is different from ordinary percentage increase/decrease, where one value is specifically the base.

---

# 4. Percentage Difference Formula

For two values `A` and `B`:

$$
\boxed{
PD=
\frac{|A-B|}
{(A+B)/2}\times100
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
PD=
\frac{2|A-B|}
{A+B}\times100
}
$$

---

# 7. Percentage Difference vs Percentage Increase

This distinction is extremely important.

Suppose:

$$
A=100
$$

$$
B=120
$$

### Percentage increase from 100 to 120

$$
\frac{120-100}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

### Percentage difference between 100 and 120

$$
\frac{20}{110}\times100
=
\boxed{18.18\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\ne18.18\%
}
$$

---

# 8. Percentage Difference vs Percentage Decrease

For:

$$
100\rightarrow120
$$

Increase relative to 100:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

But decrease from 120 back to 100:

$$
\frac{20}{120}\times100
=
\boxed{16.67\%}
$$

Percentage difference:

$$
\boxed{18.18\%}
$$

Three different values arise because the denominator is different.

---

# 9. The Three Important Denominators

For two values `A` and `B`:

### Percentage increase from A to B

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

### Percentage decrease from B to A

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{B}\times100
}
$$

### Percentage difference

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{(A+B)/2}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> **Always identify the denominator before calculating.**

---

Values:

$$
50

$$

\boxed{25\%}
$$

$$

### Percentage difference

Average:

$$

$$

$$

=
\boxed{22.22\%}
$$

---

# 11. When to Use Percentage Difference

Use percentage difference when:

- comparing two measurements
- comparing two experimental values
- comparing two prices without choosing a base
- comparing two estimates
- comparing two results
- neither value is explicitly the original value
- neither value is explicitly the true value

---

# 12. When NOT to Use It

Do not automatically use percentage difference when the question says:

- increased from
- decreased from
- increased by
- decreased by
- original value
- final value
- actual value
- true value
- percentage error

Those situations usually require a different denominator.

---

# 13. Percentage Error vs Difference

### Percentage Error

Used when one value is the known true value.

$$
\boxed{
\text{Error \%}
=
\frac{|A-T|}{T}\times100
}
$$

### Percentage Difference

Used when comparing two values symmetrically.

$$
\boxed{
\text{Difference \%}
=
\frac{|A-B|}
{(A+B)/2}\times100
}
$$

# 20. Shortcut When Values Are Close

If two values are close, percentage difference can be calculated quickly:

$$
\boxed{
PD\approx
\frac{\text{Difference}}
{\text{Average}}\times100
}
$$

This is already the exact standard formula; the shortcut is simply to calculate the average first.

---

# 21. Ratio-Based Method

$$
A:B=4:5

$$
A=4,\quad B=5

1

$$
\frac{4+5}{2}
=
4.5

Percentage difference:

$$
\frac1{4.5}\times100

\boxed{22.22\%}

# 26. Percentage Difference and Equal Values

If:

$$
A=B
$$

then:

$$
|A-B|=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{PD=0\%}
$$

---

# 27. Percentage Difference Is Symmetric

If:

$$
A=100,\quad B=120
$$

then:

$$
PD=18.18\%
$$

Switching the order:

$$
A=120,\quad B=100
$$

still gives:

$$
\boxed{18.18\%}
$$

Because:

$$
|A-B|=|B-A|
$$

and:

$$
A+B=B+A
$$

---

# 28. Percentage Increase Is Not Symmetric

From `100 → 120`:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

From `120 → 100`:

$$
\boxed{16.67\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

Therefore ordinary percentage change depends on direction.

---

# 29. Important Comparison Table

| Concept | Formula | Symmetric? |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage Increase | `(New−Old)/Old ×100` | ❌ |
| Percentage Decrease | `(Old−New)/Old ×100` | ❌ |
| Percentage Error | `|Approx−True|/True ×100` | ❌ |
| Percentage Difference | `|A−B|/Average ×100` | ✅ |

---

# 30. Finding One Value From Percentage Difference

Suppose:

Percentage difference between `A` and `100` is `20%`.

Let:

$$
A=x
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{|x-100|}
{(x+100)/2}\times100
=
20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{2|x-100|}{x+100}
=
0.2
$$

This produces two possible values because percentage difference does not specify which value is larger.

---

# 31. Example — One Value Unknown

Suppose one value is `100` and percentage difference is `20%`.

Assume:

$$
x>100
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{2(x-100)}{x+100}=0.2
$$

Multiply:

$$
2x-200=0.2x+20
$$

$$
1.8x=220
$$

$$
x=\boxed{122.22}
$$

If instead `x<100`, another value is possible.

> [!important]
> Percentage difference alone usually does not tell you which value is larger.

---

# 36. General Formula

If average = `M` and absolute difference = `D`:

$$
\boxed{
A=M+\frac D2
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
B=M-\frac D2
}
$$

---

# 37. Using Percentage Difference

If average = `M` and percentage difference = `p%`:

Difference:

$$
D=M\times\frac p{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=M+\frac{Mp}{200}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
B=M-\frac{Mp}{200}
}
$$

---

# 44. Common Trap — "A Is x% More Than B"

If the question says:

> A is `20%` more than B.

This is **not** asking percentage difference.

It means:

$$
A=1.2B
$$

The percentage difference would instead be:

$$
\frac{0.2B}{1.1B}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{18.18\%}
$$

---

# 45. Common Trap — "A Is x% Less Than B"

If:

> A is `20% less than B`.

Then:

$$
A=0.8B
$$

This is a percentage decrease, not automatically percentage difference.

Percentage difference:

$$
\frac{0.2B}{0.9B}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{22.22\%}
$$

# 48. Maximum Percentage Difference

For positive values where one value approaches zero and the other remains positive:

$$
PD
=
\frac{2|A-B|}{A+B}\times100
$$

The percentage difference approaches:

$$
\boxed{200\%}
$$

It cannot exceed `200%` for positive values under this standard formula.

---

# 49. Percentage Difference vs Relative Difference

Percentage difference is often also called **percent difference**.

Relative difference can be represented as:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{|A-B|}{(A+B)/2}
}
$$

Percentage difference is this value multiplied by `100`.

---

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Using one of the values as denominator when the question specifically asks for percentage difference.

Correct denominator:

$$
\frac{A+B}{2}
$$

### Mistake 2

Confusing percentage difference with percentage increase.

### Mistake 3

Confusing percentage difference with percentage error.

### Mistake 4

Forgetting the absolute value.

### Mistake 5

Using the sum instead of the average.

Wrong:

$$
\frac{|A-B|}{A+B}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\frac{|A-B|}{(A+B)/2}\times100
$$

\boxed{

\boxed{

$$
\boxed{

}
$$
