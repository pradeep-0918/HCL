# Expenditure

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Expenditure is the **amount of money spent** on a product, service, or group of items.

The fundamental relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expenditure}
=
\text{Price}\times\text{Quantity}
}
$$

This is the most important formula in this topic.

---

# 2. Basic Formula

If:

- Price = `P`
- Quantity = `Q`
- Expenditure = `E`

then:

$$
\boxed{E=P\times Q}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P=\frac{E}{Q}}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{E}{P}}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Example

Price of rice:

$$
₹50/\text{kg}
$$

Quantity purchased:

$$
20\text{ kg}
$$

Expenditure:

$$
50\times20
=
\boxed{₹1,000}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Quantity

Expenditure:

$$
₹2,400
$$

Price:

$$
₹60/\text{kg}
$$

Quantity:

$$
\frac{2400}{60}
=
\boxed{40\text{ kg}}
$$

---

# 5. Finding Price

Expenditure:

$$
₹3,000
$$

Quantity:

$$
50\text{ kg}
$$

Price:

$$
\frac{3000}{50}
=
\boxed{₹60/\text{kg}}
$$

---

# 6. Expenditure Increase

If price remains constant and quantity increases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expenditure also increases by }r\%
}
$$

Example:

Quantity increases by `20%`.

Price unchanged.

Therefore expenditure increases by:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 7. Expenditure Decrease

If price remains constant and quantity decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expenditure decreases by }r\%
}
$$

---

# 8. Price Increase — Quantity Constant

If quantity remains constant and price increases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expenditure increases by }r\%
}
$$

Example:

Price increases by `25%`.

Quantity unchanged.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{25\%\text{ increase in expenditure}}
$$

---

# 9. Price Decrease — Quantity Constant

If price decreases by `20%` and quantity remains constant:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\text{ decrease in expenditure}
}
$$

---

# 10. Both Price and Quantity Change

This is one of the most important aptitude patterns.

If price changes by multiplier `a` and quantity changes by multiplier `b`:

$$
\boxed{
E_{\text{new}}
=
E_{\text{old}}\times a\times b
}
$$

Because:

$$
E=P\times Q
$$

---

# 11. Example — Both Increase

Price increases by `20%`.

Quantity increases by `10%`.

Multipliers:

$$
1.2,\quad1.1
$$

Expenditure multiplier:

$$
1.2\times1.1
=
1.32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{32\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Both Decrease

Price decreases by `20%`.

Quantity decreases by `10%`.

Multipliers:

$$
0.8,\quad0.9
$$

Expenditure:

$$
0.8\times0.9
=
0.72
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{28\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 13. Price Increase + Quantity Decrease

Price increases by `20%`.

Quantity decreases by `10%`.

Multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.9
=
1.08
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\%\text{ increase in expenditure}}
$$

---

# 14. Price Decrease + Quantity Increase

Price decreases by `20%`.

Quantity increases by `25%`.

Multiplier:

$$
0.8\times1.25
=
1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No change in expenditure}}
$$

This is a very important pattern.

---

# 15. Constant Expenditure

If expenditure remains constant:

$$
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
}
$$

This means:

> **Price and quantity are inversely proportional when expenditure is fixed.**

---

# 16. Price Increase — Constant Expenditure

Price increases by `20%`.

New price:

$$
1.2P
$$

Since expenditure is constant:

$$
P Q
=
1.2P\times Q_{\text{new}}
$$

Therefore:

$$
Q_{\text{new}}
=
\frac{Q}{1.2}
=
\frac56Q
$$

Quantity decrease:

$$
1-\frac56
=
\frac16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16\frac23\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 17. Price Decrease — Constant Expenditure

Price decreases by `20%`.

New price:

$$
0.8P
$$

Therefore:

$$
Q_{\text{new}}
=
\frac{Q}{0.8}
=
1.25Q
$$

Quantity increases by:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 18. General Formula — Price Increase

If price increases by `r%` and expenditure remains constant:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Quantity decrease}
=
\frac{r}{100+r}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price increase:

`25%`

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 19. General Formula — Price Decrease

If price decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Quantity increase}
=
\frac{r}{100-r}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price decrease:

`25%`

$$
\frac{25}{75}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 26. Expenditure Change Formula

Suppose:

- Price changes by `a%`
- Quantity changes by `b%`

Convert both into multipliers.

Then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Expenditure}
=
\text{Old Expenditure}
\times
\left(1+\frac a{100}\right)
\times
\left(1+\frac b{100}\right)
}
$$

Use negative values for decreases.

# 43. Price and Expenditure — Direct Formula

If price changes from `P₁` to `P₂` and quantity changes from `Q₁` to `Q₂`:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{E_2}{E_1}
=
\frac{P_2Q_2}{P_1Q_1}
}
$$

This is useful when exact values are given.

---

# 44. Example — Direct Ratio

Old:

- Price = `₹40`
- Quantity = `25`

New:

- Price = `₹50`
- Quantity = `20`

Old expenditure:

$$
40\times25
=
1000
$$

New expenditure:

$$
50\times20
=
1000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No change}}
$$

---

# 45. Expenditure Ratio

If:

$$
P_2=1.2P_1
$$

and:

$$
Q_2=0.9Q_1
$$

then:

$$
E_2:E_1
=
1.2\times0.9
=
1.08
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
E_2:E_1=108:100=27:25
}
$$

---

# 46. Constant Expenditure — Ratio Method

If price increases by `25%`:

$$
P_1:P_2
=
100:125
=
4:5
$$

For constant expenditure:

$$
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
Q_1:Q_2
=
5:4
$$

Quantity decreases by:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 47. Constant Expenditure — Fastest Method

`100 → 125`

Quantity:

`125 → 100`

Therefore:

$$

$$

# 50. Expenditure With Three Factors

Sometimes expenditure may be:

$$
\boxed{
E=\text{Price}\times\text{Quantity}\times\text{Frequency}
}
$$

For example, monthly spending:

$$
\text{Price per item}
\times
\text{Items per purchase}
\times
\text{Purchases per month}
$$

Percentage changes in all three factors can be multiplied.

---

# 51. Example — Three Factors

Price increases `10%`.

Quantity per purchase increases `20%`.

Number of purchases decreases `10%`.

Multiplier:

$$
1.1\times1.2\times0.9
$$

$$
=1.188
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{18.8\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 52. Expenditure and Consumption

If:

$$
E=P\times Q
$$

then:

- `P ↑`, `Q ↓` → compare multipliers
- `P ↓`, `Q ↑` → compare multipliers
- `P ↑`, `Q same` → E ↑
- `P ↓`, `Q same` → E ↓

Never assume the final expenditure without calculating the combined multiplier.

---

# 57. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Thinking price and expenditure always change by the same percentage.

Only true when quantity is constant.

### Mistake 2

Adding price and quantity percentages directly.

Wrong.

Multiply their multipliers.

### Mistake 3

For constant expenditure, using the same percentage in the opposite direction.

Example:

`20% price increase` does **not** mean `20% quantity decrease`.

Correct:

$$
16\frac23\%
$$

### Mistake 4

Forgetting the base when calculating percentage change.

### Mistake 5

Using simple addition for successive changes.
