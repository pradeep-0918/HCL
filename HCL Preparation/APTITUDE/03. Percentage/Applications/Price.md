# Price

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Price-based percentage problems ask you to calculate how the **price of a product changes** when it is increased, decreased, discounted, marked up, or changed successively.

Common patterns:

- Price increase
- Price decrease
- Price reduction
- Discount
- Original price
- Selling price
- Marked price
- Successive price changes
- Price + quantity
- Price + expenditure
- Price + consumption
- Reverse price

The most important idea:

> **Price percentage problems follow the same multiplier method as ordinary percentage problems.**

---

# 2. Price Increase

If price increases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Price}
=
\text{Old Price}
\left(1+\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Old price:

$$
₹500
$$

Increase:

`20%`

New price:

$$
500\times1.2
=
\boxed{₹600}
$$

---

# 3. Price Decrease

If price decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Price}
=
\text{Old Price}
\left(1-\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Old price:

$$
₹500
$$

Decrease:

`20%`

New price:

$$
500\times0.8
=
\boxed{₹400}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Price Increase

Original price:

$$
₹800
$$

New price:

$$
₹960
$$

Increase:

$$
960-800
=
₹160
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{160}{800}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

$$

$$

\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 12. Discount

> [!summary]
> A **discount** is a reduction from the marked price.

If discount is `d%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Selling Price}
=
\text{Marked Price}
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Marked price:

$$
₹2,000
$$

Discount:

`20%`

Selling price:

$$
2000\times0.8
=
\boxed{₹1,600}
$$

---

# 13. Discount Amount

Marked price:

$$
₹5,000
$$

Discount:

`15%`

Discount amount:

$$
5000\times\frac{15}{100}
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹750}
$$

Selling price:

$$
5000-750
=
\boxed{₹4,250}
$$

---

# 14. Discount Shortcut

If marked price is `M` and discount is `d%`:

$$
\boxed{
SP=M\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
M=₹4,000
$$

Discount:

`25%`

$$
SP=4000\times\frac{75}{100}
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹3,000}
$$

---

# 15. Reverse Discount

Selling price:

$$
₹1,600
$$

Discount:

`20%`

Since:

$$
SP=80\%\text{ of MP}
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=\frac{1600}{0.8}
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹2,000}
$$

---

# 16. Reverse Discount Shortcut

$$
\boxed{
MP=
\frac{100\times SP}{100-d}
}
$$

Example:

Selling price:

$$
₹1,700
$$

Discount:

`15%`

$$
MP=
\frac{100\times1700}{85}
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹2,000}
$$

---

# 17. Marked Price vs Selling Price

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marked Price | Price written on the product |
| Discount | Reduction from marked price |
| Selling Price | Actual price paid |
| Discount Amount | MP − SP |

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-\text{Discount}
}
$$

---

# 18. Discount Percentage

If:

- Marked price = `M`
- Selling price = `S`

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Discount \%}
=
\frac{M-S}{M}\times100
}
$$

Example:

MP:

$$
₹2,500
$$

SP:

$$
₹2,000
$$

Discount:

$$
₹500
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{2500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 19. Successive Discounts

Suppose a product has:

- `10%` discount
- followed by `20%` discount

Combined multiplier:

$$
0.9\times0.8
=
0.72
$$

Therefore final price is:

$$
72\%
$$

of marked price.

Net discount:

$$
100-72
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 20. Successive Discount Shortcut

For discounts `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net Discount}
=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20-2
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 21. Example — Successive Discounts

Marked price:

$$
₹5,000
$$

Discounts:

`10%` and `20%`

First:

$$
5000\times0.9
=
4500
$$

Second:

$$
4500\times0.8
=
3600
$$

Final:

$$
\boxed{₹3,600}
$$

Net discount:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 22. Discount Is Not Added Directly

Suppose:

`20%` discount followed by `30%` discount.

Wrong:

$$
20+30=50\%
$$

Correct:

$$
0.8\times0.7
=
0.56
$$

Net discount:

$$
100-56
=
\boxed{44\%}
$$

# 23. Markup

> [!summary]
> **Markup** means increasing the cost price to obtain the marked price.

If markup is `m%`:

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP(1+m/100)
}
$$

Example:

Cost price:

$$
₹1,000
$$

Markup:

`30%`

Marked price:

$$
1000\times1.3
=
\boxed{₹1,300}
$$

---

# 24. Reverse Markup

Marked price:

$$
₹1,300
$$

Markup:

`30%`

Cost price:

$$
\frac{1300}{1.3}
=
\boxed{₹1,000}
$$

---

# 25. Markup + Discount

This is a very common aptitude pattern.

Suppose:

Cost price:

$$
₹1,000
$$

Markup:

`30%`

Discount:

`10%`

First find marked price:

$$
1000\times1.3
=
₹1,300
$$

Then discount:

$$
1300\times0.9
=
₹1,170
$$

Selling price:

$$
\boxed{₹1,170}
$$

Profit:

$$
1170-1000
=
₹170
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{170}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{17\%}
$$

---

# 26. Markup + Discount Shortcut

If markup = `m%` and discount = `d%`:

$$
SP
=
CP
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{SP}{CP}
=
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 27. Example — Equal Markup and Discount

Markup:

`20%`

Discount:

`20%`

Multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.8
=
0.96
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=96\%\text{ of CP}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{4\%\text{ loss}}
$$

> [!warning]
> `20% markup` and `20% discount` do **not** cancel.

---

# 28. Example — 25% Markup and 20% Discount

Markup:

`25%`

Discount:

`20%`

Multiplier:

$$
1.25\times0.8
=
1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{SP = CP}}
$$

No profit and no loss.

---

# 29. Required Discount for No Profit

Suppose markup is `25%`.

We need discount `d%` such that:

$$
1.25(1-d/100)=1
$$

Therefore:

$$
1-d/100=\frac1{1.25}
$$

$$
=0.8
$$

Thus:

$$
d=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ discount}}
$$

---

# 30. General Formula — No Profit/Loss

If markup is `m%`, required discount:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{m}{100+m}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Markup = `50%`

$$
d=
\frac{50}{150}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 31. Required Discount for Desired Profit

Suppose markup:

`40%`

Desired profit:

`20%`

Then:

$$
MP=1.4CP
$$

Required selling price:

$$
SP=1.2CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
1.4CP(1-d/100)=1.2CP
$$

Cancel CP:

$$
1.4(1-d/100)=1.2
$$

$$
1-d/100=\frac{1.2}{1.4}
=
\frac67
$$

Therefore:

$$
d=\frac17\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{14\frac27\%}
$$

---

# 32. Price Increase and Quantity

Suppose price increases by `20%`.

If expenditure remains constant, quantity consumed must decrease.

Let:

$$
P_1=1.2P
$$

Since expenditure is constant:

$$
P_1Q_1=PQ
$$

Therefore:

$$
1.2P Q_1=PQ
$$

Cancel P:

$$
Q_1=\frac{Q}{1.2}
$$

$$
=\frac56Q
$$

Therefore quantity decreases by:

$$
1-\frac56
=
\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

---

# 33. Price Decrease and Quantity

If price decreases by `20%` and expenditure remains constant:

$$
P_1=0.8P
$$

Therefore:

$$
Q_1=\frac{Q}{0.8}
=
1.25Q
$$

So quantity can increase by:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 34. Price-Quantity Inverse Relationship

> [!important]
> If expenditure remains constant:

$$
\boxed{
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
}
$$

Therefore:

> **Price increases → quantity decreases.**

> **Price decreases → quantity increases.**

---

# 35. Price Increase — Consumption Reduction

Price increases by `25%`.

Expenditure remains constant.

New quantity:

$$
\frac{100}{125}
=
80\%
$$

Therefore quantity decreases by:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

This is a very common aptitude question.

---

# 36. Price Decrease — Consumption Increase

Price decreases by `25%`.

Expenditure remains constant.

New quantity:

$$
\frac{100}{75}
=
\frac43
$$

Therefore quantity increases by:

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 37. General Price-Quantity Formula

If price increases by `r%` and expenditure remains constant:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Quantity decrease}
=
\frac{r}{100+r}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price increase = `20%`

$$
\frac{20}{120}\times100
=
\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

---

# 38. If Price Decreases

If price decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Quantity increase}
=
\frac{r}{100-r}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Price decrease = `20%`

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 39. Expenditure Constant Example

A person's expenditure on rice is `₹4,000`.

Price increases by `25%`.

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100

\boxed{20\%}

# 42. Price and Consumption — Shortcut

> [!tip]

### Price increases by `x%`

Quantity must decrease by:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100\%
}
$$

### Price decreases by `x%`

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100\%
}
$$

Memorize these two.

# 43. Price + Quantity + Expenditure

If:

$$
E=P\times Q
$$

then percentage changes follow:

$$
\boxed{
E_{\text{new}}
=
E_{\text{old}}
\times
\text{Price multiplier}
\times
\text{Quantity multiplier}
}
$$

Example:

Price increases `20%`.

Quantity decreases `10%`.

Expenditure multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.9
=
1.08
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\%\text{ increase in expenditure}}
$$

# 46. Price and Demand

# 47. Price Comparison

Product A:

$$
₹600
$$

Product B:

$$
₹500
$$

A is more expensive than B by:

$$
600-500=100
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 48. Reverse Price Comparison

A is `20%` more expensive than B.

Therefore:

$$
A=1.2B
$$

If A costs:

$$
₹600
$$

then:

$$
B=\frac{600}{1.2}
=
\boxed{₹500}
$$

---

# 49. Price Comparison — Common Trap

A costs `₹600`.

B costs `₹500`.

A is:

$$
20\%
$$

more than B.

But B is:

$$
\frac{100}{600}\times100
=
\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

less than A.

> [!warning]
> The base changes when the direction changes.

# 59. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Adding successive discounts.

Wrong.

### Mistake 2

Assuming equal markup and discount cancel.

Wrong.

### Mistake 3

Using final price as the percentage base when finding increase.

Wrong.

### Mistake 4

Forgetting that price and quantity are inversely related when expenditure is fixed.

### Mistake 5

Adding yearly price increases instead of compounding.

### Mistake 6

Confusing marked price with cost price.

### Mistake 7

Confusing marked price with selling price.

# 63. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Price Increase

$$
\boxed{
NP=OP(1+r/100)
}
$$

### Price Decrease

$$
\boxed{
NP=OP(1-r/100)
}
$$

### Reverse Increase

$$
\boxed{
OP=\frac{NP}{1+r/100}
}
$$

### Reverse Decrease

$$
\boxed{
OP=\frac{NP}{1-r/100}
}
$$

### Discount

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP(1-d/100)
}
$$

### Reverse Discount

$$
\boxed{
MP=\frac{SP}{1-d/100}
}
$$

### Markup

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP(1+m/100)
}
$$

### Markup + Discount

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP(1+m/100)(1-d/100)
}
$$

### Expenditure

$$
\boxed{
E=P\times Q
}
$$

### Constant Expenditure

$$
\boxed{
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
}
$$

### Annual Price Growth

$$
\boxed{
P_n=P_0(1+r/100)^n
}
$$

# 64. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Increase

$$
₹500\xrightarrow{20\%}₹600
$$

### Decrease

$$
₹500\xrightarrow{20\%}₹400
$$

### Reverse Increase

$$
600\div1.2
=
\boxed{500}
$$

### Reverse Decrease

$$
400\div0.8
=
\boxed{500}
$$

### Discount

$$
₹2000\xrightarrow{20\%\ discount}₹1600
$$

### Successive Discount

$$
10\%,20\%
\Rightarrow
\boxed{28\%\ net\ discount}
$$

### Markup + Discount

$$
25\%\ markup+20\%\ discount
\Rightarrow
\boxed{\text{No profit/no loss}}
$$

### Constant Expenditure

$$
20\%\ price\uparrow
\Rightarrow
\boxed{16\frac23\%\ quantity\downarrow}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Price changes → use multipliers. Price ↑ → quantity ↓ if expenditure is fixed. Price ↓ → quantity ↑ if expenditure is fixed.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **Whenever a price question also gives quantity or expenditure, immediately think `Expenditure = Price × Quantity`.**
