# Salary

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Salary percentage problems apply percentage increase, decrease, successive change, reverse percentage, and ratio concepts to salary.

Common patterns:

- Salary increase
- Salary decrease
- Increment
- Salary after increment
- Original salary
- New salary
- Successive increments
- Salary cut
- Bonus percentage
- Tax/deduction percentage
- Expenditure from salary
- Savings from salary

The key idea:

> **Salary percentage questions are normal percentage problems with salary as the base quantity.**

---

# 2. Salary Increase

If salary increases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Salary}
=
\text{Old Salary}
\left(1+\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Old salary:

$$
₹40,000
$$

Increase:

`20%`

New salary:

$$
40000\times1.2
=
\boxed{₹48,000}
$$

---

# 3. Salary Decrease

If salary decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Salary}
=
\text{Old Salary}
\left(1-\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Old salary:

$$
₹50,000
$$

Decrease:

`10%`

New salary:

$$
50000\times0.9
=
\boxed{₹45,000}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Salary Increase

If old salary is `P` and new salary is `F`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Increase}
=
F-P
}
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{F-P}{P}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Old salary:

$$
₹40,000
$$

New salary:

$$
₹46,000
$$

Increase:

$$
6000
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{6000}{40000}\times100
=
\boxed{15\%}
$$

---

# 5. Finding Salary Decrease

Old salary:

$$
₹60,000
$$

New salary:

$$
₹51,000
$$

Decrease:

$$
60000-51000
=
9000
$$

Percentage decrease:

$$
\frac{9000}{60000}\times100
=
\boxed{15\%}
$$

$$

# 8. Shortcut Formula

If final salary is `F` after an increase of `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Old Salary}
=
\frac{100F}{100+r}
}
$$

Example:

Final salary:

$$
₹60,000
$$

Increase:

`20%`

$$
\frac{100\times60000}{120}
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹50,000}
$$

---

$$

$$

Shortcut:

$$
\frac{100\times40000}{80}

\boxed{₹50,000}
$$

# 12. Salary Ratio

Suppose A earns:

$$
₹60,000
$$

and B earns:

$$
₹40,000
$$

Ratio:

$$
60000:40000
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{3:2}
$$

Therefore A earns:

$$
\frac{3-2}{2}\times100
=
\boxed{50\%}
$$

more than B.

---

# 13. Reverse Salary Ratio

A earns `50% more` than B.

Therefore:

$$
A=1.5B
$$

Ratio:

$$
\boxed{3:2}
$$

But B is less than A by:

$$
\frac{1}{3}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

Important:

> [!warning]
> If A earns `50% more than B`, B does **not** earn `50% less than A`.

# 16. Net Salary Increase

For successive increases of `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net Increase}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

For `10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20+2
=
\boxed{32\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
40000\times1.32
=
\boxed{₹52,800}
$$

- Salary = `₹40,000`
- Bonus = `₹8,000`

$$
\frac{8000}{40000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}

\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

> Always identify the **base** of the percentage.

# 43. Salary Increase and Savings — General Formula

If:

- Original salary = `S`
- Expenditure = `E`
- Salary increase = `r%`

New salary:

$$
S'=S(1+r/100)
$$

New savings:

$$
\boxed{
S'=S(1+r/100)-E
}
$$

Original savings:

$$
\boxed{
S-E
}
$$

---

# 44. Salary Decrease and Savings — General Formula

If salary decreases by `r%`:

$$
S'=S(1-r/100)
$$

New savings:

$$
\boxed{
S(1-r/100)-E
}
$$

---

# 45. Salary and Savings Percentage Change

Suppose original salary is `S`, expenditure is `E`, and salary changes by `r%`.

Do **not** directly apply `r%` to savings.

First calculate:

$$
\text{New Salary}
$$

Then:

$$
\text{New Savings}
=
\text{New Salary}-\text{Expenditure}
$$

Then calculate the percentage change in savings.

> [!important]
> **Salary percentage change and savings percentage change are generally different.**

# 46. Successive Salary Increment Shortcut

Two increments:

`a%`, `b%`

Net increase:

$$
\boxed{
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%`, `20%`:

$$
10+20+2
=
\boxed{32\%}
$$

---

# 47. Successive Salary Cut Shortcut

Two cuts:

`a%`, `b%`

Net decrease:

$$
\boxed{
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%`, `20%`:

$$
10+20-2
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 48. Salary Increase Followed by Decrease

Increase `a%`, decrease `b%`.

Net change:

$$
\boxed{
a-b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`20%` increase and `10%` decrease:

$$
20-10-2
=
\boxed{8\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 49. Salary Decrease Followed by Increase

Decrease `a%`, increase `b%`.

Use multipliers:

$$
\boxed{
(1-a/100)(1+b/100)
}
$$

Example:

`20%` decrease, `25%` increase:

$$
0.8\times1.25
=
1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No net change}}
$$

# 58. High-Yield Salary Patterns

> [!important] Must Master

### Pattern 1

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Salary}
=
\text{Old Salary}(1+r/100)
}
$$

### Pattern 2

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Salary}
=
\text{Old Salary}(1-r/100)
}
$$

### Pattern 3

$$
\boxed{
\text{Old Salary}
=
\frac{\text{New Salary}}{1+r/100}
}
$$

### Pattern 4

$$
\boxed{
\text{Old Salary}
=
\frac{\text{New Salary}}{1-r/100}
}
$$

### Pattern 5

$$
\boxed{
\text{Increment}
=
\text{Old Salary}\times r/100
}
$$

### Pattern 6

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net Salary}
=
\text{Gross Salary}-\text{Deductions}
}
$$

### Pattern 7

> **Salary growth every year = compound growth.**

### Pattern 8

> **Salary percentage change does not automatically equal savings percentage change.**

### Pattern 9

$$
\boxed{
20\%\downarrow\text{ then }25\%\uparrow
=
\text{no net change}
}
$$

### Pattern 10

> **Always identify the percentage base.**
