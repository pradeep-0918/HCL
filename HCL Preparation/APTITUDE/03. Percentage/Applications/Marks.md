# Marks

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Marks-based percentage problems involve **marks obtained, total marks, pass marks, percentage, increase/decrease in marks, comparison of marks, and changes required to pass**.

The most important relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Marks Obtained}}
{\text{Total Marks}}
\times100
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Marks Obtained}
=
\frac{\text{Percentage}\times\text{Total Marks}}{100}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Total Marks}
=
\frac{\text{Marks Obtained}\times100}
{\text{Percentage}}
}
$$

Suppose a student scores:

$$
450
$$

$$
600
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{450}{600}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{75\%}

# 10. Marks and Percentage

If total marks remain unchanged:

> **Percentage changes in exactly the same proportion as marks.**

Example:

Marks increase by `20%`.

Total marks unchanged.

Therefore percentage also increases by `20%` relative to the old percentage.

If old percentage = `60%`:

$$
60\times1.2
=
\boxed{72\%}
$$

# 21. Average Percentage Across Equal Subjects

Suppose marks are:

- Math = `80%`
- Physics = `70%`
- Chemistry = `90%`

If each subject has the **same maximum marks**:

$$
\text{Overall percentage}
=
\frac{80+70+90}{3}

=\boxed{80\%}
$$

> [!important]
> Averaging percentages directly is valid only when the subject weights/max marks are equal.

# 32. General Percentage Formula

Since:

$$
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{M}{T}\times100
$$

if marks change by multiplier `a` and total marks change by multiplier `b`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Percentage}
=
\text{Old Percentage}\times\frac ab
}
$$

This is a powerful shortcut.

# 51. Finding Total Marks From Two Percentages

Suppose a student gets:

`40%` and fails by `20 marks`.

If pass percentage is `50%`, difference is:

$$
50\%-40\%=10\%
$$

That `10%` corresponds to `20 marks`.

Therefore:

$$
10\%\text{ of Total}=20
$$

$$
\text{Total}
=
\frac{20\times100}{10}
=
\boxed{200}
$$

# 52. Classic Pass/Fail Pattern

> A student scores `40%` and fails by `30 marks`. Another student scores `50%` and passes by `20 marks`. Find total marks.

Difference between students:

$$
50\%-40\%
=
10\%
$$

Difference in marks:

First student:

$$
\text{Pass Marks}-30
$$

Second:

$$
\text{Pass Marks}+20
$$

Difference:

$$
30+20
=
50
$$

Therefore:

$$
10\%\text{ of Total}=50
$$

Total:

$$
\boxed{500}
$$

# 55. Important Pass/Fail Formula

If:

- First student gets `x%` and fails by `a` marks
- Second student gets `y%` and passes by `b` marks

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Total Marks}
=
\frac{(a+b)\times100}{y-x}
}
$$

---

# 56. Finding Pass Percentage

Once total marks are known:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Pass Marks}
=
x\%\text{ of Total}+a
}
$$

where the first student failed by `a` marks.

Then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Pass Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Pass Marks}}
{\text{Total Marks}}\times100
}
$$

# 59. Marks and Ranking

Percentage can help compare performance, but ranking requires information about other students.

If:

- A = `90%`
- B = `85%`
- C = `80%`

then A has the highest percentage.

But exact rank cannot be determined if additional students are not accounted for.

# 60. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Using total marks instead of marks obtained for percentage.

Correct:

$$
\frac{\text{Obtained}}{\text{Total}}\times100
$$

### Mistake 2

Confusing percentage points with percentage increase.

### Mistake 3

Averaging percentages when subject marks are unequal.

### Mistake 4

Applying percentage increase to total marks when only obtained marks changed.

### Mistake 5

Ignoring the base in bonus/grace-mark problems.

### Mistake 6

Adding successive percentage changes directly.

### Mistake 7

Using final marks as the base for percentage increase.

# 65. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Basic

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Marks Obtained}}
{\text{Total Marks}}\times100
}
$$

### Example

`450/600`:

$$
\boxed{75\%}
$$

### Marks From Percentage

`80% of 500`:

$$
\boxed{400}
$$

### Pass Marks

`40% of 500`:

$$
\boxed{200}
$$

### Reverse Increase

`480` after `20%` increase:

$$
480\div1.2
=
\boxed{400}
$$

### Reverse Decrease

`320` after `20%` decrease:

$$
320\div0.8
=
\boxed{400}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Marks percentage = Obtained ÷ Total × 100.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **Whenever a question gives marks and percentage, immediately identify which of the three quantities—obtained marks, total marks, percentage—is missing.**
