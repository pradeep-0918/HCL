# Population

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> Population problems apply percentage concepts to the number of people in a town, city, village, country, organization, etc.

The most common patterns are:

- Population increase
- Population decrease
- Annual growth
- Annual decline
- Successive growth
- Successive decline
- Finding previous population
- Finding future population
- Mixed growth and decline

The key idea:

> **Population changes are usually compound percentage changes.**

---

# 2. Population Increase

If population increases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Population}
=
\text{Old Population}
\left(1+\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Population = `50,000`

Increase = `10%`

$$
50000\times1.1
=
\boxed{55,000}
$$

---

# 3. Population Decrease

If population decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Population}
=
\text{Old Population}
\left(1-\frac r{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

Population = `50,000`

Decrease = `10%`

$$
50000\times0.9
=
\boxed{45,000}
$$

---

# 4. Population Growth Formula

If population grows by `r%` every year for `n` years:

$$
\boxed{
P_n
=
P_0
\left(1+\frac r{100}\right)^n
}
$$

where:

- `P₀` = initial population
- `Pₙ` = population after `n` years
- `r` = annual growth rate
- `n` = number of years

---

# 5. Example — One Year

Initial population:

$$
100,000
$$

Growth:

`10%`

After one year:

$$
100000\times1.1
$$

$$
=\boxed{110,000}
$$

---

# 6. Example — Two Years

Initial population:

$$
100,000
$$

Growth:

`10%` per year.

After 1 year:

$$
100000\times1.1
=
110000
$$

After 2 years:

$$
110000\times1.1
=
121000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{121,000}
$$

---

# 7. Compound Growth Formula

Directly:

$$
P_2
=
100000(1.1)^2
$$

$$
=
100000(1.21)
$$

$$
=\boxed{121,000}
$$

---

# 8. Why We Cannot Add Growth Percentages

For `10%` annual growth for 2 years:

Wrong:

$$
10\%+10\%=20\%
$$

Correct:

$$
(1.1)^2
=
1.21
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{21\%\text{ total growth}}
$$

---

# 9. Example — Three Years

Population:

$$
80,000
$$

Annual growth:

`5%`

For 3 years:

$$
80000(1.05)^3
$$

$$
=80000(1.157625)
$$

$$
=\boxed{92,610}
$$

# 11. Population Decrease Formula

If population decreases by `r%` every year:

$$
\boxed{
P_n
=
P_0
\left(1-\frac r{100}\right)^n
}
$$

Suppose:

Initial population:

$$
60,000
$$

Annual growth:

`20%`

for 2 years.

$$
60000(1.2)^2
$$

$$
=60000(1.44)
$$

$$
=\boxed{86,400}

# 15. Finding Previous Population

Suppose current population:

$$
72,000
$$

It has grown by `20%` in one year.

Previous population:

$$
\frac{72000}{1.2}
$$

$$
=\boxed{60,000}
$$

---

# 16. Reverse Population Growth

If current population is `P` after `n` years of `r%` growth:

$$
\boxed{
P_0
=
\frac{P}
{(1+r/100)^n}
}
$$

Example:

Current population:

$$
121,000
$$

Growth:

`10%` annually for 2 years.

$$
P_0
=
\frac{121000}{(1.1)^2}
$$

$$
=\boxed{100,000}
$$

---

# 17. Reverse Population Decline

If current population is `P` after `n` years of `r%` decline:

$$
\boxed{
P_0
=
\frac{P}
{(1-r/100)^n}
}
$$

Example:

Current population:

$$
81,000
$$

Annual decline:

`10%`

for 2 years.

$$
P_0
=
\frac{81000}{0.9^2}
$$

$$
=\boxed{100,000}
$$

---

Suppose population:

- increases by `20%`
- then decreases by `10%`

Multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.9
=
1.08
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\%\text{ net increase}}
$$

\boxed{8\%}

# 20. Population Decrease and Increase

Suppose population:

- decreases by `20%`

=
1

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No net change}}
$$

This is a very important pattern.

# 22. Equal Growth and Decline

If population increases by `r%` and then decreases by `r%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net decrease}
=
\frac{r^2}{100}\%
}
$$

Example:

`10%` increase and `10%` decrease:

$$
\frac{10^2}{100}
=
\boxed{1\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

$$
110,000
$$

`10%`

110000\times0.9
=
99,000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

# 24. Population Growth as Compound Interest

Population growth follows the same mathematical structure as compound interest.

Growth:

$$
r\%
$$

Number of years:

$$
n
$$

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
P_n=P_0(1+r/100)^n
}
$$

This connection is useful because many aptitude questions reuse the same pattern.

$$
=\boxed{21\%}

# 27. Finding Growth Rate

Suppose population grows from:

$$
50,000\rightarrow55,000
$$

Growth:

$$

$$

Percentage growth:

\frac{5000}{50000}\times100
$$

$$

$$

# 28. Finding Annual Growth Rate

Suppose population grows from:

$$
100,000
$$

to:

$$
121,000
$$

in 2 years.

Formula:

$$
121000
=
100000(1+r/100)^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
1.21
=
(1+r/100)^2
$$

Take square root:

$$
1.1=1+\frac r{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{r=10\%}
$$

---

# 29. Finding Annual Decline Rate

Population decreases from:

$$
100,000
$$

to:

$$
81,000
$$

in 2 years.

$$
81000
=
100000(1-r/100)^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
0.81
=
(1-r/100)^2
$$

Taking square root:

$$
0.9
=
1-\frac r{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{r=10\%}
$$

# 30. Population Ratio

If population grows by `20%`:

$$
\text{New}:\text{Old}
=
120:100
=
\boxed{6:5}
$$

If population decreases by `20%`:

$$
\text{New}:\text{Old}
=
80:100
=
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 31. Population Ratio After Multiple Years

Growth = `10%` annually for 2 years.

Each year:

$$
11:10
$$

After two years:

$$
\frac{11}{10}\times\frac{11}{10}
=
\frac{121}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P_2:P_0=121:100
}
$$

---

# 32. Population Word Problem

> A village has a population of `40,000`. It increases by `10%` in the first year and `20%` in the second year. Find the population after two years.

First year:

$$
40000\times1.1
=
44000
$$

Second year:

$$
44000\times1.2
=
52800
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{52,800}
$$

---

# 33. Shortcut for Multiple Growth Rates

For successive increases `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net growth}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20+2
=
\boxed{32\%}
$$

Therefore population becomes:

$$
1.32P
$$

---

# 34. Shortcut for Multiple Declines

For successive decreases `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net decrease}
=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

`10%` and `20%`:

$$
10+20-2
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 35. Population With Births and Deaths

Some population problems describe:

- births
- deaths
- migration
- immigration
- emigration

These can be converted into percentage changes.

Example:

Population:

$$
100,000
$$

Birth rate:

`5%`

Death rate:

`2%`

If both are based on the same initial population:

Net change:

$$
5\%-2\%
=
\boxed{3\%}
$$

New population:

$$
100000\times1.03
=
\boxed{103,000}
$$

---

# 36. Birth and Death Pattern

> [!important]
> Be careful with the **base**.

If birth and death percentages are both explicitly based on the original population:

$$
\text{Net change}
=
\text{Birth rate}-\text{Death rate}
$$

But if changes occur successively on the updated population, use multipliers.

# 40. Population and Household Problems

If average household size remains unchanged:

Population increases by `20%`.

# 41. Population and Density

Population density:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Density}
=
\frac{\text{Population}}{\text{Area}}
}
$$

If area remains constant:

> Population percentage change = density percentage change.

Example:

Population increases `25%`.

Area unchanged.

Density increases:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 42. Population Density — Area Changes

Suppose:

- Population increases `20%`
- Area increases `10%`

Density multiplier:

$$
\frac{1.2}{1.1}
$$

$$
=\frac{12}{11}
$$

Therefore density increases by:

$$
\left(\frac{12}{11}-1\right)\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{9.09\%}
$$

---

# 43. Population Density — Population Decreases

Suppose:

- population decreases `20%`
- area unchanged.

Density multiplier:

$$
0.8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 44. Population Density — Both Decrease

Population decreases `10%`.

Area decreases `20%`.

Density multiplier:

$$
\frac{0.9}{0.8}
=
1.125
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{12.5\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 50. Population Growth and Ratio

If population increases by `25%`:

$$
P_{\text{new}}=1.25P
$$

Since:

$$
1.25=\frac54
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
P_{\text{new}}:P_{\text{old}}=5:4
}
$$

This ratio form can make aptitude calculations faster.

# 56. Population Growth vs Decline

| Situation | Formula |
|---|---|
| One-time growth | `P(1+r/100)` |
| One-time decline | `P(1-r/100)` |
| Growth for n years | `P(1+r/100)^n` |
| Decline for n years | `P(1-r/100)^n` |
| Reverse growth | `F/(1+r/100)^n` |
| Reverse decline | `F/(1-r/100)^n` |
