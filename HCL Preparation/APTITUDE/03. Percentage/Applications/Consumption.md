# Consumption

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Consumption means the **quantity of a commodity used or purchased**.

Most aptitude questions connect:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expenditure}=\text{Price}\times\text{Consumption}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{E=P\times Q}
$$

where:

- `E` = expenditure
- `P` = price per unit
- `Q` = quantity/consumption

The most important pattern is:

> **If expenditure remains constant, price and consumption are inversely proportional.**

---

# 2. Basic Formula

$$
\boxed{E=P\times Q}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac EP}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{P=\frac EQ}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Example

Price of rice:

$$
₹50/\text{kg}
$$

Consumption:

$$
20\text{ kg}
$$

Expenditure:

$$
50\times20
=
\boxed{₹1,000}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Consumption

Expenditure:

$$
₹2,400
$$

Price:

$$
₹60/\text{kg}
$$

Consumption:

$$
\frac{2400}{60}
=
\boxed{40\text{ kg}}
$$

₹3,000

$$
50\text{ kg}
$$

Price:

$$
\frac{3000}{50}

\boxed{₹60/\text{kg}}
$$

$$
\boxed{1.2}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 10. Price and Consumption Both Change

If price changes by multiplier `a` and consumption changes by multiplier `b`:

$$
\boxed{
E_{\text{new}}
=
E_{\text{old}}\times a\times b
}
$$

This is the central method for mixed questions.

---

# 11. Example — Price Increase + Consumption Decrease

Price increases by `20%`.

Consumption decreases by `10%`.

Multipliers:

$$
1.2,\quad0.9
$$

Expenditure multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.9
=
1.08
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\%\text{ increase in expenditure}}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Price Decrease + Consumption Increase

Price decreases by `20%`.

Consumption increases by `25%`.

Multipliers:

$$
0.8,\quad1.25
$$

Therefore:

$$
0.8\times1.25
=
1
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{\text{Expenditure remains unchanged}}
$$

---

# 13. Constant Expenditure

If expenditure remains unchanged:

$$
E_1=E_2
$$

Since:

$$
E=P\times Q
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
P_1Q_1=P_2Q_2
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P_1:P_2=Q_2:Q_1
}
$$

Price and consumption move in opposite directions.

---

# 14. Price Increase → Consumption Reduction

Suppose price increases by `20%`.

Old price:

$$
100
$$

New price:

$$
120
$$

For constant expenditure, consumption must change inversely:

$$
Q_{\text{new}}
=
\frac{100}{120}Q
$$

$$
=
\frac56Q
$$

Therefore reduction:

$$
1-\frac56
=
\frac16
$$

Percentage:

$$
\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

---

# 15. General Formula

If price increases by `r%` and expenditure remains constant:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Consumption decrease}
=
\frac{r}{100+r}\times100\%
}
$$

# 26. Ratio Method

Suppose price increases by `25%`.

Old price:

$$
100
$$

New price:

$$
125
$$

Therefore:

$$
P_{\text{old}}:P_{\text{new}}
=
4:5
$$

For constant expenditure:

$$
Q_{\text{old}}:Q_{\text{new}}
=
5:4
$$

Therefore consumption decreases by:

$$
\frac{1}{5}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

> [!tip]
> Ratio method is often the fastest method in aptitude exams.

# 39. Consumption With Salary Change

Salary increases by `20%`.

If food expenditure remains unchanged, food consumption also remains unchanged **only if the price remains unchanged**.

> [!important]
> Salary percentage changes do not automatically determine consumption changes.

You need the relationship given in the question.

$$
1.2
$$

$$
1.1
$$

Expenditure multiplier:

$$
1.2
$$

Therefore consumption multiplier:

$$
\frac{1.2}{1.1}
=

$$

$$
\boxed{9.09\%}
$$

# 41. Consumption With Fixed Percentage of Income

If income is `I` and a fixed percentage `k%` is spent on a commodity:

$$
E=I\times\frac{k}{100}
$$

Then:

$$
Q=\frac{E}{P}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Q\propto\frac{I}{P}
}
$$

when the expenditure percentage of income and price relationship remain fixed.

---

# 42. Consumption and Price Ratio

$$

$$
\boxed{
P_1:P_2=Q_2:Q_1
}
$$

Example:

Price ratio:

$$
4:5
$$

Consumption ratio:

$$
5:4
$$

# 61. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Thinking:

`20% price increase → 20% consumption decrease`.

Wrong.

Correct:

$$
16\frac23\%
$$

### Mistake 2

Forgetting:

$$
E=P\times Q
$$

### Mistake 3

Adding price and consumption percentages.

Wrong.

### Mistake 4

Using the wrong denominator when calculating percentage reduction.

### Mistake 5

Assuming expenditure is constant when the question does not say so.

### Mistake 6

Assuming consumption changes whenever salary changes.

A relationship must be given.
