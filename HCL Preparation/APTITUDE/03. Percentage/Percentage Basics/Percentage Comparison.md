# Percentage Comparison

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> Percentage comparison tells us **how one quantity compares with another quantity**, usually by expressing the relationship as a percentage.
>
> The most important thing is to identify the **reference/base value**.

The basic formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Required Quantity}}{\text{Reference Quantity}}
\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. The Most Important Rule

> [!important]
> **Always identify the base/reference quantity before calculating the percentage.**

For:

> A is what percentage of B?

B is the reference.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac AB\times100
}
$$

---

# 3. Example — A as Percentage of B

A = `40`

B = `80`

Then:

$$
\frac{40}{80}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{50\%}
$$

Therefore:

> `40 is 50% of 80.`

---

# 4. "A Is What Percentage of B?"

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac AB\times100
}
$$

Example:

`30 is what percentage of 120?`

$$
\frac{30}{120}\times100
$$

$$
=\frac14\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 5. "B Is What Percentage of A?"

Now the reference changes.

$$
\boxed{
\frac BA\times100
}
$$

Example:

A = `30`

B = `120`

Then:

$$
\frac{120}{30}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{400\%}
$$

Notice:

$$
30=25\%\text{ of }120
$$

but:

$$
120=400\%\text{ of }30
$$

> [!warning]
> Percentage comparison is **not symmetric**.

---

# 6. A Is What Percent More Than B?

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{A-B}{B}\times100
}
$$

The denominator is B because B is the reference.

Example:

A = `120`

B = `100`

Increase:

$$
120-100=20
$$

Percentage more:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 7. A Is What Percent Less Than B?

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{B}\times100
}
$$

Example:

A = `80`

B = `100`

Difference:

$$
100-80=20
$$

Percentage less:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 8. Difference Between "Of" and "More Than"

### A is what percentage of B?

$$
\boxed{
\frac AB\times100
}
$$

### A is what percentage more than B?

$$
\boxed{
\frac{A-B}{B}\times100
}
$$

These are different questions.

Example:

A = `120`

B = `100`

A as percentage of B:

$$
\frac{120}{100}\times100
=
120\%
$$

A is percentage more than B:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
120\%\text{ of B}
}
$$

but:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\text{ more than B}
}
$$

---

# 9. Key Relationship

If:

$$
A=120\%\text{ of }B
$$

then:

$$
A=1.2B
$$

Therefore A is:

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ more than B}}
$$

General pattern:

$$
\boxed{
A=x\%\text{ of }B
\Rightarrow
A\text{ is }(x-100)\%\text{ more than B}
}
$$

when:

$$
x>100
$$

---

# 10. If A Is Less Than B

Suppose:

$$
A=80\%\text{ of }B
$$

Then:

$$
A=0.8B
$$

Therefore A is:

$$
100\%-80\%
=
\boxed{20\%\text{ less than B}}
$$

General pattern:

$$
\boxed{
A=x\%\text{ of }B
\Rightarrow
A\text{ is }(100-x)\%\text{ less than B}
}
$$

when:

$$
x<100
$$

---

# 11. Important Percentage Comparison Table

| A compared with B | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| `50% of B` | `50% less than B` |
| `75% of B` | `25% less than B` |
| `80% of B` | `20% less than B` |
| `90% of B` | `10% less than B` |
| `100% of B` | Equal to B |
| `110% of B` | `10% more than B` |
| `120% of B` | `20% more than B` |
| `125% of B` | `25% more than B` |
| `150% of B` | `50% more than B` |
| `200% of B` | `100% more than B` |

# 12. Percentage Difference

The phrase **percentage difference** can have a different formula from percentage increase/decrease.

For two values A and B:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage Difference}
=
\frac{|A-B|}
{\frac{A+B}{2}}
\times100
}
$$

Equivalent form:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{2|A-B|}{A+B}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> This uses the **average of the two values** as the reference.

---

# 13. Example — Percentage Difference

A = `80`

B = `100`

Difference:

$$
|100-80|=20
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{80+100}{2}
=
90
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{20}{90}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{22.22\%}
$$

---

# 14. Percentage Difference vs Percentage Change

Suppose:

$$
A=80
$$

and:

$$
B=100
$$

### Percentage increase from 80 to 100

Reference = `80`.

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

### Percentage difference

Reference = average = `90`.

$$
\frac{20}{90}\times100
=
\boxed{22.22\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
25\%\ne22.22\%
}
$$

---

# 15. Percentage Comparison Using Ratio

If:

$$
A:B=2:5
$$

then A as percentage of B:

$$
\frac25\times100
=
\boxed{40\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=40\%\text{ of B}
}
$$

---

# 16. Ratio to Percentage

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A\text{ as \% of B}
=
\frac mn\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
A:B=3:4
$$

Then:

$$
\frac34\times100
=
\boxed{75\%}
$$

---

# 17. Percentage of Total From Ratio

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

Total parts:

$$
3+5=8
$$

A's percentage of total:

$$
\frac38\times100
=
\boxed{37.5\%}
$$

B's percentage of total:

$$
\frac58\times100
=
\boxed{62.5\%}
$$

> [!warning]
> `A as % of B` and `A as % of total` are different.

---

# 18. Example — Two Students

Student A scored:

$$
72
$$

Student B scored:

$$
90
$$

A's score as percentage of B:

$$
\frac{72}{90}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{80\%}
$$

Therefore A scored `80% of B's marks`.

---

# 19. Example — Salary

A earns:

$$
₹30,000
$$

B earns:

$$
₹40,000
$$

A's salary as percentage of B:

$$
\frac{30000}{40000}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{75\%}
$$

Therefore A earns `75% of B's salary`.

---

# 20. Example — Price

Product A costs:

$$
₹600
$$

Product B costs:

$$
₹500
$$

A is what percentage more expensive than B?

Difference:

$$
600-500=100
$$

Reference:

$$
500
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 21. Reverse Comparison

Suppose A is `20% more than B`.

Then:

$$
A=120\%\text{ of B}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=1.2B
}
$$

But B is **not** `20% less than A`.

This is a very common aptitude trap.

---

# 22. Why the Reverse Percentage Is Different

Suppose:

$$
B=100
$$

A is `20% more`.

Therefore:

$$
A=120
$$

Now compare B with A.

Decrease:

$$
120-100=20
$$

But the reference is now `120`.

Therefore:

$$
\frac{20}{120}\times100
=
\boxed{16\frac23\%}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
A\text{ is 20% more than B}
}
$$

but:

$$
\boxed{
B\text{ is }16\frac23\%\text{ less than A}
}
$$

---

# 23. Important Reverse Formula

If A is `x% more than B`, then B is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100\%
}
$$

less than A.

Example:

A is `25% more than B`.

Then B is:

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%}
$$

less than A.

---

# 24. Reverse of Percentage Decrease

If A is `x% less than B`, then B is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100\%
}
$$

more than A.

Example:

A is `20% less than B`.

Then:

$$
A=80\%\text{ of B}
$$

B compared with A:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

> B is `25% more than A`.

---

# 25. Important Reverse Comparison Table

| First Statement | Reverse Statement |
|---|---|
| A is `10%` more than B | B is `9.09%` less than A |
| A is `20%` more than B | B is `16.67%` less than A |
| A is `25%` more than B | B is `20%` less than A |
| A is `50%` more than B | B is `33.33%` less than A |
| A is `100%` more than B | B is `50%` less than A |
| A is `20%` less than B | B is `25%` more than A |
| A is `25%` less than B | B is `33.33%` more than A |
| A is `50%` less than B | B is `100%` more than A |

A=101
$$

and:

B=100

$$
\frac{101-100}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{1\%}
$$

---

# 27. Comparison With Small Difference

Suppose:

$$
A=1000
$$

and:

$$
B=980
$$

A is what percentage more than B?

Difference:

$$
20
$$

Reference:

$$
980
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{20}{980}\times100
\approx
\boxed{2.04\%}
$$

---

# 28. Comparing Percentages

Suppose:

A = `40%`

B = `25%`

A is what percentage more than B?

Difference:

$$
40-25=15
$$

Reference:

$$
25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{15}{25}\times100
=
\boxed{60\%}
$$

> [!important]
> A difference of `15 percentage points` corresponds to a `60% increase` relative to `25%`.

---

# 29. Percentage Point Difference

If:

$$
A=65\%
$$

and:

$$
B=45\%
$$

then percentage-point difference:

$$
65-45
=
\boxed{20\text{ percentage points}}
$$

This is different from percentage increase.

---

# 30. Percentage Increase Between Percentages

Using the same example:

$$
65\%-45\%=20\%
$$

But percentage increase relative to `45%` is:

$$
\frac{20}{45}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{44.44\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\text{ percentage points}
\ne
44.44\%\text{ increase}
}
$$

---

$$
=\boxed{50\%}

# 35. "How Many Percent Is A of B?"

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac AB\times100
}
$$

Example:

`45 is how many percent of 60?`

$$
\frac{45}{60}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{75\%}
$$

---

# 36. "How Much Percent More?"

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{A-B}{B}\times100
}
$$

Example:

`75 is how much percent more than 60?`

$$
\frac{75-60}{60}\times100
$$

$$
=\frac{15}{60}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 37. "How Much Percent Less?"

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{B}\times100
}
$$

Example:

`60 is how much percent less than 75?`

$$
\frac{75-60}{75}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%}
$$

Notice:

`75 is 25% more than 60`

but:

`60 is 20% less than 75`.

---

# 38. Golden Comparison Example

Take:

$$
A=80
$$

$$
B=100
$$

Then:

### A as percentage of B

$$
\frac{80}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{80\%}
$$

### A is how much less than B?

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

### B is how much more than A?

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Three different answers:

$$
\boxed{80\%,\ 20\%,\ 25\%}
$$

depending on the question.

---

# 39. Common Trap

> [!warning]
> Never use the difference as the denominator automatically.

For:

> `80 is what percentage less than 100?`

Wrong:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
25\%
$$

Correct:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Because `100` is the reference.

---

# 40. Common Trap — Reversing the Base

Question:

> `120 is what percentage more than 100?`

Correct:

$$
\frac{120-100}{100}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Not:

$$
\frac{20}{120}\times100
$$

The latter answers a different question.

---

# 41. Common Trap — "Of" vs "More"

`120 is 120% of 100.`

But:

`120 is 20% more than 100.`

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
120\%\text{ of}\ne120\%\text{ more}
}
$$

# 50. Advanced Pattern — Same Difference, Different Percentage

Suppose:

$$
A=100,\quad B=80
$$

Difference:

$$
20
$$

A compared to B:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
25\%
$$

B compared to A:

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Same absolute difference:

$$
20
$$

but different percentages because the base changes.

---

# 51. Important Insight

> [!important]
> **Percentage is relative, not absolute.**

A difference of `20` can represent:

- `20%` of `100`
- `25%` of `80`
- `40%` of `50`
- `10%` of `200`

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Always identify the reference}
}
$$

> **The question determines the denominator. Ask: "Percentage relative to WHICH value?" That value goes in the denominator.**

> **Percentage comparison is all about the reference value: identify the base first, calculate the difference or required part, then divide by that base and multiply by `100`.**
