# Meaning of Percentage

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> **Percentage** means **per hundred**.
>
> The symbol `%` means:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\%= \frac{1}{100}}
> $$

Therefore:

$$
x\%=\frac{x}{100}
$$

Example:

$$
25\%
=
\frac{25}{100}
=
\frac14
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{25\%=\frac14}
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of "Percent"

The word **percent** comes from the idea of:

$$
\boxed{\text{per hundred}}
$$

Therefore:

$$
1\%= \frac1{100}
$$

$$
10\%= \frac{10}{100}
$$

$$
50\%= \frac{50}{100}
$$

$$
100\%= \frac{100}{100}=1
$$

---

# 3. Basic Formula

The fundamental percentage formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100
}
$$

This is the most important formula in the entire Percentage chapter.

---

# 4. Finding Percentage

If:

- Part = `20`
- Whole = `80`

then:

$$
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{20}{80}\times100
$$

$$
=
\frac14\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 5. Three Fundamental Forms

Every percentage problem can usually be reduced to one of these:

### Find Percentage

$$
\boxed{
P=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100
}
$$

### Find Part

$$
\boxed{
\text{Part}
=
\frac{P}{100}\times\text{Whole}
}
$$

### Find Whole

$$
\boxed{
\text{Whole}
=
\frac{\text{Part}\times100}{P}
}
$$

These three formulas should be memorized.

---

# 6. Percentage of a Number

To find `x%` of a number `N`:

$$
\boxed{
x\%\text{ of }N
=
\frac{x}{100}\times N
}
$$

Example:

Find `20%` of `250`.

$$
20\%\text{ of }250
=
\frac{20}{100}\times250
$$

$$
=\frac15\times250
$$

$$
=\boxed{50}
$$

---

# 7. Important Word — "Of"

In percentage questions:

$$
\boxed{\text{"of"}=\text{multiplication}}
$$

Example:

`30% of 200`

means:

$$
30\%\times200
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{30}{100}\times200
=
\boxed{60}
$$

---

# 8. Percentage to Fraction

Convert percentage to fraction by dividing by `100`.

$$
\boxed{
x\%=\frac{x}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
40\%
=
\frac{40}{100}
=
\boxed{\frac25}
$$

---

# 9. Percentage to Decimal

Divide by `100`.

$$
\boxed{
x\%= \frac{x}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
35\%
=
\frac{35}{100}
=
\boxed{0.35}
$$

---

# 10. Fraction to Percentage

Multiply the fraction by `100`.

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac34
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac34\times100
=
\boxed{75\%}
$$

---

# 11. Decimal to Percentage

Multiply by `100`.

$$
\boxed{
\text{Decimal}\times100=\text{Percentage}
}
$$

Example:

$$
0.72\times100
=
\boxed{72\%}
$$

---

# 12. Percentage Triangle

A useful memory structure:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}
=
\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100
}
$$

Think:

```text
        PART
       ────── × 100
       WHOLE
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Part}
=
\frac{\text{Percentage}\times\text{Whole}}{100}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Whole}
=
\frac{\text{Part}\times100}{\text{Percentage}}
}
$$

Sometimes it is faster to use:

$$
\boxed{
x\%=100\%-(100-x)\%
}
$$

Example:

Find `98%` of `500`.

$$
98\%=100\%-2\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
500-2\%\text{ of }500
$$

$$
=500-10
$$

$$
=\boxed{490}
$$

# 24. Percentage Comparison

Percentage comparison depends on the **reference value**.

Suppose:

- A = `80`
- B = `100`

A is what percentage of B?

$$
\frac{80}{100}\times100
=
\boxed{80\%}
$$

---

# 25. "A Is What Percentage of B?"

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac AB\times100
}
$$

Example:

`40 is what percentage of 160?`

$$
\frac{40}{160}\times100
$$

$$
=\frac14\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 26. "A Is What Percent More Than B?"

The base is **B**.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{A-B}{B}\times100
}
$$

Example:

A = `120`

B = `100`

Then:

$$
\frac{120-100}{100}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%}
$$

Therefore:

> `120 is 20% more than 100.`

---

# 27. "A Is What Percent Less Than B?"

The base is again **B**.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{B-A}{B}\times100
}
$$

Example:

A = `80`

B = `100`

$$
\frac{100-80}{100}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%}
$$

Therefore:

> `80 is 20% less than 100.`

---

# 28. The Base Rule

> [!important]
> **The number after "than" is usually the reference/base value.**

Example:

`A is 20% more than B`

means:

$$
A=B+20\%\text{ of }B
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=1.2B
}
$$

---

# 29. Percentage Representation

If a quantity is `x%` of another quantity:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{x}{100}B
}
$$

Example:

A is `40%` of B.

Then:

$$
A=\frac{40}{100}B
$$

$$
\boxed{
A=0.4B
}
$$

---

# 30. Percentage as a Ratio

If:

$$
A=40\%\text{ of }B
$$

then:

$$
A:B=40:100
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{
A:B=2:5
}
$$

---

# 31. Percentage to Ratio

To convert:

$$
x\%
$$

into a ratio:

$$
\boxed{
x:100
}
$$

Example:

$$
35\%
=
35:100
=
\boxed{7:20}
$$

---

# 32. Ratio to Percentage

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

then A as a percentage of B is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac mn\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac35\times100
=
\boxed{60\%}
$$

---

# 33. Percentage of Total

Suppose a class has:

- Boys = `30`
- Girls = `20`
- Total = `50`

Percentage of boys:

$$
\frac{30}{50}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{60\%}
$$

Percentage of girls:

$$
\frac{20}{50}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{40\%}
$$

Notice:

$$
60\%+40\%=100\%
$$

---

# 34. Part-Whole Pattern

If:

$$
\text{Part}_1+\text{Part}_2=\text{Whole}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
P_1+P_2=100\%
}
$$

For three or more parts:

$$
\boxed{
P_1+P_2+\cdots+P_n=100\%
}
$$

---

# 35. Percentage in Marks

Suppose a student scores:

`420` out of `500`.

Percentage:

$$
\frac{420}{500}\times100
$$

Cancel:

$$
\frac{42}{50}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{84\%}
$$

---

# 36. Percentage in Population

Suppose a village has:

`20,000` people.

If `35%` are children:

$$
\frac{35}{100}\times20000
$$

$$
=\boxed{7000}
$$

---

# 37. Percentage in Salary

Salary:

$$
₹40,000
$$

Savings:

`20%`

Savings:

$$
20\%\times40000
$$

$$
=\frac15\times40000
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹8,000}
$$

---

# 38. Percentage in Expenditure

Suppose income is:

$$
₹50,000
$$

and expenditure is `70%`.

Then:

$$
70\%\times50000
$$

$$
=\boxed{₹35,000}
$$

Savings:

$$
100\%-70\%=30\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
30\%\times50000
=
\boxed{₹15,000}
$$

---

# 39. Percentage in Consumption

Suppose a family consumes:

`80 kg`

of rice.

If consumption is `25%` of total available rice:

$$
25\%\times80
$$

This illustrates the general rule:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Percentage}\times\text{Reference quantity}
}
$$

Always identify what quantity the percentage is based on.

---

# 40. Percentage Increase

If original value is `O` and new value is `N`:

Increase:

$$
N-O
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{N-O}{O}\times100
}
$$

The **original value** is the denominator.

---

# 41. Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{O-N}{O}\times100
}
$$

Again, the denominator is the **original value**.

---

# 42. Important Pattern

> [!important]
> For **change questions**, the denominator is usually the **original value**.

Increase:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}\times100
}
$$

Decrease:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original}}\times100
}
$$

---

# 43. Percentage Change Example

Price increases from:

$$
₹500
$$

to:

$$
₹600
$$

Increase:

$$
600-500=100
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 44. Percentage Decrease Example

Price decreases from:

$$
₹800
$$

to:

$$
₹600
$$

Decrease:

$$
800-600=200
$$

Percentage decrease:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 45. Percentage Point vs Percentage

These are different.

Suppose a value changes from:

$$
40\%
$$

to:

$$
50\%
$$

Percentage-point increase:

$$
50\%-40\%
=
\boxed{10\text{ percentage points}}
$$

Percentage increase relative to the original percentage:

$$
\frac{50-40}{40}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

> [!warning]
> `10 percentage points` and `25% increase` are not the same thing.

---

# 46. Fractional Interpretation

Percentage can always be treated as a fraction.

$$
x\%
=
\frac{x}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
150\%
=
\frac{150}{100}
=
\boxed{1.5}
$$

So:

$$
150\%\text{ of }200
=
1.5\times200
=
\boxed{300}
$$

---

# 47. Percentages Greater Than 100%

A percentage can exceed `100%`.

Examples:

$$
120\%=1.2
$$

$$
150\%=1.5
$$

$$
200\%=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
200\%\text{ of }50
=
\boxed{100}
$$

---

# 48. Important Interpretation

If:

$$
A=150\%\text{ of }B
$$

then:

$$
A=1.5B
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A:B=3:2
}
$$

# 49. Percentage as a Multiplier

This is extremely important for percentage-change problems.

### Increase by `x%`

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Value}
=
\text{Old Value}\times
\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

### Decrease by `x%`

$$
\boxed{
\text{New Value}
=
\text{Old Value}\times
\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 50. Example — Increase

Original:

$$
500
$$

Increase:

`20%`

New value:

$$
500\times1.20
$$

$$
=\boxed{600}
$$

---

# 51. Example — Decrease

Original:

$$
500
$$

Decrease:

`20%`

New value:

$$
500\times0.80
$$

$$
=\boxed{400}
$$

---

# 52. Successive Percentage Changes

If changes are applied one after another, **do not simply add percentages** in general.

Example:

Increase by `20%`, then increase by `10%`.

First multiplier:

$$
1.20
$$

Second multiplier:

$$
1.10
$$

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.20\times1.10
=
1.32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{32\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 53. Successive Change Formula

For changes `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net change}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Use signs:

- Increase → positive
- Decrease → negative

---

# 54. Example — Increase Then Decrease

Increase by `20%`, then decrease by `10%`.

Use:

$$
20+(-10)+\frac{20(-10)}{100}
$$

$$
=10-2
$$

$$
=\boxed{8\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 55. Reverse Percentage

If the final value is known but the original value is unknown, work backward.

If a value increased by `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{\text{Final}}{1+x/100}
}
$$

If it decreased by `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Original}
=
\frac{\text{Final}}{1-x/100}
}
$$

# 56. Common Traps

> [!warning] Must Avoid

- ❌ Forgetting that `%` means `/100`.
- ❌ Using the wrong reference value.
- ❌ Dividing percentage change by the new value instead of original value.
- ❌ Adding successive percentages directly.
- ❌ Confusing percentage points with percentage increase.
- ❌ Assuming `x% of y = y% of x` without checking the actual context.
- ❌ Forgetting that percentages can exceed `100%`.
- ❌ Treating `20% increase` as adding `20` instead of multiplying by `1.20`.
- ❌ Treating `20% decrease` as multiplying by `0.20` instead of `0.80`.
- ❌ Ignoring the word "than" when identifying the base.
