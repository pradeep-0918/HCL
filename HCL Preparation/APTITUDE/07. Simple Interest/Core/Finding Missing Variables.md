# Finding Missing Variables

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> In Simple Interest problems, if any one of the four quantities is missing, use the basic formula to find it.
>
> The four main variables are:
>
> - **Principal (P)**
> - **Rate (R)**
> - **Time (T)**
> - **Simple Interest (SI)**
>
> The master formula is:
>
> $$\boxed{
> SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
> }$$

---

# 2. Master Formula

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
}
$$

From this one formula, all missing-variable formulas can be derived.

```text
          P × R × T
SI = ───────────────
             100
```

---

# 3. Finding Principal

Starting with:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Rearrange:

$$
\boxed{
P=\frac{100SI}{RT}
}
$$

### Example

SI = ₹1,500

R = 10%

T = 3 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(1500)}{10(3)}
$$

$$
=\frac{150000}{30}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹5,000}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Rate

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹8,000

SI = ₹2,400

T = 3 years.

$$
R=
\frac{100(2400)}{8000(3)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=10\%}
$$

---

# 5. Finding Time

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹10,000

R = 8%

SI = ₹2,400.

$$
T=
\frac{100(2400)}{10000(8)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 6. Finding Simple Interest

If P, R and T are known:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹15,000

R = 6%

T = 4 years.

$$
SI=
\frac{15000(6)(4)}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3,600}
$$

---

# 7. Finding Amount

Amount is:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹10,000

R = 10%

T = 2 years.

$$
A=
10000(1.2)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹12,000}
$$

---

# 8. Finding Principal From Amount

If amount is known:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=\frac{100A}{100+RT}
}
$$

### Example

A = ₹13,200

R = 10%

T = 2 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(13200)}{100+20}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹11,000}
$$

---

# 9. Finding Rate From Amount

First find SI:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

Then:

$$
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PT}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹10,000

A = ₹13,000

T = 6 years.

$$
SI=13000-10000
=
₹3000
$$

$$
R=
\frac{100(3000)}{10000(6)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 10. Finding Time From Amount

Again:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PR}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹8,000

A = ₹10,400

R = 10%.

$$
T=
\frac{100(10400-8000)}{8000(10)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 11. Finding SI From Amount

If P and A are known:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

### Example

A = ₹18,000

P = ₹15,000.

$$
SI=18000-15000
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3,000}
$$

# 14. Formula Triangle

A useful memory structure:

```text
             P × R × T
          ───────────────
                 100
                    ↓
                   SI
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

To find a missing variable, rearrange the equation.

---

# 15. Missing Principal — Direct Pattern

Question pattern:

> "A sum earns ₹2,000 as SI at 5% for 4 years. Find the principal."

Identify:

```text
SI = 2000
R  = 5
T  = 4
P  = ?
```

Use:

$$
P=\frac{100SI}{RT}
$$

$$
P=
\frac{100(2000)}{5(4)}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹10,000}
$$

---

# 16. Missing Rate — Direct Pattern

Question pattern:

> "₹10,000 earns ₹2,000 in 4 years. Find the rate."

Identify:

```text
P  = 10000
SI = 2000
T  = 4
R  = ?
```

Use:

$$
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
$$

$$
R=
\frac{100(2000)}{10000(4)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 17. Missing Time — Direct Pattern

Question pattern:

> "₹10,000 earns ₹3,000 at 10% simple interest. Find the time."

Identify:

```text
P  = 10000
SI = 3000
R  = 10
T  = ?
```

Use:

$$
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
$$

$$
T=
\frac{100(3000)}{10000(10)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 18. Missing SI — Direct Pattern

Question pattern:

> "Find SI on ₹20,000 at 8% for 3 years."

Use:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

$$
SI=
\frac{20000(8)(3)}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹4,800}
$$

---

# 19. Missing Amount — Direct Pattern

Question pattern:

> "Find the amount on ₹20,000 at 8% for 3 years."

First:

$$
SI=
\frac{20000(8)(3)}{100}
=
₹4800
$$

Then:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

$$
A=20000+4800
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹24,800}
$$

Or directly:

$$
A=
20000\left(1+\frac{8(3)}{100}\right)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹24,800}
$$

---

# 20. Reverse Problem With Amount

> "A sum amounts to ₹13,200 in 2 years at 10% SI. Find the principal."

$$

\frac{100A}{100+RT}
$$

$$
P=
\frac{100(13200)}{100+20}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹11,000}
$$

# 21. Reverse Problem With SI

Question:

> "A sum earns ₹2,400 SI at 8% for 5 years. Find the principal."

$$
P=
\frac{100(2400)}{8(5)}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹6,000}
$$

# 22. Reverse Problem With Amount and Principal

Question:

> "A sum of ₹10,000 becomes ₹13,000 in 6 years. Find the rate."

First:

$$
SI=13000-10000
$$

$$
SI=₹3000
$$

Then:

$$
R=
\frac{100(3000)}{10000(6)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 23. Reverse Problem With Amount and Rate

Question:

> "₹10,000 becomes ₹12,000 at 10% SI. Find the time."

SI:

$$
12000-10000
=
₹2000
$$

Then:

$$
T=
\frac{100(2000)}{10000(10)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=2\ years}
$$

# 24. Amount Percentage Shortcut

If the question says:

> "The amount becomes 140% of the principal."

Then:

$$
SI=140\%-100\%
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=40\%\text{ of P}}
$$

Since:

$$
SI\%=RT
$$

we get:

$$
RT=40
$$

If `R = 8%`:

$$
T=\frac{40}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=5\ years}
$$

---

# 25. Doubling Shortcut

If:

$$
A=2P
$$

then:

$$
SI=P
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=100
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100}{T}
}
$$

---

# 26. Tripling Shortcut

If:

$$
A=3P
$$

then:

$$
SI=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=200
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

---

# 27. k-Times Shortcut

If:

$$
A=kP
$$

then:

$$
SI=(k-1)P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
RT=100(k-1)
}
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(k-1)}{R}
}
$$

---

# 28. Example — 2.5 Times

A sum becomes 2.5 times in 15 years.

Find the rate.

$$
R=
\frac{100(2.5-1)}{15}
$$

$$
=
\frac{150}{15}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=10\%}
$$

# 29. Time Conversion

> [!important]
> In the standard SI formula, time is measured in **years**.

### Months

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{M}{12}
}
$$

### Days — 365-day convention

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{D}{365}
}
$$

### Days — 360-day convention

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{D}{360}
}
$$

---

# 30. Example — Months

P = ₹12,000

R = 10%

T = 9 months.

Convert:

$$
T=\frac9{12}
=
\frac34
$$

Then:

$$
SI=
\frac{12000(10)(3/4)}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹900}
$$

---

# 31. Example — Find Months

P = ₹10,000

R = 12%

SI = ₹500.

First find years:

$$
T=
\frac{100(500)}{10000(12)}
$$

$$
T=\frac5{12}
$$

Convert to months:

$$
12\times\frac5{12}
=
\boxed{5\ months}
$$

---

# 32. Same Interest Comparison

For equal SI:

$$
\boxed{
P_1R_1T_1=P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

### Example

First investment:

$$
P_1=₹10,000
$$

$$
R_1=5\%
$$

$$
T_1=4
$$

Second:

$$
P_2=₹20,000
$$

$$
R_2=5\%
$$

Find `T₂`.

$$
10000(5)(4)
=
20000(5)T_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{T_2=2\ years}
$$

---

# 33. Same Principal and Rate

If P and R are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto T
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Time ×2 → SI ×2
Time ×3 → SI ×3
```

---

# 34. Same Principal and Time

If P and T are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto R
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Rate ×2 → SI ×2
Rate ×3 → SI ×3
```

---

# 35. Same Rate and Time

If R and T are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto P
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Principal ×2 → SI ×2
Principal ×3 → SI ×3
```

---

# 36. Difference in Simple Interest

If P and R are fixed:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta SI=
\frac{PR\Delta T}{100}
}
$$

If P and T are fixed:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta SI=
\frac{PT\Delta R}{100}
}
$$

If R and T are fixed:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta SI=
\frac{RT\Delta P}{100}
}
$$

---

# 37. Example — Difference in Rate

P = ₹20,000

T = 3 years

Rate changes from 5% to 8%.

Difference:

$$
\Delta R=3\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Delta SI=
\frac{20000(3)(3)}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta SI=₹1800}
$$

---

# 38. Multiple Missing Variables

Sometimes the question gives an amount and asks for two variables.

Example:

> A sum of ₹10,000 becomes ₹12,000 in 4 years. Find the rate.

Here:

```text
P = 10000
A = 12000
T = 4
R = ?
```

First:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

$$
SI=2000
$$

Then:

$$
R=
\frac{100(2000)}{10000(4)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 39. Finding Two Unknowns

If only one equation is available and two variables are unknown, the problem generally needs an additional condition.

For example:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

If both `R` and `T` are unknown, one equation alone is insufficient.

> [!important]
> Look for an additional condition such as:
>
> - Amount becomes twice
> - Difference in interest
> - Rate changes
> - Time changes
> - Two investments earn equal interest

---

# 40. Example — Two Conditions

A sum earns ₹2,000 SI in 4 years at rate `R`.

Then:

$$
2000=
\frac{P(R)(4)}{100}
$$

If the principal is known, `R` can be found.

If the principal is also unknown, another condition is necessary.

# 41. Different Rates for Different Periods

Suppose:

P = ₹20,000

First 2 years at 5%

Next 3 years at 8%.

First SI:

$$
SI_1=
\frac{20000(5)(2)}{100}
=
₹2000
$$

Second SI:

$$
SI_2=
\frac{20000(8)(3)}{100}
=
₹4800
$$

Total:

$$
SI=6800
$$

Amount:

$$
A=20000+6800
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹26,800}
$$

# 42. Equivalent Rate

For different rates over different periods:

$$
\boxed{
R_{eq}=
\frac{R_1T_1+R_2T_2}
{T_1+T_2}
}
$$

Example:

2 years at 5%

3 years at 8%.

$$
R_{eq}
=
\frac{5(2)+8(3)}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{R_{eq}=6.8\%}
$$

# 46. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using Amount in SI Formula

Wrong:

$$
SI=\frac{ART}{100}
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting `A − P`

When amount is given:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Not Converting Months

6 months:

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing SI and Amount

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Additional Conditions

If two variables are unknown, look for another equation or condition.
