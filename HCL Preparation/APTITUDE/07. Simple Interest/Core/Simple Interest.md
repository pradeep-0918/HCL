# Simple Interest

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Simple Interest (SI)** is the interest calculated only on the **original Principal (P)** for the entire period.
>
> The interest does **not** get added to the principal for calculating future interest.

The main formula is:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
}
$$

where:

- `P` = Principal
- `R` = Rate of interest per year
- `T` = Time in years
- `SI` = Simple Interest

---

# 2. Meaning of Simple Interest

Suppose ₹10,000 is invested at 10% per annum for 3 years.

Interest for one year:

$$
10000\times\frac{10}{100}
=
₹1000
$$

Since Simple Interest uses the same principal every year:

```text
Year 1 → ₹1,000
Year 2 → ₹1,000
Year 3 → ₹1,000
```

Total:

$$
SI=1000+1000+1000
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3000}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

This is the most important formula in the entire Simple Interest chapter.

### Memory Trick

> **P × R × T → SI**

```text
Principal × Rate × Time
          ↓
      Simple Interest
```

---

# 4. Components of Simple Interest

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P | Principal |
| R | Rate % |
| T | Time |
| SI | Simple Interest |
| A | Amount |

The complete relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

---

# 5. Basic Example

Find the Simple Interest on ₹5,000 at 8% per annum for 3 years.

Given:

$$
P=5000
$$

$$
R=8
$$

$$
T=3
$$

Formula:

$$
SI=
\frac{5000\times8\times3}{100}
$$

$$
SI=
\frac{120000}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹1200}
$$

---

# 6. Year-by-Year Understanding

Take:

$$
P=₹10,000
$$

$$
R=10\%
$$

### Year 1

$$
SI=₹1000
$$

### Year 2

Interest is still calculated on ₹10,000:

$$
SI=₹1000
$$

### Year 3

Again:

$$
SI=₹1000
$$

Total:

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

> [!important]
> Under Simple Interest, the principal remains the base for every year.

---

# 7. Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

### Simple Interest

Interest is always calculated on:

$$
\boxed{Original\ Principal}
$$

### Compound Interest

Interest is calculated on:

$$
\boxed{Principal+\text{Accumulated Interest}}
$$

Example:

```text
Simple Interest:
₹10,000 → Interest based on ₹10,000 every year

Compound Interest:
₹10,000 → ₹11,000 → Interest on ₹11,000 → ...
```

---

# 8. Finding Principal

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
P=\frac{100SI}{RT}
}
$$

### Example

SI = ₹2,000

Rate = 10%

Time = 4 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(2000)}{10\times4}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹5000}
$$

---

# 9. Finding Rate

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹5,000

SI = ₹1,500

T = 3 years.

$$
R=
\frac{100(1500)}{5000(3)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=10\%}
$$

---

# 10. Finding Time

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹8,000

R = 5%

SI = ₹1,200.

$$
T=
\frac{100(1200)}{8000(5)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 11. Finding Amount

Amount is the total money received or repaid.

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Example:

Principal:

$$
₹10,000
$$

SI:

$$
₹2,000
$$

Then:

$$
A=10000+2000
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹12000}
$$

---

# 12. Direct Amount Formula

Since:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

and:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

we get:

$$
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Taking `P` common:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 13. Example — Direct Amount

P = ₹20,000

R = 5%

T = 3 years.

$$
A=
20000\left(1+\frac{5\times3}{100}\right)
$$

$$
=20000(1.15)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹23000}
$$

---

# 14. Simple Interest as a Percentage of Principal

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

divide both sides by `P`:

$$
\frac{SI}{P}
=
\frac{RT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI\%=\frac{RT}{100}\times100
}
$$

or simply:

$$
\boxed{
SI\text{ as % of }P=R\times T
}
$$

### Example

Rate = 8%

Time = 3 years.

Therefore:

$$
SI=8\times3
=
\boxed{24\%\text{ of Principal}}
$$

---

# 15. Fast SI Calculation

If:

$$
R\times T
$$

is easy to calculate, use:

$$
\boxed{
SI=P\times\frac{RT}{100}
}
$$

Example:

P = ₹12,000

R = 6%

T = 5 years.

$$
RT=30
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=12000\times\frac{30}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3600}
$$

---

# 16. Percentage Method

Suppose:

P = ₹15,000

Rate = 4%

Time = 5 years.

Interest percentage:

$$
R\times T
=
4\times5
=
20\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=20\%\text{ of }15000
$$

$$
=₹3000
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3000}
$$

---

# 17. Amount as a Percentage of Principal

If:

$$
SI=RT\%\text{ of }P
$$

then:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=(100+RT)\%\text{ of }P
}
$$

Example:

Rate = 10%

Time = 4 years.

Interest:

$$
40\%
$$

Amount:

$$
100\%+40\%
=
\boxed{140\%\text{ of Principal}}
$$

---

# 18. Amount Multiplier

The direct formula is:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

Equivalent percentage form:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\frac{100+RT}{100}
}
$$

---

# 19. Reverse Amount Problem

If amount is known:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=
\frac{100A}{100+RT}
}
$$

### Example

A = ₹12,000

R = 10%

T = 2 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(12000)}{100+20}
$$

$$
=
\frac{1200000}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹10000}
$$

---

# 20. Amount and Interest Relationship

Always remember:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
P=A-SI
}
$$

---

# 21. Time in Months

The standard SI formula uses years.

If time is given in months:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{Months}{12}
}
$$

### Example

P = ₹12,000

R = 10%

T = 6 months.

$$
T=\frac6{12}
=
\frac12
$$

Then:

$$
SI=
\frac{12000\times10\times\frac12}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹600}
$$

---

# 22. Direct Formula for Months

If `M` is the number of months:

$$
T=\frac M{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=
\frac{PRM}{1200}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRM}{1200}
}
$$

---

# 23. Time in Days

If the problem specifies:

$$
1\ year=365\ days
$$

then:

$$
T=\frac{D}{365}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRD}{36500}
}
$$

If the problem uses a 360-day year:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRD}{36000}
}
$$

> [!warning]
> Always follow the day-count convention given in the question.

---

# 24. Fractional Time

Suppose:

$$

Then:

$$
SI=\frac{PR(2.5)}{100}
$$

Example:

P = ₹8,000

R = 10%

T = 2.5 years.

$$
SI=
\frac{8000\times10\times2.5}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹2000}
$$

# 26. Doubling of Money

If money doubles:

$$
A=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=P
$$

Using:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
RT=100
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100}{T}
}
$$

---

# 27. Example — Doubling

Rate = 10%.

Time required:

$$
T=\frac{100}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{10\ years}
$$

---

# 28. Tripling of Money

If:

$$
A=3P
$$

then:

$$
SI=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=200
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

### Example

Rate = 8%.

$$
T=\frac{200}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\ years}
$$

---

# 29. k Times the Principal

If:

$$
A=kP
$$

then:

$$
SI=(k-1)P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
RT=100(k-1)
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(k-1)}{R}
}
$$

---

# 30. Example — Four Times

Money becomes 4 times in 20 years.

$$
R=
\frac{100(4-1)}{20}
$$

$$
=\frac{300}{20}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=15\%}
$$

---

# 31. Same Principal and Rate

If `P` and `R` are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto T
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Time ×2 → SI ×2
Time ×3 → SI ×3
Time ÷2 → SI ÷2
```

---

# 32. Same Principal and Time

If `P` and `T` are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto R
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Rate ×2 → SI ×2
Rate ×3 → SI ×3
```

---

# 33. Same Rate and Time

If `R` and `T` are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto P
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Principal ×2 → SI ×2
Principal ×3 → SI ×3
```

---

# 34. Same Simple Interest

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

if two investments earn the same SI:

$$
\boxed{
P_1R_1T_1=P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

This is a very useful aptitude shortcut.

---

# 35. Example — Same SI

First investment:

$$
P_1=₹10,000
$$

$$
R_1=5\%
$$

$$
T_1=4
$$

Second:

$$
P_2=₹20,000
$$

$$
R_2=5\%
$$

Find `T₂`.

Using:

$$
10000(5)(4)
=
20000(5)T_2
$$

Cancel common factors:

$$
2T_2=4
$$

$$
\boxed{T_2=2\ years}
$$

---

# 36. Different Rates for Different Periods

Suppose:

P = ₹10,000

First 2 years at 5%

Next 3 years at 8%.

First period:

$$
SI_1=
\frac{10000\times5\times2}{100}
=
₹1000
$$

Second period:

$$
SI_2=
\frac{10000\times8\times3}{100}
=
₹2400
$$

Total:

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3400}
$$

Amount:

$$
10000+3400
=
\boxed{₹13400}
$$

---

# 37. Equivalent Rate

For different rates over different periods:

$$
\boxed{
R_{eq}=
\frac{R_1T_1+R_2T_2+\cdots}
{T_1+T_2+\cdots}
}
$$

Example:

2 years at 5%

3 years at 8%.

$$
R_{eq}
=
\frac{5(2)+8(3)}{5}
$$

$$
=
\frac{34}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{6.8\%}
$$

---

# 38. Difference in Simple Interest

If principal and rate are fixed:

$$
SI_1-SI_2
=
\frac{PR(T_1-T_2)}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta SI=
\frac{PR\Delta T}{100}
}
$$

---

# 39. Example — Difference in SI

P = ₹20,000

R = 5%

Difference in time = 3 years.

$$
\Delta SI=
\frac{20000\times5\times3}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta SI=₹3000}
$$

---

# 40. Simple Interest and Ratios

Since:

$$
SI\propto PRT
$$

the ratio of two simple interests is:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=
P_1R_1T_1:P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

---

# 41. Example — SI Ratio

Given:

$$
P_1:P_2=2:3
$$

$$
R_1:R_2=3:4
$$

$$
T_1:T_2=2:1
$$

Then:

$$
SI_1:SI_2
=
(2)(3)(2):(3)(4)(1)
$$

$$
=12:12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{SI_1:SI_2=1:1}
$$

# 48. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using Compound Interest Formula

Simple Interest:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

Do not calculate interest on accumulated interest.

---

### Mistake 2 — Using Amount Instead of Principal

The SI formula uses:

$$
\boxed{P}
$$

not `A`.

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting to Convert Months

6 months:

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing SI and Amount

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

not:

$$
A=P-SI
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Forgetting the 100

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$
