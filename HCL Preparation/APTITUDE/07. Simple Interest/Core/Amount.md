# Amount

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Amount (A)** is the total money obtained after adding Simple Interest to the original Principal.
>
> $$\boxed{
> Amount=Principal+Simple\ Interest
> }$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

where:

- `A` = Amount
- `P` = Principal
- `SI` = Simple Interest

---

# 2. Basic Formula

The most important relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
P=A-SI
}
$$

---

# 3. Example

Principal:

$$
₹10,000
$$

Simple Interest:

$$
₹2,000
$$

Amount:

$$
A=10000+2000
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹12,000}
$$

---

# 4. Amount Using Simple Interest Formula

We know:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

and:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Taking `P` common:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

This is the direct Amount formula.

---

# 5. Alternative Amount Formula

Multiply by 100:

$$
A=
P\left(\frac{100+RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{P(100+RT)}{100}
}
$$

---

# 6. Example — Direct Formula

Principal:

$$
₹20,000
$$

Rate:

$$
5\%
$$

Time:

$$
3\ years
$$

Using:

$$
A=
20000\left(1+\frac{5\times3}{100}\right)
$$

$$
=20000(1.15)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹23,000}
$$

---

# 7. Amount as a Percentage of Principal

From:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

If:

$$
R=8\%
$$

and:

$$
T=3
$$

then:

$$
RT=24
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=124\%\text{ of }P
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
A=124\%\ of\ Principal
}
$$

---

# 8. Fast Percentage Method

For Simple Interest:

$$
\boxed{
SI\% = R\times T
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Amount\%=100+RT
}
$$

Example:

Rate = 10%

Time = 4 years.

Interest percentage:

$$
10\times4=40\%
$$

Amount percentage:

$$
100+40
=
\boxed{140\%}
$$

---

# 9. Example — Percentage Method

Principal:

$$
₹15,000
$$

Rate:

$$
6\%
$$

Time:

$$
5\ years
$$

Interest percentage:

$$
6\times5=30\%
$$

Amount:

$$
130\%\text{ of }15000
$$

$$
=15000(1.3)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹19,500}
$$

---

# 10. Finding Amount From SI

If Principal and SI are known:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Example:

P = ₹8,000

SI = ₹1,600.

$$
A=8000+1600
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹9,600}
$$

---

# 11. Finding Principal From Amount

Starting with:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=
\frac{A}{1+\frac{RT}{100}}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
P=
\frac{100A}{100+RT}
}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Find Principal

Amount:

$$
₹12,000
$$

Rate:

$$
10\%
$$

Time:

$$
2\ years
$$

Then:

$$
P=
\frac{100(12000)}{100+20}
$$

$$
=
\frac{1200000}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹10,000}
$$

---

# 13. Finding Amount From Principal and SI

Example:

Principal:

$$
₹7,500
$$

Simple Interest:

$$
₹2,250
$$

Then:

$$
A=7500+2250
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹9,750}
$$

---

# 14. Finding SI From Amount

If Principal and Amount are known:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

Example:

Amount = ₹15,000

Principal = ₹12,000.

$$
SI=15000-12000
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3,000}
$$

---

# 15. Finding Rate From Amount

Since:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

and:

$$
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
R=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PT}
}
$$

Example:

P = ₹10,000

A = ₹12,000

T = 4 years.

$$
R=
\frac{100(2000)}{10000(4)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 16. Finding Time From Amount

Since:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

and:

$$
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
T=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PR}
}
$$

Example:

P = ₹8,000

A = ₹10,400

R = 10%.

$$
T=
\frac{100(2400)}{8000(10)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 17. Amount Multiplier

The multiplier for Simple Interest is:

$$
\boxed{
1+\frac{RT}{100}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\times Amount\ Multiplier
}
$$

Example:

Rate = 8%

Time = 5 years.

$$
1+\frac{8\times5}{100}
=
1.4
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{A=1.4P}
$$

---

# 18. Amount When Money Doubles

If:

$$
A=2P
$$

then:

$$
SI=P
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=100
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
A=2P
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

---

# 19. Example — Double

P = ₹10,000

R = 10%

T = 10 years.

$$
A=
10000\left(1+\frac{10\times10}{100}\right)
$$

$$
=10000(2)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹20,000}
$$

---

# 20. Amount When Money Triples

$$

$$

Therefore:

$$

$$

So:

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

---

# 21. Example — Triple

P = ₹10,000

$$

10000\left(1+\frac{10\times20}{100}\right)
$$

=10000(3)

\boxed{A=₹30,000}
$$

---

# 22. Amount Becomes k Times

If:

$$
A=kP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A=kP
}
$$

and:

$$
SI=(k-1)P
$$

The required time is:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(k-1)}{R}
}
$$

---

# 23. Example — Four Times

P = ₹5,000

Rate = 10%

Time = 30 years.

$$
A=
5000\left(1+\frac{10\times30}{100}\right)
$$

$$
=5000(4)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹20,000}
$$

---

# 24. Amount and Interest Ratio

Suppose:

$$
P:SI=4:1
$$

Since:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

we get:

$$
A=4+1=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=4:5
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
SI:A=1:5
}
$$

---

# 25. General Ratio Formula

If:

$$
P:SI=a:b
$$

then:

$$
A=a+b
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=a:(a+b)
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
SI:A=b:(a+b)
}
$$

---

# 26. Example

If:

$$
P:SI=5:2
$$

then:

$$
A=5+2=7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=5:7
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
SI:A=2:7
}
$$

---

# 27. Amount and Principal Ratio

If:

$$
A:P=7:5
$$

then:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI:P=2:5
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
SI=40\%\text{ of Principal}
}
$$

---

# 28. Example — Amount Ratio

Suppose:

$$
A:P=6:5
$$

Then:

$$
SI:P=1:5
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=20\%\text{ of Principal}
$$

If the rate is 5%:

$$
5T=20
$$

$$
\boxed{T=4\ years}
$$

---

# 29. Amount as Percentage of Principal

$$
A=120\%\text{ of }P
$$

$$
SI=20\%\text{ of }P
$$

If:

$$
R=5\%

$$
5T=20

$$
\boxed{T=4\ years}
$$

# 30. Amount and Percentage Increase

If principal increases to an amount that is `k%` greater:

$$
A=(100+k)\%\text{ of }P
$$

Then:

$$
SI=k\%\text{ of }P
$$

Example:

Amount is 135% of principal.

Therefore:

$$
SI=35\%\text{ of }P
$$

$$
A=20000(1.35)
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹27,000}
$$

$$
27000-20000
=
\boxed{₹7,000}
$$

# 35. Different Rates for Different Periods

Suppose:

P = ₹10,000

First 2 years at 5%

Next 3 years at 8%.

First interest:

$$
SI_1=
\frac{10000\times5\times2}{100}
=
₹1000
$$

Second interest:

$$
SI_2=
\frac{10000\times8\times3}{100}
=
₹2400
$$

$$
₹3400
$$

Amount:

$$
A=10000+3400
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹13,400}
$$

# 36. Equal Amount Problems

Suppose two investments produce the same amount.

Since:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

we can equate:

$$
\boxed{
P_1\left(1+\frac{R_1T_1}{100}\right)
=
P_2\left(1+\frac{R_2T_2}{100}\right)
}
$$

This is useful in comparison problems.

---

# 37. Example — Equal Amount

Investment A:

$$
P_1=₹10,000,\quad R_1=10\%,\quad T_1=2
$$

Amount:

$$
A_1=10000(1.2)
=
₹12,000
$$

Investment B has:

$$
P_2=₹8,000
$$

and rate:

$$
R_2=10\%
$$

Find time for same amount.

$$
12000=
8000\left(1+\frac{10T}{100}\right)
$$

$$
1.5=1+0.1T
$$

$$
0.5=0.1T
$$

$$
\boxed{T=5\ years}
$$

---

# 38. Amount Comparison

For two investments:

$$
A_1=
P_1\left(1+\frac{R_1T_1}{100}\right)
$$

$$
A_2=
P_2\left(1+\frac{R_2T_2}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A_1:A_2=
P_1\left(1+\frac{R_1T_1}{100}\right)
:
P_2\left(1+\frac{R_2T_2}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 39. Amount Difference

For same principal and rate:

$$
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
A_1-A_2=
\frac{PR(T_1-T_2)}{100}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta A=\Delta SI
}
$$

when `P` and `R` are the same.

---

# 40. Example — Amount Difference

P = ₹15,000

R = 8%

Time difference = 2 years.

Difference in amount:

$$
\frac{15000\times8\times2}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

---

# 41. Amount Increase Due to Rate Change

Suppose:

P = ₹10,000

T = 4 years.

Rate increases from 5% to 8%.

Difference in rate:

$$
3\%
$$

Difference in SI:

$$
\frac{10000\times3\times4}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

Therefore amount also increases by:

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

# 42. Amount Increase Due to Time Change

P = ₹20,000

Difference:

3\ years
$$

$$
\frac{20000\times5\times3}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

Therefore amount increases by ₹3,000.

# 48. Application — Find Original Investment

An investment grows to ₹14,400 after 4 years at 10% simple interest.

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{10\times4}{100}\right)
$$

$$
14400=1.4P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P=₹10,285.71}

# 49. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing Amount and Interest

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

not:

$$
A=SI
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting Principal

Amount is always based on:

$$
\boxed{P}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Using Compound Interest Formula

Simple Interest:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Not Converting Time

6 months:

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Subtracting Interest Incorrectly

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
SI=A-P
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=A-SI
}
$$
