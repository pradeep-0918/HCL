# Time

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> In Simple Interest, **Time (T)** represents the period for which money is invested, deposited, borrowed, or lent.
>
> The standard Simple Interest formula is:
>
> $$\boxed{
> SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
> }$$
>
> where:
>
> - `P` = Principal
> - `R` = Rate of interest
> - `T` = Time
> - `SI` = Simple Interest

The standard unit for `T` is usually:

$$
\boxed{\text{Years}}
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of Time

Suppose:

$$
P=₹10,000
$$

Rate:

$$
R=8\%
$$

for:

$$
T=3\ years
$$

Then:

$$
SI=
\frac{10000\times8\times3}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹2400}
$$

Here:

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 3. Formula for Finding Time

Starting with:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Multiply by 100:

$$
100SI=PRT
$$

Divide by `PR`:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
}
$$

This is the main formula for finding time.

---

# 4. Example — Find Time

Principal:

$$
₹5000
$$

Rate:

$$
10\%
$$

Simple Interest:

$$
₹1500
$$

Then:

$$
T=
\frac{100(1500)}{5000(10)}
$$

$$
T=
\frac{150000}{50000}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 5. Finding Time From Principal and Amount

Since:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

we can substitute into:

$$
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PR}
}
$$

---

# 6. Example — Time From Amount

Principal:

$$
₹8000
$$

Amount:

$$
₹9600
$$

Rate:

$$
10\%
$$

First find SI:

$$
SI=9600-8000
=
₹1600
$$

Then:

$$
T=
\frac{100(1600)}{8000(10)}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=2\ years}
$$

---

# 7. Time From Interest Percentage

Suppose Simple Interest is `x%` of the principal.

We know:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{SI}{P}=\frac{RT}{100}
$$

If:

$$
SI=x\%\text{ of }P
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
RT=x
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{x}{R}
}
$$

---

# 8. Example

Simple Interest is 30% of the principal.

Rate = 6%.

Then:

$$
RT=30
$$

Therefore:

$$
T=\frac{30}{6}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=5\ years}
$$

---

# 9. Time to Double Money

When money doubles:

$$
A=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
SI=P
$$

Using:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
P=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
RT=100
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

---

# 10. Example — Doubling

Rate:

$$
8\%
$$

Time required to double:

$$
T=\frac{100}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{T=12.5\ years}
$$

---

# 11. Time to Triple Money

If money becomes three times:

$$
A=3P
$$

Then:

$$
SI=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{PRT}{100}=2P
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
RT=200
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Tripling

Rate:

$$
10\%
$$

Time:

$$
T=\frac{200}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\ years}
$$

---

# 13. Time to Become k Times

If the amount becomes `k` times the principal:

$$
A=kP
$$

Then:

$$
SI=(k-1)P
$$

Using:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\frac{PRT}{100}=(k-1)P
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
RT=100(k-1)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(k-1)}{R}
}
$$

---

# 14. Example — Four Times

Money becomes 4 times at 10% simple interest.

$$
T=
\frac{100(4-1)}{10}
$$

$$
=
\frac{300}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{30\ years}
$$

---

# 15. Time and Rate Relationship

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

when `P` and `SI` are fixed:

$$
RT=\text{constant}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R\propto\frac1T
}
$$

This means:

```text
Rate increases
→ Required time decreases

Rate decreases
→ Required time increases
```

---

# 16. Example — Same Interest

A sum earns the same SI at:

$$
10\%
$$

for:

$$
4\ years
$$

Find the time required at:

$$
8\%
$$

Since SI and P are the same:

$$
10\times4=8\times T
$$

$$
40=8T
$$

$$
\boxed{T=5\ years}
$$

---

# 17. Time Ratio

For the same principal and same simple interest:

$$
R_1T_1=R_2T_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T_1:T_2=R_2:R_1
}
$$

Example:

Rates:

$$
5\%:10\%
$$

Then time ratio:

$$
\boxed{T_1:T_2=2:1}
$$

---

# 18. Example — Time Ratio

At 6%, a sum takes 5 years to earn a certain SI.

At 10%, how long will it take?

$$
6(5)=10T
$$

$$
30=10T
$$

$$
\boxed{T=3\ years}
$$

---

# 19. Time in Months

The SI formula normally uses time in years.

If time is given in months:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{\text{Months}}{12}
}
$$

Example:

6 months:

$$
T=\frac6{12}
=
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

---

₹12,000

$$
10\%

$$
6\ months

$$
\boxed{SI=₹600}

# 21. Direct Formula for Months

If time is `M` months:

$$
T=\frac M{12}
$$

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRM}{1200}
}
$$

If:

$$
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
$$

then:

$$
Months=12T
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Months=
\frac{1200SI}{PR}
}
$$

---

# 23. Example — Find Months

Principal:

$$
₹10,000
$$

Rate:

$$
12\%
$$

SI:

$$
₹500
$$

Years:

$$
T=
\frac{100(500)}{10000(12)}
$$

$$
=\frac{50000}{120000}
=
\frac5{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
Months=12\times\frac5{12}
$$

$$
\boxed{5\ months}
$$

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{D}{365}
}
$$

where `D` = number of days.

Therefore:

$$
SI=
\frac{PRD}{36500}
$$

# 26. 360-Day Convention

Some financial problems use:

$$
1\ year=360\ days
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{D}{360}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRD}{36000}
}
$$

> [!warning]
> Do not assume 360 or 365 if the question gives a different convention. Follow the stated condition.

# 39. Equivalent Rate for Different Periods

For the same principal:

$$
SI=
\frac{P}{100}
(R_1T_1+R_2T_2)
$$

An equivalent constant rate `R` for total time `T` satisfies:

$$
RT=R_1T_1+R_2T_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=
\frac{R_1T_1+R_2T_2}
{T_1+T_2}
}
$$

---

# 40. Example — Equivalent Rate

2 years at 5%

and

3 years at 8%.

$$
R=
\frac{5(2)+8(3)}{2+3}
$$

$$
=
\frac{34}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=6.8\%}
$$

---

# 41. Time With Different Rates — Same SI

Suppose a sum earns the same SI:

$$
5T_1=8T_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T_1:T_2=8:5
}
$$

# 43. Time Comparison

If the principal and rate remain fixed:

$$
SI\propto T
$$

Therefore:

```text
Time doubles
→ SI doubles

Time triples
→ SI triples

Time becomes half
→ SI becomes half
```

---

# 44. Example — Time Doubles

P = ₹10,000

R = 5%.

At 2 years:

$$
SI=₹1000
$$

At 4 years:

$$
SI=₹2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{SI\ doubles}
$$

---

# 45. Time Difference and Interest Difference

If P and R are fixed:

$$
SI_1-SI_2=
\frac{PR(T_1-T_2)}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Difference\ in\ SI
=
\frac{PR\times Difference\ in\ Time}{100}
}
$$

---

# 46. Example

P = ₹20,000

R = 5%

Difference in time = 3 years.

Difference in SI:

$$
\frac{20000\times5\times3}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 47. Finding Time Difference

Suppose:

P = ₹15,000

R = 8%

Difference in SI = ₹2,400.

Then:

$$
2400=
\frac{15000\times8\times\Delta T}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Delta T=
\frac{2400\times100}{15000\times8}
$$

$$
\boxed{\Delta T=2\ years}
$$

# 50. Time and Rate and Principal

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

for fixed SI:

$$
\boxed{
PRT=\text{constant}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P_1R_1T_1=P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

This is a powerful shortcut.

---

# 51. Example

A sum earns the same SI under two conditions:

First:

$$
P=₹10,000,\quad R=5\%,\quad T=4
$$

Second:

$$
P=₹20,000,\quad R=4\%,\quad T=?
$$

Use:

$$
10000(5)(4)
=
20000(4)T
$$

$$
200000=80000T
$$

$$
\boxed{T=2.5\ years}
$$

---

# 52. Finding Time From a Ratio

Suppose:

$$
P:R:T=?
$$

The fundamental relationship is:

$$
SI\propto PRT
$$

For equal SI:

$$
\boxed{
P_1R_1T_1=P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

This should be your first thought in comparison questions.

# 56. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Not Converting Months

6 months is:

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

not `6 years`.

---

### Mistake 2 — Using Amount Instead of Principal

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

not:

$$
\frac{ART}{100}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting Time Is Usually in Years

If the formula expects years, convert months/days first.

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing Simple and Compound Interest

For Simple Interest:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Day Convention

If the problem says 360-day year, use 360.

If it says 365-day year, use 365.

# 59. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

### Find Time

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100SI}{PR}
}
$$

### If Amount Is Given

First:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(A-P)}{PR}
}
$$

### Time Conversion

```text
Months → Months / 12 = Years

Days → Days / 365
       or
       Days / 360
       according to the question
```

### Special Cases

```text
Double → T = 100/R

Triple → T = 200/R

k times → T = 100(k−1)/R
```

### Comparison Shortcut

For equal SI:

$$
\boxed{
P_1R_1T_1=P_2R_2T_2
}
$$
