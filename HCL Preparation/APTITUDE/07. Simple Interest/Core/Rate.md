# Rate

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Rate (R)** is the percentage of the principal charged or earned as interest for a specified period, usually **per year**.
>
> In Simple Interest problems, the rate determines how much interest is generated on the principal.

$$
\boxed{
R=\text{Rate of Interest}
}
$$

The standard formula is:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
}
$$

where:

- `P` = Principal
- `R` = Rate %
- `T` = Time
- `SI` = Simple Interest

---

# 2. Meaning of Rate

Suppose:

$$
P=₹10,000
$$

and:

$$
R=8\%
$$

This means:

> For every ₹100 of principal, ₹8 interest is earned/charged per year.

Therefore annual interest is:

$$
10000\times\frac8{100}
=
\boxed{₹800}
$$

---

# 3. Rate Is a Percentage

The rate is generally expressed as:

$$
\boxed{\%}
$$

Examples:

```text
5%
8%
10%
12.5%
15%
```

A rate of 10% means:

$$
\frac{10}{100}
=
0.10
$$

---

# 4. Basic Simple Interest Formula

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

From this:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
}
$$

This is the main formula for finding the rate.

---

# 5. Example — Find Rate

Principal:

$$
₹5000
$$

Simple Interest:

$$
₹1000
$$

Time:

$$
4\ years
$$

Using:

$$
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
$$

$$
R=
\frac{100(1000)}{5000(4)}
$$

$$
R=
\frac{100000}{20000}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 6. Rate From Annual Interest

Suppose:

Principal = ₹20,000

Annual interest = ₹1,600.

Then:

$$
R=
\frac{1600}{20000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{R=8\%}
$$

> [!tip]
> When time is exactly **1 year**, rate can be found directly from:
>
> $$\boxed{
> R=\frac{SI}{P}\times100
> }$$

---

# 7. Rate From Principal and Amount

Since:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

we get:

$$
SI=A-P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=
\frac{100(A-P)}{PT}
}
$$

---

# 8. Example — Rate From Amount

Principal:

$$
₹8000
$$

Amount:

$$
₹9200
$$

Time:

$$
3\ years
$$

Interest:

$$
SI=9200-8000
=
₹1200
$$

Rate:

$$
R=
\frac{100(1200)}{8000(3)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 9. Rate and Simple Interest Relationship

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

when `P` and `T` are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto R
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Rate doubles
→ SI doubles

Rate triples
→ SI triples

Rate becomes half
→ SI becomes half
```

---

# 10. Example — Rate Doubles

Suppose:

$$
P=₹10,000
$$

$$
T=2\ years
$$

At 5%:

$$
SI=
\frac{10000\times5\times2}{100}
=
₹1000
$$

If rate becomes 10%:

$$
SI=
\frac{10000\times10\times2}{100}
=
₹2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI\ doubles
}
$$

---

# 11. Rate Ratio

If two investments have the same principal and time:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=R_1:R_2
}
$$

Example:

Rates:

$$
5\%:8\%
$$

Same `P` and `T`.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=5:8
}
$$

---

# 12. Rate Comparison

Suppose:

Investment A:

$$
P=₹10,000,\quad R=6\%
$$

Investment B:

$$
P=₹10,000,\quad R=9\%
$$

Same time.

Then:

$$
SI_A:SI_B=6:9
$$

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 13. Rate With Different Principal

If rates and principals differ but time is the same:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=P_1R_1:P_2R_2
}
$$

Example:

$$
P_1:P_2=2:3
$$

$$
R_1:R_2=3:4
$$

Then:

$$
SI_1:SI_2=
(2)(3):(3)(4)
$$

$$
=6:12
$$

$$
\boxed{1:2}
$$

---

# 14. General SI Ratio

For two investments:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=
P_1R_1T_1:P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

This formula is extremely useful in aptitude exams.

---

# 15. Rate With Different Time

If principal is the same:

$$
SI_1:SI_2=R_1T_1:R_2T_2
$$

Example:

Rate ratio:

$$
4:5
$$

Time ratio:

$$
3:2
$$

Then:

$$
SI_1:SI_2=
4(3):5(2)
$$

$$
=12:10
$$

$$
\boxed{6:5}
$$

---

# 16. Rate and Time Product

The Simple Interest formula can be viewed as:

$$
SI=P\left(\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI\ depends\ on\ the\ product\ RT
}
$$

If `P` is fixed:

$$
R_1T_1=R_2T_2
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1=SI_2
}
$$

---

# 17. Example — Same Interest

Rate = 10%

Time = 2 years.

Then:

$$
RT=20
$$

Another investment:

Rate = 5%

Time = 4 years.

$$
RT=20
$$

If the principal is the same:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1=SI_2
}
$$

---

# 18. Rate-Time Inverse Relationship

For the same principal and same simple interest:

$$
PR_1T_1=PR_2T_2
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
R_1T_1=R_2T_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R_1:R_2=T_2:T_1
}
$$

So rate and time are inversely proportional when SI and P are fixed.

---

# 19. Example — Find New Rate

A sum earns the same simple interest at:

$$
8\%
$$

for:

$$
5\ years
$$

What rate is required for:

$$
4\ years?
$$

Since SI and P are same:

$$
8\times5=R\times4
$$

$$
40=4R
$$

$$
\boxed{R=10\%}
$$

---

# 20. Example — Find New Time

A sum is invested at 12% for 5 years.

What time is required at 15% to earn the same SI?

$$
12(5)=15T
$$

$$
60=15T
$$

$$
\boxed{T=4\ years}
$$

---

# 21. Rate and Amount

Since:

$$
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

Therefore, for fixed `P` and `T`, amount increases as rate increases.

---

# 22. Example — Different Rates

P = ₹10,000

T = 2 years.

### At 5%

$$
A=
10000(1.10)
=
₹11000
$$

### At 8%

$$
A=
10000(1.16)
=
₹11600
$$

Difference:

$$
11600-11000
=
\boxed{₹600}
$$

---

# 23. Finding Rate From Amount Multiplier

Suppose:

$$
A=1.25P
$$

and:

$$
T=5\ years
$$

From:

$$
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

we have:

$$
1.25=1+\frac{5R}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
0.25=\frac{5R}{100}
$$

$$
R=5
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 24. Rate When Amount Is a Percentage of Principal

Suppose amount is 130% of principal after 5 years.

Then:

$$
A=1.3P
$$

Therefore:

$$
1.3=1+\frac{5R}{100}
$$

$$
0.3=\frac{5R}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=6\%}
$$

---

# 25. Rate for Doubling

If money doubles under Simple Interest:

$$
A=2P
$$

Then:

$$
2=1+\frac{RT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{RT}{100}=1
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
RT=100
}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100}{T}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

---

# 26. Example — Rate for Doubling

Money must double in 8 years.

$$
R=\frac{100}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=12.5\%}
$$

---

# 27. Rate for Tripling

If amount becomes three times:

$$
A=3P
$$

Then:

$$
3=1+\frac{RT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=200
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{200}{T}
}
$$

---

# 28. Example — Triple in 10 Years

$$
R=
\frac{200}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 29. Rate for k Times

If:

$$
A=kP
$$

then:

$$
k=1+\frac{RT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=
\frac{100(k-1)}{T}
}
$$

---

# 30. Example — Four Times

Money becomes 4 times in 15 years.

$$
R=
\frac{100(4-1)}{15}
$$

$$
=\frac{300}{15}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 31. Rate When SI Is a Fraction of Principal

Suppose:

$$
SI=\frac14P
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{PRT}{100}=\frac14P
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
\frac{RT}{100}=\frac14
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=25
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{25}{T}
}
$$

---

# 32. Example

SI is one-fourth of principal after 5 years.

$$
R=\frac{25}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 33. Rate When SI Is Half of Principal

If:

$$
SI=\frac12P
$$

then:

$$
RT=50
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{50}{T}
}
$$

Example:

T = 10 years:

$$
R=\frac{50}{10}
=
\boxed{5\%}
$$

---

# 34. Rate When SI Equals Principal

If:

$$
SI=P
$$

then:

$$
RT=100
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100}{T}
}
$$

This is the doubling condition.

---

# 35. Rate From Principal and Amount Difference

Suppose:

Principal = ₹12,000

Amount = ₹15,600

Time = 6 years.

Interest:

$$
SI=15600-12000
=
₹3600
$$

Rate:

$$
R=
\frac{100(3600)}{12000(6)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=5\%}
$$

---

# 36. Rate From Interest Percentage

Suppose simple interest earned is 24% of principal in 4 years.

Then:

$$
\frac{SI}{P}=24\%
$$

But:

$$
\frac{SI}{P}
=
\frac{RT}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{RT}{100}=24\%
$$

Thus:

$$
RT=24
$$

For 4 years:

$$
R=\frac{24}{4}
$$

$$
\boxed{6\%}
$$

---

# 37. Rate From Amount Percentage

Suppose amount is 140% of principal after 8 years.

Then interest is:

$$
140\%-100\%
=
40\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
RT=40
$$

$$
R=\frac{40}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{5\%}
$$

> [!tip]
> **Amount % − 100 = Interest % of Principal.**

---

# 38. Rate and Time in Months

The standard formula assumes time in years:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

If time is given in months:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{\text{months}}{12}
}
$$

---

# 39. Example — Months

P = ₹12,000

R = 10%

T = 6 months.

Convert:

$$
T=\frac6{12}
=
\frac12
$$

Then:

$$
SI=
\frac{12000\times10\times\frac12}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹600}
$$

---

# 40. Direct Months Formula

If time is given in months:

$$
SI=
\frac{PR}{100}\times\frac{M}{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRM}{1200}
}
$$

where `M` = number of months.

---

# 41. Finding Rate With Months

Suppose:

P = ₹10,000

SI = ₹500

Time = 6 months.

Using:

$$
R=\frac{1200SI}{PM}
$$

$$
R=
\frac{1200(500)}{10000(6)}
$$

$$
\boxed{R=10\%}
$$

---

# 42. Time in Days

For day-based questions, the problem may specify a convention such as:

```text
1 year = 365 days
```

Then:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{D}{365}
}
$$

and:

$$
SI=
\frac{PRD}{36500}
$$

If the question specifies a 360-day financial year, use 360 instead.

> [!warning]
> Always follow the convention given in the question.

---

# 43. Rate Conversion — Annual to Monthly

If a question uses simple proportional rates:

Annual rate = 12%.

Monthly rate:

$$
\frac{12}{12}
=
\boxed{1\%\ per\ month}
$$

This works under simple proportional-rate assumptions.

---

# 44. Rate Conversion — Annual to Quarterly

Annual rate = 16%.

Quarterly rate:

$$
\frac{16}{4}
=
\boxed{4\%\ per\ quarter}
$$

Again, use this only when the problem assumes proportional simple interest.

---

# 45. Different Rates for Different Periods

Sometimes a question gives different rates for different time intervals.

Example:

P = ₹10,000

First 2 years at 5%

Next 3 years at 8%.

Calculate each interest separately:

$$
SI_1=
\frac{10000\times5\times2}{100}
=
₹1000
$$

$$
SI_2=
\frac{10000\times8\times3}{100}
=
₹2400
$$

Total:

$$
\boxed{SI=₹3400}
$$

> [!important]
> Under simple interest, each period can be calculated on the original principal.

---

# 46. Weighted Rate for Different Periods

For the same principal with different rates for different periods:

$$
SI=
\frac{P}{100}
(R_1T_1+R_2T_2+\cdots)
$$

Therefore an equivalent average rate is:

$$
\boxed{
R_{avg}
=
\frac{R_1T_1+R_2T_2+\cdots}
{T_1+T_2+\cdots}
}
$$

---

# 47. Example — Average Rate

A sum is invested:

- 2 years at 5%
- 3 years at 8%

Equivalent average rate:

$$
R_{avg}
=
\frac{5(2)+8(3)}{2+3}
$$

$$
=
\frac{10+24}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{6.8\%}
$$

---

# 48. Rate Change

Suppose a bank increases its simple interest rate from 6% to 8%.

Increase:

$$
8-6=2
$$

Percentage increase in rate:

$$
\frac{2}{6}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

> [!important]
> A change from 6% to 8% is a **2 percentage-point increase**, but a **33⅓% relative increase**.

---

# 49. Difference in Interest Due to Rate Change

P = ₹20,000

T = 3 years.

Rate changes from 5% to 8%.

Difference in rate:

$$
3\%
$$

Difference in SI:

$$
\frac{20000\times3\times3}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1800}
$$

---

# 50. Finding Rate Difference

If principal and time are known and difference in SI is known:

$$
\boxed{
R_1-R_2=
\frac{100(SI_1-SI_2)}{PT}
}
$$

Example:

P = ₹10,000

T = 2 years

Difference in SI = ₹600.

$$
R_1-R_2=
\frac{100(600)}{10000(2)}
$$

$$
\boxed{3\%}
$$

---

# 51. Rate Difference With Same Principal

For same `P` and `T`:

$$
SI_1-SI_2=
\frac{P(R_1-R_2)T}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Difference\ in\ SI
\propto
Difference\ in\ Rate
}
$$

---

# 52. Rate Difference With Different Times

If same principal:

$$
SI_1=\frac{PR_1T_1}{100}
$$

$$
SI_2=\frac{PR_2T_2}{100}
$$

Compare using:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=R_1T_1:R_2T_2
}
$$

# 56. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Forgetting the 100

Correct:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Using Amount Instead of Principal

Simple Interest is based on:

$$
\boxed{P}
$$

not `A`.

---

### Mistake 3 — Not Converting Months

6 months:

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ year}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing Percentage Points

From 5% to 8%:

```text
Absolute increase = 3 percentage points
Relative increase = 60%
```

---

### Mistake 5 — Using Compound Interest Logic

Simple Interest:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

The principal remains the base.

# 59. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Rate

$$
\boxed{
R=\text{Interest percentage per period}
}
$$

### Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

### Find Rate

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100SI}{PT}
}
$$

### If Amount Is Given

$$
SI=A-P
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
R=\frac{100(A-P)}{PT}
}
$$

### Same Interest

$$
\boxed{
R_1T_1=R_2T_2
}
$$

### Double

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

### Triple

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

### Main Recognition

```text
SI known
   +
P and T known
   ↓
Find R

Same SI + same P
   ↓
R × T = constant

Amount = kP
   ↓
R × T = 100(k − 1)
```
