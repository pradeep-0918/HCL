# Principal

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Principal (P)** is the original amount of money that is invested, borrowed, deposited, or lent.
>
> In Simple Interest problems, the principal is the **base amount** on which interest is calculated.

$$
\boxed{P=\text{Original Money}}
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of Principal

If a person:

- Deposits ₹10,000 in a bank
- Borrows ₹50,000 from a bank
- Lends ₹20,000 to another person
- Invests ₹1,00,000

then the original amount is called the:

$$
\boxed{\text{Principal}}
$$

---

# 3. Symbol

Principal is represented by:

$$
\boxed{P}
$$

The standard Simple Interest formula is:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
}
$$

where:

- `P` = Principal
- `R` = Rate of interest per year
- `T` = Time in years
- `SI` = Simple Interest

---

# 4. Principal vs Interest vs Amount

| Term | Meaning | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | Original money | P |
| Interest | Extra money earned/paid | SI |
| Amount | Principal + Interest | A |

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

---

# 5. Basic Example

A person deposits ₹5,000.

Here:

$$
\boxed{P=₹5000}
$$

If the bank pays ₹500 as interest:

$$
SI=₹500
$$

Then amount:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

$$
A=5000+500
$$

$$
\boxed{A=₹5500}
$$

---

# 6. Principal Is the Base

Suppose:

Principal = ₹10,000

Rate = 10%

Time = 2 years.

Simple Interest:

$$
SI=
\frac{10000\times10\times2}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{SI=₹2000}
$$

Notice that the interest is calculated using the original principal.

---

# 7. Principal Does Not Include Interest

Suppose:

$$
P=₹10,000
$$

After one year:

$$
SI=₹1000
$$

Amount:

$$
₹11,000
$$

The principal remains:

$$
\boxed{₹10,000}
$$

The amount is:

$$
\boxed{₹11,000}
$$

> [!important]
> **Principal = Original amount**
>
> **Amount = Principal + Interest**

---

# 8. Simple Interest Formula

The central formula is:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto P
}
$$

when `R` and `T` remain constant.

---

# 9. Finding Principal From SI

Starting with:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Multiply both sides by 100:

$$
100SI=PRT
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=\frac{100SI}{RT}
}
$$

---

# 10. Example — Find Principal

Simple Interest = ₹1,200

Rate = 10%

Time = 2 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(1200)}{10\times2}
$$

$$
P=
\frac{120000}{20}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹6000}
$$

---

# 11. Finding Principal From Amount

Since:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

and:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
A=
P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Take `P` common:

$$
A=
P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=
\frac{A}{1+\frac{RT}{100}}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
P=
\frac{100A}{100+RT}
}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Find Principal From Amount

Amount = ₹6,600

Rate = 10%

Time = 2 years.

$$
P=
\frac{100(6600)}{100+(10)(2)}
$$

$$
P=
\frac{660000}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹5500}
$$

Check:

$$
SI=
\frac{5500\times10\times2}{100}
=
₹1100
$$

Amount:

$$
5500+1100
=
₹6600
$$

Correct.

---

# 13. Finding Principal From Amount and SI

If:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
P=A-SI
}
$$

Example:

Amount = ₹8,500

SI = ₹1,500.

$$
P=8500-1500
$$

$$
\boxed{P=₹7000}
$$

# 22. Principal in Ratio Form

Suppose principal and interest are in the ratio:

\boxed{P=₹10000}

---

# 23. Principal and Amount Ratio

Since:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

Suppose:

$$
P:SI=5:1
$$

Then:

$$
A=5+1=6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=5:6
}
$$

---

# 24. Important Ratio Relationship

If:

$$
P:SI=a:b
$$

then:

$$
A=P+SI
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=a:(a+b)
}
$$

Example:

$$
P:SI=4:1
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
P:A=4:5
}
$$

---

# 25. Principal and Amount Percentage

Suppose:

$$
SI=20\%\text{ of Principal}
$$

Then:

$$
SI=0.2P
$$

Amount:

$$
A=P+0.2P
$$

$$
A=1.2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=120\%\ of\ P
}
$$

---

# 26. Finding Principal From Amount Percentage

If amount is 120% of principal:

$$
A=1.2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
P=\frac{A}{1.2}
$$

Example:

Amount = ₹12,000.

$$
P=
\frac{12000}{1.2}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹10000}
$$

---

# 27. Principal When SI Is a Percentage of P

# 28. Principal and Amount Multiplier

From:

$$
A=P+\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
A=P\left(1+\frac{RT}{100}\right)
}
$$

Therefore:

```text
Principal
   ×
[1 + RT/100]
   =
Amount
```

This is the fastest method when `A`, `R`, and `T` are known.

---

# 29. Example — Multiplier Method

P = ₹12,000

R = 5%

T = 4 years.

$$
A=
12000\left(1+\frac{5\times4}{100}\right)
$$

$$
=12000(1.2)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹14400}
$$

---

# 30. Finding Principal Using Multiplier

Amount = ₹14,400

Rate = 5%

Time = 4 years.

$$
A=P(1.2)
$$

Therefore:

$$
P=
\frac{14400}{1.2}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹12000}
$$

---

# 31. Principal and Doubling

Under Simple Interest, if money doubles:

$$
A=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
2P=P+SI
$$

So:

$$
SI=P
$$

Thus the interest earned equals the original principal.

From:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
P=
\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
RT=100
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

---

# 32. Time to Double

At `R%` simple interest:

$$
\boxed{
Time\ to\ Double=
\frac{100}{R}
}
$$

Example:

Rate = 10%.

$$
T=\frac{100}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{10\ years}
$$

---

# 33. Principal and Tripling

If amount becomes three times:

$$
A=3P
$$

Then:

$$
SI=2P
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{PRT}{100}=2P
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
RT=200
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{200}{R}
}
$$

---

# 34. Example — Tripling

Rate = 8%.

Time required to triple:

$$
T=\frac{200}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\ years}
$$

---

# 35. Principal and Multiplication of Money

If amount becomes `k` times the principal:

$$
A=kP
$$

Then:

$$
SI=(k-1)P
$$

Since:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

we get:

$$
\frac{PRT}{100}=(k-1)P
$$

Cancel `P`:

$$
\boxed{
RT=100(k-1)
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{100(k-1)}{R}
}
$$

---

# 36. Example — Four Times

Money becomes 4 times.

Rate = 10%.

$$
T=
\frac{100(4-1)}{10}
$$

$$
=\frac{300}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{30\ years}
$$

---

# 37. Principal Comparison

Since:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

if `R` and `T` are constant:

$$
\boxed{
SI\propto P
}
$$

Therefore:

If principal doubles:

$$
SI\rightarrow2SI
$$

If principal triples:

$$
SI\rightarrow3SI
$$

---

# 38. Example — Principal Doubles

Original:

$$
P=₹5000
$$

SI = ₹1,000.

If principal becomes:

$$
₹10000
$$

with same rate and time:

$$
SI=₹2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI\ doubles
}
$$

---

# 39. Principal Ratio

If two principals are:

$$
P_1:P_2=2:3
$$

and rate and time are the same, then:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=2:3
}
$$

---

# 40. Example — Principal Ratio

Two people invest:

$$
P_1=₹10,000
$$

$$
P_2=₹15,000
$$

at the same rate for the same time.

Then:

$$
P_1:P_2=2:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=2:3
}
$$

---

# 41. Principal With Different Rates

If:

$$
P_1:P_2=a:b
$$

and:

$$
R_1:R_2=c:d
$$

with the same time:

$$
SI_1:SI_2=
P_1R_1:P_2R_2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=ac:bd
}
$$

---

# 42. Example

Principal ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Rate ratio:

$$
3:4
$$

Same time.

Therefore:

$$
SI_1:SI_2=
(2)(3):(3)(4)
$$

$$
=6:12
$$

$$
\boxed{1:2}
$$

---

# 43. Principal With Different Time

If:

$$
P_1:P_2=a:b
$$

and:

$$
T_1:T_2=c:d
$$

with the same rate:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=ac:bd
}
$$

---

# 44. General SI Ratio

Since:

$$
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
$$

for two investments:

$$
\boxed{
SI_1:SI_2=
P_1R_1T_1:P_2R_2T_2
}
$$

This is a very powerful comparison formula.

# 45. Finding Principal From SI Ratio

Same rate and time.

SI_1:SI_2=3:5

$$
\boxed{
P_1:P_2=3:5
}
$$

# 51. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing Principal and Amount

Wrong:

$$
P=A+SI
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
A=P+SI
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
P=A-SI
}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Using Amount in the SI Formula

Simple Interest uses:

$$
\boxed{P}
$$

not:

$$
A
$$

The formula is:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting Time Conversion

If time is given in months:

$$
\boxed{
T=\frac{Months}{12}
}
$$

If time is given in days, the convention specified by the problem should be followed.

---

### Mistake 4 — Treating Simple Interest Like Compound Interest

Under simple interest:

$$
\boxed{
SI=\frac{PRT}{100}
}
$$

Interest is calculated on the original principal.
