# Continued Proportion

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Three quantities `a, b, c` are said to be in **continued proportion** if the ratio of the first to the second is equal to the ratio of the second to the third.

$$
\boxed{a:b=b:c}
$$

In fraction form:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab=\frac bc}
$$

Cross multiplying:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

This is the **most important formula**.

---

# 2. Basic Example

Are `2, 4, 8` in continued proportion?

Check:

$$
2:4
$$

and:

$$
4:8
$$

Both simplify to:

$$
1:2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2,4,8\text{ are in continued proportion}}
$$

---

# 3. Main Condition

For:

$$
a,b,c
$$

to be in continued proportion:

$$
\boxed{a:b=b:c}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac ab=\frac bc
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
ac=b^2
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

---

# 4. Middle Term Formula

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{b=\sqrt{ac}}
$$

So the middle term is the **geometric mean** of the first and third terms.

---

# 5. Example — Find Middle Term

Find `x` so that:

$$
4,x,25
$$

are in continued proportion.

Use:

$$
x^2=4\times25
$$

$$
x^2=100
$$

For positive quantities:

$$
\boxed{x=10}
$$

Therefore:

$$
4:10=10:25
$$

---

# 6. Another Example

Find the middle term between:

`9` and `16`.

$$
x=\sqrt{9\times16}
$$

$$
x=\sqrt{144}
$$

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{9:12=12:16}
$$

---

# 7. Geometric Mean

> [!important]
> The middle term in continued proportion is called the **geometric mean** of the first and third terms.

For:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

the geometric mean is:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{ac}}
$$

---

# 8. Arithmetic Mean vs Geometric Mean

### Arithmetic Mean

$$
\boxed{\frac{a+c}{2}}
$$

### Geometric Mean

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{ac}}
$$

Continued proportion uses the **geometric mean**, not the arithmetic mean.

---

# 9. Example

For `4` and `25`:

Arithmetic mean:

$$
\frac{4+25}{2}
=
14.5
$$

Geometric mean:

$$
\sqrt{4\times25}
=
10
$$

Therefore the continued-proportion middle term is:

$$
\boxed{10}
$$

---

# 10. Finding the Third Term

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{c=\frac{b^2}{a}}
$$

---

# 11. Example

Find `c` if:

$$
3:6=6:c
$$

Use:

$$
6^2=3c
$$

$$
36=3c
$$

$$
\boxed{c=12}
$$

Check:

$$
3:6=1:2
$$

$$
6:12=1:2
$$

Correct.

---

# 12. Finding the First Term

From:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

we have:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{a=\frac{b^2}{c}}
$$

Example:

$$
a:8=8:32
$$

$$
8^2=32a
$$

$$
64=32a
$$

$$
\boxed{a=2}
$$

---

# 13. Finding the Missing Middle Term

If:

$$
5:x=x:45
$$

then:

$$
x^2=5\times45
$$

$$
x^2=225
$$

$$
\boxed{x=15}
$$

Therefore:

$$
5:15=15:45
$$

---

# 14. Continued Proportion Using Ratio

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then there exists a constant `k` such that:

$$
a:b=b:c=1:k
$$

For example:

$$
2:6=6:18
$$

Both ratios are:

$$
1:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:6:18}
$$

---

# 15. General Form

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

and:

$$
a:b=m:n
$$

then:

$$
b:c=m:n
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{a:b:c=m:n:\frac{n^2}{m}}
$$

A more convenient integer form is obtained by scaling.

---

# 16. Standard Integer Pattern

If:

$$
a:b=m:n
$$

then a continued proportion can be written as:

$$
\boxed{m^2:mn:n^2}
$$

because:

$$
m^2:mn
=
m:n
$$

and:

$$
mn:n^2
=
m:n
$$

---

# 17. Example

Given ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Find three terms in continued proportion.

Use:

$$
m^2:mn:n^2
$$

$$
2^2:(2)(3):3^2
$$

$$
\boxed{4:6:9}
$$

Check:

$$
4:6=2:3
$$

$$
6:9=2:3
$$

Correct.

---

# 18. Another Example

Ratio:

$$
3:5
$$

Three terms in continued proportion:

$$
3^2:(3)(5):5^2
$$

$$
\boxed{9:15:25}
$$

Check:

$$
9:15=3:5
$$

$$
15:25=3:5
$$

---

# 19. Important Pattern

If:

$$
a:b=m:n
$$

then continued-proportion terms are:

$$
\boxed{m^2:mn:n^2}
$$

This is a high-value shortcut.

---

# 20. Example — Ratio `4:7`

Three terms in continued proportion:

$$
4^2:(4)(7):7^2
$$

$$
\boxed{16:28:49}
$$

Check:

$$
16:28=4:7
$$

$$
28:49=4:7
$$

---

# 21. Checking Continued Proportion

For:

$$
a,b,c
$$

simply calculate:

$$
\boxed{b^2}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{ac}
$$

If:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

then they are in continued proportion.

---

# 22. Example — Check

Are `6, 12, 24` in continued proportion?

$$
12^2=144
$$

$$
6\times24=144
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Yes}}
$$

---

# 23. Example — Not in Continued Proportion

Are `4, 8, 20` in continued proportion?

$$
8^2=64
$$

$$
4\times20=80
$$

Since:

$$
64\neq80
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No}}
$$

---

# 24. Continued Proportion and Proportion

Ordinary proportion:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

Continued proportion:

$$
\boxed{a:b=b:c}
$$

The key difference is that the **second term of the first ratio is the first term of the second ratio**.

---

# 25. Continued Proportion vs Equivalent Ratios

### Equivalent ratios

$$
2:3=4:6
$$

The terms do not necessarily overlap.

### Continued proportion

$$
2:4=4:8
$$

The middle term `4` appears in both ratios.

---

# 26. Continued Proportion and Geometric Progression

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
\frac ba=\frac cb
$$

So the common ratio is the same.

Therefore:

$$
a,b,c
$$

form a **3-term geometric progression**.

Example:

$$
2,6,18
$$

Common ratio:

$$
3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2,6,18}
$$

is a geometric progression.

---

# 27. GP Connection

For three terms of a GP:

$$
a,ar,ar^2
$$

we have:

$$
(ar)^2=a(ar^2)
$$

Therefore:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

So:

> **Three positive terms are in continued proportion exactly when they form a 3-term geometric progression.**

---

# 28. Example

Find the third term if:

$$
4,12,x
$$

are in continued proportion.

Since the common ratio is:

$$
12/4=3
$$

then:

$$
x=12\times3
$$

$$
\boxed{x=36}
$$

---

# 29. Finding Three Terms

Find three terms in continued proportion with ratio `2:5`.

Use:

$$
m^2:mn:n^2
$$

$$
4:10:25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:10:25}
$$

---

# 30. Continued Proportion With Fractions

Suppose:

$$
a:b=\frac23
$$

Then three terms can be:

$$
1:\frac32:\frac94
$$

because the common ratio is:

$$
\frac32
$$

To avoid fractions, multiply by `4`:

$$
\boxed{4:6:9}
$$

---

# 31. Continued Proportion With Decimals

Suppose the common ratio is:

$$
1.5
$$

Choose first term `2`:

$$
2,\ 3,\ 4.5
$$

Check:

$$
2:3=2/3
$$

and:

$$
3:4.5=2/3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2,3,4.5}
$$

---

# 32. Continued Proportion With Percentages

If:

$$
a:b=2:5
$$

then a continued ratio is:

$$
4:10:25
$$

The percentages themselves can be scaled as needed.

The important condition remains:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

---

# 33. Continued Proportion With Negative Terms

Mathematically, the condition remains:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

Example:

$$
-2,4,-8
$$

Check:

$$
4^2=16
$$

$$
(-2)(-8)=16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{-2,4,-8}
$$

satisfy the algebraic condition.

> [!note]
> Standard aptitude questions generally use positive quantities.

---

# 34. Finding Mean Proportional

The phrase **mean proportional** usually means the middle term `b` satisfying:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{b=\sqrt{ac}}
$$

Example:

Mean proportional between `9` and `25`:

$$
\sqrt{9\times25}
=
\sqrt{225}
=
\boxed{15}
$$

---

# 35. Mean Proportional Shortcut

To find the mean proportional between `a` and `c`:

### Step 1

Multiply:

$$
ac
$$

### Step 2

Take square root:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{ac}}
$$

---

# 36. Example

Mean proportional between `16` and `81`:

$$
\sqrt{16\times81}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{1296}
$$

$$
\boxed{36}
$$

---

# 37. Mean Proportional Between Fractions

Find the mean proportional between:

$$
\frac14
$$

and:

$$
\frac94
$$

$$
\sqrt{
\frac14\times\frac94
}
$$

$$
=
\sqrt{\frac9{16}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac34}
$$

---

# 38. Mean Proportional Between Decimals

Between `0.04` and `0.25`:

$$
\sqrt{0.04\times0.25}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{0.01}
$$

$$
\boxed{0.1}
$$

---

# 39. Finding the Missing Term

Given:

$$
7:x=x:63
$$

Then:

$$
x^2=7\times63
$$

$$
x^2=441
$$

$$
\boxed{x=21}
$$

---

# 40. Finding Missing First Term

Given:

$$
x:12=12:48
$$

Then:

$$
12^2=48x
$$

$$
144=48x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=3}
$$

---

# 41. Finding Missing Third Term

Given:

$$
5:15=15:x
$$

Then:

$$
15^2=5x
$$

$$
225=5x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=45}
$$

---

# 42. Continued Proportion With Algebra

If:

$$
x,\ 2x,\ 4x
$$

then:

$$
\frac{2x}{x}=2
$$

and:

$$
\frac{4x}{2x}=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x:2x=2x:4x}
$$

---

# 43. Algebraic Condition

If:

$$
a,b,c
$$

are in continued proportion:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

This can be used to solve algebraic equations.

Example:

$$
x,\ 6,\ 18
$$

Then:

$$
6^2=18x
$$

$$
36=18x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=2}
$$

---

# 44. Continued Proportion With Variables

Suppose:

$$
x,\ y,\ z
$$

are in continued proportion.

Then:

$$
\boxed{y^2=xz}
$$

This is often the fastest equation to write.

---

# 45. Example

If:

$$
x,y,20
$$

are in continued proportion and:

$$
x=5
$$

then:

$$
y^2=5(20)
$$

$$
y^2=100
$$

$$
\boxed{y=10}
$$

---

# 46. Product Property

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

Therefore the square of the middle term equals the product of the extremes.

This is the key identity.

---

# 47. Extreme Terms

In:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

- `a` = first extreme
- `c` = second extreme
- `b` = mean proportional

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}^2=\text{Product of extremes}}
$$

---

# 48. Continued Proportion and Squares

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
\frac ba=\frac cb
$$

Squaring is not required to establish the relationship, but:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

provides the fastest test.

---

# 49. Ratio Construction

Given:

$$
a:b=3:4
$$

construct three terms in continued proportion:

$$
3^2:(3)(4):4^2
$$

$$
\boxed{9:12:16}
$$

Check:

$$
9:12=3:4
$$

$$
12:16=3:4
$$

---

# 50. Another Construction

Given:

$$
5:6
$$

three terms:

$$
5^2:5(6):6^2
$$

$$
\boxed{25:30:36}
$$

---

# 51. Continued Proportion and Scale

If:

$$
a:b:b:c
$$

has common ratio `r`, then:

$$
b=ar
$$

and:

$$
c=ar^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{a:b:c=1:r:r^2}
$$

after scaling.

---

# 52. Example

Common ratio:

$$
2
$$

Choose first term `3`:

$$
3,\ 6,\ 12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3:6:12}
$$

---

# 53. Continued Proportion and Powers

For common ratio `r`:

$$
a,\ ar,\ ar^2
$$

The third term is related to the first by:

$$
\boxed{c=ar^2}
$$

This is another useful GP interpretation.

---

# 54. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using Arithmetic Mean

Wrong:

$$
b=\frac{a+c}{2}
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{b=\sqrt{ac}}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting the Middle Term

Continued proportion:

$$
\boxed{a:b=b:c}
$$

not:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting the Square

Correct:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Using `a+c`

The relationship uses:

$$
ac
$$

not:

$$
a+c
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Not Checking

Always verify:

$$
b^2=ac
$$

when the question asks whether three terms are in continued proportion.
