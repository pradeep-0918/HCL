# Division in Given Ratio

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Division in a given ratio** means splitting a total quantity into parts according to the specified ratio.

If a quantity `T` is divided in the ratio:

$$
a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{First Part}=\frac{a}{a+b}\times T
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Second Part}=\frac{b}{a+b}\times T
}
$$

---

# 2. Golden Method

> [!important]
> **Ratio → Add Parts → Find One Part → Multiply**

For:

$$
a:b
$$

### Step 1

Add:

$$
a+b
$$

### Step 2

Find one part:

$$
\frac{T}{a+b}
$$

### Step 3

Multiply by each ratio term.

---

# 3. Basic Example

Divide `100` in the ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Total parts:

$$
2+3=5
$$

One part:

$$
100/5=20
$$

First part:

$$
2(20)=40
$$

Second part:

$$
3(20)=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{40,\ 60}
$$

---

# 4. Formula

If:

$$
T
$$

is divided in ratio:

$$
a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{aT}{a+b}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
B=\frac{bT}{a+b}
}
$$

# 52. Division in Ratio vs Direct Proportion

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and total is fixed, we divide the total.

If a quantity changes according to another quantity, we use proportionality.

### Division

$$
\boxed{A+B=T}
$$

### Direct Proportion

$$
\boxed{\frac AB=k}
$$

# 54. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Forgetting to Add Ratio Parts

For:

$$
3:5
$$

total parts are:

$$
\boxed{8}
$$

not `5`.

---

### Mistake 2 — Treating Ratio as Actual Values

`3:5` means:

$$
3k:5k
$$

not necessarily `3` and `5`.

---

### Mistake 3 — Not Simplifying

Always simplify:

$$
12:18=2:3
$$

before dividing.

---

### Mistake 4 — Wrong Unit

Do not divide:

`2 kg` using `grams` without conversion.

---

### Mistake 5 — Using Difference Instead of Sum

Total problem:

$$
a+b
$$

Difference problem:

$$
b-a
$$

---

### Mistake 6 — Forgetting Verification

After division:

$$
\boxed{\text{Sum of parts must equal total}}
$$

---

# 57. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Two Parts

If:

$$
T\text{ divided in }a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{aT}{a+b}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
B=\frac{bT}{a+b}
}
$$

### Three Parts

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{aT}{a+b+c}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
B=\frac{bT}{a+b+c}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
C=\frac{cT}{a+b+c}
}
$$

### One Part

$$
\boxed{
k=\frac{T}{a+b+c+\cdots}
}
$$

### Difference

For `a:b`, with `b > a`:

$$
\boxed{
D=\frac{b-a}{a+b}T
}
$$

### Given Difference

$$
\boxed{
k=\frac{D}{b-a}
}
$$

### Percentage Share

$$
\boxed{
\text{Share \%}
=
\frac{\text{Ratio Part}}{\text{Total Ratio Parts}}
\times100
}
$$
