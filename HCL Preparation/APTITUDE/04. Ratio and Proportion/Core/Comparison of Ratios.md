# Comparison of Ratios

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Comparison of ratios** means determining which of two or more ratios is greater, smaller, or equal.

For:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:d
$$

we compare:

$$
\frac ab
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac cd
$$

The safest method is usually **cross multiplication**.

---

# 2. Basic Comparison

Compare:

$$
2:3
$$

and:

$$
3:5
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
2\times5=10
$$

$$
3\times3=9
$$

Since:

$$
10>9
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3>3:5}
$$

---

# 3. Cross-Multiplication Rule

For positive ratios:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:d
$$

compare:

$$
\boxed{ad\text{ and }bc}
$$

If:

$$
ad>bc
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab>\frac cd}
$$

If:

$$
ad<bc
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab<\frac cd}
$$

If:

$$
ad=bc
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab=\frac cd}
$$

---

# 4. Example — Greater Ratio

Compare:

$$
5:8
$$

and:

$$
3:5
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
5\times5=25
$$

$$
8\times3=24
$$

Since:

$$
25>24
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{5:8>3:5}
$$

---

# 5. Example — Smaller Ratio

Compare:

$$
4:7
$$

and:

$$
5:8
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
4\times8=32
$$

$$
7\times5=35
$$

Since:

$$
32<35
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:7<5:8}
$$

---

# 6. Equal Ratios

Compare:

$$
6:9
$$

and:

$$
10:15
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
6\times15=90
$$

$$
9\times10=90
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{6:9=10:15}
$$

---

# 7. Simplification Method

Sometimes simplifying first is easier.

Compare:

$$
24:36
$$

and:

$$
20:30
$$

Simplify:

$$
24:36=2:3
$$

$$
20:30=2:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Equal}}
$$

---

# 8. Comparison Using Decimal Values

Compare:

$$
3:5
$$

and:

$$
5:8
$$

Convert:

$$
\frac35=0.6
$$

$$
\frac58=0.625
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5:8>3:5}
$$

> [!tip]
> Decimal conversion works, but cross multiplication is usually safer and faster in aptitude exams.

---

# 9. Comparison of Three Ratios

Compare:

$$
2:3,\quad3:5,\quad5:8
$$

Convert approximately:

$$
\frac23=0.6667
$$

$$
\frac35=0.6
$$

$$
\frac58=0.625
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
2:3>5:8>3:5
}
$$

---

# 10. Comparing Ratios With the Same Denominator

If:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:b
$$

have the same second term, compare the first terms.

Example:

$$
5:9
$$

and:

$$
7:9
$$

Since:

$$
7>5
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{7:9>5:9}
$$

---

# 11. Same Numerator

Compare:

$$
5:7
$$

and:

$$
5:9
$$

For the same positive numerator, the smaller denominator gives the larger fraction.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5:7>5:9}
$$

---

# 12. Ratio Comparison Using Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

and:

$$
C:D=4:7
$$

Compare:

$$
\frac35
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac47
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
3\times7=21
$$

$$
5\times4=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3:5>4:7}
$$

---

# 13. Close Ratios

Some aptitude questions use ratios that are very close.

Example:

$$
49:100
$$

and:

$$
50:101
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
49\times101=4949
$$

$$
100\times50=5000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{50:101>49:100}
$$

---

# 14. Comparing Ratios by Cross Difference

For:

$$
\frac ab
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac cd
$$

calculate:

$$
ad-bc
$$

If positive:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab>\frac cd}
$$

If negative:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab<\frac cd}
$$

If zero:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab=\frac cd}
$$

---

# 15. Example

Compare:

$$
17:29
$$

and:

$$
18:31
$$

Calculate:

$$
17\times31=527
$$

$$
29\times18=522
$$

Since:

$$
527>522
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{17:29>18:31}
$$

---

# 16. Comparing Ratio With 1

This is a useful shortcut.

For:

$$
a:b
$$

compare `a` and `b`.

### If:

$$
a>b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{a:b>1}
$$

### If:

$$
a=b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{a:b=1}
$$

### If:

$$
a<b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{a:b<1}
$$

---

# 17. Example

$$
7:5
$$

Since:

$$
7>5
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{7:5>1}
$$

---

# 18. Comparing Ratio With Percentage

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

Then:

$$
\frac AB=\frac35
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac35\times100
=
60\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A=60\%\text{ of B}}
$$

---

# 19. Comparing Ratios With Percentages

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:5
$$

Then:

$$
A=40\%\text{ of B}
$$

If:

$$
C:D=3:5
$$

then:

$$
C=60\%\text{ of D}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C:D>A:B}
$$

---

# 20. Ratio Comparison and Percentage Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=4:5
$$

and:

$$
C:D=3:4
$$

Convert:

$$
\frac45=0.8
$$

$$
\frac34=0.75
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:5>3:4}
$$

---

# 21. Comparing Ratios With Different Units

> [!important]
> **Never compare quantities with incompatible units without converting them first.**

Example:

Compare:

$$
2m:5m
$$

and:

$$
30cm:80cm
$$

First ratio:

$$
2:5=0.4
$$

Second:

$$
30:80=\frac38=0.375
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:5>30:80}
$$

---

# 22. Comparing Decimal Ratios

Compare:

$$
0.4:0.7
$$

and:

$$
0.5:0.9
$$

Convert:

$$
\frac{0.4}{0.7}
=
\frac47
$$

$$
\frac{0.5}{0.9}
=
\frac59
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
4\times9=36
$$

$$
7\times5=35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0.4:0.7>0.5:0.9}
$$

---

# 23. Comparing Fraction Ratios

Compare:

$$
\frac23:\frac45
$$

and:

$$
\frac34:\frac78
$$

First ratio:

$$
\frac{\frac23}{\frac45}
=
\frac23\times\frac54
=
\frac56
$$

Second:

$$
\frac{\frac34}{\frac78}
=
\frac34\times\frac87
=
\frac67
$$

Compare:

$$
\frac56
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac67
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
5\times7=35
$$

$$
6\times6=36
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac34:\frac78
>
\frac23:\frac45
}
$$

---

# 24. Comparing Three-Term Ratios

Suppose:

$$
A:B:C=2:3:5
$$

and:

$$
X:Y:Z=3:4:6
$$

You cannot simply compare corresponding terms independently unless the question specifies what is being compared.

For example, comparing A:X:

$$
2:3
$$

Comparing B:Y:

$$
3:4
$$

Comparing C:Z:

$$
5:6
$$

The exact comparison depends on the question.

> [!warning]
> Always identify **what quantity or share** is being compared.

---

# 25. Comparing Parts of a Total

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
C:D=3:4
$$

A's share of its total:

$$
\frac2{2+3}
=
\frac25
=
40\%
$$

C's share:

$$
\frac3{3+4}
=
\frac37
\approx42.86\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C\text{'s share is greater}}
$$

---

# 26. Comparing Ratios by Percentage Share

For:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

A's share of total:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{m}{m+n}
}
$$

B's share:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{n}{m+n}
}
$$

This is useful when the question asks:

> Who has the greater share?

---

# 27. Comparing Two Boys-to-Girls Ratios

Class A:

$$
Boys:Girls=2:3
$$

Class B:

$$
Boys:Girls=3:5
$$

Boys percentage:

Class A:

$$
\frac25=40\%
$$

Class B:

$$
\frac35=60\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Class B has the higher boys-to-girls ratio}}
$$

---

# 28. Comparing Efficiency Ratios

Worker A completes:

`10 units/hour`

Worker B:

`8 units/hour`

Efficiency ratio:

$$
10:8
=
\boxed{5:4}
$$

Therefore A is:

$$
\frac{10-8}{8}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

more efficient than B.

---

# 29. Ratio Comparison in Speed

A:

$$
60\text{ km/h}
$$

B:

$$
75\text{ km/h}
$$

Speed ratio:

$$
60:75
=
\boxed{4:5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{B\text{ is faster}}
$$

---

# 30. Comparing Ratios Using LCM

Sometimes ratios can be made comparable by scaling them.

Compare:

$$
2:3
$$

and:

$$
5:7
$$

We can use cross multiplication, but for intuition:

$$
2/3\approx0.667
$$

$$
5/7\approx0.714
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5:7>2:3}
$$

---

# 31. Comparing Ratios Near 1

Suppose:

$$
99:100
$$

and:

$$
100:101
$$

Both are close to `1`.

Cross multiply:

$$
99\times101=9999
$$

$$
100\times100=10000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{100:101>99:100}
$$

---

# 32. Shortcut for Ratios Near 1

For:

$$
\frac{n-1}{n}
$$

the value is:

$$
1-\frac1n
$$

For:

$$
\frac n{n+1}
$$

the value is:

$$
1-\frac1{n+1}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac n{n+1}
>
\frac{n-1}{n}
$$

for positive `n`.

---

# 33. Comparing Ratios With Same Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=(x):(x+d)
$$

and:

$$
C:D=(y):(y+d)
$$

For positive values, the ratio with the larger numbers is generally closer to `1`.

Example:

$$
9:10
$$

and:

$$
99:100
$$

Both have difference `1`.

Since `99:100` is closer to `1`:

$$
\boxed{99:100>9:10}
$$

---

# 34. Ratio Comparison Through Reciprocal

If all quantities are positive:

Comparing:

$$
\frac ab
$$

and:

$$
\frac cd
$$

is equivalent to comparing reciprocals in the **opposite direction**:

$$
\frac ba
$$

and:

$$
\frac dc
$$

Example:

$$
2:3<3:4
$$

because:

$$
\frac32>\frac43
$$

---

# 35. Comparing Compound Ratios

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
C:D=4:5
$$

compound ratio:

$$
AC:BD
$$

becomes:

$$
(2\times4):(3\times5)
$$

$$
\boxed{8:15}
$$

To compare compound ratios, simplify or cross multiply.

---

# 36. Example — Compound Ratio Comparison

Compare:

$$
(2:3)\times(4:5)
$$

with:

$$
(3:4)\times(3:5)
$$

First:

$$
\frac23\times\frac45
=
\frac8{15}
$$

Second:

$$
\frac34\times\frac35
=
\frac9{20}
$$

Compare:

$$
\frac8{15}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac9{20}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
8\times20=160
$$

$$
15\times9=135
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac8{15}>\frac9{20}}
$$

---

# 37. Comparison of Equivalent Ratios

Before comparing, always check whether the ratios are actually different.

Example:

$$
15:20
$$

and:

$$
30:40
$$

Both simplify to:

$$
3:4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Equal}}
$$

---

# 38. Comparing Ratios With a Common Numerator

If:

$$
\frac{a}{b}
$$

and:

$$
\frac{a}{c}
$$

where:

$$
c>b>0
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab>\frac ac
}
$$

Example:

$$
7:8>7:10
$$

---

# 39. Comparing Ratios With a Common Denominator

If:

$$
\frac ab
$$

and:

$$
\frac cb
$$

where:

$$
a>c
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
a:b>c:b
}
$$

Example:

$$
9:11>7:11
$$

---

# 40. Comparing Ratios Using Percentages

Suppose:

$$
A:B=4:5
$$

and:

$$
C:D=5:6
$$

Convert:

$$
4/5=80\%
$$

$$
5/6=83.33\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5:6>4:5}
$$

# 41. Ratio Comparison in Profit

Company A:

Revenue : Cost

$$
5:4
$$

Company B:

$$
6:5
$$

Profit ratios:

A:

$$
5-4=1
$$

B:

$$
6-5=1
$$

But profit as a percentage of cost:

A:

$$
\frac15\times100
=
25\%
$$

B:

$$
\frac16\times100
=
20\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Company A has higher profit percentage}}

Student A:

Scored `80` out of `100`.

Student B:

Scored `90` out of `120`.

Compare percentages:

A:

$$
80\%
$$

B:

$$
\frac{90}{120}\times100

\boxed{\text{Student A has the higher score ratio}}

# 43. Comparing Two Fractions Quickly

For:

$$
\frac ab
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac cd
$$

use:

$$
ad\ ?\ bc
$$

This avoids decimal calculations.

> [!tip]
> **Cross multiplication is the safest exam method.**

---

# 44. Comparison of Ratios With Large Numbers

Compare:

$$
123:157
$$

and:

$$
124:159
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
123\times159
=
19557
$$

$$
157\times124
=
19468
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{123:157>124:159}
$$

Even though the first numerator is smaller.

---

# 45. Comparison Using Difference From 1

For ratios less than `1`, calculate:

$$
1-\frac ab
=
\frac{b-a}{b}
$$

Example:

$$
99:100
$$

difference from 1:

$$
\frac1{100}
$$

For:

$$
100:101
$$

difference:

$$
\frac1{101}
$$

Since:

$$
\frac1{101}<\frac1{100}
$$

the second ratio is closer to `1`.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{100:101>99:100}
$$

---

# 46. Comparison of Ratios and Ordering

Arrange in ascending order:

$$
\frac23,\quad\frac35,\quad\frac58,\quad\frac79
$$

Approximate:

$$
0.6,\quad0.625,\quad0.667,\quad0.778
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac35<
\frac58<
\frac23<
\frac79
}
$$

---

# 47. Ordering Many Ratios

For aptitude questions with many ratios:

### Method 1

Convert to decimals.

### Method 2

Cross multiply pairwise.

### Method 3

Use common denominators when convenient.

### Method 4

Use proximity to `1` for ratios near `1`.

# 48. Comparing Ratios After Changes

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

A increases by `20%`.

B decreases by `10%`.

New ratio:

$$
2(1.2):3(0.9)
$$

$$
2.4:2.7
$$

Multiply by `10`:

$$
24:27
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{8:9}
$$

Compare with original:

$$
\frac23
\quad\text{vs}\quad
\frac89
$$

Since:

$$
\frac89>\frac23
$$

the ratio has increased.

---

# 49. Comparing Ratios After Percentage Changes

General:

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

A changes by `x%` and B by `y%`, then:

$$
\boxed{
A':B'
=
m(1+x/100):
n(1+y/100)
}
$$

Use `−` for decreases.

---

# 50. Ratio Comparison Through Cross Products

For:

$$
A:B
$$

and:

$$
C:D
$$

define:

$$
X=AD
$$

$$
Y=BC
$$

Then:

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| `X > Y` | First ratio greater |
| `X < Y` | Second ratio greater |
| `X = Y` | Ratios equal |

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Comparing numerators only.

Wrong:

`7:10` vs `8:20`

You cannot say `8:20` is greater just because `8 > 7`.

---

### Mistake 2

Comparing denominators only.

---

### Mistake 3

Using decimals unnecessarily.

Cross multiplication is often faster.

---

### Mistake 4

Not simplifying equivalent ratios.

---

### Mistake 5

Ignoring units.

---

### Mistake 6

Confusing ratio comparison with percentage difference.

---

### Mistake 7

Comparing parts instead of shares.

For `2:3`, the first part is `2`, but its share of total is:

# 53. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Comparison

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\ ?\ \frac cd
}
$$

Compare:

$$
\boxed{ad\ ?\ bc}
$$

### Equal

$$
\boxed{ad=bc}
$$

### First Greater

$$
\boxed{ad>bc}
$$

### First Smaller

$$
\boxed{ad<bc}
$$

### Ratio as Percentage

$$
\boxed{
a:b
\Rightarrow
\frac ab\times100
}
$$

### Share of Total

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A\text{'s share}=\frac{m}{m+n}
}
$$

### Changed Ratio

$$
\boxed{
A':B'
=
m(1\pm x/100):
n(1\pm y/100)
}
$$

# 54. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Compare `2:3` and `3:5`

$$
2\times5=10
$$

$$
3\times3=9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3>3:5}
$$

### Compare `4:7` and `5:8`

$$
4\times8=32
$$

$$
5\times7=35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:7<5:8}
$$

### Equal

$$
6:9
$$

and:

$$
10:15
$$

Both:

$$
=2:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Equal}}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **To compare `a:b` and `c:d`, cross multiply: compare `a×d` with `b×c`.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **Never judge a ratio by its numerator or denominator alone—compare the complete fractions.**
