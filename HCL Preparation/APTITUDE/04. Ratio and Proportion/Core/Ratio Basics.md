# Ratio Basics

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **ratio** compares two quantities of the same kind using division.

If two quantities are `a` and `b`, their ratio is:

$$
\boxed{a:b}
$$

which means:

$$
\boxed{\frac ab}
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of Ratio

If there are:

- 10 boys
- 15 girls

then:

$$
\text{Boys:Girls}=10:15
$$

Simplify by dividing by `5`:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

Therefore, for every `2` boys, there are `3` girls.

---

# 3. Important Rule

> [!important]
> **The two quantities in a ratio must have the same unit before comparison.**

Example:

`2 m : 50 cm`

Convert:

$$
2m=200cm
$$

Therefore:

$$
200:50
=
\boxed{4:1}
$$

---

# 4. Ratio as a Fraction

The ratio:

$$
3:5
$$

can be written as:

$$
\frac35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3:5=\frac35}
$$

But remember:

> `3:5` is a ratio; `3/5` is its fractional representation.

---

# 5. Terms of a Ratio

In:

$$
7:9
$$

- `7` = first term / antecedent
- `9` = second term / consequent

So:

$$
\boxed{\text{Antecedent}=7}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Consequent}=9}
$$

---

# 6. Ratio of Two Numbers

Find the ratio of `24` and `36`.

$$
24:36
$$

HCF:

$$
HCF(24,36)=12
$$

Divide both:

$$
\frac{24}{12}:\frac{36}{12}
$$

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 7. Ratio of Three Numbers

Suppose:

$$
A=20,\quad B=30,\quad C=50
$$

Then:

$$
A:B:C=20:30:50
$$

Divide by `10`:

$$
\boxed{2:3:5}
$$

---

# 8. Simplifying a Ratio

To simplify:

$$
48:72
$$

Find HCF:

$$
HCF=24
$$

Therefore:

$$
48:72
=
2:3
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{48:72=2:3}
$$

---

# 9. Multiplying a Ratio

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

multiply both terms by `4`:

$$
8:12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3=8:12}
$$

---

# 10. Dividing a Ratio

If:

$$
20:30
$$

divide both terms by `10`:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 11. Equivalent Ratios

Ratios remain equivalent when both terms are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number.

$$
\boxed{
a:b=ka:kb
}
$$

Example:

$$
2:5
$$

Multiply by `3`:

$$
6:15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:5=6:15}
$$

---

# 12. Ratio and Proportion

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab=\frac cd
}
$$

This is called a **proportion**.

Cross multiplication gives:

$$
\boxed{ad=bc}
$$

---

# 13. Example — Proportion

Check whether:

$$
2:3=8:12
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
2\times12=24
$$

$$
3\times8=24
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3=8:12}
$$

---

# 14. Finding an Unknown

Suppose:

$$
3:5=x:20
$$

Write:

$$
\frac35=\frac{x}{20}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
5x=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

---

# 15. Ratio to Actual Values

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

then we can represent:

$$
\boxed{A=2k,\quad B=3k}
$$

where `k` is a common multiplier.

This is one of the **most important tricks in ratio problems**.

---

# 16. Example

Boys : Girls:

$$
2:3
$$

Suppose the common multiplier is `5`.

Then:

$$
Boys=2(5)=10
$$

$$
Girls=3(5)=15
$$

Total:

$$
\boxed{25}
$$

---

# 17. Ratio and Total

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
A+B=50
$$

Total ratio parts:

$$
2+3=5
$$

One part:

$$
\frac{50}{5}=10
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=2(10)=20
$$

$$
B=3(10)=30
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{A=20,\ B=30}
$$

---

# 18. General Formula — Two Parts

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

and:

$$
A+B=T
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac{m}{m+n}T
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
B=\frac{n}{m+n}T
}
$$

---

# 19. Example

Ratio:

$$
3:7
$$

Total:

$$
200
$$

Total parts:

$$
3+7=10
$$

A:

$$
\frac3{10}\times200
=
\boxed{60}
$$

B:

$$
\frac7{10}\times200
=
\boxed{140}
$$

---

# 20. Ratio and Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

and:

$$
B-A=20
$$

Difference in ratio parts:

$$
5-3=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
2k=20
$$

$$
k=10
$$

Thus:

$$
A=30
$$

$$
B=50
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{30,\ 50}
$$

---

# 21. General Formula — Difference

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

and:

$$
B-A=D
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
k=\frac{D}{n-m}
}
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{A=mk,\quad B=nk}
$$

---

# 22. Ratio and Sum + Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

and:

$$
A+B=80
$$

We already know:

$$
3+5=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
k=10
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{A=30,\ B=50}
$$

Difference:

$$
50-30
=
\boxed{20}
$$

$$

$$

# 28. Ratio and Fraction

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

then:

$$
\frac{A}{B}=\frac23
$$

But A's fraction of total is:

$$
\frac{A}{A+B}
=
\frac25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A=\frac25\text{ of total}
}
$$

This distinction is very important.

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

$$
3

$$
\boxed{3:3=1:1}

# 38. Combining Ratios

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

B must have the same value in both ratios.

First ratio:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

Second:

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

LCM of `3` and `4`:

$$
12
$$

Make B = `12`.

First ratio ×4:

$$
A:B=8:12
$$

Second ratio ×3:

$$
B:C=12:15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A:B:C=8:12:15}
$$

$$

$$

$$

$$

$$

LCM:

$$
30
$$

$$
A:B:C=16:24:30

$$

$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A:B:C:D=16:24:30:21}

# 40. Ratio Multiplication

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
C:D=4:5
$$

$$
AC:BD
$$

is:

$$
(2\times4):(3\times5)
$$

$$
=\boxed{8:15}
$$

---

# 41. Ratio Inversion

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

then:

$$
B:A=3:2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
a:b\Rightarrow b:a
}
$$

---

# 42. Ratio Scaling

If:

$$
A:B=3:4
$$

then all of these are equivalent:

$$

$$

$$
9:12
$$

$$
12:16
$$

$$

$$

because both terms are multiplied by the same number.

# 43. Ratio Must Be Dimensionless

You can compare:

- `5 kg : 10 kg`
- `2 m : 4 m`
- `₹50 : ₹100`

But not directly:

`5 kg : 10 m`

unless a meaningful conversion/relationship is provided.

# 51. Ratio and Proportion — Key Property

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{ad=bc}
$$

Also:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab=\frac cd
}
$$

This is the foundation of proportion questions.

---

# 52. Mean Proportional

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{b=\sqrt{ac}}
$$

Example:

Find the mean proportional between `4` and `9`.

$$
b=\sqrt{4\times9}
$$

$$
=\boxed6
$$

---

# 53. Continued Proportion

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then `a, b, c` are in continued proportion.

Equivalent condition:

$$
\boxed{b^2=ac}
$$

---

# 54. Direct Proportion

Two quantities are directly proportional if:

$$
\boxed{
\frac xy=\text{constant}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
x\propto y
}
$$

If one increases, the other increases in the same ratio.

Example:

More notebooks → more cost, if price per notebook is fixed.

---

# 55. Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are inversely proportional if:

$$
\boxed{
xy=\text{constant}
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
x\propto\frac1y
}
$$

If one increases, the other decreases.

Example:

More workers → fewer days, assuming work and efficiency remain constant.

---

# 56. Direct vs Inverse


::contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}


| Type | Relationship | Key Test |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | `x ∝ y` | `x/y = constant` |
| Inverse | `x ∝ 1/y` | `xy = constant` |

Solve for `k`.

# 64. Ratio Changes After Addition

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

Add `10` to both quantities and ratio becomes `3:4`.

Initially:

$$
A=2k,\quad B=3k
$$

After addition:

$$
\frac{2k+10}{3k+10}=\frac34
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
8k+40=9k+30
$$

Therefore:

$$
k=10
$$

Original:

$$
\boxed{A=20,\ B=30}
$$

---

# 65. Ratio Changes After Subtraction

Suppose:

$$
A:B=5:7
$$

Subtract `10` from both and ratio becomes `3:4`.

Initially:

$$
A=5k,\quad B=7k
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{5k-10}{7k-10}
=
\frac34
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
20k-40=21k-30
$$

Therefore:

$$
k=10
$$

Original values:

$$
\boxed{50,\ 70}
$$

---

# 66. Ratio Changes After Adding Different Amounts

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

`A` increases by `10` and `B` increases by `20`.

Then:

$$
A=2k,\quad B=3k
$$

New ratio:

$$
(2k+10):(3k+20)
$$

Use the given new ratio to solve `k`.

> [!tip]
> Always represent the original values using a common multiplier `k`.

# 67. Important Ratio Patterns

> [!important]

### Sum Given

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

$$
A+B=T
$$

Use:

$$
\boxed{k=\frac{T}{m+n}}
$$

### Difference Given

$$
B-A=D
$$

Use:

$$
\boxed{k=\frac{D}{n-m}}
$$

### Ratio Given

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

Use:

$$
\boxed{A=mk,\ B=nk}
$$

# 68. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Not converting units.

`2 m : 50 cm` is not `2:50`.

### Mistake 2

Adding ratio terms incorrectly.

For `2:3`, total parts are:

$$
2+3=5
$$

### Mistake 3

Confusing A:B with A:(A+B).

For `2:3`:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

but:

$$
A:\text{Total}=2:5
$$

### Mistake 4

Changing only one term when creating an equivalent ratio.

### Mistake 5

Forgetting the common multiplier `k`.

### Mistake 6

Confusing direct and inverse proportion.

> **Ratio gives parts. `k` converts parts into actual values.**

### One-Line Recognition
