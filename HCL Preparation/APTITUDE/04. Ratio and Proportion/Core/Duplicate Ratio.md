# Duplicate Ratio

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> The **duplicate ratio** of `a:b` is the ratio of their squares.

If:

$$
a:b
$$

then its duplicate ratio is:

$$
\boxed{a^2:b^2}
$$

In other words:

> **Duplicate ratio = square both terms.**

---

# 2. Basic Example

Given:

$$
2:3
$$

Duplicate ratio:

$$
2^2:3^2
$$

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

---

# 3. Formula

If:

$$
a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Duplicate ratio}=a^2:b^2
}
$$

Equivalent form:

$$
\boxed{
(a:b)^2=a^2:b^2
}
$$

---

# 4. Example

Find the duplicate ratio of:

$$
3:5
$$

Square both terms:

$$
3^2:5^2
$$

$$
\boxed{9:25}
$$

---

# 5. Example — Larger Numbers

Find the duplicate ratio of:

$$
4:7
$$

$$
4^2:7^2
$$

$$
\boxed{16:49}
$$

---

# 6. Duplicate Ratio From a Fraction

If:

$$
\frac ab
$$

is the ratio, its duplicate ratio is:

$$
\left(\frac ab\right)^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
a^2:b^2
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac23
$$

Duplicate:

$$
\frac49
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

---

# 7. Duplicate Ratio vs Original Ratio

Original:

$$
2:3
$$

Duplicate:

$$
4:9
$$

Notice:

$$
2:3\neq4:9
$$

The duplicate ratio is a **new ratio** obtained by squaring both terms.

---

# 8. Why Is It Called Duplicate?

A ratio compounded with itself gives:

$$
(a:b)(a:b)
$$

Therefore:

$$
a\times a:b\times b
$$

$$
=a^2:b^2
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{\text{Duplicate Ratio}=\text{Ratio compounded with itself}}
$$

---

# 9. Connection With Compound Ratio

Compound ratio:

$$
(a:b)(c:d)=ac:bd
$$

If both ratios are the same:

$$
(a:b)(a:b)
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{a^2:b^2}
$$

Therefore:

> **Duplicate ratio is a special case of compound ratio.**

---

# 10. Example

Compound:

$$
3:4
$$

with itself:

$$
3:4\times3:4
$$

$$
3\times3:4\times4
$$

$$
\boxed{9:16}
$$

---

# 12. Duplicate Ratio of `7:9`

---

# 14. Simplifying After Squaring

Suppose:

$$
2:4
$$

First simplify:

$$
1:2
$$

Duplicate:

$$
1^2:2^2
=
\boxed{1:4}
$$

If you square first:

$$
4:16
$$

then simplify:

$$
\boxed{1:4}
$$

Both methods give the same answer.

---

# 15. Important Shortcut

> [!tip]
> **Simplify the original ratio first, then square.**

Example:

$$
12:18
$$

Simplify:

$$
2:3
$$

Duplicate:

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

This avoids unnecessary large numbers.

\frac23:\frac45

=10:12

$$

# 20. Duplicate Ratio of Three Terms

For:

$$
a:b:c
$$

the duplicate ratio is:

$$
\boxed{a^2:b^2:c^2}
$$

Example:

$$
2:3:5
$$

Duplicate:

$$
\boxed{4:9:25}
$$

---

# 21. Duplicate Ratio of Four Terms

Given:

$$
2:3:4:5
$$

Duplicate:

$$
2^2:3^2:4^2:5^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:9:16:25}
$$

---

# 22. Finding Original Ratio From Duplicate Ratio

Suppose duplicate ratio is:

$$
25:36
$$

Take square roots:

$$
\sqrt{25}:\sqrt{36}
$$

$$
\boxed{5:6}
$$

Therefore the original ratio is:

$$
\boxed{5:6}
$$

---

# 23. Another Example

Duplicate ratio:

$$
49:81
$$

Original ratio:

$$
\sqrt{49}:\sqrt{81}
$$

$$
\boxed{7:9}
$$

---

# 24. Finding Original Ratio

If duplicate ratio is:

$$
16:25
$$

then:

$$
a^2:b^2=16:25
$$

Therefore:

$$
a:b=\sqrt{16}:\sqrt{25}
$$

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 25. Duplicate Ratio and Square Roots

If:

$$
a:b=3:4
$$

then:

$$
a^2:b^2=9:16
$$

Conversely, if:

$$
a^2:b^2=9:16
$$

then:

$$
a:b=3:4
$$

For positive quantities:

$$
\boxed{
a:b=\sqrt{a^2}:\sqrt{b^2}
}
$$

---

# 26. Geometry Connection — Area

If two squares have side ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

then their areas are proportional to the squares of their sides:

$$
2^2:3^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

This is one of the most important applications of duplicate ratio.

---

# 27. Rectangle Area

Suppose two rectangles have the same breadth.

Their lengths are in ratio:

$$
2:5
$$

Since:

$$
Area=Length\times Breadth
$$

and breadth is common:

$$
Area_1:Area_2=2:5
$$

> [!important]
> Duplicate ratio applies when the quantity depends on the **square** of the changing quantity.

---

# 28. Circle Area

Circle area:

$$
A=\pi r^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
A_1:A_2=r_1^2:r_2^2
$$

If:

$$
r_1:r_2=3:5
$$

then:

$$
A_1:A_2=9:25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{9:25}
$$

---

# 29. Sphere Surface Area

Surface area:

$$
S=4\pi r^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
S_1:S_2=r_1^2:r_2^2
$$

If:

$$
r_1:r_2=2:3
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

---

# 30. Duplicate Ratio in Geometry

Whenever a quantity is proportional to:

$$
x^2
$$

and:

$$
x_1:x_2=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Q_1:Q_2=a^2:b^2
}
$$

Common examples:

- Area of a square
- Area of a circle
- Surface area of a sphere
- Area based on radius
- Other square-dependent quantities

---

# 31. Duplicate Ratio and Algebra

Suppose:

$$
x:y=3:7
$$

Then:

$$
x^2:y^2
=
3^2:7^2
$$

$$
\boxed{9:49}
$$

---

# 32. Higher Powers

If:

$$
x:y=2:3
$$

then:

### First power

$$
x:y=2:3
$$

### Second power

$$
x^2:y^2=4:9
$$

### Third power

$$
x^3:y^3=8:27
$$

### Fourth power

$$
x^4:y^4=16:81
$$

---

# 33. General Power Rule

If:

$$
x:y=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
x^n:y^n=a^n:b^n
}
$$

Therefore:

- `n = 1` → original ratio
- `n = 2` → duplicate ratio
- `n = 3` → triplicate ratio

---

# 34. Duplicate Ratio vs Triplicate Ratio

| Type | Operation | Example `2:3` |
|---|---|---|
| Original | Power 1 | `2:3` |
| Duplicate | Power 2 | `4:9` |
| Triplicate | Power 3 | `8:27` |

---

# 35. Subduplicate Ratio

The reverse operation of duplicate ratio is called **subduplicate ratio**.

If:

$$
a:b
$$

then its subduplicate ratio is:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt a:\sqrt b}
$$

Example:

$$
25:49
$$

Subduplicate:

$$
\boxed{5:7}
$$

---

# 36. Duplicate and Subduplicate

### Duplicate

Square:

$$
a:b
\rightarrow
\boxed{a^2:b^2}
$$

### Subduplicate

Square root:

$$
a:b
\rightarrow
\boxed{\sqrt a:\sqrt b}
$$

They are inverse operations for positive quantities.

---

# 37. Duplicate Ratio and Proportion

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then squaring both ratios gives:

$$
a^2:b^2=c^2:d^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
a^2:b^2=c^2:d^2
}
$$

---

# 38. Example

Given:

$$
2:3=8:12
$$

Duplicate both sides:

$$
4:9=64:144
$$

Simplify right side:

$$
64:144=4:9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4:9=4:9}
$$

---

# 39. Finding Unknown Using Duplicate Ratio

Suppose:

The duplicate ratio of `x:5` is `16:25`.

Then:

$$
x^2:25=16:25
$$

Therefore:

$$
x^2=16
$$

For positive ratio terms:

$$
\boxed{x=4}
$$

---

# 40. Another Unknown Example

The duplicate ratio of:

$$
3:x
$$

is:

$$
9:49
$$

Then:

$$
3^2:x^2=9:49
$$

Therefore:

$$
x^2=49
$$

$$
\boxed{x=7}
$$

Given:

$$
6:8

$$

# 47. Duplicate Ratio of Equivalent Ratios

Suppose:

$$
2:3
$$

and:

$$
10:15
$$

are equivalent.

Duplicate first:

$$
4:9
$$

Duplicate second:

$$
100:225
$$

Simplify:

$$
4:9
$$

Therefore both produce the same duplicate ratio.

---

# 48. Important Property

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
a^2:b^2=c^2:d^2
}
$$

So taking the duplicate ratio preserves equality.

---

# 49. Comparison After Duplication

For positive ratios:

If:

$$
a:b>c:d
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
a^2:b^2>c^2:d^2
}
$$

because squaring is increasing for positive numbers.

Example:

$$
2:3>1:2
$$

because:

$$
0.667>0.5
$$

Duplicate:

$$
4:9>1:4
$$

---

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Multiply by 2

Duplicate does **not** mean multiply both terms by `2`.

Wrong:

$$
3:5\rightarrow6:10
$$

Correct:

$$
3^2:5^2
=
\boxed{9:25}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Add the Terms

Wrong:

$$
3:5\rightarrow3+3:5+5
$$

Duplicate means **square**.

---

### Mistake 3 — Cube Instead of Square

Wrong:

$$
2:3\rightarrow8:27
$$

That is the **triplicate ratio**.

Correct duplicate:

$$
\boxed{4:9}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Forgetting Simplification

$$
6^2:8^2
=
36:64
=
\boxed{9:16}
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Applying Duplicate Automatically

If a quantity depends linearly on `x`, use the original ratio.

Only use duplicate when the relationship involves:

$$
x^2
$$
