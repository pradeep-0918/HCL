# Compound Ratio

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> The **compound ratio** of two or more ratios is obtained by multiplying their corresponding terms.

If:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:d
$$

then their compound ratio is:

$$
\boxed{ac:bd}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Example

Given:

$$
2:3
$$

and:

$$
4:5
$$

Compound ratio:

$$
(2\times4):(3\times5)
$$

$$
8:15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8:15}
$$

---

# 3. Formula

For:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:d
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Compound Ratio}=ac:bd
}
$$

In fraction form:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times\frac cd
=
\frac{ac}{bd}
}
$$

---

# 4. Three Ratios

If:

$$
a:b,\quad c:d,\quad e:f
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
ace:bdf
}
$$

Example:

$$
2:3,\quad4:5,\quad6:7
$$

Compound ratio:

$$
(2\times4\times6):(3\times5\times7)
$$

$$
48:105
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{16:35}
$$

---

# 5. Four Ratios

Given:

$$
2:3,\quad3:4,\quad5:6,\quad7:8
$$

Compound ratio:

$$
(2\times3\times5\times7):
(3\times4\times6\times8)
$$

Cancel common `3`:

$$
210:576
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{35:96}
$$

---

# 6. Why Multiplication?

A ratio represents a fraction.

For example:

$$
2:3=\frac23
$$

and:

$$
4:5=\frac45
$$

Their compound ratio is:

$$
\frac23\times\frac45
=
\frac8{15}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8:15}
$$

---

# 7. Example — Simple

Find the compound ratio of:

$$
3:4
$$

and:

$$
5:6
$$

Multiply corresponding terms:

$$
3\times5:4\times6
$$

$$
15:24
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:8}
$$

---

# 8. Example — Larger Numbers

Find the compound ratio:

$$
6:7
$$

and:

$$
14:15
$$

Multiply:

$$
6\times14:7\times15
$$

$$
84:105
$$

Divide by `21`:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 9. Cancellation Shortcut

Instead of multiplying first, cancel common factors.

Example:

$$
6:7
$$

and:

$$
14:15
$$

Write:

$$
\frac67\times\frac{14}{15}
$$

Cancel:

$$
14\div7=2
$$

$$
6\div15=\frac25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{6\times14}{7\times15}
=
\frac45
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

> [!tip]
> **Cancel before multiplying** to avoid large numbers.

---

# 10. Compound Ratio of Three Ratios

Given:

$$
2:5,\quad3:4,\quad10:9
$$

Write:

$$
\frac25\times\frac34\times\frac{10}{9}
$$

Cancel:

$$
10/5=2
$$

$$
3/9=1/3
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{2\times3\times2}{4\times3}
=
\frac4{4}
=
1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1:1}
$$

---

$$

$$

$$

compound ratio:

$$

$$

# 12. Compound Ratio of Equal Ratios

If both ratios are:

$$
a:b
$$

then compound ratio is:

$$
a^2:b^2
$$

This is called the **duplicate ratio**.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
(a:b)\times(a:b)=a^2:b^2
}
$$

---

# 20. Compound Ratio in Word Problems

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

Be careful:

These ratios **cannot simply be multiplied** to obtain `A:C` unless the common term is handled correctly.

First make the common term B equal.

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

LCM of B parts:

$$
LCM(3,4)=12
$$

First:

$$
A:B=8:12
$$

Second:

$$
B:C=12:15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A:C=8:15}
$$

> [!important]
> **Compound ratio multiplication and chaining ratios are related but not identical operations.**

---

# 21. Finding A:C Directly

Given:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

We can write:

$$
\frac AB=\frac23
$$

and:

$$
\frac BC=\frac45
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac AC
=
\frac AB\times\frac BC
$$

$$
=
\frac23\times\frac45
=
\frac8{15}
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{A:C=8:15}
$$

---

# 22. Chain Ratio Formula

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

and:

$$
B:C=p:q
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A:C=\frac{mp}{nq}
}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
A:C=mp:nq
}
$$

after ensuring the common middle quantity is correctly interpreted.

---

# 23. Important Example

Given:

$$
A:B=3:4
$$

and:

$$
B:C=6:7
$$

Then:

$$
\frac AC
=
\frac34\times\frac67
$$

Cancel `6` with `3`:

$$
=\frac{1\times2}{4\times7}
=
\frac2{28}
=
\frac17
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A:C=1:7}
$$

> [!warning]
> For chain ratios, always verify the middle quantity if the ratios represent actual quantities. The algebraic multiplication works when the ratios are expressed consistently.

---

# 24. Compound Ratio and Proportion

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then the ratios are equivalent.

If another ratio is:

$$
e:f
$$

the compound ratio can be formed:

$$
ac:bd
$$

or:

$$
ae:bf
$$

depending on the quantities being combined.

> [!important]
> Identify what the ratio represents before combining it.

---

# 26. Compound Ratio Using Prime Factors

Suppose:

$$
12:25
$$

and:

$$
15:18
$$

Compound:

$$
12\times15:25\times18
$$

Instead of multiplying:

$$
\frac{12}{25}\times\frac{15}{18}
$$

Factor:

$$
12=2^2\times3
$$

$$
15=3\times5
$$

$$
18=2\times3^2
$$

Cancel:

$$
\frac{2^2\times3\times3\times5}
{5^2\times2\times3^2}
$$

Cancel common factors:

$$
\frac{2}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:5}
$$

---

# 48. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Add Instead of Multiply

Wrong:

$$
2:3+4:5
$$

Compound ratio requires multiplication:

$$
\boxed{8:15}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Multiply Incorrect Terms

Correct:

$$
a:b,\ c:d
\Rightarrow
ac:bd
$$

Not:

$$
ad:bc
$$

`ad:bc` is related to cross multiplication for checking equality, not the compound ratio.

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting Simplification

$$
12:18
$$

should become:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing Compound and Duplicate

Compound:

$$
a:b,\ c:d
\Rightarrow
ac:bd
$$

Duplicate:

$$
a:b
\Rightarrow
a^2:b^2
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring the Meaning of the Ratios

When ratios are chained, make sure the common quantity is aligned correctly.
