# Equivalent Ratios

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Equivalent ratios** are ratios that represent the same relationship even though their terms may be different.

If:

$$
a:b
$$

is multiplied or divided by the **same non-zero number**, the resulting ratio remains equivalent.

$$
\boxed{
a:b=ka:kb
}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Example

Consider:

$$
2:3
$$

Multiply both terms by `2`:

$$
4:6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3=4:6}
$$

Both ratios represent the same relationship.

---

# 3. More Examples

$$
2:3=4:6=6:9=8:12=10:15
$$

All are equivalent ratios.

The simplest form is:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 4. Multiplying Both Terms

Starting ratio:

$$
3:5
$$

Multiply by `4`:

$$
3\times4:5\times4
$$

$$
\boxed{12:20}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3:5=12:20}
$$

---

# 5. Dividing Both Terms

Given:

$$
24:36
$$

Divide both terms by `12`:

$$
24\div12:36\div12
$$

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{24:36=2:3}
$$

---

# 6. Fundamental Rule

> [!important]
> To create an equivalent ratio, **multiply or divide both terms by exactly the same non-zero number**.

Correct:

$$
2:5\rightarrow6:15
$$

because both are multiplied by `3`.

Incorrect:

$$
2:5\rightarrow4:15
$$

because different multipliers were used.

---

# 7. Equivalent Ratios as Fractions

Since:

$$
a:b=\frac ab
$$

equivalent ratios follow the same rule as equivalent fractions.

Example:

$$
\frac23=\frac46=\frac8{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2:3=4:6=8:12}
$$

---

# 8. Finding a Missing Term

Suppose:

$$
3:5=x:20
$$

Write as fractions:

$$
\frac35=\frac{x}{20}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
5x=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{3:5=12:20}
$$

---

# 9. Missing Second Term

Suppose:

$$
4:7=20:x
$$

Then:

$$
\frac47=\frac{20}{x}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
4x=140
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=35}
$$

---

# 10. Missing Both Terms

Suppose:

$$
2:3=x:12
$$

Since:

$$
3\times4=12
$$

multiply the first term by `4`:

$$
2\times4=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=8}
$$

---

# 11. Fast Method

For:

$$
a:b=c:x
$$

use:

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{bc}{a}}
$$

Example:

$$
3:4=12:x
$$

$$
x=\frac{4\times12}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{x=16}
$$

---

# 12. Another Fast Method

For:

$$
a:b=x:d
$$

use:

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{ad}{b}}
$$

Example:

$$
5:8=x:24
$$

$$
x=\frac{5\times24}{8}
$$

$$
\boxed{x=15}
$$

---

# 13. Checking Equivalent Ratios

To check whether:

$$
6:9
$$

and:

$$
10:15
$$

are equivalent:

Cross multiply:

$$
6\times15=90
$$

$$
9\times10=90
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Equivalent}}
$$

---

# 14. Cross-Multiplication Test

For:

$$
a:b
$$

and:

$$
c:d
$$

they are equivalent if:

$$
\boxed{ad=bc}
$$

This is one of the most important tests.

---

# 15. Example

Check:

$$
8:12
$$

and:

$$
14:21
$$

Cross multiplication:

$$
8\times21=168
$$

$$
12\times14=168
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Equivalent}}
$$

---

# 16. Non-Equivalent Ratios

Check:

$$
3:5
$$

and:

$$
8:12
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
3\times12=36
$$

$$
5\times8=40
$$

Since:

$$
36\ne40
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Not equivalent}}
$$

---

# 17. Simplest Equivalent Ratio

Given:

$$
45:60
$$

HCF:

$$
15
$$

Divide:

$$
45\div15:60\div15
$$

$$
\boxed{3:4}
$$

Therefore `3:4` is the simplest equivalent ratio.

---

$$

$$

$$

$$

$$

$$

# 19. Ratio Scale Factor

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:7
$$

and:

$$
A:B=15:35
$$

Scale factor:

$$
\frac{15}{3}=5
$$

and:

$$
\frac{35}{7}=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Scale factor}=5}
$$

---

# 20. Finding the Scale Factor

Given:

$$
4:9
$$

and:

$$
20:45
$$

Scale factor:

$$
\frac{20}{4}=5
$$

Check:

$$
\frac{45}{9}=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

---

# 21. Equivalent Ratios and Total

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

Equivalent ratio:

$$
A:B=8:12
$$

Total parts in the first:

$$
2+3=5
$$

Total parts in the second:

$$
8+12=20
$$

Both represent the same relationship.

---

# 22. Ratio and Actual Values

If:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

then:

$$
A=3k
$$

$$
B=5k
$$

An equivalent ratio could be:

$$
6:10
$$

which corresponds to:

$$
A=6k'
$$

$$
B=10k'
$$

Both represent the same relationship.

---

# 23. Equivalent Ratio With Total

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

and:

$$
A+B=64
$$

Total ratio parts:

$$
3+5=8
$$

One part:

$$
64/8=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=24
$$

$$
B=40
$$

Ratio:

$$
24:40
$$

Divide by `8`:

$$
\boxed{3:5}
$$

---

# 24. Equivalent Ratio With Difference

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

and:

$$
B-A=16
$$

Ratio difference:

$$
5-3=2
$$

One ratio part:

$$
16/2=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=24
$$

$$
B=40
$$

Ratio:

$$
24:40
=
\boxed{3:5}
$$

---

# 25. Equivalent Ratio With Three Quantities

Given:

$$
A:B:C=2:3:5
$$

Multiply by `4`:

$$
\boxed{8:12:20}
$$

Multiply by `10`:

$$
\boxed{20:30:50}
$$

All are equivalent.

---

# 26. Finding a Three-Part Missing Value

Suppose:

$$
A:B:C=2:3:5
$$

and:

$$
B=18
$$

Since:

$$
3k=18
$$

$$
k=6
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=12
$$

$$
C=30
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{A:B:C=12:18:30}
$$

---

# 27. Ratio and Percentage

Suppose:

$$
A:B=3:5
$$

Then:

$$
\frac AB=\frac35
$$

Therefore A is:

$$
\frac35\times100
=
\boxed{60\%}
$$

of B.

---

# 28. Percentage to Equivalent Ratio

If:

A is `40%` of B.

Then:

$$
A:B=40:100
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{2:5}
$$

Equivalent ratios:

$$
4:10
$$

$$
6:15
$$

$$
8:20
$$

---

# 29. Equivalent Ratios With Units

Suppose:

$$
2kg:500g
$$

Convert:

$$
2kg=2000g
$$

Therefore:

$$
2000:500
=
4:1
$$

Equivalent forms:

$$
8:2
$$

$$
12:3
$$

etc.

---

# 30. Equivalent Ratios With Decimals

Given:

$$
0.4:0.8
$$

Multiply by `10`:

$$
4:8
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{1:2}
$$

---

# 31. Equivalent Ratios With Fractions

Given:

$$
\frac12:\frac34
$$

LCM of denominators = `4`.

Multiply both by `4`:

$$
2:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac12:\frac34=2:3}
$$

---

# 32. Cross-Multiplication With Fractions

Suppose:

$$
\frac23:\frac45
$$

Convert ratio:

$$
2\times5:3\times4
$$

$$
10:12
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:6}
$$

---

# 33. Equivalent Ratios and Proportion

If:

$$
a:b=c:d
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
a:b::c:d
}
$$

This notation means:

> `a` is to `b` as `c` is to `d`.

---

# 34. Example

$$
2:5::8:20
$$

Check:

$$
2\times20=40
$$

$$
5\times8=40
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Proportion is true}}
$$

---

# 35. Fourth Proportional

If:

$$
a:b=c:x
$$

then `x` is called the **fourth proportional**.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
x=\frac{bc}{a}
}
$$

Example:

Find the fourth proportional to `3, 5, 12`.

$$
3:5=12:x
$$

Therefore:

$$
x=\frac{5\times12}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{x=20}
$$

---

# 36. Third Proportional

If:

$$
a:b=b:x
$$

then `x` is the third proportional.

Since:

$$
\frac ab=\frac bx
$$

cross multiply:

$$
b^2=ax
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
x=\frac{b^2}{a}
}
$$

---

# 37. Example — Third Proportional

Find the third proportional to `4` and `8`.

$$
4:8=8:x
$$

Therefore:

$$
4x=64
$$

$$
\boxed{x=16}
$$

---

# 38. Mean Proportional

If:

$$
a:b=b:c
$$

then `b` is the mean proportional.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
b=\sqrt{ac}
}
$$

Example:

Mean proportional between `4` and `25`:

$$
\sqrt{4\times25}
=
\sqrt{100}
=
\boxed{10}
$$

---

# 39. Equivalent Ratio From Algebra

Suppose:

$$
A:B=4:7
$$

Then:

$$
\frac A4=\frac B7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{7A=4B}
$$

This form can be useful in algebraic ratio problems.

---

# 40. Ratio Equality

If:

$$
\frac A B=\frac C D
$$

then:

$$
AD=BC
$$

This allows us to transform ratio questions into equations.

---

# 41. Multiple Equivalent Ratios

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
B:C=3:4
$$

The common term `B` is already equal.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A:B:C=2:3:4
}
$$

---

# 42. Different Common Terms

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and:

$$
B:C=4:5
$$

B values differ:

`3` and `4`.

LCM:

$$
12
$$

First ratio:

$$
2:3
\rightarrow
8:12
$$

Second:

$$
4:5
\rightarrow
12:15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
A:B:C=8:12:15
}
$$

---

# 43. Equivalent Ratios in Mixtures

Suppose a mixture contains:

Milk : Water:

$$
2:3
$$

If total mixture is `25 L`:

Total parts:

$$
5
$$

One part:

$$
5L
$$

Milk:

$$
2\times5
=
\boxed{10L}
$$

Water:

$$
3\times5
=
\boxed{15L}
$$

---

# 44. Adding the Same Quantity

Suppose:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

If `10` is added to both and the ratio remains the same:

$$
\frac{2k+10}{3k+10}
=
\frac23
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
6k+30=6k+20
$$

This is impossible.

Therefore:

> [!important]
> **Adding the same positive quantity to two unequal quantities cannot preserve their original ratio.**

---

# 45. Multiplying Both Quantities

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and both quantities are doubled:

$$
2A:2B
=
4:6
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

The ratio remains unchanged.

---

# 46. Adding Proportional Quantities

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and we add quantities in the same ratio `2:3`, the overall ratio remains:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

Example:

Original:

$$
20:30
$$

Add:

$$
10:15
$$

New:

$$
30:45
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

---

# 47. Equivalent Ratios and Direct Proportion

If:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

and B doubles:

$$
B=6
$$

then A must become:

$$
A=4
$$

because:

$$
4:6=2:3
$$

This is the basic idea behind direct proportion.

---

# 48. Equivalent Ratios and Inverse Proportion

For inverse proportion:

$$
AB=\text{constant}
$$

Example:

Workers:

`4 → 8`

Days:

`12 → ?`

Since workers double:

$$
4\times12=8\times d
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{d=6}
$$

The ratio changes inversely.

---

# 49. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Multiplying only one term.

Wrong:

$$
2:3\rightarrow4:3
$$

### Mistake 2

Using different multipliers.

Wrong:

$$
2:3\rightarrow4:9
$$

### Mistake 3

Not reducing to simplest form.

$$
24:36
$$

should become:

$$
\boxed{2:3}
$$

### Mistake 4

Ignoring units.

### Mistake 5

Confusing ratio with percentage.

`2:3` means:

$$
\frac23
$$

but A's share of total is:

$$
\frac25
$$

### Mistake 6

Using direct proportion when the relationship is inverse.

# 51. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Equivalent Ratio

$$
\boxed{
a:b=ka:kb
}
$$

### Proportion

$$
\boxed{
a:b=c:d
\iff
ad=bc
}
$$

### Missing Fourth Term

$$
\boxed{
a:b=c:x
\Rightarrow
x=\frac{bc}{a}
}
$$

### Third Proportional

$$
\boxed{
a:b=b:x
\Rightarrow
x=\frac{b^2}{a}
}
$$

### Mean Proportional

$$
\boxed{
b=\sqrt{ac}
}
$$

### Ratio Representation

$$
\boxed{
A:B=m:n
\Rightarrow
A=mk,\ B=nk
}
$$

### Sum

$$
\boxed{
k=\frac{A+B}{m+n}
}
$$

### Difference

$$
\boxed{
k=\frac{|A-B|}{|m-n|}
}
$$
