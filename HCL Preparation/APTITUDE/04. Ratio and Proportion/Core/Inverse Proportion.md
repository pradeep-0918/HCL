# Inverse Proportion

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when an increase in one quantity causes a proportional decrease in the other, such that their product remains constant.

If:

$$
x\propto\frac1y
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{xy=k}
$$

where `k` is a constant.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

In inverse proportion:

> **One quantity increases → the other decreases.**

Example:

More workers → fewer days.

Fewer workers → more days.

If the total work remains fixed:

$$
\boxed{\text{Workers}\times\text{Days}=\text{constant}}
$$

---

# 3. Main Formula

If `x` and `y` are inversely proportional:

$$
\boxed{xy=k}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x_1y_1=x_2y_2}
$$

This is the most important formula.

---

# 4. Basic Example

5 workers complete a job in 12 days.

How many days will 10 workers take?

Since workers and days are inversely proportional:

$$
5\times12=10\times x
$$

$$
60=10x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=6\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 5. Why Inverse?

Workers doubled:

$$
5\rightarrow10
$$

Days became half:

$$
12\rightarrow6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Workers}\propto\frac1{\text{Days}}}
$$

---

# 6. Direct vs Inverse

| Direct Proportion | Inverse Proportion |
|---|---|
| One ↑ → Other ↑ | One ↑ → Other ↓ |
| `x/y = constant` | `xy = constant` |
| `x = ky` | `x = k/y` |
| Same direction | Opposite direction |
| Quantity ↑ → Cost ↑ | Workers ↑ → Days ↓ |

---

# 7. Recognition Trick

Look for phrases such as:

- inversely proportional
- varies inversely as
- more workers, fewer days
- fewer workers, more days
- higher speed, less time
- lower speed, more time
- more taps, less filling time
- more machines, less production time

These often indicate inverse proportion.

---

# 8. Inverse Ratio Rule

If:

$$
x\propto\frac1y
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{x_1:x_2=y_2:y_1}
$$

Notice that the order is **reversed**.

---

# 9. Example

Workers:

$$
5:10
$$

Since days are inversely proportional:

$$
Days=10:5
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{2:1}
$$

If 5 workers take 12 days:

10 workers take:

$$
12/2
=
\boxed{6\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 10. Doubling Rule

If:

$$
x\propto\frac1y
$$

and `x` doubles:

$$
x\rightarrow2x
$$

then:

$$
y\rightarrow\frac y2
$$

Example:

8 workers → 15 days.

16 workers:

$$
15/2
=
\boxed{7.5\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 11. Tripling Rule

If workers increase from:

$$
5\rightarrow15
$$

that is ×3.

Days become:

$$
\frac13
$$

of the original.

If original time is 18 days:

$$
18/3
=
\boxed{6\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 12. Halving Rule

If:

$$
x\rightarrow\frac x2
$$

then:

$$
y\rightarrow2y
$$

Example:

12 workers take 8 days.

6 workers take:

$$
8\times2
=
\boxed{16\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 13. Finding Unknown

7 workers finish a job in 20 days.

How long for 14 workers?

$$
7\times20=14\times x
$$

$$
140=14x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=10}
$$

---

# 14. Finding Workers

8 workers take 15 days.

How many workers are required to finish in 6 days?

$$
8\times15=x\times6
$$

$$
120=6x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=20}
$$

---

# 15. Work Problems

For a fixed amount of work:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Workers}\times\text{Days}=\text{Constant}
}
$$

provided:

- efficiency per worker is constant
- working hours per day are constant
- total work is fixed

---

# 16. Workers and Days

Example:

12 workers → 18 days.

9 workers → ?

$$
12\times18=9x
$$

$$
216=9x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=24\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 17. Workers and Hours

If both number of workers and working hours change:

$$
\boxed{
W_1D_1H_1=W_2D_2H_2
}
$$

for fixed work and equal efficiency.

Where:

- `W` = workers
- `D` = days
- `H` = hours/day

---

# 18. Example — Workers and Hours

10 workers working 8 hours/day complete a job in 12 days.

How many workers are needed to complete it in 8 days at 10 hours/day?

Fixed work:

$$
10\times8\times12
=
x\times10\times8
$$

$$
960=80x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

---

# 19. Work Formula

If efficiency is constant:

$$
Work=Workers\times Days\times Hours
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
W_1D_1H_1=W_2D_2H_2
}
$$

---

# 20. Speed and Time

For a fixed distance:

$$
Distance=Speed\times Time
$$

Since distance is constant:

$$
\boxed{Speed\times Time=Constant}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Speed\propto\frac1{Time}}
$$

---

# 21. Example — Speed

A car travels a fixed distance at 60 km/h in 5 hours.

If speed becomes 75 km/h, find the new time.

$$
60\times5=75x
$$

$$
300=75x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=4\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 22. Speed Ratio Shortcut

If:

$$
Speed_1:Speed_2=3:5
$$

then for the same distance:

$$
Time_1:Time_2=5:3
$$

> [!important]
> **Speed ratio and time ratio are reversed when distance is fixed.**

---

# 23. Example

Speed ratio:

$$
40:60=2:3
$$

Therefore time ratio:

$$
\boxed{3:2}
$$

If the slower speed takes 6 hours:

Faster speed takes:

$$
6\times\frac23
=
\boxed{4\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 24. Machines and Time

If more machines perform the same fixed job:

$$
\boxed{\text{Machines}\propto\frac1{\text{Time}}}
$$

Example:

4 machines → 15 hours.

10 machines → ?

$$
4\times15=10x
$$

$$
60=10x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=6\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 25. Taps and Filling Time

If identical taps fill the same tank:

$$
\boxed{\text{Number of taps}\propto\frac1{\text{Time}}}
$$

Example:

2 taps fill a tank in 30 minutes.

5 taps:

$$
2\times30=5x
$$

$$
60=5x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=12\text{ minutes}}
$$

---

# 26. Pipes and Time

If identical pipes operate together under the same conditions:

More pipes → less time.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P_1T_1=P_2T_2}
$$

---

# 27. Men and Days

Suppose:

15 men complete a job in 20 days.

10 men will take:

$$
15\times20=10x
$$

$$
300=10x
$$

$$
\boxed{30\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 28. Women and Days

The same principle applies if each worker has equal efficiency.

Example:

8 workers → 15 days.

12 workers:

$$
8\times15=12x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=10\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 29. Machines and Production Time

If a fixed quantity must be produced:

6 machines take 10 hours.

12 machines take:

$$
6\times10=12x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=5\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 30. Inverse Proportion and Percentage

If one quantity increases by `20%`, the other does **not** decrease by exactly `20%`.

This is a very important trap.

Suppose:

$$
x\propto\frac1y
$$

If `x` increases by `20%`:

$$
x'=1.2x
$$

Then:

$$
y'=\frac y{1.2}
$$

$$
y'=\frac56y
$$

Decrease:

$$
1-\frac56
=
\frac16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16\tfrac23\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 31. Inverse Percentage Formula

If one quantity increases by `p%`, the corresponding inverse quantity decreases by:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{p}{100+p}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

`25%` increase:

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 32. Inverse Percentage — Decrease

If one quantity decreases by `p%`, the other increases by:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{p}{100-p}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Time decreases by `20%`.

Speed increases by:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 33. Important Comparison

### Direct

20% increase → 20% increase.

### Inverse

20% increase → 16⅔% decrease.

This distinction is frequently tested.

---

# 34. Example — Speed Increase

A car's speed increases by `25%`.

For the same distance, what happens to travel time?

New speed:

$$
1.25S
$$

New time:

$$
\frac{T}{1.25}
=
0.8T
$$

Therefore time decreases by:

$$
20\%
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 35. Example — Time Decrease

Travel time decreases by `20%`.

Since speed and time are inversely proportional:

$$
T'=0.8T
$$

Therefore:

$$
S'=\frac{S}{0.8}
=
1.25S
$$

Speed increases by:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 36. Inverse Proportion With Fractions

Suppose:

6 workers take 15 days.

How many days for 10 workers?

$$
6\times15=10x
$$

$$
x=9
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{9\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 37. Inverse Proportion With Decimals

A machine takes 12.5 hours to complete a job.

If the number of machines doubles:

$$
12.5/2
=
\boxed{6.25\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 38. Inverse Proportion With Ratios

If:

$$
A:B=2:5
$$

and `A` and `B` are inversely proportional to another quantity, reverse the corresponding ratio when appropriate.

For inverse quantities:

$$
\boxed{x_1:x_2=y_2:y_1}
$$

---

# 39. Direct vs Inverse Formula

### Direct

$$
x\propto y
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x_1}{x_2}
=
\frac{y_1}{y_2}
}
$$

### Inverse

$$
x\propto\frac1y
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x_1}{x_2}
=
\frac{y_2}{y_1}
}
$$

---

# 40. Graph

For inverse proportion:

$$
y=\frac{k}{x}
$$

The graph is a rectangular hyperbola.

> [!important]
> Direct proportion:

$$
y=kx
$$

Inverse proportion:

$$
y=\frac{k}{x}
$$

---

# 41. Constant Product

Example:

| Workers | Days |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 30 |
| 3 | 20 |
| 5 | 12 |
| 6 | 10 |
| 10 | 6 |

Check:

$$
2\times30=60
$$

$$
3\times20=60
$$

$$
5\times12=60
$$

$$
6\times10=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{WD=60}
$$

---

# 42. Finding Missing Value From Constant

Given:

$$
x\times y=120
$$

If:

$$
x=15
$$

then:

$$
15y=120
$$

$$
\boxed{y=8}
$$

---

# 43. Multiple Variables

Sometimes:

$$
z\propto\frac{x}{y}
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
z=k\frac{x}{y}
}
$$

Therefore:

- `z` is directly proportional to `x`
- `z` is inversely proportional to `y`

Example:

$$
z=\frac{6x}{y}
$$

If x doubles while y stays constant:

$$
z\text{ doubles}
$$

If y doubles while x stays constant:

$$
z\text{ halves}
$$

---

# 44. Work With Efficiency

For fixed work:

$$
Work=Efficiency\times Time
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Efficiency\propto\frac1{Time}
}
$$

If efficiency doubles, required time halves.

---

# 45. Example — Efficiency

A worker completes a job in 12 days.

Another worker is twice as efficient.

Time required:

$$
12/2
=
\boxed{6\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 46. Efficiency Ratio

If:

$$
E_1:E_2=3:5
$$

then for the same work:

$$
T_1:T_2=5:3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Time ratio is reciprocal of efficiency ratio}}
$$

---

# 47. Fuel and Distance — Important Condition

Fuel consumed and distance traveled can be directly proportional if fuel efficiency is fixed:

$$
\boxed{Fuel\propto Distance}
$$

But fuel consumption **per unit distance** and distance are not inverse quantities in the same sense.

Always identify the exact quantities.

---

# 48. Inverse Proportion in Electricity

For a fixed power requirement:

Some quantities may vary inversely, but only if the governing relationship supports it.

> [!warning]
> Do not assume inverse proportion simply because one quantity increases while another decreases. The **product or mathematical relationship must remain constant**.

---

# 49. Inverse Proportion Test

To determine whether two quantities are inversely proportional:

### Step 1

Multiply corresponding values.

### Step 2

Check whether the product is constant.

Example:

$$
(2,30),(3,20),(5,12)
$$

Products:

$$
60,60,60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Inverse proportion}}
$$

---

# 50. Direct Proportion Test

For comparison:

Divide corresponding values.

Example:

$$
(2,10),(4,20),(6,30)
$$

Ratios:

$$
5,5,5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Direct proportion}}
$$

---

# 51. Recognition Table

| Test | Direct | Inverse |
|---|---|---|
| Main relation | `x/y = k` | `xy = k` |
| Formula | `y = kx` | `y = k/x` |
| x doubles | y doubles | y halves |
| x triples | y triples | y becomes 1/3 |
| x ↑ | y ↑ | y ↓ |
| x ↓ | y ↓ | y ↑ |

# 52. Multi-Step Work Problem

12 workers working 8 hours/day complete a job in 15 days.

How many workers are needed if they work 10 hours/day and must finish in 12 days?

Fixed work:

$$
12\times8\times15
=
x\times10\times12
$$

$$
1440=120x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

So the same number of workers is sufficient because increased daily hours offset fewer days.

---

# 53. Workers Change and Days Change

8 workers complete a job in 24 days.

If workers increase by `25%`:

New workers:

$$
8\times1.25=10
$$

New days:

$$
8\times24=10x
$$

$$
x=19.2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{19.2\text{ days}}
$$

---

# 54. Speed Change

A journey takes 8 hours at 50 km/h.

If speed becomes 80 km/h:

$$
50\times8=80x
$$

$$
400=80x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=5\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 55. Inverse Proportion and Ratio

If:

$$
x_1:x_2=2:3
$$

then inverse quantity:

$$
y_1:y_2=3:2
$$

This is one of the fastest methods in exams.

---

# 56. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using direct formula

Wrong:

$$
\frac{x_1}{y_1}
=
\frac{x_2}{y_2}
$$

for an inverse problem.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{x_1y_1=x_2y_2}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting to Reverse the Ratio

If workers:

$$
4:6
$$

days are:

$$
\boxed{6:4}
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Assuming Every Opposite Movement Is Inverse

One increasing and another decreasing is not enough.

The product must be constant.

---

### Mistake 4 — Percentage Trap

20% increase in one does not mean 20% decrease in the other.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{16\tfrac23\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Conditions

For workers and days, efficiency and working hours must be considered.
