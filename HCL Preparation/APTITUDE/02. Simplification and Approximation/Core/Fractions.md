# Fractions

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> A **fraction** represents a part of a whole or the ratio of two quantities.
>
> A fraction is written as:
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{a}{b}}
> $$
>
> where:
>
> - `a` = **Numerator**
> - `b` = **Denominator**
> - $b\ne0$

Example:

$$
\frac35
$$

Here:

- Numerator = `3`
- Denominator = `5`

---

# 2. Meaning of a Fraction

The fraction:

$$
\frac35
$$

means:

$$
3\div5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab=a\div b
}
$$

provided:

$$
b\ne0
$$

---

# 3. Types of Fractions

## Proper Fraction

Numerator is smaller than denominator.

$$
\boxed{
a<b
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac37
$$

---

## Improper Fraction

Numerator is greater than or equal to denominator.

$$
\boxed{
a\ge b
}
$$

Examples:

$$
\frac73
$$

$$
\frac55
$$

---

## Mixed Fraction

A whole number combined with a proper fraction.

Example:

$$
2\frac13
$$

---

## Unit Fraction

Numerator is `1`.

$$
\boxed{
\frac1n
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac17
$$

---

# 4. Proper Fraction Value

For a positive proper fraction:

$$
a<b
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{
0<\frac ab<1
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac37<1
$$

---

# 5. Improper Fraction Value

For a positive improper fraction:

$$
a\ge b
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\ge1
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac73>1
$$

---

# 6. Equivalent Fractions

Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number does not change the value.

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab=\frac{ak}{bk}
}
$$

where:

$$
k\ne0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac23
=
\frac{2\times4}{3\times4}
=
\frac8{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac23=\frac8{12}
}
$$

---

# 7. Simplest Form

A fraction is in simplest form when numerator and denominator have no common factor other than `1`.

Example:

$$
\frac{18}{24}
$$

HCF:

$$
\gcd(18,24)=6
$$

Divide both by `6`:

$$
\frac{18}{24}
=
\frac36
=
\boxed{\frac34}
$$

---

# 8. Simplifying a Fraction

### Method

1. Find HCF of numerator and denominator.
2. Divide both by the HCF.
3. Stop when HCF becomes `1`.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab
=
\frac{a\div\gcd(a,b)}
{b\div\gcd(a,b)}
}
$$

---

# 9. Mixed Fraction to Improper Fraction

For:

$$
n\frac ab
$$

multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator.

$$
\boxed{
n\frac ab
=
\frac{nb+a}{b}
}
$$

### Example

Convert:

$$
2\frac35
$$

$$
=\frac{2(5)+3}{5}
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac{13}{5}}
$$

---

# 10. Improper Fraction to Mixed Fraction

Divide numerator by denominator.

Example:

$$
\frac{17}{5}
$$

Division:

$$
17\div5=3\text{ remainder }2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{17}{5}=3\frac25
}
$$

---

# 11. Comparing Fractions

There are several methods.

### Method 1 — Same denominator

Compare numerators.

Example:

$$
\frac37
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac57
$$

Since:

$$
3<5
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
\frac37<\frac57
}
$$

---

# 12. Comparing Same Numerators

If numerators are equal, the fraction with the smaller positive denominator is larger.

Example:

$$
\frac35
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac38
$$

Since:

$$
5<8
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
\frac35>\frac38
}
$$

---

# 13. Cross Multiplication

For positive fractions:

$$
\frac ab
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac cd
$$

compare:

$$
ad
$$

and:

$$
bc
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab>\frac cd
\iff
ad>bc
}
$$

when denominators are positive.

---

# 14. Example — Cross Multiplication

Compare:

$$
\frac57
$$

and:

$$
\frac34
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
5\times4=20
$$

$$
3\times7=21
$$

Since:

$$
20<21
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac57<\frac34
}
$$

---

# 15. Addition of Fractions

If denominators are the same:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab+\frac cb
=
\frac{a+c}{b}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac25+\frac15
=
\frac35
$$

---

# 16. Subtraction of Fractions

If denominators are the same:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab-\frac cb
=
\frac{a-c}{b}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac45-\frac15
=
\frac35
$$

---

# 17. Addition With Different Denominators

For:

$$
\frac ab+\frac cd
$$

use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{ad+bc}{bd}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac23+\frac14
$$

$$
=
\frac{2(4)+1(3)}{12}
$$

$$
=
\frac{11}{12}
$$

---

# 18. Subtraction With Different Denominators

For:

$$
\frac ab-\frac cd
$$

use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{ad-bc}{bd}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac34-\frac15
$$

$$
=
\frac{3(5)-1(4)}{20}
$$

$$
=
\boxed{\frac{11}{20}}
$$

---

# 19. LCM Method

For multiple fractions, using the LCM of denominators is often faster.

Example:

$$
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16
$$

LCM:

$$
\operatorname{LCM}(2,3,6)=6
$$

Convert:

$$
\frac36+\frac26+\frac16
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed1
$$

---

# 20. Fraction Multiplication

To multiply fractions:

> Multiply numerators together and denominators together.

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times\frac cd
=
\frac{ac}{bd}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac23\times\frac45
=
\frac8{15}
$$

---

# 21. Cross-Cancellation

Before multiplying, cancel common factors.

Example:

$$
\frac{6}{7}\times\frac{14}{15}
$$

Cancel:

$$
14\div7=2
$$

$$
6\div3=2
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{2\times2}{5}
=
\boxed{\frac45}
$$

This is faster than multiplying first.

---

# 22. Important Shortcut — Cancellation

> [!tip]
> **Always check for common factors before multiplying fractions.**

Example:

$$
\frac{15}{28}\times\frac{14}{25}
$$

Cancel:

$$
15\div5=3
$$

$$
25\div5=5
$$

$$
14\div14=1
$$

$$
28\div14=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac3{10}}
$$

---

# 23. Division of Fractions

To divide by a fraction:

> **Multiply by its reciprocal.**

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\div\frac cd
=
\frac ab\times\frac dc
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\div\frac cd
=
\frac{ad}{bc}
}
$$

---

# 24. Example — Fraction Division

$$
\frac35\div\frac27
$$

Invert the second fraction:

$$
\frac35\times\frac72
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{21}{10}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{2\frac1{10}}
$$

---

# 25. Reciprocal

The reciprocal of:

$$
\frac ab
$$

is:

$$
\boxed{\frac ba}
$$

provided:

$$
a\ne0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac37
\rightarrow
\boxed{\frac73}
$$

---

# 26. Reciprocal of an Integer

An integer `n` can be written as:

$$
\frac n1
$$

Therefore its reciprocal is:

$$
\boxed{\frac1n}
$$

Example:

$$
8
\rightarrow
\boxed{\frac18}
$$

---

# 27. Important Reciprocal Pattern

For non-zero `x`:

$$
\boxed{
x\times\frac1x=1
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Reciprocal of reciprocal}=x
}
$$

---

# 28. Fraction of a Number

To find a fraction of a number:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\text{ of }N
=
\frac ab\times N
}
$$

### Example

Find:

$$
\frac35\text{ of }100
$$

$$
=\frac35\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{60}
$$

---

# 29. Shortcut — Fraction of a Number

For:

$$
\frac ab\times N
$$

try dividing first:

$$
N\div b
$$

then multiply by:

$$
a
$$

Example:

$$
\frac7{12}\times48
$$

First:

$$
48\div12=4
$$

Then:

$$
4\times7=28
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{28}
$$

---

# 30. Fraction as Percentage

To convert:

$$
\frac ab
$$

to percentage:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac34\times100\%
=
\boxed{75\%}
$$

---

# 31. Percentage as Fraction

To convert percentage to fraction:

$$
\boxed{
x\%=\frac{x}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
40\%
=
\frac{40}{100}
=
\boxed{\frac25}
$$

---

# 32. Fraction to Decimal

Divide numerator by denominator.

Example:

$$
\frac14
=
\boxed{0.25}
$$

Important common conversions:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\frac12\) | `0.5` | `50%` |
| \(\frac14\) | `0.25` | `25%` |
| \(\frac34\) | `0.75` | `75%` |
| \(\frac15\) | `0.2` | `20%` |
| \(\frac25\) | `0.4` | `40%` |
| \(\frac35\) | `0.6` | `60%` |
| \(\frac45\) | `0.8` | `80%` |
| \(\frac18\) | `0.125` | `12.5%` |

---

# 33. Fraction to Decimal — Termination

A fraction in lowest terms has a terminating decimal if its denominator is of the form:

$$
\boxed{
2^a5^b
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac38
$$

Since:

$$
8=2^3
$$

it terminates:

$$
\frac38=0.375
$$

---

# 34. Fraction to Decimal — Recurring

If the denominator in lowest terms contains any prime factor other than `2` or `5`, the decimal repeats.

Example:

$$
\frac13
=
0.\overline3
$$

because:

$$
3
$$

is not a factor of:

$$
2^a5^b
$$

---

# 35. Fraction Between Two Numbers

Suppose:

$$
\frac ab
$$

and we want to determine whether it lies between `m` and `n`.

Multiply carefully by the positive denominator:

$$
m<\frac ab<n
$$

is equivalent to:

$$
\boxed{
mb<a<nb
}
$$

when:

$$
b>0
$$

---

# 36. Fraction Simplification in BODMAS

Fractions can appear inside larger expressions.

Example:

$$
\frac23+\frac14\times\frac85
$$

Multiplication first:

$$
\frac14\times\frac85
=
\frac25
$$

Then:

$$
\frac23+\frac25
$$

LCM:

$$
15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{10}{15}+\frac6{15}
=
\boxed{\frac{16}{15}}
$$

---

# 37. Complex Fractions

A fraction whose numerator or denominator contains another fraction is called a complex fraction.

Example:

$$
\frac{\frac23}{\frac45}
$$

Convert division:

$$
\frac23\div\frac45
$$

$$
=\frac23\times\frac54
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac56}
$$

---

# 38. Shortcut for Complex Fractions

For:

$$
\frac{\frac ab}{\frac cd}
$$

directly use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{ad}{bc}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{\frac35}{\frac79}
$$

$$
=
\frac{3\times9}{5\times7}
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac{27}{35}}
$$

---

# 39. Compound Fraction

Consider:

$$
\frac{1}{1+\frac12}
$$

Simplify denominator:

$$
1+\frac12
=
\frac32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac1{\frac32}
=
\frac23
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\frac23}
$$

---

# 40. Fraction Comparison Shortcut

For positive fractions close to `1`, compare their differences from `1`.

Example:

Compare:

$$
\frac{99}{100}
$$

and:

$$
\frac{98}{99}
$$

First:

$$
1-\frac{99}{100}=\frac1{100}
$$

Second:

$$
1-\frac{98}{99}=\frac1{99}
$$

Since:

$$
\frac1{100}<\frac1{99}
$$

the first fraction is closer to `1`.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{99}{100}>\frac{98}{99}
}
$$

---

# 41. Important Pattern — Fraction Near 1

For:

$$
\frac{n-1}{n}
$$

we can write:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{n-1}{n}=1-\frac1n
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{99}{100}
=
1-\frac1{100}
$$

This is useful for comparison and approximation.

---

# 42. Important Pattern — Fraction Near 0

For a small numerator:

$$
\frac1n
$$

the fraction is close to zero when `n` is large.

Example:

$$
\frac1{1000}=0.001
$$

This is useful in approximation questions.

---

# 43. Difference Between Two Fractions

For:

$$
\frac ab-\frac cd
$$

the general formula is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{ad-bc}{bd}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac35-\frac14
$$

$$
=
\frac{12-5}{20}
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac7{20}}
$$

---

# 44. Sum of Two Fractions

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab+\frac cd
=
\frac{ad+bc}{bd}
}
$$

Remember:

> **Cross multiply, then add for addition.**

---

# 45. Product of Fractions

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times\frac cd
=
\frac{ac}{bd}
}
$$

Remember:

> **Top × top, bottom × bottom.**

---

# 46. Division of Fractions

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\div\frac cd
=
\frac{ad}{bc}
}
$$

Remember:

> **Keep the first, change division to multiplication, flip the second.**

---

# 47. Fraction of a Fraction

Example:

$$
\frac23\text{ of }\frac35
$$

"of" means multiplication:

$$
\frac23\times\frac35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac25}
$$

---

# 48. Important Word Pattern — "Of"

Whenever aptitude says:

> **`x` of `y`**

usually interpret it as:

$$
\boxed{x\times y}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac25\text{ of }50
$$

means:

$$
\frac25\times50=20
$$

---

# 49. Fractional Increase

If a quantity increases by:

$$
\frac ab
$$

of its original value, then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New value}
=
\text{Original}\left(1+\frac ab\right)
}
$$

Example:

Increase `100` by:

$$
\frac15
$$

New value:

$$
100\left(1+\frac15\right)
$$

$$
=100\times\frac65
$$

$$
\boxed{120}
$$

---

# 50. Fractional Decrease

If a quantity decreases by:

$$
\frac ab
$$

of its original value:

$$
\boxed{
\text{New value}
=
\text{Original}\left(1-\frac ab\right)
}
$$

Example:

Decrease `100` by:

$$
\frac14
$$

$$
100\left(1-\frac14\right)
$$

$$
=100\times\frac34
$$

$$
\boxed{75}
$$

---

# 51. Fractional Change

Change from original `O` to new value `N`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Fractional change}
=
\frac{N-O}{O}
}
$$

Percentage change:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{N-O}{O}\times100\%
}
$$

---

# 52. Ratio to Fraction

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Fraction of total belonging to A}
=
\frac{m}{m+n}
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Fraction of total belonging to B}
=
\frac{n}{m+n}
}
$$

### Example

Ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Fraction belonging to A:

$$
\frac25
$$

Fraction belonging to B:

$$
\frac35
$$

---

# 53. Fraction to Ratio

If:

$$
A=\frac23B
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
A:B=2:3
}
$$

The numerator and denominator can directly form the ratio after ensuring the quantities are represented consistently.

---

# 54. Important Fraction Identity

For non-zero `a` and `b`:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times\frac ba=1
}
$$

This is frequently useful for simplification.

---

# 55. Reciprocal Comparison

For positive numbers:

If:

$$
a>b>0
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1a<\frac1b
}
$$

The larger positive number has the smaller reciprocal.

---

# 56. Fraction Inequality

For positive `b`:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab>\frac cb
\iff a>c
}
$$

For the same positive denominator, compare numerators directly.

---

# 57. Important Warning — Negative Denominator

Usually, rewrite a fraction so that the denominator is positive.

Example:

$$
\frac{3}{-5}
=
\boxed{-\frac35}
$$

This makes comparison and simplification easier.

---

# 58. Sign of a Fraction

### Same signs

$$
\frac{+a}{+b}=+
$$

$$
\frac{-a}{-b}=+
$$

### Different signs

$$
\frac{+a}{-b}=-
$$

$$
\frac{-a}{+b}=-
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Same signs → positive}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Different signs → negative}
}
$$

---

# 59. Zero Fraction

If:

$$
a=0
$$

and:

$$
b\ne0
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\frac0b=0
}
$$

But:

$$
\boxed{
\frac b0
}
$$

is undefined.

---

# 60. Fraction Multiplication Shortcut

For:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}
$$

look for:

- `a` with `d`
- `c` with `b`

and cancel common factors before multiplying.

This reduces calculation time and prevents large intermediate numbers.

---

# 61. Example — Fast Cancellation

Calculate:

$$
\frac{18}{35}\times\frac{70}{27}
$$

Cancel:

$$
70\div35=2
$$

$$
18\div9=2
$$

$$
27\div9=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{2\times2}{3}
=
\boxed{\frac43}
$$

---

# 62. Fraction Addition Shortcut

When denominators are related, avoid unnecessary LCM calculations.

Example:

$$
\frac18+\frac38
$$

Same denominator:

$$
\frac48
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac12}
$$

---

# 63. Denominator Relationship

If one denominator is a multiple of another, use the larger denominator.

Example:

$$
\frac13+\frac1{12}
$$

Since:

$$
12=3\times4
$$

convert:

$$
\frac13=\frac4{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac4{12}+\frac1{12}
=
\boxed{\frac5{12}}
$$

---

# 64. Common Aptitude Pattern — Remaining Fraction

If:

$$
\frac25
$$

of a quantity is used, the remaining fraction is:

$$
1-\frac25
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac35}
$$

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Remaining fraction}=1-\text{Used fraction}
}
$$

---

# 65. Example

A student completes:

$$
\frac37
$$

of a task.

Remaining:

$$
1-\frac37
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac47}
$$

---

# 66. Fraction of Remaining Quantity

Be careful when a question says:

> `1/3` of the **remaining** quantity.

The denominator is based on the new remaining quantity, not the original quantity.

Always identify the reference quantity first.

---

# 67. Common Traps

> [!warning] Must Avoid

- ❌ Adding numerators and denominators directly:
  $$\frac ab+\frac cd\ne\frac{a+c}{b+d}$$
- ❌ Multiplying fractions without checking cancellation.
- ❌ Forgetting to flip the second fraction during division.
- ❌ Treating "of" as addition instead of multiplication.
- ❌ Comparing fractions only by numerator.
- ❌ Comparing fractions only by denominator.
- ❌ Forgetting denominator cannot be zero.
- ❌ Forgetting to simplify the final answer.
- ❌ Confusing mixed fractions with multiplication.
- ❌ Using the original quantity when the question asks for a fraction of the remaining quantity.

# 69. High-Yield Patterns

> [!important] Must Master

### Pattern 1 — Addition
