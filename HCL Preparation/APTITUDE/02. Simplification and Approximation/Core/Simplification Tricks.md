# Simplification Tricks

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> **Simplification** means reducing a mathematical expression to its simplest form using arithmetic properties, identities, fractions, percentages, powers, roots, and cancellation.
>
> In aptitude, the objective is not just to calculate correctly but to calculate **quickly and efficiently**.

The main strategy is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Observe the pattern before calculating}
}
$$

---

# 2. Golden Simplification Strategy

Use this order:

$$
\boxed{
\text{BODMAS}
\rightarrow
\text{Cancel}
\rightarrow
\text{Factor}
\rightarrow
\text{Convert}
\rightarrow
\text{Calculate}
}
$$

Before doing long calculations, ask:

1. Can I cancel something?
2. Can I factor something?
3. Can I convert a decimal to a fraction?
4. Can I use a known identity?
5. Can I approximate?
6. Can I combine terms?

---

# 3. BODMAS

The standard order is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Brackets}
\rightarrow
\text{Orders}
\rightarrow
\text{Division}
\rightarrow
\text{Multiplication}
\rightarrow
\text{Addition}
\rightarrow
\text{Subtraction}
}
$$

Where:

- **B** = Brackets
- **O** = Orders / powers / roots
- **D** = Division
- **M** = Multiplication
- **A** = Addition
- **S** = Subtraction

---

# 4. Important BODMAS Rule

Multiplication and division have the **same priority**.

Addition and subtraction also have the **same priority**.

When operations have equal priority, evaluate:

$$
\boxed{\text{Left to right}}
$$

Example:

$$
20\div5\times2
$$

Do division first because it appears first:

$$
20\div5=4
$$

Then:

$$
4\times2
=
\boxed8
$$

---

# 5. Cancellation Before Multiplication

Example:

$$
\frac{25\times16}{8}
$$

Instead of:

$$
25\times16=400
$$

then:

$$
400\div8
$$

cancel first:

$$
\frac{16}{8}=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
25\times2
=
\boxed{50}
$$

> [!tip]
> **Cancel first whenever possible.**

---

# 6. Cross Cancellation

Example:

$$
\frac{15\times28}{21}
$$

Cancel:

$$
\frac{15}{21}=\frac57
$$

Then:

$$
\frac{5\times28}{7}
$$

Cancel:

$$
28\div7=4
$$

Therefore:

$$
5\times4
=
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 7. Fraction Simplification

Always look for a common factor.

Example:

$$
\frac{84}{126}
$$

Divide by `42`:

$$
\frac{84}{126}
=
\boxed{\frac23}
$$

---

# 8. HCF Cancellation

If:

$$
\frac{a\times c}{b\times c}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a\times c}{b\times c}
=
\frac ab
}
$$

provided:

$$
c\ne0
$$

This is one of the fastest simplification techniques.

# 36. Fast Base-100 Method

For:

$$
(100-a)(100-b)
$$

calculate:

### Step 1

Subtract crosswise:

$$
100-a-b
$$

### Step 2

Multiply deficits:

$$
ab
$$

For:

$$
98\times97
$$

crosswise:

$$
100-2-3=95
$$

deficit product:

$$
2\times3=6
$$

Since the base is `100`, write:

$$
\boxed{9506}
$$

# 46. Successive Percentage Shortcut

For two successive percentage changes `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net change}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Use signs:

- increase → positive
- decrease → negative

Example:

Increase by `20%`, then increase by `10%`:

$$
20+10+\frac{20\times10}{100}
$$

$$
=32\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{32\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 47. Increase Followed by Decrease

Example:

Increase by `20%`, then decrease by `20%`.

Use:

$$
20+(-20)+\frac{20(-20)}{100}
$$

$$
=-4\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

> [!warning]
> A `20%` increase followed by a `20%` decrease does **not** return to the original value.

---

# 48. Reciprocal Percentage Trick

If a quantity increases by `x%`, the percentage decrease required to return to the original value is:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100\%
}
$$

Example:

Increase by `25%`.

Required decrease:

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 81. Formula Sheet

### Difference of Squares

$$

### Square of Sum

$$
\boxed{
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2

### Square of Difference

$$
\boxed{
(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
}
$$

### Sum of Cubes

$$
\boxed{
a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)
}
$$

### Difference of Cubes

$$

$$

a(b+c)=ab+ac
}

}
$$
