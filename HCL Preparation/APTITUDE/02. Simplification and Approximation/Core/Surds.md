# Surds

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> A **surd** is an irrational root that cannot be simplified into a rational number.
>
> Examples:
>
> $$
> \sqrt2,\quad \sqrt3,\quad \sqrt5
> $$
>
> These cannot be expressed as exact rational numbers.

A square root such as:

$$
\sqrt4=2
$$

is **not** a surd because its value is rational.

---

# 2. Rational vs Irrational Roots

### Rational Root

$$
\sqrt4=2
$$

$$
\sqrt9=3
$$

$$
\sqrt{25}=5
$$

These are rational.

### Surds

$$
\sqrt2
$$

$$
\sqrt3
$$

$$
\sqrt7
$$

These are irrational.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Root of a non-perfect square can be a surd}
}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Surd Form

A simple surd is generally written as:

$$
\boxed{
a\sqrt b
}
$$

where `a` and `b` are rational numbers and `b` is not a perfect square after simplification.

Example:

$$
3\sqrt5
$$

# 5. Simplifying Square Roots

Look for perfect-square factors.

Example:

$$
\sqrt{12}
$$

Factor:

$$
12=4\times3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{12}
=
\sqrt4\sqrt3
$$

$$
=\boxed{2\sqrt3}
$$

---

# 6. General Square Root Rule

For positive numbers:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b
}
$$

provided the expressions are defined appropriately.

This is one of the most important surd rules.

---

# 7. Example

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{72}
$$

Factor:

$$
72=36\times2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{72}
=
\sqrt{36}\sqrt2
$$

$$
=\boxed{6\sqrt2}
$$

---

# 8. Example

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{180}
$$

Factor:

$$
180=36\times5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{180}
=
6\sqrt5
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{6\sqrt5}
$$

---

# 9. Prime Factorization Method

Another reliable method is prime factorization.

Example:

$$
\sqrt{72}
$$

Prime factorization:

$$
72=2^3\times3^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{72}
=
\sqrt{2^3\times3^2}
$$

Take pairs outside:

$$
=2\times3\sqrt2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{6\sqrt2}
$$

---

# 10. Pair Method

For square roots:

> [!tip]
> **Every pair of identical prime factors contributes one factor outside the root.**

Example:

$$
\sqrt{2^4\times3^2}
$$

Pairs:

- `2²` → `2²` outside
- `3²` → `3` outside

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{2^4\times3^2}=12
}
$$

---

# 11. Simplifying Cube Roots

For cube roots:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt[3]{ab}
=
\sqrt[3]a\sqrt[3]b
}
$$

For every group of **three identical prime factors**, one factor comes outside.

Example:

$$
\sqrt[3]{54}
$$

Factor:

$$
54=27\times2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt[3]{54}
=
\sqrt[3]{27}\sqrt[3]2
$$

$$
=\boxed{3\sqrt[3]2}
$$

---

# 12. Addition of Surds

Only **like surds** can be directly added.

Example:

$$
2\sqrt3+5\sqrt3
$$

Add coefficients:

$$
\boxed{7\sqrt3}
$$

---

# 13. Like Surds

Surds are like surds when their radical parts are identical after simplification.

Example:

$$
3\sqrt5
\quad\text{and}\quad
7\sqrt5
$$

are like surds.

Therefore:

$$
3\sqrt5+7\sqrt5
=
\boxed{10\sqrt5}
$$

---

# 14. Unlike Surds

Consider:

$$
2\sqrt3+5\sqrt2
$$

The radical parts differ.

Therefore they cannot be combined directly:

$$
\boxed{
2\sqrt3+5\sqrt2
}
$$

is already simplified.

---

# 15. Important Trap

Consider:

$$
\sqrt2+\sqrt3
$$

It is **not**:

$$
\sqrt5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt a+\sqrt b\ne\sqrt{a+b}
}
$$

in general.

This is a very important aptitude trap.

---

# 16. Subtraction of Surds

Like surds can be subtracted.

Example:

$$
8\sqrt7-3\sqrt7
$$

$$
=\boxed{5\sqrt7}
$$

---

# 17. Multiplication of Surds

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\sqrt3\times\sqrt5
$$

$$
=\sqrt{15}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{15}}
$$

---

# 18. Example

Calculate:

$$
2\sqrt3\times4\sqrt5
$$

Multiply coefficients:

$$
2\times4=8
$$

Multiply surds:

$$
\sqrt3\sqrt5=\sqrt{15}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\sqrt{15}}
$$

---

# 19. Multiplying Like Surds

Example:

$$
3\sqrt5\times2\sqrt5
$$

$$
=6(\sqrt5)^2
$$

Since:

$$
(\sqrt5)^2=5
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 20. Square of a Surd

For:

$$
a\sqrt b
$$

square it:

$$
(a\sqrt b)^2
$$

$$
=a^2b
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
(a\sqrt b)^2=a^2b
}
$$

Example:

$$
(3\sqrt2)^2
=
9\times2
=
\boxed{18}
$$

---

# 21. Product of Conjugates

A very important identity:

$$
\boxed{
(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)=a^2-b
}
$$

This is based on:

$$
(x+y)(x-y)=x^2-y^2
$$

---

# 22. Example — Conjugates

Calculate:

$$
(3+\sqrt5)(3-\sqrt5)
$$

Using the identity:

$$
3^2-(\sqrt5)^2
$$

$$
=9-5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed4
$$

---

# 23. Surd Conjugates

The conjugate of:

$$
a+\sqrt b
$$

is:

$$
\boxed{
a-\sqrt b
}
$$

Similarly, the conjugate of:

$$
a-\sqrt b
$$

is:

$$
\boxed{
a+\sqrt b
}
$$

---

# 24. Rationalization

> [!summary] Definition
> **Rationalization** means removing a surd from the denominator of a fraction.

Example:

$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}
$$

The denominator contains a surd.

Multiply numerator and denominator by:

$$
\sqrt2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\times
\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}
$$

$$
=
\frac{\sqrt2}{2}
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}
}
$$

---

# 25. Rationalization of `1 / √a`

General formula:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{\sqrt a}
=
\frac{\sqrt a}{a}
}
$$

provided:

$$
a>0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac1{\sqrt5}
=
\boxed{\frac{\sqrt5}{5}}
$$

---

# 26. Rationalizing `a / √b`

For:

$$
\frac a{\sqrt b}
$$

multiply by:

$$
\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt b}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac a{\sqrt b}
=
\frac{a\sqrt b}{b}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac3{\sqrt5}
=
\boxed{\frac{3\sqrt5}{5}}
$$

---

# 27. Rationalizing a Binomial Surd

Consider:

$$
\frac1{a+\sqrt b}
$$

Multiply by the conjugate:

$$
a-\sqrt b
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac1{a+\sqrt b}
\times
\frac{a-\sqrt b}{a-\sqrt b}
$$

Denominator:

$$
(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)
$$

$$
=a^2-b
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{a+\sqrt b}
=
\frac{a-\sqrt b}{a^2-b}
}
$$

---

# 28. Example

Rationalize:

$$
\frac1{3+\sqrt2}
$$

Multiply by conjugate:

$$
\frac{3-\sqrt2}{3-\sqrt2}
$$

Denominator:

$$
9-2=7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{3-\sqrt2}{7}
}
$$

---

# 29. Rationalizing `1 / (a - √b)`

Use the conjugate:

$$
a+\sqrt b
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{a-\sqrt b}
=
\frac{a+\sqrt b}{a^2-b}
}
$$

---

# 30. Important Rationalization Pattern

> [!important]

### One Surd in denominator

Multiply by the same surd.

$$
\frac1{\sqrt a}
\rightarrow
\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt a}
$$

### Two terms in denominator

Multiply by the conjugate.

$$
\frac1{a+\sqrt b}
\rightarrow
\frac{a-\sqrt b}{a-\sqrt b}
$$

---

# 31. Surd Addition After Simplification

Always simplify the surds **before** deciding whether they are like.

Example:

$$
\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{27}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3
$$

$$
\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3
$$

Therefore:

$$
2\sqrt3+3\sqrt3
=
\boxed{5\sqrt3}
$$

---

# 32. Important Pattern

> [!tip]
> **Simplify first → identify like surds → combine.**

Do not compare the original numbers under the radical before simplifying.

---

# 33. Example

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{50}+\sqrt8
$$

First:

$$
\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt2
$$

and:

$$
\sqrt8=2\sqrt2
$$

Therefore:

$$
5\sqrt2+2\sqrt2
=
\boxed{7\sqrt2}
$$

---

# 34. Subtraction After Simplification

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{75}-\sqrt{12}
$$

First:

$$
\sqrt{75}=5\sqrt3
$$

$$
\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3
$$

Therefore:

$$
5\sqrt3-2\sqrt3
=
\boxed{3\sqrt3}
$$

---

# 35. Nested Surds

Expressions may contain roots inside roots.

Example:

$$
\sqrt{5+2\sqrt6}
$$

Some expressions can be represented as:

$$
\sqrt a+\sqrt b
$$

because:

$$
(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2
=
a+b+2\sqrt{ab}
$$

Therefore, compare:

$$
a+b
$$

and:

$$
ab
$$

with the expression.

---

# 36. Important Nested Surd Pattern

If:

$$
\sqrt{a+b+2\sqrt{ab}}
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b+2\sqrt{ab}}
=
\sqrt a+\sqrt b
}
$$

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b-2\sqrt{ab}}
=
|\sqrt a-\sqrt b|
}
$$

---

# 37. Example — Nested Surd

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{7+4\sqrt3}
$$

We want:

$$
\sqrt a+\sqrt b
$$

such that:

$$
a+b=7
$$

and:

$$
2\sqrt{ab}=4\sqrt3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{ab}=2\sqrt3
$$

so:

$$
ab=12
$$

Numbers satisfying:

$$
a+b=7
$$

and:

$$
ab=12
$$

are:

$$
a=3,\quad b=4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{7+4\sqrt3}
=
\sqrt3+\sqrt4
$$

$$
=\boxed{2+\sqrt3}
$$

---

# 38. Surd Inequality

For positive numbers, the square-root function preserves order:

If:

$$
a>b\ge0
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt a>\sqrt b
}
$$

Example:

$$
\sqrt7>\sqrt5
$$

---

# 39. Comparing Surds

To compare positive square roots:

$$
\sqrt a
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sqrt b
$$

compare:

$$
a
$$

and:

$$
b
$$

Example:

$$
\sqrt{11}>\sqrt9=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{11}>3}
$$

---

# 40. Comparing `a√b` and `c√d`

For positive quantities, square both sides if appropriate.

Example:

Compare:

$$
2\sqrt3
$$

and:

$$
3\sqrt2
$$

Square:

$$
(2\sqrt3)^2=12
$$

$$
(3\sqrt2)^2=18
$$

Since:

$$
12<18
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{
2\sqrt3<3\sqrt2
}
$$

---

# 41. Surd Rationality

A simplified expression such as:

$$
a\sqrt b
$$

is irrational when `a` is a non-zero rational number and `b` is a positive integer that is not a perfect square.

Example:

$$
3\sqrt2
$$

is irrational.

---

# 42. Important Surd Identities

Memorize:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}
=
\sqrt{\frac ab}
}
$$

when defined.

$$
\boxed{
(\sqrt a)^2=a
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
(a\sqrt b)^2=a^2b
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2
}
$$

---

# 43. Important Non-Identities

These are generally false:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b}\ne\sqrt a+\sqrt b
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a-b}\ne\sqrt a-\sqrt b
}
$$

in general.

Also:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b}\ne\sqrt{ab}
}
$$

Do not split a square root across addition or subtraction.

---

# 44. Surd Powers

Important:

$$
(\sqrt a)^2=a
$$

and:

$$
(\sqrt a)^3
=
a\sqrt a
$$

because:

$$
(\sqrt a)^3
=
(\sqrt a)^2\sqrt a
=
a\sqrt a
$$

---

# 45. Higher Roots

General exponent form:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt[n]{a}=a^{1/n}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt a=a^{1/2}
$$

and:

$$
\sqrt[3]a=a^{1/3}
$$

This connects directly to:

[[Indices and Exponents]]

---

# 46. Surd Multiplication Shortcut

For:

$$
a\sqrt b\times c\sqrt d
$$

use:

$$
\boxed{
ac\sqrt{bd}
}
$$

Then simplify the resulting surd.

Example:

$$
2\sqrt6\times3\sqrt{10}
$$

$$
=6\sqrt{60}
$$

Since:

$$
\sqrt{60}=2\sqrt{15}
$$

therefore:

$$
\boxed{12\sqrt{15}}
$$

---

# 47. Surd Division

For:

$$
\frac{a\sqrt b}{c\sqrt d}
$$

you can simplify coefficients and rationalize if necessary.

Example:

$$
\frac{6\sqrt5}{3\sqrt5}
$$

Cancel:

$$
\boxed2
$$

---

# 48. Example — Surd Division

Simplify:

$$
\frac{\sqrt{18}}{\sqrt2}
$$

Combine:

$$
\sqrt{\frac{18}{2}}
$$

$$
=\sqrt9
$$

$$
\boxed3
$$

---

# 49. Conjugate Shortcut

If you see:

$$
(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)
$$

immediately use:

$$
\boxed{a^2-b}
$$

Do not expand term by term.

Example:

$$
(5+\sqrt7)(5-\sqrt7)
$$

$$
=25-7
$$

$$
\boxed{18}
$$

---

# 50. Rationalization Shortcut

If denominator is:

$$
a+\sqrt b
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{a-\sqrt b}
$$

If denominator is:

$$
a-\sqrt b
$$

think:

$$
\boxed{a+\sqrt b}
$$

This is the conjugate pattern.

---

# 51. Surd and Approximation

Common approximate square roots:

$$
\sqrt2\approx1.414
$$

$$
\sqrt3\approx1.732
$$

$$
\sqrt5\approx2.236
$$

$$
\sqrt6\approx2.449
$$

$$
\sqrt7\approx2.646
$$

$$
\sqrt8\approx2.828
$$

$$
\sqrt{10}\approx3.162
$$

These are useful when exact surd form is not required.

---

# 52. Important Approximation Pattern

If:

$$
a^2<N<(a+1)^2
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
a<\sqrt N<a+1
}
$$

Example:

$$
25<30<36
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
5<\sqrt{30}<6
}
$$

---

# 53. Common Aptitude Pattern

If the answer choices contain:

- exact surd forms → keep the answer exact.
- decimal options → approximate.
- rational numbers → simplify/rationalize first.

Do not convert every surd into a decimal unnecessarily.

---

# 54. Common Traps

> [!warning] Must Avoid

- ❌ Assuming:
  $$\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$$
- ❌ Adding unlike surds.
- ❌ Forgetting to simplify the radical first.
- ❌ Rationalizing incorrectly.
- ❌ Forgetting the conjugate for binomial denominators.
- ❌ Treating a perfect-square root as a surd.
- ❌ Leaving an obvious perfect-square factor inside the root.
- ❌ Expanding conjugates unnecessarily.
- ❌ Comparing surds by looking only at coefficients.
- ❌ Forgetting absolute value in:
  $$\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$$

---

# 55. Important Identity — Square Root of a Square

For real `x`:

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{x^2}=|x|
}
$$

Not always:

$$
\sqrt{x^2}=x
$$

Example:

If:

$$
x=-5
$$

then:

$$
\sqrt{x^2}
=
\sqrt{25}
=
5
$$

and:

$$
|x|=5
$$

$$

### Conjugate

$$
\boxed{
(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)=a^2-b
}
$$

### Rationalization

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{\sqrt a}=\frac{\sqrt a}{a}
}
$$

### Nested Surd

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b+2\sqrt{ab}}
=
\sqrt a+\sqrt b
}
$$

### Important Warning

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{a+b}\ne\sqrt a+\sqrt b
}
$$

### Square Root of Square

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{x^2}=|x|
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Simplify the root → combine like surds → use conjugates for rationalization → never split a root across `+` or `−`.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **If a square root contains a perfect-square factor, pull it outside first; if a surd is in the denominator, rationalize it.**
