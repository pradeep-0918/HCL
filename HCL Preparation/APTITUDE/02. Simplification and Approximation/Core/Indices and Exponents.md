# Indices and Exponents

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary] Definition
> An **exponent** tells us how many times a number is multiplied by itself.
>
> In:
>
> $$
> a^n
> $$
>
> - `a` = **base**
> - `n` = **exponent / index / power**
>
> Example:
>
> $$
> 2^4=2\times2\times2\times2=16
> $$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2^4=16}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Terminology

For:

$$
5^3
$$

we have:

- Base = `5`
- Exponent = `3`
- Power = `3`
- Value = `125`

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5^3=125}
$$

---

# 3. Repeated Multiplication

The fundamental meaning is:

$$
\boxed{
a^n=
\underbrace{a\times a\times a\times\cdots\times a}_{n\text{ times}}
}
$$

Example:

$$
3^5
=
3\times3\times3\times3\times3
$$

$$
=\boxed{243}
$$

---

# 4. Exponent `1`

Any non-zero number raised to power `1` remains unchanged.

$$
\boxed{
a^1=a
}
$$

Examples:

$$
7^1=7
$$

$$
100^1=100
$$

---

# 5. Exponent `0`

For:

$$
a\ne0
$$

we have:

$$
\boxed{
a^0=1
}
$$

Examples:

$$
5^0=1
$$

$$
100^0=1
$$

$$
(-7)^0=1
$$

> [!warning]
> `0^0` is not treated as an ordinary defined value in basic aptitude mathematics.

---

# 6. Multiplication — Same Base

When multiplying powers with the same base, **add the exponents**.

$$
\boxed{
a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
2^3\times2^4
=
2^{3+4}
=
\boxed{2^7}
$$

---

# 7. Example

Simplify:

$$
5^2\times5^3
$$

Add exponents:

$$
5^{2+3}
$$

$$
=\boxed{5^5}
$$

---

# 8. Division — Same Base

When dividing powers with the same non-zero base, **subtract the exponents**.

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{2^7}{2^3}
=
2^{7-3}
=
\boxed{2^4}
$$

---

# 9. Negative Exponents

A negative exponent means reciprocal.

$$
\boxed{
a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}
}
$$

where:

$$
a\ne0
$$

Example:

$$
2^{-3}
=
\frac1{2^3}
=
\boxed{\frac18}
$$

---

# 10. Moving a Power Across a Fraction

A negative exponent can be moved from numerator to denominator.

$$
\boxed{
a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}
}
$$

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{
\frac1{a^{-n}}=a^n
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac1{3^{-2}}
=
\boxed9
$$

---

# 11. Power of a Power

When a power is raised to another power, **multiply the exponents**.

$$
\boxed{
(a^m)^n=a^{mn}
}
$$

Example:

$$
(2^3)^4
=
2^{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4096}
$$

---

# 12. Power of a Product

For:

$$
(ab)^n
$$

the exponent applies to both factors.

$$
\boxed{
(ab)^n=a^nb^n
}
$$

Example:

$$
(2\times3)^4
=
2^4\times3^4
$$

---

# 13. Power of a Quotient

For:

$$
\left(\frac ab\right)^n
$$

the exponent applies to numerator and denominator.

$$
\boxed{
\left(\frac ab\right)^n
=
\frac{a^n}{b^n}
}
$$

where:

$$
b\ne0
$$

Example:

$$
\left(\frac23\right)^3
=
\boxed{\frac8{27}}
$$

---

# 14. Fractional Exponents

A fractional exponent represents a root.

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}
}
$$

Examples:

$$
a^{1/2}=\sqrt a
$$

$$
a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
16^{1/2}=4
}
$$

---

# 15. Fractional Exponent — General Formula

The most important formula is:

$$
\boxed{
a^{m/n}
=
\sqrt[n]{a^m}
=
(\sqrt[n]a)^m
}
$$

Example:

$$
8^{2/3}
$$

First take cube root:

$$
\sqrt[3]8=2
$$

Then square:

$$
2^2
=
\boxed4
$$

---

# 16. Example

Simplify:

$$
27^{2/3}
$$

Cube root:

$$
\sqrt[3]{27}=3
$$

Square:

$$
3^2=9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed9
$$

---

# 17. Important Root-Power Connection

Remember:

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/2}=\sqrt a
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/4}=\sqrt[4]a
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]a
}
$$

This connects directly with:

[[Surds]]

---

# 18. Exponent of a Product

For suitable real quantities:

$$
\boxed{
(ab)^n=a^nb^n
}
$$

Example:

$$
(2\times5)^3
=
2^3\times5^3
$$

$$
=8\times125
$$

$$
=\boxed{1000}
$$

---

# 19. Exponent of a Quotient

$$
\boxed{
\left(\frac ab\right)^n
=
\frac{a^n}{b^n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\left(\frac35\right)^2
=
\boxed{\frac9{25}}
$$

---

# 20. Same Exponent — Different Bases

If:

$$
a^n\times b^n
$$

combine the bases:

$$
\boxed{
a^nb^n=(ab)^n
}
$$

Example:

$$
2^3\times5^3
=
(2\times5)^3
=
10^3
=
\boxed{1000}
$$

---

# 21. Same Exponent in Division

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a^n}{b^n}
=
\left(\frac ab\right)^n
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{10^3}{2^3}
=
\left(\frac{10}{2}\right)^3
=
5^3
=
\boxed{125}
$$

---

# 22. Important Pattern — Match the Base

When simplifying exponent expressions, try to express all numbers using a common base.

Example:

$$
8^2\times4^3
$$

Convert:

$$
8=2^3
$$

and:

$$
4=2^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
(2^3)^2\times(2^2)^3
$$

$$
=2^6\times2^6
$$

$$
=\boxed{2^{12}}
$$

---

# 23. Important Aptitude Pattern

> [!tip]
> **If bases are different, convert them into the same base before applying exponent rules.**

Common conversions:

$$
4=2^2
$$

$$
8=2^3
$$

$$
16=2^4
$$

$$
25=5^2
$$

$$
27=3^3
$$

$$
32=2^5
$$

$$
64=2^6
$$

$$
81=3^4
$$

$$
125=5^3
$$

$$
128=2^7
$$

$$
256=2^8
$$

$$
625=5^4
$$

---

# 24. Exponent `-1`

The exponent `-1` gives the reciprocal.

$$
\boxed{
a^{-1}=\frac1a
}
$$

Example:

$$
5^{-1}
=
\boxed{\frac15}
$$

---

# 25. Reciprocal Form

If:

$$
x=\frac ab
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
x^{-1}=\frac ba
}
$$

Example:

$$
\left(\frac23\right)^{-1}
=
\boxed{\frac32}
$$

---

# 26. Zero Base

For positive integer `n`:

$$
\boxed{
0^n=0
}
$$

Example:

$$
0^5=0
$$

But:

$$
0^0
$$

should not be treated as an ordinary defined expression.

---

# 27. Negative Base — Even Exponent

If the exponent is even:

$$
(-a)^{2n}>0
$$

Example:

$$
(-3)^2=9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Negative base + even exponent → positive}
}
$$

---

# 28. Negative Base — Odd Exponent

If the exponent is odd:

$$
(-a)^{2n+1}<0
$$

Example:

$$
(-3)^3=-27
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Negative base + odd exponent → negative}
}
$$

---

# 29. Important Sign Trap

Compare:

$$
-2^2
$$

and:

$$
(-2)^2
$$

The first means:

$$
-(2^2)=-4
$$

The second means:

$$
(-2)^2=4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
-2^2=-4
}
$$

but:

$$
\boxed{
(-2)^2=4
}
$$

Parentheses matter.

---

# 30. Exponent of 10

Powers of `10` are extremely useful in aptitude.

$$
10^1=10
$$

$$
10^2=100
$$

$$
10^3=1000
$$

$$
10^4=10000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
10^n=\text{1 followed by }n\text{ zeros}
}
$$

---

# 31. Negative Powers of 10

$$
10^{-1}=\frac1{10}=0.1
$$

$$
10^{-2}=\frac1{100}=0.01
$$

$$
10^{-3}=\frac1{1000}=0.001
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
10^{-n}=\frac1{10^n}
}
$$

---

# 32. Scientific Notation

A number can be represented as:

$$
\boxed{
a\times10^n
}
$$

where:

$$
1\le|a|<10
$$

Example:

$$
450000
=
4.5\times10^5
$$

---

# 33. Small Number in Scientific Notation

Example:

$$
0.00072
$$

Move decimal four places right:

$$
7.2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
0.00072=7.2\times10^{-4}
}
$$

---

# 34. Scientific Notation — Large Number

Example:

$$
7200000
$$

Move decimal six places left:

$$
7.2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
7200000=7.2\times10^6
}
$$

---

# 35. Scientific Notation Multiplication

For:

$$
(a\times10^m)(b\times10^n)
$$

multiply coefficients and add powers:

$$
\boxed{
ab\times10^{m+n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
(3\times10^4)(2\times10^3)
$$

$$
=6\times10^7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{60000000}
$$

---

# 36. Scientific Notation Division

For:

$$
\frac{a\times10^m}{b\times10^n}
$$

divide coefficients and subtract powers:

$$
\boxed{
\frac ab\times10^{m-n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{6\times10^8}{2\times10^3}
$$

$$
=3\times10^5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{300000}
$$

---

# 37. Power of Scientific Notation

$$
(a\times10^m)^n
$$

becomes:

$$
\boxed{
a^n\times10^{mn}
}
$$

Example:

$$
(2\times10^3)^2
$$

$$
=4\times10^6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4000000}
$$

---

# 38. Important Exponent Identity

For non-zero `a`:

$$
\boxed{
a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}
}
$$

For division:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}
}
$$

For powers:

$$
\boxed{
(a^m)^n=a^{mn}
}
$$

These three should be memorized first.

---

# 39. Important Pattern — Addition Trap

Exponent rules do **not** work like this:

$$
\boxed{
a^m+a^n\ne a^{m+n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
2^2+2^3
=
4+8
=
12
$$

but:

$$
2^{2+3}=32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
a^m+a^n
}
$$

must normally be calculated or factored differently.

---

# 40. Important Pattern — Subtraction Trap

Similarly:

$$
\boxed{
a^m-a^n\ne a^{m-n}
}
$$

Example:

$$
2^5-2^2
=
32-4
=
28
$$

but:

$$
2^{5-2}=8
$$

---

# 41. Factoring Powers

Sometimes addition/subtraction can be simplified by taking out the smaller power.

Example:

$$
2^5+2^3
$$

Take out:

$$
2^3
$$

$$
=2^3(2^2+1)
$$

$$
=8(5)
$$

$$
\boxed{40}
$$

---

# 42. Example

Simplify:

$$
3^5-3^3
$$

Take out:

$$
3^3
$$

$$
=3^3(3^2-1)
$$

$$
=27(8)
$$

$$
\boxed{216}
$$

---

# 43. Exponent Equation — Same Base

If:

$$
a^x=a^y
$$

and:

$$
a>0,\quad a\ne1
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{x=y}
$$

Example:

$$
2^{x+1}=2^5
$$

Therefore:

$$
x+1=5
$$

$$
\boxed{x=4}
$$

---

# 44. Different Bases

Convert to a common base.

Example:

$$
4^x=8^2
$$

Convert:

$$
4=2^2
$$

and:

$$
8=2^3
$$

Therefore:

$$
(2^2)^x=(2^3)^2
$$

$$
2^{2x}=2^6
$$

Hence:

$$
2x=6
$$

$$
\boxed{x=3}
$$

---

# 45. Exponential Fractions

Example:

$$
\frac{2^5}{2^2}
$$

Subtract exponents:

$$
2^{5-2}
$$

$$
=\boxed8
$$

---

# 46. Negative Exponent Simplification

Example:

$$
\frac{2^3}{2^5}
$$

$$
=2^{3-5}
$$

$$
=2^{-2}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac14}
$$

---

# 47. Important Pattern — Negative Difference

If:

$$
m<n
$$

then:

$$
a^{m-n}
=
a^{-(n-m)}
=
\boxed{\frac1{a^{n-m}}}
$$

Example:

$$
3^2\div3^5
=
3^{-3}
=
\boxed{\frac1{27}}
$$

---

# 48. Fractional Exponent Shortcut

When exponent is:

$$
\frac1n
$$

think:

> **nth root**

When exponent is:

$$
\frac mn
$$

think:

> **nth root, then power m**

Example:

$$
32^{2/5}
$$

Fifth root:

$$
\sqrt[5]{32}=2
$$

Square:

$$
2^2
=
\boxed4
$$

---

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a

$$

$$
\boxed{
a^{1/4}=\sqrt[4]a
}

$$
\boxed{

8^{-2/3}

$$
16^{3/4}
$$

$$
16=2^4
$$

$$
(2^4)^{3/4}
$$

81^{3/4}
$$

$$
81=3^4
$$

therefore:

$$
(3^4)^{3/4}

=

# 54. Common Aptitude Pattern — Same Power

If:

$$
a^n+b^n
$$

there is generally no direct exponent-combination rule.

But if:

$$
a^n
$$

and:

$$
b^n
$$

share a common factor, factor it.

Example:

$$
5^3+5^2
$$

$$
=5^2(5+1)
$$

$$
=25\times6
$$

$$
\boxed{150}
$$

# 55. Exponent Simplification in BODMAS

Follow the order:

1. Brackets
2. Powers / roots
3. Multiplication / division
4. Addition / subtraction

Example:

$$
2+3^2\times4
$$

Power first:

$$
3^2=9
$$

Then:

$$
2+9\times4
$$

$$
=2+36
$$

$$
\boxed{38}
$$

# 56. Common Traps

> [!warning] Must Avoid

- ❌ Adding exponents during addition.
- ❌ Multiplying exponents during ordinary multiplication.
- ❌ Forgetting that `(a^m)^n` multiplies exponents.
- ❌ Forgetting negative exponent means reciprocal.
- ❌ Forgetting parentheses around negative bases.
- ❌ Applying exponent only to part of a bracket.
- ❌ Confusing:
  $$a^0$$
  with `0`.
- ❌ Confusing:
  $$a^{-n}$$
  with `-a^n`.
- ❌ Using:
  $$\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$$
- ❌ Forgetting to convert different bases into a common base.

# 62. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Same Base — Multiply

$$
\boxed{
a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}
}
$$

### Same Base — Divide

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}
}
$$

### Power of Power

$$
\boxed{
(a^m)^n=a^{mn}
}
$$

### Zero Power

$$
\boxed{
a^0=1
}
$$

### Negative Power

$$
\boxed{
a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}
}
$$

### Fractional Power

$$
\boxed{
a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}
}
$$

### Same Exponent

$$
\boxed{
a^nb^n=(ab)^n
}
$$

### Critical Warning

$$
\boxed{
a^m+a^n\ne a^{m+n}
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Multiply → add powers. Divide → subtract powers. Power of power → multiply powers. Negative → reciprocal. Fractional → root.**

### One-Line Recognition

> **When solving exponent questions, first convert all bases to a common base; then apply the exponent laws.**
