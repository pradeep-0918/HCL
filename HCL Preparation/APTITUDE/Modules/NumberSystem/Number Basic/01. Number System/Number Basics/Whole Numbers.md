# Whole Numbers

## 1. What are Whole Numbers?

Whole numbers are **Natural Numbers including 0**.

Natural Numbers:

$$  
1,2,3,4,5,\ldots  
$$

Whole Numbers:

$$  
\boxed{0,1,2,3,4,5,\ldots}  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{W={0,1,2,3,\ldots}}  
$$

### Key Difference

|Type|Starts from|
|---|--:|
|Natural Numbers|1|
|Whole Numbers|0|

> ⭐ Remember: **Every natural number is a whole number, but 0 is not a natural number.**

---

## 2. Smallest Whole Number

The smallest whole number is:

$$  
\boxed{0}  
$$

There is **no largest whole number**.

Because for every whole number $n$:

$$  
n+1  
$$

is also a whole number.

---

# 3. Important Properties

## Closure Property

Whole numbers are closed under:

### Addition

If $a,b$ are whole numbers:

$$  
a+b  
$$

is also a whole number.

Example:

$$  
5+7=12  
$$

### Multiplication

$$  
a\times b  
$$

is also a whole number.

Example:

$$  
5\times7=35  
$$

### Subtraction

Whole numbers are **not always closed** under subtraction.

Example:

$$  
3-5=-2  
$$

$-2$ is not a whole number.

### Division

Whole numbers are **not always closed** under division.

Example:

$$  
5\div2=2.5  
$$

$2.5$ is not a whole number.

---

# 4. Identity Elements

## Additive Identity

Adding 0 does not change a number:

$$  
a+0=a  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{0\text{ is the additive identity}}  
$$

Example:

$$  
25+0=25  
$$

---

## Multiplicative Identity

Multiplying by 1 does not change a number:

$$  
a\times1=a  
$$

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{1\text{ is the multiplicative identity}}  
$$

Example:

$$  
25\times1=25  
$$

---

# 5. Multiplication by Zero

Any whole number multiplied by zero is zero:

$$  
\boxed{a\times0=0}  
$$

Example:

$$  
125\times0=0  
$$

> ⚠️ Don't confuse **multiplicative identity (1)** with **multiplication by zero**.

---

# 6. Commutative Property

The order does not matter.

### Addition

$$  
a+b=b+a  
$$

Example:

$$  
5+8=8+5  
$$

### Multiplication

$$  
a\times b=b\times a  
$$

Example:

$$  
5\times8=8\times5  
$$

### But subtraction and division are NOT commutative.

$$  
8-5\neq5-8  
$$

$$  
8\div4\neq4\div8  
$$

---

# 7. Associative Property

Grouping does not matter.

### Addition

$$  
(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)  
$$

Example:

$$  
(2+3)+4=2+(3+4)  
$$

### Multiplication

$$  
(a\times b)\times c=a\times(b\times c)  
$$

Example:

$$  
(2\times3)\times4=2\times(3\times4)  
$$

> ⭐ Subtraction and division are **not associative**.

---

# 8. Distributive Property

Multiplication distributes over addition:

$$  
\boxed{a(b+c)=ab+ac}  
$$

Example:

$$  
5(10+2)  
$$

Instead of calculating inside first:

$$  
5(10)+5(2)  
$$

$$  
=50+10  
$$

$$  
=60  
$$

This is useful for **mental calculation**.

---

# ⚡ Exam Shortcuts

### Shortcut 1 — Add Zero

$$  
n+0=n  
$$

### Shortcut 2 — Multiply by One

$$  
n\times1=n  
$$

### Shortcut 3 — Multiply by Zero

$$  
n\times0=0  
$$

### Shortcut 4 — Mental Multiplication

Use distributive property.

For example:

$$  
49\times11  
$$

Think:

$$  
49(10+1)  
$$

$$  
=490+49  
$$

$$  
=\boxed{539}  
$$
