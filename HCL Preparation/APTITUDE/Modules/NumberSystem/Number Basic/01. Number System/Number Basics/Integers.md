# Integers

## 1. What are Integers?

Integers are numbers that include:

- Positive numbers
    
- Negative numbers
    
- Zero
    

They are represented by:

$$  
\boxed{\mathbb{Z}}  
$$

The set of integers is:

$$  
\boxed{\mathbb{Z}={\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots}}  
$$

---

# 2. Types of Integers

### Positive Integers

$$  
1,2,3,4,5,\ldots  
$$

### Negative Integers

$$  
-1,-2,-3,-4,-5,\ldots  
$$

### Zero

$$  
0  
$$

> ⚠️ **0 is neither positive nor negative.**

---

# 3. Number Line

Think of integers on a number line:

$$  
\ldots\ -3\quad -2\quad -1\quad 0\quad 1\quad 2\quad 3\ \ldots  
$$

### Important rule

Moving **right** → numbers increase.

Moving **left** → numbers decrease.

Therefore:

$$  
-5<-2<0<3<7  
$$

---

# 4. Comparing Negative Numbers

This is extremely important for aptitude.

For negative numbers:

> **The number closer to zero is greater.**

Example:

$$  
-3>-7  
$$

Why?

On the number line, (-3) is to the right of (-7).

Another example:

$$  
-2>-10  
$$

### Shortcut

Ignore the minus sign temporarily and compare magnitudes:

[  
2<10  
]

Therefore:

[  
-2>-10  
]

> ⭐ **Bigger magnitude does NOT mean bigger negative number.**

---

# 5. Absolute Value

Absolute value means the **distance from zero**.

It is represented by:

$$  
|x|  
$$

Examples:

$$  
|5|=5  
$$

$$  
|-5|=5  
$$

$$  
|0|=0  
$$

Therefore:

[  
\boxed{|x|\geq0}  
]

Absolute value is never negative.

---

# 6. Additive Inverse

The additive inverse of a number is the number that gives **zero when added to it**.

For $a$:

$$  
a+(-a)=0  
$$

Therefore:

- Additive inverse of (5) → (-5)
    
- Additive inverse of (-8) → (8)
    
- Additive inverse of (0) → (0)
    

Formula:

[  
\boxed{\text{Additive inverse of }a=-a}  
]

---

# 7. Rules for Addition

## Same Signs

Add the magnitudes and keep the common sign.

Example:

$$  
(+5)+(+3)=+8  
$$

$$  
(-5)+(-3)=-8  
$$

---

## Different Signs

Subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger magnitude.

Keep the sign of the number with the **larger magnitude**.

Example:

$$  
8+(-3)  
$$

[  
8-3=5  
]

Answer:

[  
\boxed{5}  
]

Example:

$$  
-8+3  
$$

[  
8-3=5  
]

The larger magnitude is 8 and its sign is negative:

[  
\boxed{-5}  
]

---

# 8. Rules for Multiplication

### Same Signs → Positive

$$  
(+)(+)=+  
$$

$$  
(-)(-)=+  
$$

Example:

$$  
(-5)(-3)=15  
$$

### Different Signs → Negative

$$  
(+)(-) = -  
$$

$$  
(-)(+) = -  
$$

Example:

$$  
(-5)(3)=-15  
$$

### ⭐ Shortcut

> **Same sign → +**  
> **Different sign → −**

---

# 9. Rules for Division

The same sign rule applies.

### Same signs

$$  
(+)\div(+)=+  
$$

$$  
(-)\div(-)=+  
$$

### Different signs

$$  
(+)\div(-)=-  
$$

$$  
(-)\div(+) = -  
$$

---

# 10. Closure of Integers

Unlike whole numbers, integers are closed under:

### Addition ✅

$$  
(-5)+3=-2  
$$

### Subtraction ✅

$$  
5-8=-3  
$$

### Multiplication ✅

$$  
(-4)(3)=-12  
$$

But integers are **not closed under division**.

Example:

$$  
5\div2=2.5  
$$

(2.5) is not an integer.

Therefore:

|Operation|Integers|
|---|---|
|Addition|✅ Closed|
|Subtraction|✅ Closed|
|Multiplication|✅ Closed|
|Division|❌ Not closed|

# 🧠 Pattern Recognition

| If you see... | Think... |
|---|---|
| Negative comparison | Closer to 0 = greater |
| \(|x|\) | Distance from 0 |
| \(|-x|\) | Same as \(|x|\) |
| Same signs × or ÷ | Positive |
| Different signs × or ÷ | Negative |
| Integer ÷ Integer | Result may not be an integer |
| 0 as numerator | Result is 0 if denominator ≠ 0 |
| 0 as denominator | Undefined |
| \(a+0\) | \(a\) |
| \(a\times0\) | \(0\) |
