# Real Numbers

## 1. What are Real Numbers?

Real numbers are **all numbers that can be represented on the number line**.

They include:

- Rational numbers
    
- Irrational numbers
    

Therefore:

$$  
\boxed{\text{Real Numbers}=\text{Rational Numbers}+\text{Irrational Numbers}}  
$$

The set of real numbers is represented by:

$$  
\boxed{\mathbb{R}}  
$$

---

# 2. Examples of Real Numbers

### Natural Numbers

$$  
1,2,3,4,\ldots  
$$

All are real numbers.

### Whole Numbers

$$  
0,1,2,3,\ldots  
$$

All are real numbers.

### Integers

$$  
-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots  
$$

All are real numbers.

### Rational Numbers

$$  
\frac12,\quad\frac34,\quad-5,\quad0.25  
$$

All are real numbers.

### Irrational Numbers

$$  
\sqrt2,\quad\sqrt3,\quad\pi  
$$

All are real numbers.

---

# 3. Number System Hierarchy

This is one of the most important diagrams to remember:

$$  
\boxed{  
\mathbb{N}  
\subset  
\mathbb{W}  
\subset  
\mathbb{Z}  
\subset  
\mathbb{Q}  
\subset  
\mathbb{R}  
}  
$$

Where:

- (\mathbb{N}) = Natural Numbers
    
- (\mathbb{W}) = Whole Numbers
    
- (\mathbb{Z}) = Integers
    
- (\mathbb{Q}) = Rational Numbers
    
- (\mathbb{R}) = Real Numbers
    

But remember:

[  
\boxed{\text{Irrational Numbers are also inside Real Numbers}}  
]

They are **not** part of Rational Numbers.

---


# 5. Number Line

Every real number has a position on the number line.

Example:

$$  
-3<-2<-1<0<1<2<3  
$$

Irrational numbers also have positions.

For example:

$$  
1<\sqrt2<2  
$$

# 6. Important Fact About (\sqrt{-1})

A common aptitude trap:
is **not a real number**.

Why?

There is no real number whose square is (-1).
Every real number squared gives a non-negative result.
