# Average-Based Applications

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> Average-based application problems use the same fundamental relationship:
>
> $$\boxed{Average=\frac{Total}{Number}}$$
>
> Therefore:
>
> $$\boxed{Total=Average\times Number}$$
>
> The application may involve:
>
> - marks
> - salary
> - age
> - runs
> - prices
> - production
> - sales
> - consumption
> - distance
> - scores
> - quantities
>
> The context changes, but the mathematics remains the same.

---

# 2. Golden Method

Whenever you see an average problem:

```text
Average
   ↓
Find Total
   ↓
Apply the Change
   ↓
Find New Total
   ↓
Find New Count
   ↓
New Average
```

The most important habit is:

> **Convert average into total before modifying the data.**

---

# 3. Fundamental Formula

If:

- Number of observations = `n`
- Average = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{Total=nA}
$$

And:

$$
\boxed{A=\frac{Total}{n}}
$$

---

# 4. Example — Marks

The average marks of 20 students is 65.

Total marks:

$$
20(65)
$$

$$
\boxed{1300}
$$

This total can now be used in addition, removal, or replacement problems.

# 9. Average After Addition

If a new value is added:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA+x}{n+1}
}
$$

where:

- `n` = old number
- `A` = old average
- `x` = added value

---

# 10. Application — New Employee

20 employees have an average salary of ₹30,000.

A new employee with salary ₹50,000 joins.

Old total:

$$
20(30000)=600000
$$

New total:

$$
600000+50000=650000
$$

New count:

$$
21
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{₹30,952.38}
$$

---

# 11. Application — New Student

30 students have average marks 60.

A new student scores 80.

New average:

$$
\frac{30(60)+80}{31}
$$

$$
=\frac{1880}{31}
$$

$$
\boxed{60.65}
$$

---

# 12. Application — New Customer

50 customers have an average purchase of ₹800.

A new customer purchases ₹1,200.

New average:

$$
\frac{50(800)+1200}{51}
$$

$$
=\frac{41200}{51}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹807.84}
$$

---

# 13. Average After Removal

If one value is removed:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA-x}{n-1}
}
$$

---

# 14. Application — Employee Leaves

50 employees have average salary ₹40,000.

An employee earning ₹30,000 leaves.

Remaining average:

$$
\frac{50(40000)-30000}{49}
$$

$$
=
\frac{1970000}{49}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹40,204.08}
$$

Since a below-average salary was removed, the average increased.

---

# 15. Application — Student Leaves

40 students have average marks 70.

A student scoring 50 leaves.

Remaining average:

$$
\frac{40(70)-50}{39}
$$

$$
=
\frac{2750}{39}
$$

$$
\boxed{70.51}
$$

---

# 16. Average After Replacement

If one value is replaced:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
Old\ Average+
\frac{New-Old}{n}
}
$$

---

# 17. Application — Salary Replacement

100 employees have average salary ₹30,000.

An employee earning ₹20,000 is replaced by an employee earning ₹50,000.

Change in total:

$$
50000-20000=30000
$$

Average increase:

$$
30000/100=300
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{₹30,300}
$$

---

# 18. Application — Marks Correction

The average marks of 50 students is 60.

One student's mark was recorded as 40 instead of 70.

Difference:

$$
70-40=30
$$

Average correction:

$$
30/50=0.6
$$

Correct average:

$$
\boxed{60.6}
$$

# 19. Combined Average

For two groups:

$$
\boxed{
A_c=
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

# 23. Weighted Average

> [!important]
> When groups have different sizes, the combined average is a **weighted average**.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Weighted\ Average=
\frac{\sum(weight\times value)}
{\sum weight}
}
$$

For average problems:

$$
\boxed{Weight=Number\ of\ Observations}
$$

---

# 24. Example — Weighted Average

Three batches:

| Batch | Students | Average |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 10 | 50 |
| B | 20 | 60 |
| C | 30 | 70 |

Combined:

$$
\frac{10(50)+20(60)+30(70)}
{60}
$$

$$
=\frac{3800}{60}
$$

$$
\boxed{63.33}
$$

---

# 25. Average Percentage

Percentage problems can also use weighted averages.

Suppose:

- 100 students → 60% passed
- 300 students → 80% passed

Total passed:

$$
100(0.60)+300(0.80)
$$

$$
=60+240
$$

$$
=300
$$

Total students:

$$
400
$$

Combined percentage:

$$
300/400\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{75\%}
$$

\boxed{35}

# 35. Average Speed — Special Case

> [!warning]
> Average speed must not always be calculated by simply averaging speeds.

The fundamental formula is:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{Total\ Distance}
{Total\ Time}
}
$$

---

# 36. Equal Distance

A car travels equal distances at speeds `u` and `v`.

Average speed:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{2uv}{u+v}
}
$$

Example:

Speeds = 40 km/h and 60 km/h.

$$
\frac{2(40)(60)}
{40+60}
$$

$$
\boxed{48\text{ km/h}}
$$

---

# 37. Equal Time

If equal time is spent at speeds `u` and `v`:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{u+v}{2}
}
$$

Example:

40 km/h and 60 km/h for equal time:

$$
\frac{40+60}{2}
=
\boxed{50\text{ km/h}}
$$

# 55. Average and Multiplication

If every value is multiplied by `k`:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=k\times Old\ Average
}
$$

Example:

Average = 20.

Every value is doubled.

New average:

$$
2(20)
=
\boxed{40}
$$

---

# 56. Average and Division

If every value is divided by `k`:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{Old\ Average}{k}
}
$$

Example:

Average = 80.

Every value is divided by 4.

New average:

$$
80/4
=
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 57. Average of Transformed Values

If each value `x` becomes:

$$
ax+b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=a(Old\ Average)+b
}
$$

Example:

Average = 20.

Every value becomes:

$$
3x+5
$$

New average:

$$
3(20)+5
=
\boxed{65}
$$

# 61. Average of Two Groups With Different Sizes

> [!warning]
> Never use:

$$
\frac{A_1+A_2}{2}
$$

unless the two groups have equal sizes.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

---

# 62. Average and Dominant Group

If one group is much larger than another, the combined average will be closer to the larger group's average.

Example:

- 90 people → average 80
- 10 people → average 40

Combined:

$$
\frac{90(80)+10(40)}
{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{76}
$$

It is much closer to 80.

---

# 63. Average as a Weighted Value

Think of average as a balance point.

If one group contains more observations, its average has more influence.

This is why:

$$
\boxed{Weight=Number\ of\ Observations}
$$

# 65. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Averaging Averages

Do not average group averages when group sizes differ.

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting Total

Always remember:

$$
Total=Average\times Count
$$

### Mistake 3 — Wrong Count After Change

Addition:

$$
n+1
$$

Removal:

$$
n-1
$$

Replacement:

$$
n
$$

### Mistake 4 — Incorrect Speed Average

Average speed depends on total distance and total time.

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Weights

Larger groups have greater influence.

# 67. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Basic

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{Total}{n}
}
$$

### Total

$$
\boxed{
Total=n\times Average
}
$$

### Addition

$$
\boxed{
A_{new}=
\frac{nA+x}{n+1}
}
$$

### Removal

$$
\boxed{
A_{new}=
\frac{nA-x}{n-1}
}
$$

### Replacement

$$
\boxed{
A_{new}
=
A+\frac{New-Old}{n}
}
$$

### Combined Average

$$
\boxed{
A_c=
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

### Group Addition

$$
\boxed{
A_c=
\frac{nA+mB}
{n+m}
}
$$

### Group Removal

$$
\boxed{
A_r=
\frac{nA-mB}
{n-m}
}
$$

### Fixed Increase

If every value increases by `k`:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=Old\ Average+k
}
$$

### Fixed Decrease

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=Old\ Average-k
}
$$

### Multiplication

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=kA
}
$$

### Transformation

For:

$$
y=ax+b
$$

$$
\boxed{
Average(y)=a\,Average(x)+b
}
$$

# 68. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Golden Formula

$$
\boxed{
Total=Average\times Number
}
$$

### Application Strategy

```text
Read the problem
      ↓
Identify the average
      ↓
Identify the count
      ↓
Calculate total
      ↓
Apply addition/removal/replacement
      ↓
Update count if necessary
      ↓
Calculate answer
```

### Three Critical Cases

| Operation | Total | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Add | `+` | `+` |
| Remove | `−` | `−` |
| Replace | `−Old + New` | Same |

### Golden Recognition

> Whenever a problem gives **average + number**, immediately calculate:

$$
\boxed{Total=Average\times Number}
$$
