# Average of Numbers

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> The average of a group of numbers is obtained by dividing their total sum by the number of values.

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{Sum\ of\ numbers}{Number\ of\ numbers}
}
$$

For:

$$
x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots,x_n
$$

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}
}
$$

---

# 2. Three Fundamental Forms

### Average

$$
\boxed{A=\frac{S}{n}}
$$

### Sum

$$
\boxed{S=A\times n}
$$

### Number of values

$$
\boxed{n=\frac{S}{A}}
$$

> [!important]
> In aptitude, converting **average → total** is often the fastest way to solve a problem.

---

# 3. Example

Find the average of:

$$
12,\ 18,\ 24,\ 30
$$

Sum:

$$
12+18+24+30=84
$$

Number of values:

$$
4
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{84}{4}
=
\boxed{21}
$$

---

# 4. Average of Two Numbers

For two numbers `a` and `b`:

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{a+b}{2}
}
$$

Example:

$$
40,\ 70
$$

$$
\frac{40+70}{2}
=
\boxed{55}
$$

---

}
$$

Example:

$$
15,\ 20,\ 25
$$

$$
\frac{60}{3}
=
\boxed{20}
$$

# 8. Average as Equal Distribution

Suppose the numbers are:

$$
10,\ 20,\ 30
$$

Total:

$$
60
$$

If `60` is equally distributed among 3 values:

$$
60/3=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Average=20}
$$

Think of average as:

> **The value every observation would have if the total were distributed equally.**

---

# 9. Finding the Total

If 15 numbers have an average of 24:

$$
Total=15\times24
$$

$$
\boxed{360}
$$

---

# 10. Finding the Number of Values

Total = `450`.

Average = `30`.

Therefore:

$$
n=\frac{450}{30}
$$

$$
\boxed{15}
$$

---

# 11. Finding a Missing Number

The average of:

$$
12,\ 18,\ 20,\ x
$$

is `20`.

Required total:

$$
20\times4=80
$$

Known total:

$$
12+18+20=50
$$

Therefore:

$$
x=80-50
$$

$$
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 12. Missing Number Formula

If:

- Average = `A`
- Number of observations = `n`
- Known sum = `S`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Missing=An-S
}
$$

---

# 13. Two Missing Numbers

The average of five numbers is `20`.

Three numbers are:

$$
10,\ 15,\ 25
$$

Let the remaining numbers be `x` and `y`.

Required total:

$$
5(20)=100
$$

Known total:

$$
10+15+25=50
$$

Therefore:

$$
x+y=50
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{x+y=50}
$$

> [!important]
> If there is not enough information, you may determine only the **sum of the missing values**, not each value individually.

$$

\boxed{
\frac{a+l}{2}
}
$$

# 26. Average of Symmetric Numbers

Consider:

$$
18,\ 22,\ 26,\ 30,\ 34
$$

The center is:

$$
26
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Average=26}
$$

Because the deviations around 26 cancel.

# 27. Deviation Method

Suppose the numbers are close to `100`.

Take `100` as assumed average.

Example:

$$
97,\ 99,\ 100,\ 102,\ 102
$$

Deviations:

$$
-3,-1,0,+2,+2
$$

Sum:

$$
0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Average=100}
$$

---

# 28. Assumed Average Formula

If assumed average is `A₀` and deviations are `d₁,d₂,...,dₙ`:

$$
\boxed{
Actual\ Average
=
A_0+\frac{\sum d}{n}
}
$$

---

# 30. Average After Adding a Number

Average of 10 numbers is `25`.

Add `35`.

Old total:

$$
10(25)=250
$$

New total:

$$
250+35=285
$$

New count:

$$
11
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{\frac{285}{11}\approx25.91}
$$

---

# 31. Average After Removing a Number

Average of 8 numbers is `30`.

Remove `38`.

Old total:

$$
8(30)=240
$$

New total:

$$
240-38=202
$$

New count:

$$
7
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{\frac{202}{7}\approx28.86}
$$

---

# 32. Average After Replacement

Average of 12 numbers is `40`.

A value `35` is replaced by `47`.

Change:

$$
47-35=12
$$

Average increase:

$$
\frac{12}{12}=1
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{41}
$$

---

# 33. Replacement Shortcut

If one number `x` is replaced by `y` among `n` values:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average
=
Old\ Average+\frac{y-x}{n}
}
$$

This is one of the most useful average shortcuts.

---

# 34. Average Change Principle

If a new value is added:

### New value > old average

Average increases.

### New value < old average

Average decreases.

### New value = old average

Average remains unchanged.

---

# 35. Equal Addition

If `k` is added to every number:

$$
x_i\rightarrow x_i+k
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=Old\ Average+k
}
$$

Example:

Average = `35`.

Add `7` to every number.

New average:

$$
\boxed{42}
$$

---

# 36. Equal Subtraction

If `k` is subtracted from every value:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=Old\ Average-k
}
$$

Example:

Average = `40`.

Subtract `6`.

New average:

$$
\boxed{34}
$$

---

# 37. Multiplication

If every number is multiplied by `k`:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=k\times Old\ Average
}
$$

Example:

Average = `12`.

Every value is multiplied by `5`.

New average:

$$
\boxed{60}
$$

---

# 38. Division

If every number is divided by `k`:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=\frac{Old\ Average}{k}
}
$$

Example:

Average = `72`.

Every value is divided by `8`.

New average:

$$
\boxed{9}
$$

---

# 39. Linear Transformation

If:

$$
x\rightarrow ax+b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=aA+b
}
$$

Example:

Average = `15`.

Every value becomes:

$$
2x+10
$$

New average:

$$
2(15)+10
=
\boxed{40}
$$

---

# 40. Average and Range

If minimum = `10` and maximum = `40`, then:

$$
\boxed{
10\le Average\le40
}
$$

The average must lie between the smallest and largest observations.

---

# 41. Average and Extreme Values

Consider:

$$
10,\ 10,\ 10,\ 100
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{130}{4}
=
\boxed{32.5}
$$

The value `100` significantly increases the average.

> [!important]
> Average is affected by extreme values.

# 43. Frequency Method

If a value `x` occurs `f` times:

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}
}
$$

Example:

| Value | Frequency |
|---|---:|
| 10 | 2 |
| 20 | 3 |
| 30 | 5 |

Then:

$$
Average=
\frac{10(2)+20(3)+30(5)}{2+3+5}
$$

$$
=\frac{230}{10}
$$

$$
\boxed{23}
$$

---

# 44. Average of Two Groups

Group 1:

- `10` values
- Average = `20`

Group 2:

- `20` values
- Average = `30`

Totals:

$$
10(20)=200
$$

$$
20(30)=600
$$

Combined:

$$
\frac{200+600}{30}

\boxed{\frac{80}{3}}

$$
\boxed{26.67}
$$

# 45. Combined Average Formula

For groups:

$$
(n_1,A_1),\ (n_2,A_2)
$$

$$
\boxed{
A_c=
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

---

# 46. Equal-Sized Groups

If two groups have equal sizes:

$$
\boxed{
Combined\ Average=
\frac{A_1+A_2}{2}
}
$$

Example:

Average 60 and 80, with equal group sizes:

$$
\frac{60+80}{2}
=
\boxed{70}
$$

---

# 47. Weighted Average

Weighted average:

$$
\boxed{
A_w=
\frac{\sum wx}{\sum w}
}
$$

Example:

Values:

- 80 with weight 2
- 60 with weight 3

$$
A_w=
\frac{80(2)+60(3)}{2+3}
$$

$$
=\frac{340}{5}
=
\boxed{68}
$$

---

# 48. Average Age

}
$$

Example:

15 students have average age 18.

$$

=
\boxed{270}

# 52. Average Speed Warning

> [!warning]
> Do **not** automatically calculate average speed by averaging the speeds.

Correct formula:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{Total\ Distance}{Total\ Time}
}
$$

For equal distances at speeds `a` and `b`:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Speed=
\frac{2ab}{a+b}
}
$$

---

# 53. Example — Equal Distance

Speeds:

$$
40,\ 60
$$

Equal distances.

Average speed:

$$
\frac{2(40)(60)}{40+60}
$$

$$
=\boxed{48\text{ km/h}}
$$

Not `50 km/h`.

# 67. Important Pattern — Average Remains Same

If values are modified symmetrically:

$$
x-d,\ x+d
$$

their average is:

$$
\frac{(x-d)+(x+d)}2
=
\boxed{x}
$$

Therefore adding equal opposite deviations does not change the average.

---

# 68. Example

A number `50` is replaced by two numbers `40` and `60`.

Their average:

$$
\frac{40+60}{2}
=
50
$$

So the average contribution remains:

$$
\boxed{50}
$$

# 69. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1

Confusing sum with average.

$$
10+20+30=60
$$

but:

$$
Average=20
$$

### Mistake 2

Forgetting the number of observations.

### Mistake 3

Averaging group averages without considering group sizes.

### Mistake 4

Blindly averaging speeds.

### Mistake 5

Forgetting that removal changes the count.

### Mistake 6

Forgetting that replacement changes the total but not the count.

# 72. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Core

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{Total}{Count}
}
$$

### Reverse

$$
\boxed{
Total=Average\times Count
}
$$

### Consecutive Numbers

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{First+Last}{2}
}
$$

### Missing Number

$$
\boxed{
Missing=Required\ Total-Known\ Total
}
$$

### Replacement

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Change=
\frac{New-Old}{Count}
}
$$

### Group Average

$$
\boxed{
Combined=
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

### Frequency

$$
\boxed{
Average=\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **AVERAGE → TOTAL → COUNT**

Whenever you see an average problem, immediately write:

$$
\boxed{Total=Average\times Number}
$$

Then solve using the changed or missing information.
