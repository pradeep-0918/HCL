# Average Age

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Average age** problems follow the same average principle:
>
> $$\boxed{Average\ Age=\frac{Total\ Age}{Number\ of\ People}}$$
>
> Therefore:
>
> $$\boxed{Total\ Age=Average\ Age\times Number\ of\ People}$$

Most aptitude questions become easy when you convert the average age into **total age** first.

---

# 2. Basic Formula

If:

- Number of people = `n`
- Average age = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{Total\ Age=nA}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A=\frac{Total\ Age}{n}}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Example

The average age of 5 students is 20 years.

Total age:

$$
5\times20
$$

$$
\boxed{100\text{ years}}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Average Age

A group contains ages:

$$
10,\ 15,\ 20,\ 25,\ 30
$$

Total:

$$
10+15+20+25+30=100
$$

Number of people:

$$
5
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{100}{5}
=
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 5. Golden Rule

> [!important]
> In almost every average-age problem, use:
>
> **Average × Number = Total Age**

Then adjust the total age when people:

- join
- leave
- are replaced
- grow older
- have children
- are grouped together

---

# 6. Combined Average Age

Group A:

- 20 people
- average age = 25

Group B:

- 30 people
- average age = 35

Combined age:

$$
20(25)+30(35)
$$

$$
=500+1050
$$

$$
=1550
$$

Total people:

$$
20+30=50
$$

Combined average:

$$
\frac{1550}{50}
=
\boxed{31}
$$

---

# 7. Combined Average Age Formula

For two groups:

$$
\boxed{
A_c=
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

This is simply a weighted average.

---

# 8. Adding a New Person

A group of 10 people has average age 20.

A new person aged 30 joins.

Old total:

$$
10(20)=200
$$

New total:

$$
200+30=230
$$

New count:

$$
11
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{\frac{230}{11}\approx20.91}
$$

---

# 9. Shortcut — One Person Joins

If:

- Old average = `A`
- Number = `n`
- New person's age = `x`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA+x}{n+1}
}
$$

---

# 10. Average Increase After Joining

The change in average is:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average-Old\ Average
=
\frac{x-A}{n+1}
}
$$

Therefore:

- If `x > A`, average increases.
- If `x < A`, average decreases.
- If `x = A`, average remains unchanged.

---

# 11. Example

10 people have average age 25.

A person aged 35 joins.

Increase:

$$
\frac{35-25}{11}
=
\frac{10}{11}
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{25.91}
$$

---

# 12. Finding New Person's Age

10 people have average age 20.

After one person joins, average becomes 22.

Old total:

$$
10(20)=200
$$

New total:

$$
11(22)=242
$$

New person's age:

$$
242-200
=
\boxed{42}
$$

---

# 13. Formula — Age of New Person

If:

- Original count = `n`
- Original average = `A`
- New average = `B`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Person's\ Age=(n+1)B-nA
}
$$

---

# 14. Example

15 people have average age 30.

After one person joins, average becomes 32.

New person's age:

$$
16(32)-15(30)
$$

$$
=512-450
$$

$$
\boxed{62}
$$

---

# 15. One Person Leaves

A group of 10 people has average age 30.

One person aged 40 leaves.

Old total:

$$
10(30)=300
$$

Remaining total:

$$
300-40=260
$$

Remaining people:

$$
9
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{\frac{260}{9}\approx28.89}
$$

---

# 16. Shortcut — One Person Leaves

If:

- Original average = `A`
- Number = `n`
- Leaving person's age = `x`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA-x}{n-1}
}
$$

---

# 17. Finding the Leaving Person's Age

Average age of 10 people is 25.

After one person leaves, average becomes 24.

Original total:

$$
10(25)=250
$$

Remaining total:

$$
9(24)=216
$$

Leaving person's age:

$$
250-216
=
\boxed{34}
$$

---

# 18. Formula — Leaving Person's Age

If:

- Original count = `n`
- Original average = `A`
- New average = `B`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Leaving\ Person's\ Age
=
nA-(n-1)B
}
$$

---

# 19. Replacing One Person

A group of 20 people has average age 30.

A person aged 20 is replaced by a person aged 40.

Change in total:

$$
40-20=20
$$

Average increase:

$$
20/20=1
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{31}
$$

---

# 20. Replacement Formula

If:

- Total people = `n`
- Old person's age = `x`
- New person's age = `y`
- Old average = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
A+\frac{y-x}{n}
}
$$

---

# 21. Group Joining

20 people have average age 25.

10 new people have average age 35.

New average:

$$
\frac{20(25)+10(35)}
{30}
$$

$$
=\frac{500+350}{30}
$$

$$
\boxed{28.33}
$$

---

# 22. Formula — Group Joining

If:

- Existing group = `n`, average `A`
- New group = `m`, average `B`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA+mB}
{n+m}
}
$$

---

# 23. Group Leaving

50 people have average age 40.

10 people with average age 30 leave.

Original total:

$$
50(40)=2000
$$

Removed total:

$$
10(30)=300
$$

Remaining total:

$$
1700
$$

Remaining people:

$$
40
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{42.5}
$$

---

# 24. Formula — Group Leaving

$$
\boxed{
Remaining\ Average=
\frac{nA-mB}
{n-m}
}
$$

Where:

- `n` = original people
- `A` = original average age
- `m` = people leaving
- `B` = average age of people leaving

---

# 25. Age Difference Between Two People

If person A is 5 years older than person B:

$$
\boxed{
A-B=5
}
$$

The age difference remains constant over time.

This is one of the most important ideas in age problems.

---

# 26. Age Difference Never Changes

Suppose:

Person A = 20

Person B = 15

Difference:

$$
20-15=5
$$

After 10 years:

A:

$$
30
$$

B:

$$
25
$$

Difference:

$$
30-25=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Age\ Difference\ is\ Constant}
$$

---

# 27. Adding the Same Number of Years

If every person's age increases by `k` years:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average\ Age=
Old\ Average\ Age+k
}
$$

Example:

Average age = 25.

After 5 years:

$$
25+5
=
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 28. Subtracting Years

If we move `k` years into the past:

$$
\boxed{
Past\ Average\ Age=
Current\ Average\ Age-k
}
$$

Example:

Current average = 30.

5 years ago:

$$
30-5
=
\boxed{25}
$$

---

# 29. Average Age After 10 Years

If a group has average age 25 today, after 10 years:

$$
25+10
=
\boxed{35}
$$

No individual calculation is required.

---

# 30. Average Age 5 Years Ago

Current average age = 28.

Five years ago:

$$
28-5
=
\boxed{23}
$$

---

# 31. Total Age After `k` Years

If there are `n` people and everyone becomes `k` years older:

Each person's age increases by `k`.

Total increase:

$$
nk
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Total\ Age=Old\ Total\ Age+nk
}
$$

---

# 32. Example

20 people have total age 500.

After 5 years:

Total increase:

$$
20(5)=100
$$

New total:

$$
500+100
=
\boxed{600}
$$

New average:

$$
600/20
=
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 33. Birth and Age Problems

For age questions involving children:

> [!important]
> A newborn has age `0` at the time of birth.

If a child is born today, the total age of the existing group does not immediately increase due to the child's age.

But the number of people increases.

---

# 34. Example — Child Born

A family has 4 members with average age 30.

A baby is born.

Baby's age = 0.

Old total:

$$
4(30)=120
$$

New total:

$$
120+0=120
$$

New count:

$$
5
$$

New average:

$$
\boxed{24}
$$

---

# 35. Important Shortcut — Child Born

If:

- Existing people = `n`
- Existing average = `A`
- Newborn age = `0`

then:

$$
\boxed{
New\ Average=
\frac{nA}{n+1}
}
$$

---

# 36. Example

A family has 5 members with average age 24.

A child is born.

Old total:

$$
5(24)=120
$$

New average:

$$
\frac{120}{6}
=
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 37. Child Added After Some Years

Suppose a baby is born and after 5 years the family has changed.

Remember:

> The child is not always age `0`.

If the problem says the child is currently 5 years old, use `5`.

---

# 38. Husband and Wife Age Problems

Suppose:

- Husband = 30
- Wife = 25

Average:

$$
\frac{30+25}{2}
=
\boxed{27.5}
$$

Age difference:

$$
30-25
=
\boxed{5}
$$

The difference remains 5 years.

---

# 39. Husband-Wife Average After Years

Current ages:

- Husband = 35
- Wife = 30

After 10 years:

- Husband = 45
- Wife = 40

Average:

$$
\frac{45+40}{2}
=
\boxed{42.5}
$$

Or:

Current average:

$$
\frac{35+30}{2}=32.5
$$

After 10 years:

$$
32.5+10
=
\boxed{42.5}
$$

---

# 40. Parent and Child

Suppose:

Father = 40

Son = 10

Difference:

$$
\boxed{30}
$$

After 10 years:

Father = 50

Son = 20

Difference:

$$
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 41. Finding Age From Average and Difference

Two people have average age 30.

Their age difference is 10.

Let ages be:

$$
x,\ y
$$

Then:

$$
x+y=60
$$

and:

$$
x-y=10
$$

Adding:

$$
2x=70
$$

$$
x=35
$$

Then:

$$
y=25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{35,\ 25}
$$

---

# 42. Shortcut — Two Ages

If:

- Average age = `A`
- Difference = `D`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Older=A+\frac{D}{2}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Younger=A-\frac{D}{2}
}
$$

---

# 43. Example

Average age = 40.

Difference = 12.

Older:

$$
40+6
=
\boxed{46}
$$

Younger:

$$
40-6
=
\boxed{34}
$$

---

# 44. Three People With Equal Age Difference

Suppose three ages are in arithmetic progression:

$$
20,\ 25,\ 30
$$

Average:

$$
\frac{20+25+30}{3}
=
\boxed{25}
$$

For an arithmetic progression, the middle age is the average.

---

# 45. Three Ages in AP

If three ages are:

$$
A-d,\ A,\ A+d
$$

then average:

$$
\frac{A-d+A+A+d}{3}
$$

$$
=\boxed{A}
$$

So the middle age equals the average age.

---

# 46. Age Ratio Problems

Suppose ages are in ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Let ages be:

$$
2x,\ 3x
$$

If their average is 25:

$$
\frac{2x+3x}{2}=25
$$

$$
5x=50
$$

$$
x=10
$$

Ages:

$$
\boxed{20,\ 30}
$$

---

# 47. Ratio + Difference

Ages are in ratio:

$$
2:3
$$

Difference = 10.

Let:

$$
2x,\ 3x
$$

Difference:

$$
3x-2x=x=10
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20,\ 30}
$$

---

# 48. Ratio + Sum

Ages are in ratio:

$$
3:5
$$

Their total age is 40.

Let:

$$
3x+5x=40
$$

$$
8x=40
$$

$$
x=5
$$

Ages:

$$
\boxed{15,\ 25}
$$

---

# 49. Average + Ratio

Ages of two people are in ratio `3:5`.

Average = 24.

Total:

$$
2(24)=48
$$

Ratio parts:

$$
3+5=8
$$

One part:

$$
48/8=6
$$

Ages:

$$
\boxed{18,\ 30}
$$

---

# 50. Average Age of a Group

If there are `n` people and average age is `A`:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ Age=nA
}
$$

This should be your first step whenever the question provides an average.

---

# 51. Finding Number of People

Average age = 30.

Number of people:

$$
\frac{600}{30}
=
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 52. Finding Average From Total Age

Average:

$$
\frac{720}{24}
=
\boxed{30}
$$

---

# 53. Finding Total Age

Average = 35.

# 63. Age Problems With Future Ratios

Suppose:

Father is currently 40.

Son is currently 10.

After `x` years:

Father:

$$
40+x
$$

Son:

$$
10+x
$$

If their ratio becomes `3:1`:

$$
\frac{40+x}{10+x}=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
40+x=30+3x
$$

$$
10=2x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=5}
$$

---

# 64. Age Problems With Past Ratios

Father = 40.

Son = 10.

`x` years ago:

Father:

$$
40-x
$$

Son:

$$
10-x
$$

Suppose ratio was `3:1`.

$$
\frac{40-x}{10-x}=3
$$

$$
40-x=30-3x
$$

$$
2x=-10
$$

No positive solution.

Therefore the ratio `3:1` could not have occurred in the past for these ages.

# 67. Important Warning About Births

> [!warning]
> When time passes, everyone ages.
>
> But when a new person is born, the new person's age depends on **when the birth occurred**.

For a newborn at the exact moment of birth:

$$
Age=0
$$

After 5 years:

$$
Age=5
$$

# 70. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Forgetting That Everyone Ages

After `k` years:

$$
Age\rightarrow Age+k
$$

### Mistake 2 — Changing Age Difference

Age difference remains constant between the same two people.

### Mistake 3 — Treating Birth as Age 1

At birth:

$$
\boxed{Age=0}
$$

### Mistake 4 — Using Simple Average for Unequal Groups

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{n_1A_1+n_2A_2}
{n_1+n_2}
}
$$

### Mistake 5 — Forgetting Count Changes

Joining:

$$
n\rightarrow n+1
$$

Leaving:

$$
n\rightarrow n-1
$$

Replacement:

$$
n\rightarrow n
$$

# 73. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Core Formula

$$
\boxed{
Average\ Age=
\frac{Total\ Age}{Number\ of\ People}
}
$$

### Total Age

$$
\boxed{
Total=Average\times Number
}
$$

### Time

Everyone gets older by the same number of years:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ also\ increases\ by\ that\ number
}
$$

### Addition

$$
\boxed{
New\ Total=Old\ Total+Added\ Age
}
$$

### Removal

$$
\boxed{
New\ Total=Old\ Total-Removed\ Age
}
$$

### Replacement

$$
\boxed{
New\ Total=Old\ Total-Old\ Age+New\ Age
}
$$

### Age Difference

$$
\boxed{
Age\ Difference=Constant
}
$$

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Average age problems = Total age problems.**

Convert average to total first whenever the group changes.

> $$\boxed{Total\ Age=Average\ Age\times Number\ of\ People}$$
>
> Then update the total and the number of people according to the event.
