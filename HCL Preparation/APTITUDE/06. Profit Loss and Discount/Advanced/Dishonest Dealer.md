# Dishonest Dealer

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **Dishonest Dealer** is a seller who manipulates the transaction to earn an extra profit.
>
> Common methods include:
>
> - Using a false weight
> - Using a false measure
> - Charging more than the actual cost
> - Giving less quantity
> - Using a combination of markup, discount, and false weight
>
> The key idea is:
>
> $$\boxed{
> Profit=\text{Revenue}-\text{Actual Cost}
> }$$

---

# 2. Main Types of Dishonest Dealer Problems

```text
DISHONEST DEALER
│
├── False Weight
│   └── Gives less quantity
│
├── False Measure
│   └── Uses smaller measuring unit
│
├── False Price
│   └── Charges an unfair price
│
├── Discount + False Weight
│   └── Gives discount but supplies less
│
├── Markup + Discount
│   └── Inflates MP before discount
│
└── Mixed Problems
    ├── Profit
    ├── Discount
    ├── False Weight
    └── False Measure
```

# 3. False Weight

Suppose a dealer claims to sell:

$$
1kg
$$

but actually gives:

$$
800g
$$

while charging the price of 1 kg.

Then:

$$
Claimed=1000g
$$

$$
Actual=800g
$$

Extra quantity charged:

$$
1000-800=200g
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1000-800}{800}\times100
}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 4. Standard False-Weight Formula

If:

- Claimed quantity = `C`
- Actual quantity = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> The denominator is **Actual Quantity**, not Claimed Quantity.

---

# 5. False Measure

False measure works exactly like false weight.

Example:

A dealer claims to give:

$$
1L
$$

but actually gives:

$$
800mL
$$

while charging for 1 litre.

Convert:

$$
1L=1000mL
$$

Therefore:

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{1000-800}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 6. False Weight vs False Measure

| Type | Claimed | Actual |
|---|---:|---:|
| Weight | 1 kg | 800 g |
| Liquid | 1 L | 800 mL |
| Length | 1 m | 80 cm |

The formula remains:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{Claimed-Actual}{Actual}\times100
}
$$

---

# 7. Quantity Shortage vs Profit

This is a common trap.

Suppose:

Claimed = 1000 g

Actual = 800 g.

### Shortage Percentage

$$
\frac{1000-800}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

### Profit Percentage

$$
\frac{1000-800}{800}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\ shortage\neq25\%\ profit
}
$$

---

# 8. Ratio Method

If:

$$
Claimed:Actual=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{a-b}{b}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
Claimed:Actual=5:4
$$

Then:

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{5-4}{4}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 9. Finding Actual Quantity From Profit

Suppose:

Claimed quantity = 1 kg

Profit = 25%.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Actual=
Claimed\frac{100}{100+Profit\%}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
Actual=
1000\times\frac{100}{125}
$$

$$
\boxed{800g}
$$

# 21. Normal Profit + False Weight

Suppose:

Normal profit = 20%

False weight = 800 g instead of 1 kg.

Normal selling price multiplier:

$$
1.20
$$

False-weight multiplier:

$$
\frac{1000}{800}
=
1.25
$$

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.20\times1.25
=
1.50
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{50\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 22. Example — Normal Profit + False Weight

CP of 1 kg = ₹100.

Normal profit = 20%.

Normal SP:

$$
100(1.2)
=
₹120
$$

Dealer gives 800 g.

Cost of 800 g:

$$
₹80
$$

Revenue:

$$
₹120
$$

Profit:

$$
120-80
=
₹40
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{40}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{50\%}
$$

---

# 23. General Formula — Normal Profit + False Weight

If:

- Normal profit = `p%`
- Claimed quantity = `C`
- Actual quantity = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Multiplier=
\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
\frac CA
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Profit\%=
\left[
\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
\frac CA
-1
\right]100
}
$$

- Discount = 10%
- Actual quantity = 800 g

Price after discount:

$$
100(0.9)
=
₹90
$$

$$
₹80

$$
90-80=₹10

# 25. General Formula — Discount + False Weight

If:

- Discount = `d%`
- Claimed quantity = `C`
- Actual quantity = `A`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Multiplier=
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)\frac CA
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\left[
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
\frac CA
-1
\right]100
}
$$

---

# 26. Markup + Discount + False Weight

Suppose:

- Markup = `m%`
- Discount = `d%`
- Claimed quantity = `C`
- Actual quantity = `A`

Then:

$$
MP=CP\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
$$

After discount:

$$
SP=MP\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

False weight increases effective revenue per actual quantity:

$$
\frac CA
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Multiplier=
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
\frac CA
}
$$

Profit:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=(Multiplier-1)\times100
}
$$

---

# 27. Example — Markup + Discount + False Weight

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%

Discount = 20%

Claimed = 1 kg

Actual = 800 g.

Markup multiplier:

$$
1.5
$$

Discount multiplier:

$$
0.8
$$

False-weight multiplier:

$$
\frac{1000}{800}=1.25
$$

Overall:

$$
1.5\times0.8\times1.25
$$

$$
=1.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{50\%\ profit}
$$

# 30. Finding Discount for No Profit

If:

Claimed quantity = `C`

Actual quantity = `A`

and the seller charges the normal price for the claimed quantity, the discount needed for no profit is:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\left(1-\frac AC\right)100
}
$$

Example:

$$
A=800,\quad C=1000
$$

$$
Discount=
\left(1-\frac{800}{1000}\right)100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 31. Finding Discount for Desired Profit

Suppose:

- Claimed quantity = `C`
- Actual quantity = `A`
- Desired profit = `p%`

Revenue required:

$$
A\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
$$

Normal price for claimed quantity = `C`.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\left[
1-
\frac{A}{C}
\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
\right]100
}
$$

Example:

C = 1000

A = 800

p = 10%.

$$
Discount=
\left[
1-\frac{800}{1000}(1.1)
\right]100
$$

$$
=12\%
$$

# 32. Effective Selling Price

If a dealer charges ₹100 for a claimed 1 kg but gives only 800 g:

Effective price per actual kg:

$$
100\times\frac{1000}{800}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹125/kg}
$$

Thus the customer effectively pays ₹125 per kg.

# 33. Effective Price Formula

If price charged for claimed quantity is `P`:

$$
\boxed{
Effective\ Price=
P\frac{Claimed}{Actual}
}
$$

Example:

Price charged = ₹200

Claimed = 1 kg

Actual = 800 g.

$$
Effective=
200\times\frac{1000}{800}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹250/kg}
$$

# 37. Finding the Actual Quantity From Overall Profit

Suppose:

Dealer charges ₹120 for 1 kg.

Cost price = ₹100/kg.

Overall profit = 50%.

Let actual quantity be `x kg`.

Cost of actual quantity:

$$
100x
$$

Revenue:

$$
120
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{120-100x}{100x}\times100
=
50
$$

So:

$$
120=150x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=0.8kg}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{800g}
$$

# 39. Successive Effects in Mixed Problems

Use multipliers.

```text
Normal profit → × (1 + p/100)

Discount → × (1 - d/100)

False weight → × Claimed/Actual
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Multiplier=
\prod Profit\ Multipliers
\times
\prod Discount\ Multipliers
\times
\prod False\ Weight\ Multipliers
}
$$

---

# 40. Example — Mixed Multipliers

Normal profit = 20%

Discount = 10%

False weight = 900 g instead of 1 kg.

Multipliers:

$$
1.2
$$

$$
0.9
$$

$$
\frac{1000}{900}
$$

Overall:

$$
1.2\times0.9\times\frac{1000}{900}
$$

Since:

$$
0.9\times\frac{1000}{900}
=
1
$$

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 41. Interesting Cancellation Pattern

A 10% discount gives:

$$
0.9
$$

Giving 900 g instead of 1 kg gives:

$$
\frac{1000}{900}
$$

Their product:

$$
0.9\times\frac{1000}{900}
=
1
$$

So the discount and false weight exactly cancel each other.

Therefore, if the seller had a normal 20% markup/profit:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ final\ profit}
$$

# 42. Common Exam Trap

A seller gives 800 g instead of 1 kg and says:

> "I am giving a 20% discount."

Do not immediately conclude there is no profit.

The effective multiplier is:

$$
0.8\times\frac{1000}{800}
$$

$$
=1
$$

So yes, in this specific case the discount cancels the false-weight effect.

But if the discount is only 10%:

$$
0.9\times1.25
=
1.125
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{12.5\%\ profit}
$$

# 44. Shortcut From Shortage Percentage

If shortage = `s%` of claimed quantity, then actual quantity is:

$$
100-s
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{s}{100-s}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Shortage = 20%.

$$
Profit=
\frac{20}{80}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 45. Reverse Shortcut

If false-weight profit = `p%`, shortage percentage is:

$$
\boxed{
Shortage\%=
\frac{p}{100+p}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
Shortage=
\frac{25}{125}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 46. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Wrong Denominator

False-weight profit:

$$
\frac{Claimed-Actual}{Actual}\times100
$$

Not:

$$
\frac{Claimed-Actual}{Claimed}\times100
$$

### Mistake 2 — Confusing Shortage With Profit

20% shortage gives 25% profit.

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring Unit Conversion

Always convert:

```text
1 kg = 1000 g
1 L = 1000 mL
1 m = 100 cm
```

### Mistake 4 — Adding Percentages in Mixed Problems

Use multipliers.

### Mistake 5 — Forgetting That Discount Is Based on MP

Discount:

$$
\boxed{MP\text{ is the base}}
$$

### Mistake 6 — Treating False Weight as a Normal Discount

False weight changes the quantity supplied, not simply the price.
