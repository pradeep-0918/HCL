# False Weight

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **False Weight** problems involve a dishonest seller using a weight that is smaller than the stated weight while charging the customer for the full stated quantity.
>
> Example:
>
> A seller claims to give **1 kg**, but actually gives only **800 g** while charging the price of 1 kg.
>
> The seller receives the price of:
>
> $$1\text{ kg}$$
>
> but gives:
>
> $$800\text{ g}$$
>
> This creates an additional gain for the seller.

---

# 2. The Core Idea

Suppose the seller claims:

$$
1\text{ kg}
$$

but actually gives:

$$
800\text{ g}
$$

The customer pays for:

$$
1000\text{ g}
$$

but receives:

$$
800\text{ g}
$$

So the seller gains because:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Price charged for more quantity than quantity delivered}
}
$$

---

# 3. Most Important Formula

If the seller gives actual quantity `A` while claiming quantity `C`:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

where:

- `C` = claimed quantity
- `A` = actual quantity given

---

# 4. Why the Denominator Is Actual Quantity

Suppose:

Claimed = 1 kg

Actual = 800 g.

The seller charges the price of 1 kg.

Assume:

Cost of 1 kg = ₹100.

Cost of actual 800 g:

$$
₹100\times\frac{800}{1000}
=
₹80
$$

Seller charges:

$$
₹100
$$

Profit:

$$
100-80=₹20
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

> Giving 800 g while charging for 1 kg gives a **25% profit**, assuming the seller's cost is based on the actual quantity delivered.

---

# 5. Classic Example — 800 g Instead of 1 kg

Claimed quantity:

$$
1000g
$$

Actual quantity:

$$
800g
$$

Profit:

$$
1000-800=200g
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 6. Fast Formula

If a seller gives `x` units instead of `100` units:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{100-x}{x}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Actual quantity = 80%.

Then:

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{100-80}{80}\times100
$$

$$
=\frac{20}{80}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

$$

$$

The seller charges for 100 units but gives 80.

$$

$$

$$
100
$$

$$

$$

$$

=

$$

---

# 8. General Ratio Formula

If:

$$
Claimed:Actual=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{a-b}{b}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
Claimed:Actual=5:4
$$

Therefore:

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{5-4}{4}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

# 15. False Weight With No Additional Markup

Suppose a seller buys goods at:

$$
₹100/kg
$$

and sells them at the same rate:

$$
₹100/kg
$$

but gives only 800 g for every 1 kg charged.

Although the stated selling rate equals the cost rate, the seller makes:

$$
\boxed{25\%\ profit}
$$

because the actual quantity supplied is only 800 g.

---

# 16. False Weight + Normal Profit

Suppose:

Normal profit = 20%.

False weight = 800 g instead of 1 kg.

First profit multiplier:

$$
1.20
$$

False-weight multiplier:

$$
\frac{1000}{800}
=
1.25
$$

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.20\times1.25
=
1.50
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{50\%\ overall\ profit}
$$

---

# 17. Example — Normal Profit + False Weight

Cost price of 1 kg = ₹100.

Seller normally wants 20% profit.

Normal SP for 1 kg:

$$
100(1.2)
=
₹120
$$

But seller gives only 800 g.

Cost of 800 g:

$$
₹80
$$

Revenue:

$$
₹120
$$

Profit:

$$
120-80
=
₹40
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{40}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{50\%}
$$

---

# 18. General Formula — Profit + False Weight

Suppose:

- normal profit = `p%`
- actual quantity = `a`
- claimed quantity = `c`

Normal selling price for claimed quantity:

$$
CP\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Actual cost of quantity delivered:

$$
CP\frac{a}{c}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Profit\%=
\left[
\frac{100+p}{100}
\frac{c}{a}
-1
\right]100
}
$$

---

# 19. Example

Normal profit = 25%.

False weight:

$$
800g
$$

instead of:

$$
1000g
$$

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.25\times\frac{1000}{800}
$$

$$
=1.25\times1.25
$$

$$
=1.5625
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{56.25\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 20. Finding Actual Quantity From Profit %

Suppose a seller charges for 1 kg but makes a 25% profit with no normal markup.

Let actual quantity be `x` grams.

Using:

$$
25=
\frac{1000-x}{x}\times100
$$

Therefore:

$$
0.25=\frac{1000-x}{x}
$$

$$
0.25x=1000-x
$$

$$
1.25x=1000
$$

$$
\boxed{x=800g}
$$

---

# 21. Direct Formula for Actual Quantity

If the seller claims quantity `C` and wants a profit of `p%` through false weight alone:

$$
\boxed{
Actual\ Quantity=
C\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

Example:

Claimed = 1 kg

Desired profit = 25%.

$$
Actual=
1000\times\frac{100}{125}
$$

$$
\boxed{800g}
$$

---

# 22. Finding Claimed Quantity

Suppose actual quantity = `A` and desired profit = `p%`.

Then:

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
C=A\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Actual = 800 g

Profit = 25%.

$$
C=
800\times\frac{125}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{1000g}
$$

---

# 23. False Weight as a Percentage of Claimed Quantity

Suppose:

Claimed = 1000 g

Actual = 800 g.

Actual quantity percentage:

$$
\frac{800}{1000}\times100
=
80\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ quantity\ shortage}
$$

But profit percentage is:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

> [!important]
> **Quantity shortage % and profit % are different.**

---

# 24. Quantity Shortage Percentage

If:

Claimed = `C`

Actual = `A`

then shortage percentage relative to claimed quantity:

$$
\boxed{
Shortage\%=
\frac{C-A}{C}\times100
}
$$

But false-weight profit percentage is:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

---

# 25. Example — Difference Between Shortage and Profit

Claimed = 1,000 g

Actual = 800 g.

Shortage:

$$
\frac{1000-800}{1000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

False-weight profit:

$$
\frac{1000-800}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
20\%\ shortage\neq25\%\ profit
}
$$

---

# 26. False Weight and Equivalent Price

Suppose:

Actual quantity = 800 g

Price charged = ₹100.

The effective price per actual kilogram is:

$$
\frac{100}{800}\times1000
$$

$$
=₹125/kg
$$

So the customer is effectively paying:

$$
\boxed{₹125/kg}
$$

instead of:

$$
₹100/kg
$$

This is a 25% increase.

---

A seller charges ₹60 for a stated 1 kg but gives only 750 g.

Effective price per kg:

$$
60\times\frac{1000}{750}
$$

$$
=₹80/kg
$$

Extra effective price:

$$
80-60
=

# 28. False Weight and Discount

A seller may combine a discount with false weight.

Example:

Seller claims 1 kg.

Marked price = ₹100/kg.

Discount = 10%.

Actual weight = 800 g.

First calculate price charged:

$$
100(0.9)=₹90
$$

Cost of 800 g at ₹100/kg:

$$
₹80
$$

Profit:

$$
90-80=₹10
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{10}{80}\times100
=
\boxed{12.5\%}
$$

---

# 29. General Formula — Discount + False Weight

Suppose:

- marked price for claimed quantity = `M`
- discount = `d%`
- actual quantity = `A`
- claimed quantity = `C`

Revenue:

$$
M\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Cost of actual quantity:

$$
M\frac{A}{C}
$$

if M represents the cost-equivalent price of the claimed quantity.

Then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\left[
\frac{100-d}{100}
\frac{C}{A}
-1
\right]100
}
$$

---

# 30. Finding Discount That Eliminates False-Weight Profit

Suppose:

Actual quantity = 800 g

Claimed quantity = 1 kg.

The seller wants to give a discount but still make no profit.

Required revenue must equal the cost of 800 g.

Take price of 1 kg as ₹100.

Cost of 800 g:

$$
₹80
$$

Therefore required selling price:

$$
₹80
$$

Discount from ₹100:

$$
100-80
=
₹20
$$

Discount:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 31. Finding Discount While Maintaining a Desired Profit

Suppose:

Actual quantity = 800 g

Claimed quantity = 1 kg

Desired profit = 10%.

Assume cost of 1 kg = ₹100.

Cost of actual 800 g:

$$
₹80
$$

Desired revenue:

$$
80(1.1)
=
₹88
$$

Therefore discount from ₹100:

$$
100-88
=
₹12
$$

Discount:

$$
\boxed{12\%}
$$

---

# 32. False Weight + False Price

A seller may:

1. Increase the price.
2. Give a discount.
3. Use a false weight.

Example:

```text
CP
 ↓
Markup
 ↓
MP
 ↓
Discount
 ↓
False Weight
 ↓
Final Effective Profit
```

Such problems should be solved using **multipliers**.

---

# 33. Multiplier Method

For normal profit `p%`:

$$
1+\frac p{100}
$$

For discount `d%`:

$$
1-\frac d{100}
$$

For false weight:

$$
\frac{Claimed}{Actual}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Revenue/Cost\ Multiplier=
\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
\frac{C}{A}
}
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=(Multiplier-1)\times100
}
$$

---

# 34. Example — Mixed Problem

Normal markup/profit = 20%

Discount = 10%

Claimed quantity = 1 kg

Actual quantity = 800 g.

Overall multiplier:

$$
1.2\times0.9\times\frac{1000}{800}
$$

$$
=1.2\times0.9\times1.25
$$

$$
=1.35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{35\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 35. Important Special Case

If the seller:

- charges the exact cost rate,
- gives a false weight,
- gives no discount,

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

This is the standard false-weight problem.

# 45. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using Claimed Quantity as Denominator

Wrong:

$$
\frac{C-A}{C}\times100
$$

This gives **shortage percentage**, not profit percentage.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{C-A}{A}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Forgetting Unit Conversion

Convert:

```text
1 kg = 1000 g
1 L = 1000 mL
1 m = 100 cm
```

before calculating.

### Mistake 3 — Assuming 20% Shortage = 20% Profit

Example:

800 g instead of 1 kg:

Shortage = 20%

Profit = 25%

### Mistake 4 — Adding False Weight Profit to Normal Profit

Use multipliers for combined problems.

### Mistake 5 — Applying Discount to the Wrong Base

Discount is based on MP.
