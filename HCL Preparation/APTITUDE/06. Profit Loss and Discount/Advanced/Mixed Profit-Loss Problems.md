# Mixed Profit-Loss Problems

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Mixed Profit-Loss Problems** combine multiple concepts such as:
>
> - Cost Price
> - Selling Price
> - Marked Price
> - Profit
> - Loss
> - Markup
> - Discount
> - Successive Discount
> - False Weight
> - Tax/GST
> - Desired Profit
> - Reverse Percentage
>
> The most important skill is to identify the **correct base** and follow the transaction in the correct order.

The master flow is:

$$
\boxed{
CP
\rightarrow
Markup
\rightarrow
MP
\rightarrow
Discount
\rightarrow
SP
\rightarrow
Profit/Loss
}
$$

---

# 2. Fundamental Definitions

### Cost Price

Amount paid by the seller to purchase the article.

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

### Marked Price

Price written on the product before discount.

$$
\boxed{MP}
$$

### Selling Price

Final price received from the customer.

$$
\boxed{SP}
$$

### Profit

When:

$$
SP>CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit=SP-CP
}
$$

### Loss

When:

$$
SP<CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

---

# 3. Percentage Bases

> [!important]
> This is the most important concept in mixed problems.

| Quantity | Percentage Base |
|---|---|
| Profit % | CP |
| Loss % | CP |
| Markup % | CP |
| Discount % | MP |
| Tax % | Taxable amount |
| False-weight profit % | Actual quantity/cost |

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 4. Master Formula Set

### Profit

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\left(1+\frac p{100}\right)
}
$$

### Loss

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\left(1-\frac l{100}\right)
}
$$

### Markup

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
}
$$

### Discount

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 5. Master Multiplier Method

> [!tip]
> For complicated mixed problems, convert every percentage operation into a multiplier.

```text
Profit p%     → × (1 + p/100)

Loss l%       → × (1 - l/100)

Markup m%     → × (1 + m/100)

Discount d%   → × (1 - d/100)

Tax t%        → × (1 + t/100)

Decrease d%   → × (1 - d/100)
```

Then multiply the relevant factors.

# 6. Example — Markup + Discount + Profit

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%

Discount = 20%.

Take:

$$
CP=1000
$$

Markup:

$$
MP=1000(1.5)
=
₹1500
$$

Discount:

$$
SP=1500(0.8)
=
₹1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
₹200
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 7. Direct Shortcut

For markup `m%` and discount `d%`:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

$$
m=50,\quad d=20
$$

$$
Profit\%=50-20-10
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

$$

=

$$

$$
\frac{400}{2500}\times100

# 9. Sign Method

> [!tip]
> Instead of separately calculating every amount, use signs.

```text
Markup  → positive effect
Profit  → positive effect
Discount → negative effect
Loss     → negative effect
```

For example:

Markup = 40%

Discount = 10%

$$
40-10-\frac{40(10)}{100}
=
26\%
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{26\%\ profit}
$$

# 19. Finding Required Markup

Desired profit = 25%

Discount = 20%.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
m=
\frac{100(p+d)}{100-d}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
m=
\frac{100(25+20)}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{56.25\%}
$$

---

# 20. Finding Required Discount

Markup = 60%

Desired profit = 20%.

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100(m-p)}{100+m}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
d=
\frac{100(60-20)}{160}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 21. No Profit / No Loss

For:

$$
SP=CP
$$

If markup = `m%`, required discount:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%.

$$
d=
\frac{100(50)}{150}
$$

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 22. Equal Markup and Discount

If:

$$
Markup=Discount=x\%
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{x^2}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 20%

Discount = 20%.

$$
Loss=
\frac{20^2}{100}
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

If discounts are `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{

$$

20% and 10%:

$$
20+10-2
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

# 33. Mixed Problem — Two Articles

A shopkeeper buys two articles for ₹1,000 each.

### Article A

Sold at 20% profit:

$$
SP_A=1000(1.2)
=
₹1200
$$

### Article B

Sold at 20% loss:

$$
SP_B=1000(0.8)
=
₹800
$$

Total CP:

$$
1000+1000
=
₹2000
$$

Total SP:

$$
1200+800
=
₹2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No Profit, No Loss}}
$$

# 34. Equal CP, Equal Profit and Loss

If two articles have the same CP and one is sold at `x%` profit and the other at `x%` loss:

$$
\boxed{
Overall=No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss
}
$$

Example:

CP of each = ₹1,000.

One at 20% profit:

$$
₹1200
$$

One at 20% loss:

$$
₹800
$$

Total:

$$
₹2000=Total\ CP
$$

---

Article A:

$$

$$

# 37. Important Formula — Equal SP

If one article is sold at `p%` profit and another at `p%` loss, both at equal SP:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{p^2}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% profit and 20% loss:

$$
Loss\%=
\frac{20^2}{100}
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

---

# 38. Equal CP vs Equal SP

> [!important]

### Equal CP

```text
20% profit + 20% loss
→ No Profit / No Loss
```

### Equal SP

```text
20% profit + 20% loss
→ 4% loss
```

This is a very common aptitude trap.

# 40. Weighted Overall Profit

If multiple articles have different CPs and profit/loss percentages:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Profit\%=
\frac{Total\ Profit}{Total\ CP}\times100
}
$$

Do **not** simply average the percentages unless the CPs are equal.

# 42. Successive Profit and Loss

If a price undergoes:

- increase `p%`
- then decrease `l%`

net change:

$$
\boxed{
p-l-\frac{pl}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Increase = 20%

Decrease = 10%.

$$
20-10-2
=
\boxed{8\%\ increase}
$$

---

# 43. Equal Increase and Decrease

If a value increases by `x%` and then decreases by `x%`:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Loss\%=
\frac{x^2}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% increase and 20% decrease:

$$
\frac{20^2}{100}
=
\boxed{4\%\ loss}
$$

This same pattern appears in profit-loss questions.

# 58. Important Multi-Step Strategy

When the question looks complicated:

> [!tip]
> **Do not calculate everything at once. Break it into stages.**

Use:

```text
Stage 1 → Identify CP
Stage 2 → Find MP / markup
Stage 3 → Apply discount
Stage 4 → Apply successive discount if any
Stage 5 → Apply false-weight effect if present
Stage 6 → Apply tax if asked
Stage 7 → Compare SP with CP
Stage 8 → Calculate profit/loss %
```

---

# 59. Multiplier Chain

For complicated questions:

$$
\boxed{
Final=
Initial
\times M_1
\times M_2
\times M_3
\times\cdots
}
$$

Examples:

# 65. Common Traps

> [!warning] Important

### Trap 1 — Wrong Base

Profit % uses CP.

Discount % uses MP.

### Trap 2 — Adding Successive Percentages

20% + 10% discounts:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

not 30%.

### Trap 3 — Equal Markup and Discount

20% markup + 20% discount:

$$
\boxed{4\%\ loss}
$$

### Trap 4 — Equal Profit and Loss

Equal CP:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

Equal SP:

$$
\boxed{Loss}
$$

### Trap 5 — Including GST as Profit

GST is generally a tax collected from the customer, not ordinary business profit.

### Trap 6 — Ignoring Quantity Manipulation

False weight must be represented separately.

# 67. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

### Profit

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Loss

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Markup

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
}
$$

### Discount

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

### Direct SP

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
}
$$

### Profit With Discount

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

### Required Discount

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100(m-p)}{100+m}
}
$$

### Required Markup

$$
\boxed{
m=
\frac{100(p+d)}{100-d}
}
$$

### Required MP

$$
\boxed{
MP=
CP\frac{100+p}{100-d}
}
$$

### No Profit/Loss

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

### Equal Markup & Discount

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{x^2}{100}
}
$$

### Successive Discounts

$$
\boxed{
D_{net}
=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

### Multiple Discounts

$$
\boxed{
SP=
MP\prod_i
\left(1-\frac{d_i}{100}\right)
}
$$

### False Weight

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{Claimed-Actual}{Actual}\times100
}
$$

### Overall Multiple-Transaction Method

$$
\boxed{
Final\ Multiplier=
\prod(\text{all applicable multipliers})
}
$$
