# Selling Price (SP)

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Selling Price (SP)** is the price at which an article is sold to the customer.

It is the final price received by the seller for the article.

### Example

A shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹800 and sells it for ₹1,000.

$$
CP=₹800
$$

$$
SP=₹1000
$$

Profit:

$$
1000-800=\boxed{₹200}
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of Selling Price

Selling Price is:

> **The amount for which the seller sells the article.**

It is compared with Cost Price to determine whether the seller makes:

- Profit
- Loss
- No profit/no loss

---

# 3. Three Important Prices

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| MP | Marked Price |

Remember:

$$
\boxed{CP=\text{Buying Price}}
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=\text{Actual Selling Price}}
$$

$$
\boxed{MP=\text{Marked/Listed Price}}
$$

---

# 4. SP Greater Than CP

If:

$$
SP>CP
$$

then the seller earns a profit.

$$
\boxed{
Profit=SP-CP
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹650

Profit:

$$
650-500
=
\boxed{₹150}
$$

---

# 5. SP Less Than CP

If:

$$
SP<CP
$$

then the seller suffers a loss.

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹700

Loss:

$$
800-700
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

---

# 6. SP Equal to CP

If:

$$
SP=CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹500

Therefore:

$$
Profit=0
$$

---

# 7. Finding SP From CP and Profit

If the profit is known:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP+Profit
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹1,000

Profit = ₹200

Therefore:

$$
SP=1000+200
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 8. Finding SP From CP and Loss

If the loss is known:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹1,000

Loss = ₹150

Therefore:

$$
SP=1000-150
$$

$$
\boxed{₹850}
$$

---

# 9. SP When Profit Percentage Is Given

If profit = `p%`:

$$
Profit=\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP+\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 10. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Profit = 20%

SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

---

# 11. SP When Loss Percentage Is Given

If loss = `l%`:

$$
Loss=\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP-\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 12. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Loss = 15%

SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{85}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

---

# 13. Percentage Interpretation

> [!important]
> In profit and loss problems, **CP is normally considered 100%**.

If profit = 25%:

$$
CP=100
$$

$$
SP=125
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=125\%\ of\ CP
}
$$

---

# 14. SP Ratio at Profit

If profit = `p%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100+p)
$$

### Example

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 15. SP Ratio at Loss

If loss = `l%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100-l)
$$

### Example

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP:SP=100:80
$$

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 16. Finding SP From Ratio

CP:SP = `4:5`.

If CP = ₹800:

One part:

$$
800/4=200
$$

SP:

$$
5(200)
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 17. Finding CP From SP

If profit = `p%`:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\times\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

### Example

SP = ₹1,500

Profit = 20%

$$
CP=
1500\times\frac{100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1250}
$$

---

# 18. Finding CP From SP at Loss

If loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\times\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

### Example

SP = ₹1,600

Loss = 20%

$$
CP=
1600\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 19. Finding Profit From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Profit=SP-CP
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹700

SP = ₹840

$$
Profit=840-700
$$

$$
\boxed{₹140}
$$

---

# 20. Finding Loss From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹900

SP = ₹720

$$
Loss=900-720
$$

$$
\boxed{₹180}
$$

---

# 21. Finding Profit Percentage From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹600

$$
Profit=100
$$

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{100}{500}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 22. Finding Loss Percentage From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Example

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹600

$$
Loss=200
$$

$$
Loss\%=
\frac{200}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 23. SP and Discount

When a discount is given on the Marked Price:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

If discount = `d%`:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

> [!important]
> Discount is calculated on **Marked Price**, not Cost Price.

---

# 24. Example — SP After Discount

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = 10%

Discount:

$$
2000\times\frac{10}{100}
=
₹200
$$

SP:

$$
2000-200
=
\boxed{₹1800}
$$

---

# 25. CP, MP and SP Relationship

A typical transaction may follow:

```text
Cost Price
    ↓
Markup
    ↓
Marked Price
    ↓
Discount
    ↓
Selling Price
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP\rightarrow MP\rightarrow SP
}
$$

Profit or loss is determined by comparing:

$$
\boxed{SP\text{ with }CP}
$$

---

# 26. Profit With Discount

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%

SP:

$$
1500\times\frac{80}{100}
=
₹1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
\boxed{₹200}
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 27. Finding SP for a Required Profit

CP = ₹2,000.

Required profit = 25%.

Required SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{125}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

---

# 28. Finding SP for a Required Loss

CP = ₹2,000.

Required loss = 15%.

Required SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{85}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

---

# 29. SP When Profit Amount and Percentage Are Both Given

Suppose CP = ₹800 and profit = 25%.

Profit:

$$
800\times25/100
=
₹200
$$

SP:

$$
800+200
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 30. SP for Multiple Articles

A trader buys 20 articles at ₹100 each.

Total CP:

$$
20(100)=₹2000
$$

If total profit = ₹500:

$$
SP_{total}=2000+500
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

SP per article:

$$
2500/20
=
\boxed{₹125}
$$

---

# 31. SP Per Article at Profit

If:

- CP per article = `C`
- Profit = `p%`

then:

$$
\boxed{
SP_{per\ article}
=
C\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 32. Total SP at Profit

If:

- Number of articles = `n`
- CP per article = `C`
- Profit = `p%`

then:

$$
Total\ CP=nC
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ SP=
nC\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 33. Example

100 pens cost ₹20 each.

They are sold at 25% profit.

Total CP:

$$
100(20)=₹2000
$$

Total SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{125}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

---

# 34. SP When Different Articles Have Different CP

Suppose:

- 10 articles have CP ₹100
- 20 articles have CP ₹200

Total CP:

$$
10(100)+20(200)
$$

$$
=1000+4000
$$

$$
₹5000
$$

If the entire collection is sold at 20% profit:

$$
Total\ SP=
5000\times\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹6000}
$$

---

# 35. Average SP

If total selling price of `n` articles is known:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ SP=
\frac{Total\ SP}{n}
}
$$

Example:

10 articles are sold for a total of ₹5,000.

Average SP:

$$
5000/10
=
\boxed{₹500}
$$

---

# 36. Different SPs but Same CP

Suppose 2 identical products have CP ₹500 each.

One is sold for ₹600.

Another is sold for ₹400.

Total CP:

$$
500+500=1000
$$

Total SP:

$$
600+400=1000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{No\ overall\ Profit,\ No\ overall\ Loss}
$$

---

# 37. Equal Profit and Loss Amount

If one article is sold at profit `x` and another identical article at loss `x`:

$$
\boxed{Overall\ Profit/Loss=0}
$$

provided their CPs are equal.

---

# 38. Important Equal Percentage Trap

If two identical articles are sold:

- one at 20% profit
- one at 20% loss

then overall result is **not** zero.

Assume each CP = ₹100.

First SP:

$$
120
$$

Second SP:

$$
80
$$

Total CP:

$$
200
$$

Total SP:

$$
200
$$

So in this specific identical-CP case:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

But this happens because the CPs are equal.

---

# 39. Unequal CP Warning

If the CPs are different, equal profit and loss percentages do not necessarily cancel.

Example:

Article A:

CP = ₹100

20% profit:

$$
SP=120
$$

Article B:

CP = ₹200

20% loss:

$$
SP=160
$$

Total CP:

$$
300
$$

Total SP:

$$
280
$$

Loss:

$$
\boxed{₹20}
$$

---

# 40. SP and Percentage Change

If CP = ₹500 and SP = ₹600:

Increase relative to CP:

$$
\frac{600-500}{500}\times100
=
20\%
$$

This is the profit percentage.

---

# 41. Important Base Rule

> [!important]
> The denominator determines the percentage.

For profit:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

For loss:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

For discount:

$$
\boxed{MP}
$$

Do not mix these bases.

# 47. Finding Required SP After Discount

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

Required profit = 20%

Required SP:

$$
1000\times1.2
=
₹1200
$$

If a discount of 20% is offered:

$$
SP=0.8MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
1200=0.8MP
$$

$$
MP=
\frac{1200}{0.8}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

This connects SP with discount and marked price.

# 48. SP as a Fraction of CP

If profit = 25%:

$$
SP=125\%\ CP
$$

$$
=\frac{125}{100}CP
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac54CP}
$$

If loss = 20%:

$$
SP=80\%\ CP
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac45CP}
$$

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing CP and SP

CP is buying price.

SP is selling price.

### Mistake 2 — Using MP Instead of SP

After discount:

$$
SP=MP-Discount
$$

### Mistake 3 — Wrong Percentage Base

Profit/loss percentage uses CP.

Discount percentage uses MP.

### Mistake 4 — Adding Profit Percentage Directly to SP

Profit percentage is based on CP.

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Quantity

For multiple products, calculate total CP and total SP.
