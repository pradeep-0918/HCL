# Profit

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Profit** is the gain obtained when an article is sold for more than its Cost Price.
>
> $$\boxed{SP>CP}$$
>
> The basic formula is:
>
> $$\boxed{Profit=SP-CP}$$

Example:

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹1,000

$$
Profit=1000-800
$$

$$
\boxed{₹200}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price / Buying Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| MP | Marked Price |
| Profit | Gain from selling above CP |
| Profit % | Profit as a percentage of CP |

---

# 3. Profit Condition

There are three possible cases:

### Profit

$$
SP>CP
$$

### No Profit / No Loss

$$
SP=CP
$$

### Loss

$$
SP<CP
$$

For profit:

$$
\boxed{Profit=SP-CP}
$$

---

# 4. Example — Basic Profit

A trader buys a mobile phone for ₹10,000 and sells it for ₹12,000.

$$
CP=₹10000
$$

$$
SP=₹12000
$$

Profit:

$$
12000-10000
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 5. Profit Percentage

> [!important]
> **Profit percentage is calculated on Cost Price.**

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Profit=SP-CP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 6. Example — Profit Percentage

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹600

Profit:

$$
600-500=100
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 7. CP as 100%

> [!important]
> In profit percentage problems, take:
>
> $$\boxed{CP=100\%}$$

If profit = 20%:

$$
CP=100
$$

Profit:

$$
20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=120
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{SP=120\%\ of\ CP}
$$

---

# 8. Profit Percentage to SP

If profit = `p%`:

$$
SP=CP+\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 9. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Profit = 25%

$$
SP=
2000\times\frac{125}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

---

# 10. Finding Profit Amount From CP

If CP = `C` and profit = `p%`:

$$
\boxed{
Profit=C\frac{p}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,500

Profit = 20%

$$
Profit=
1500\times\frac{20}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹300}
$$

SP:

$$
1500+300
=
\boxed{₹1800}
$$

---

# 11. Finding SP From Profit Amount

If:

- CP = `C`
- Profit = `P`

then:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP+P
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹700

Profit = ₹140

$$
SP=700+140
$$

$$
\boxed{₹840}
$$

---

# 12. Finding CP From SP and Profit

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

Example:

SP = ₹1,200

Profit = ₹200

$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

$$
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

Correct.

\boxed{

\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100

# 17. Ratio Method

If profit = `p%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100+p)
$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 18. Example — Ratio Method

An article is sold at 25% profit.

If SP = ₹2,000, find CP.

Ratio:

$$
CP:SP=4:5
$$

5 parts = ₹2,000

1 part:

$$
2000/5=400
$$

CP:

$$
4(400)
=
\boxed{₹1600}
$$

# 19. Profit as a Fraction of CP

Common conversions:

| Profit % | Profit / CP | SP / CP |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 1/10 | 11/10 |
| 20% | 1/5 | 6/5 |
| 25% | 1/4 | 5/4 |
| 50% | 1/2 | 3/2 |
| 100% | 1 | 2 |

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
SP=\frac54CP
$$

# 35. Overall Profit Percentage

For multiple articles:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Profit\%=
\frac{Total\ Profit}
{Total\ CP}\times100
}
$$

Do not calculate separate profit percentages and simply average them unless the relevant CP weights are equal.

---

# 36. Example — Unequal CP

Article A:

CP = ₹100

Profit = 20%

SP = ₹120

Article B:

CP = ₹200

Profit = 30%

SP = ₹260

Total CP:

$$
100+200=300
$$

Total SP:

$$
120+260=380
$$

Total profit:

$$
380-300=80
$$

Overall profit:

$$
\frac{80}{300}\times100
=
\boxed{26.67\%}
$$

# 37. Equal CP — Average Profit %

If two articles have the same CP and are sold at 20% and 30% profit:

Overall profit percentage:

$$
\frac{20+30}{2}
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

> [!warning]
> This shortcut works because the CPs are equal.

---

# 38. Profit and Equal Quantity

If equal numbers of products have equal CP, the average of the profit percentages can be used.

Example:

10 products at 20% profit and 10 products at 30% profit, with the same CP per product.

Overall profit:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

$$

# 40. Profit and Successive Changes

If the Cost Price changes before the final sale, calculate the actual final CP first.

Do not apply profit percentage blindly to the original amount.

> [!important]
> Profit percentage always depends on the **actual Cost Price used as the base**.

---

# 41. Successive Profit

Suppose an amount increases by 20% and then by 10%.

Net increase:

$$
20+10+\frac{20(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=30+2
$$

$$
\boxed{32\%}
$$

The same successive-percentage principle can appear in profit-related applications.

---

# 42. Successive Profit and Loss

Suppose a value increases by 20% and then decreases by 10%.

Net change:

$$
20-10-\frac{20(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-10-2
$$

$$
\boxed{8\%\ Increase}
$$

---

# 43. Profit and Loss Comparison

Suppose:

Article A is sold at 20% profit.

Article B is sold at 20% loss.

If both have the same CP:

$$
Overall\ result=0
$$

But if their CPs differ, the result may be profit or loss.

Always compare:

$$
\boxed{Total\ SP\ with\ Total\ CP}
$$

---

# 44. Equal SP — Different Profit Percentages

Two articles are sold for the same SP.

One gives 20% profit.

The other gives 20% loss.

Let SP = ₹120.

First article:

$$
CP=\frac{120}{1.2}
=
₹100
$$

Second article:

$$
CP=\frac{120}{0.8}
=
₹150
$$

Total CP:

$$
100+150=250
$$

Total SP:

$$
120+120=240
$$

Overall loss:

$$
10
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{10}{250}\times100
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

> [!important]
> Equal SP + equal profit/loss percentage does not cancel.

---

# 45. Equal SP — Profit and Loss

General result:

If one article is sold at `p%` profit and another at `p%` loss for the **same SP**, then:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss=
\frac{2p^2}{10000-p^2}\times100\%
}
$$

Equivalent percentage:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{p^2}{10000-p^2}\times2? 
}
$$

For aptitude, it is safer to use the total CP method rather than memorizing the generalized expression.

For `20%`:

$$
\boxed{4\%\ Loss}
$$

# 47. Markup + Discount Formula

If markup = `m%` and discount = `d%`:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 48. Net Profit Formula

From the above:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%

Discount = 20%

$$
50-20-\frac{50(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=50-20-10
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 49. Finding Discount for Desired Profit

Suppose:

Markup = 50%

Desired profit = 20%

Use:

$$
p=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
$$

$$
20=50-d-\frac{50d}{100}
$$

$$
20=50-1.5d
$$

$$
d=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ Discount}
$$

If a tax is added to the selling price and the question asks for the customer's final payment, distinguish:

- SP before tax
- Tax amount
- Final price including tax

For example:

SP before tax = ₹1,000

Tax = 10%

Tax:

$$
1000(0.10)=₹100
$$

Final amount:

$$
1000+100
=
\boxed{₹1100}
$$

> [!warning]
> Tax is not automatically part of profit. Follow the exact wording of the question.

# 55. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Calculating Profit % on SP

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Profit}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Using MP for Profit %

Profit is based on CP.

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring Discount

If MP and discount are given, calculate SP first.

### Mistake 4 — Averaging Unequal Profit Percentages

Use total CP as the denominator.

### Mistake 5 — Assuming Equal Profit and Loss Percentages Always Cancel

They cancel only under appropriate equal-CP conditions.

### Mistake 6 — Confusing Markup and Profit

Markup:

$$
MP-CP
$$

Profit:

$$
SP-CP
$$

$$
\boxed{SP>CP\Rightarrow Profit}

\boxed{

}
$$

> **Profit % → CP**

### Discount Base

> **Discount % → MP**

### Transaction Flow

```text
CP
 ↓
Markup
 ↓
MP
 ↓
Discount
 ↓
SP
 ↓
Compare SP with CP
 ↓
Profit / Loss
```

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Profit = What you got − What you paid**
>
> $$\boxed{Profit=SP-CP}$$
>
> **Profit percentage always uses what you paid:**
>
> $$\boxed{Profit\%=\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100}$$
