# Cost Price (CP)

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Cost Price (CP)** is the price at which an article is purchased by the seller.

It is the **original investment** made to acquire the product.

### Example

A shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹800.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{CP=₹800}
$$

If the bag is sold for ₹1,000:

$$
SP=₹1000
$$

Profit:

$$
1000-800=₹200
$$

---

# 2. Meaning of Cost Price

Cost Price can be understood as:

> **The amount paid to obtain an article.**

It is the base value used to calculate:

- Profit
- Loss
- Profit percentage
- Loss percentage
- Selling price
- Discount-related profit
- Markup

---

# 3. Basic Terms

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| MP | Marked Price |
| P | Profit |
| L | Loss |
| D | Discount |

The three most important prices are:

$$
\boxed{CP,\ SP,\ MP}
$$

---

# 4. Cost Price vs Selling Price

### Cost Price

Price at which the seller buys the article.

### Selling Price

Price at which the seller sells the article.

Example:

A trader buys a watch for ₹2,000 and sells it for ₹2,400.

$$
CP=₹2000
$$

$$
SP=₹2400
$$

Profit:

$$
2400-2000
=
\boxed{₹400}
$$

---

# 5. Profit Condition

If:

$$
SP>CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{Profit=SP-CP}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹650

Profit:

$$
650-500
=
\boxed{₹150}
$$

---

# 6. Loss Condition

If:

$$
SP<CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{Loss=CP-SP}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹700

Loss:

$$
800-700
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

---

# 7. No Profit No Loss

If:

$$
SP=CP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹500

Therefore:

$$
Profit=0
$$

$$
Loss=0
$$

---

# 8. Cost Price as 100%

> [!important]
> In profit and loss percentage problems, **CP is normally taken as 100%**.

If CP = ₹500:

$$
CP=100\%
$$

If profit = 20%:

$$
SP=120\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=500\times\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹600}
$$

---

# 9. Cost Price and Profit

If profit is `P`:

$$
\boxed{
Profit=SP-CP
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP+Profit
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP-Profit
}
$$

---

# 10. Cost Price and Loss

If loss is `L`:

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP+Loss
}
$$

---

# 11. Profit Percentage

Profit percentage is calculated on **Cost Price**.

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Profit=SP-CP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 12. Example — Profit Percentage

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹600

Profit:

$$
600-500=100
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 13. Loss Percentage

Loss percentage is calculated on **Cost Price**.

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 14. Example — Loss Percentage

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹600

Loss:

$$
800-600=200
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 15. Finding SP From CP and Profit %

If profit is `p%`:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP\times\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 16. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Profit = 15%

SP:

$$
2000\times\frac{115}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2300}
$$

---

# 17. Finding SP From CP and Loss %

If loss is `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP\left(1-\frac{l}{100}\right)
}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP\times\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 18. Example

CP = ₹1,500

Loss = 20%

SP:

$$
1500\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 19. Finding CP From SP and Profit %

If profit = `p%`:

$$
SP=CP\times\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\times\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

---

# 20. Example

SP = ₹1,200

Profit = 20%

$$
CP=
1200\times\frac{100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 21. Finding CP From SP and Loss %

If loss = `l%`:

$$
SP=CP\times\frac{100-l}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\times\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

---

# 22. Example

SP = ₹800

Loss = 20%

$$
CP=
800\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 23. Ratio Method

If profit = `p%`:

$$
CP:SP=
100:(100+p)
$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 24. Ratio Method for Loss

If loss = `l%`:

$$
CP:SP=
100:(100-l)
$$

Example:

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP:SP=100:80
$$

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 25. Example — Find CP Using Ratio

SP = ₹2,000.

Profit = 25%.

CP:SP:

$$
100:125
$$

$$
4:5
$$

If SP = ₹2,000 corresponds to 5 parts:

$$
1\ part=400
$$

CP:

$$
4(400)
=
\boxed{₹1600}
$$

---

# 26. Example — Find CP Using Loss Ratio

SP = ₹1,600.

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP:SP=100:80
$$

$$
=5:4
$$

If 4 parts = ₹1,600:

$$
1\ part=400
$$

CP:

$$
5(400)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 27. Cost Price in Real-Life Problems

Cost Price may represent:

- purchase price
- buying price
- acquisition cost
- manufacturing cost
- original price paid
- investment per article

Always identify what the question calls the **cost**.

---

# 28. Manufacturing Cost

A manufacturer spends:

- ₹500 on raw materials
- ₹100 on labor
- ₹50 on transportation

If all these costs are included in the problem's definition of cost:

$$
CP=500+100+50
$$

$$
\boxed{₹650}
$$

> [!warning]
> In real business problems, "cost" can include multiple expenses. Use the definition given by the question.

---

# 29. Cost of Multiple Articles

A trader buys 20 pens for ₹2,000.

Cost per pen:

$$
\frac{2000}{20}
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
CP_{\text{one pen}}=₹100
$$

---

# 30. Different Cost Prices

Suppose a trader buys:

- 10 items at ₹100 each
- 20 items at ₹150 each

Total cost:

$$
10(100)+20(150)
$$

$$
=1000+3000
$$

$$
=₹4000
$$

Total items:

$$
30
$$

Average CP:

$$
\frac{4000}{30}
=
\boxed{₹133.33}
$$

---

# 31. Important: Average Cost Price

When multiple units are purchased at different prices:

$$
\boxed{
Average\ CP=
\frac{Total\ Cost}{Total\ Units}
}
$$

This may be required before calculating overall profit or loss.

---

# 32. Example — Mixed Purchase

A shopkeeper buys:

- 5 watches at ₹1,000 each
- 10 watches at ₹1,500 each

Total CP:

$$
5(1000)+10(1500)
$$

$$
=5000+15000
$$

$$
=₹20,000
$$

Total watches:

$$
15
$$

Average CP:

$$
\frac{20000}{15}
=
\boxed{₹1333.33}
$$

---

# 33. Total Cost Price

If:

- number of articles = `n`
- CP per article = `c`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ CP=n\times c
}
$$

Example:

50 articles at ₹80 each:

$$
50(80)
=
\boxed{₹4000}
$$

---

# 34. Total Profit

If each article gives profit `P`:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ Profit=
n\times P
}
$$

Example:

100 items give ₹20 profit each:

$$
100(20)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 35. Total Loss

If each article has loss `L`:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ Loss=nL
}
$$

Example:

50 items have loss ₹10 each:

$$
50(10)
=
\boxed{₹500}
$$

---

# 36. Profit Based on CP

If CP = `C` and profit = `p%`:

$$
Profit=
C\times\frac{p}{100}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹800

Profit = 25%

$$
Profit=800\times\frac{25}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹200}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=800+200
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 37. Loss Based on CP

If CP = `C` and loss = `l%`:

$$
Loss=
C\times\frac{l}{100}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

Loss = 15%

$$
Loss=1000\times\frac{15}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹150}
$$

SP:

$$
1000-150
=
\boxed{₹850}
$$

---

# 38. Important Percentage Base

> [!important]
> **Profit percentage and loss percentage are calculated on CP unless the question explicitly specifies another base.**

Example:

CP = ₹500

Profit = ₹100

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Not:

$$
100/600\times100
$$

---

# 39. Profit Percentage vs SP Percentage

$$
CP=100
$$

Profit:

$$

$$
SP=120\%\ of\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

# 40. Loss Percentage vs SP Percentage

If loss = 20%:

$$
CP=100
$$

Loss:

$$
20
$$

SP:

$$
80
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

# 41. Cost Price as Base Value

> [!tip]
> A very useful exam technique is to assume:
>
> $$\boxed{CP=100}$$
>
> whenever only percentages are given and no actual amount is required initially.

Example:

Profit = 30%.

Take:

$$
CP=100
$$

Then:

$$
SP=130
$$

This makes ratio-based problems much easier.

# 46. Profit and Loss Are Not Symmetric

Suppose:

An article is sold at 20% profit.

Then:

$$
SP=120\%\ of\ CP
$$

If it is sold at 20% loss:

$$
SP=80\%\ of\ CP
$$

These percentages are both calculated relative to CP.

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using SP as the Percentage Base

Profit percentage is normally based on CP.

### Mistake 2 — Confusing CP and MP

CP = buying price.

MP = displayed/marked price.

### Mistake 3 — Using Profit Formula for Loss

Profit:

$$
SP-CP
$$

Loss:

$$
CP-SP
$$

### Mistake 4 — Forgetting Total Quantity

For multiple articles:

$$
Total\ CP=Quantity\times CP\ per\ article
$$

### Mistake 5 — Assuming Every "Price" Means CP

Read the wording carefully.

$$
\boxed{SP=(100+p)\%}

$$
\boxed{SP=(100-l)\%}

### Golden Memory Trick

> **CP → Cost / Buying**
>
> **SP → Selling**
>
> **MP → Marked**
>
> **Profit → SP − CP**
>
> **Loss → CP − SP**
