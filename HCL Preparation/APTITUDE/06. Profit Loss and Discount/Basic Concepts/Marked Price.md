# Marked Price (MP)

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Marked Price (MP)** is the price displayed or printed on an article before applying any discount.

It is also commonly called:

- List Price
- Label Price
- Tagged Price

The customer may not necessarily pay the Marked Price because a discount may be offered.

### Basic Flow

```text
Cost Price (CP)
      ↓
Markup
      ↓
Marked Price (MP)
      ↓
Discount
      ↓
Selling Price (SP)
```

---

# 2. Three Important Prices

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price |
| MP | Marked Price |
| SP | Selling Price |

### Remember

$$
\boxed{CP=\text{Buying Price}}
$$

$$
\boxed{MP=\text{Displayed Price}}
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=\text{Actual Selling Price}}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Example

A shopkeeper buys a shirt for ₹800.

He marks it at ₹1,200 and gives a discount of ₹200.

Therefore:

$$
CP=₹800
$$

$$
MP=₹1200
$$

$$
Discount=₹200
$$

Selling Price:

$$
SP=1200-200
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=₹1000}
$$

Profit:

$$
1000-800
=
\boxed{₹200}
$$

---

# 4. MP vs SP

> [!important]
> Marked Price and Selling Price are not necessarily the same.

If there is no discount:

$$
MP=SP
$$

If a discount is given:

$$
\boxed{MP>SP}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = ₹300

SP:

$$
2000-300
=
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

---

# 5. Discount Formula

Discount is:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
MP=SP+Discount
}
$$

---

# 6. Discount Percentage

Discount percentage is calculated on **Marked Price**.

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Discount=MP-SP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 7. Example — Discount Percentage

MP = ₹1,000

SP = ₹800

Discount:

$$
1000-800
=
₹200
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 8. SP After Discount

If discount = `d%`:

$$
Discount=
\frac{d}{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP-\frac{d}{100}MP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 9. Example

MP = ₹2,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
2500\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 10. Finding MP From SP and Discount %

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
SP\frac{100}{100-d}
}
$$

---

# 11. Example

SP = ₹1,600

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP=
1600\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 12. Finding Discount Amount

If MP = ₹3,000 and discount = 15%:

$$
Discount=
3000\times\frac{15}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹450}
$$

SP:

$$
3000-450
=
\boxed{₹2550}
$$

---

# 13. MP as 100%

> [!important]
> In discount problems, **MP is the percentage base**.

If:

$$
MP=100
$$

and discount = 20%:

$$
Discount=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=80
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{SP=80\%\ of\ MP}
$$

---

# 14. Ratio Method

If discount = `d%`:

$$
MP:SP=
100:(100-d)
$$

### Example

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 15. Example — Find SP Using Ratio

MP = ₹2,500.

Discount = 20%.

Ratio:

$$
MP:SP=5:4
$$

If 5 parts = ₹2,500:

$$
1\ part=500
$$

SP:

$$
4(500)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 16. Example — Find MP Using Ratio

SP = ₹1,800.

Discount = 10%.

$$
MP:SP=100:90
$$

$$
=10:9
$$

If 9 parts = ₹1,800:

$$
1\ part=200
$$

MP:

$$
10(200)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 17. MP and Profit

Profit or loss is **not directly calculated using MP**.

It is calculated using:

$$
\boxed{SP\text{ compared with }CP}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%

SP:

$$
1500(0.8)=1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
\boxed{₹200}
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 18. Important Relationship

> [!important]
> **Discount is based on MP, while profit/loss is based on CP.**

Remember:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%\rightarrow MP
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%\rightarrow CP
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%\rightarrow CP
}
$$

---

# 19. MP Greater Than CP

Usually, in discount-and-profit questions:

$$
MP>CP
$$

because the seller marks the product above its cost and then offers a discount.

But the actual profit/loss depends on SP.

---

# 20. Example — MP High but Loss Occurs

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 40%

SP:

$$
1500\times\frac{60}{100}
=
₹900
$$

Since:

$$
SP<CP
$$

Loss:

$$
1000-900
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{10\%}
$$

> [!warning]
> A high MP does not guarantee profit.

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 22. Required MP for a Desired SP

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

Required profit = 20%.

Required SP:

$$
1000\times1.2
=
₹1200
$$

If discount = 20%, find MP.

$$
SP=0.8MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=\frac{1200}{0.8}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

---

# 23. General Formula — MP for Required Profit

If:

- CP = `C`
- Required profit = `p%`
- Discount = `d%`

Required SP:

$$
SP=C\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Since:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
C\frac{100+p}{100-d}
}
$$

---

# 24. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Required profit = 25%

Discount = 20%

Required MP:

$$
MP=
2000\times\frac{125}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3125}
$$

Check:

20% discount on ₹3,125:

$$
3125\times0.8=2500
$$

Profit:

$$
2500-2000=500
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
500/2000\times100
=
25\%
$$

Correct.

$$
1000\times1.2
=

$$
1500-1200
=

$$
\frac{300}{1500}\times100

$$

---

# 26. Maximum Discount Formula

If:

- CP = `C`
- MP = `M`
- Required profit = `p%`

Required SP:

$$
C\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Maximum discount:

$$
M-C\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Maximum discount percentage:

$$
\boxed{
d_{max}
=
\left(
1-
\frac{C(100+p)}
{100M}
\right)100
}
$$

---

# 27. Finding Discount Allowed for No Profit No Loss

If:

$$
SP=CP
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

then:

$$
CP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\left(
1-\frac{CP}{MP}
\right)100
}
$$

---

# 28. Example

CP = ₹800

MP = ₹1,000

What discount gives no profit and no loss?

$$
d=
\left(
1-\frac{800}{1000}
\right)100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

$$
\boxed{

# 33. Formula — SP From Markup and Discount

If markup = `m%` and discount = `d%`:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

Then:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\left(\frac{100+m}{100}\right)
\left(\frac{100-d}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 34. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Markup = 30%

Discount = 10%

SP:

$$
2000\times1.3\times0.9
$$

$$
=₹2340
$$

Profit:

$$
2340-2000
=
₹340
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{17\%}
$$

---

# 35. Net Profit From Markup and Discount

If:

- Markup = `m%`
- Discount = `d%`

then:

$$
\frac{SP}{CP}
=
\frac{100+m}{100}
\times
\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore net profit/loss percentage:

$$
\boxed{
Net\%=
\left[
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}-1
\right]100
}
$$

Simplified:

$$
\boxed{
Net\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

This formula gives the final profit percentage when the result is positive and loss percentage when negative.

---

# 36. Example

Markup = 40%

Discount = 20%

Net:

$$
40-20-\frac{40(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=40-20-8
$$

$$
\boxed{12\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 37. Example — Markup 25%, Discount 10%

$$
25-10-\frac{25(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=25-10-2.5
$$

$$
\boxed{12.5\%\ Profit}
$$

# 38. Finding Discount for a Desired Profit

Suppose markup = 50%.

Required profit = 20%.

Find discount.

Use:

$$
p=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
20=50-d-\frac{50d}{100}
$$

$$
20=50-1.5d
$$

$$
1.5d=30
$$

$$
\boxed{d=20\%}
$$

---

# 39. Direct Formula for Discount

If:

- markup = `m%`
- desired profit = `p%`

then:

$$
p=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
$$

Rearranging:

$$
d\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)=m-p
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100(m-p)}
{100+m}
}
$$

---

# 40. Example

Markup = 60%.

Required profit = 20%.

Discount:

$$
d=
\frac{100(60-20)}
{160}
$$

$$
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

# 46. Successive Discounts

If two discounts are given:

- `d₁%`
- `d₂%`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Discount
=
d_1+d_2-\frac{d_1d_2}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% and 10%.

$$
20+10-\frac{20(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=30-2
$$

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 47. Example — Successive Discounts

MP = ₹1,000.

First discount = 20%.

Price after first discount:

$$
1000(0.8)=800
$$

Second discount = 10%.

Final SP:

$$
800(0.9)=720
$$

Total discount:

$$
1000-720
=
280
$$

Net discount:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 48. Important: Discounts Are Not Added Directly

Wrong:

$$
20\%+10\%=30\%
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

because the second discount applies to the already-discounted price.

---

# 49. Three Successive Discounts

For discounts `a%`, `b%`, `c%`:

$$
\boxed{
Final\ SP=
MP
\left(1-\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac b{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac c{100}\right)
}
$$

Example:

10%, 20%, 30%.

$$
SP=MP(0.9)(0.8)(0.7)
$$

$$
=0.504MP
$$

Net discount:

$$
1-0.504
=
0.496
$$

$$
\boxed{49.6\%}
$$

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Treating MP as CP

MP is the marked price, not necessarily the buying price.

### Mistake 2 — Calculating Discount on CP

Discount is normally calculated on MP.

### Mistake 3 — Assuming MP = SP

They are equal only when there is no discount.

### Mistake 4 — Adding Successive Discounts

20% + 10% is not 30%.

Correct net discount:

$$
28\%
$$

### Mistake 5 — Calculating Profit on MP

Profit is based on CP.

### Mistake 6 — Assuming High MP Means High Profit

Profit depends on the final SP compared with CP.

# 53. Quick Revision

### Price Flow

$$
\boxed{
CP\rightarrow MP\rightarrow SP
}
$$

### Markup

$$
\boxed{
MP>CP
}
$$

Usually:

$$
MP=CP+Markup
$$

### Discount

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

$$
\boxed{MP}
$$

### Profit/Loss Base

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

$$
\boxed{

$$

$$
\boxed{

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
}

### Golden Memory Trick

> **CP → Bought**
>
> **MP → Marked**
>
> **SP → Sold**
>
> **Discount → MP**
>
> **Profit/Loss → CP**
