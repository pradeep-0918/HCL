# Loss

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Loss** occurs when an article is sold for less than its Cost Price.
>
> $$\boxed{SP<CP}$$
>
> The basic formula is:
>
> $$\boxed{Loss=CP-SP}$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

SP = ₹800

$$
Loss=1000-800
$$

$$
\boxed{₹200}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price / Buying Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| MP | Marked Price |
| Loss | Amount lost by seller |
| Loss % | Loss as a percentage of CP |

---

# 3. Three Possible Cases

### Profit

$$
SP>CP
$$

### No Profit / No Loss

$$
SP=CP
$$

### Loss

$$
SP<CP
$$

For loss:

$$
\boxed{Loss=CP-SP}
$$

---

# 4. Example — Basic Loss

A shopkeeper buys a watch for ₹2,000 and sells it for ₹1,600.

$$
CP=₹2000
$$

$$
SP=₹1600
$$

Loss:

$$
2000-1600
=
\boxed{₹400}
$$

---

# 5. Loss Percentage

> [!important]
> **Loss percentage is calculated on Cost Price.**

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Loss=CP-SP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 6. Example — Loss Percentage

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹600

Loss:

$$
800-600=200
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 7. CP as 100%

> [!important]
> In loss percentage problems, take:
>
> $$\boxed{CP=100\%}$$

If loss = 20%:

$$
CP=100
$$

Loss:

$$
20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100-20
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=80}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{SP=80\%\ of\ CP}
$$

---

# 8. Finding SP From CP and Loss %

If loss = `l%`:

$$
Loss=\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP-\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 9. Example

CP = ₹2,500

Loss = 20%

$$
SP=
2500\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 10. Finding Loss Amount From CP

If CP = `C` and loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
Loss=C\frac{l}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,500

Loss = 20%.

$$
Loss=
1500\times\frac{20}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹300}
$$

SP:

$$
1500-300
=
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 11. Finding SP From Loss Amount

If:

- CP = `C`
- Loss = `L`

then:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹900

Loss = ₹180

$$
SP=900-180
$$

$$
\boxed{₹720}
$$

---

# 12. Finding CP From SP and Loss Amount

If loss amount is known:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP+Loss
}
$$

Example:

SP = ₹700

Loss = ₹100

$$
CP=700+100
$$

$$
\boxed{₹800}
$$

---

# 13. Finding CP From SP and Loss %

If loss = `l%`:

$$
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

---

# 14. Example

SP = ₹1,600

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP=
1600\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Check:

$$
2000-1600=400
$$

$$
400/2000\times100=20\%
$$

---

# 15. Finding Loss From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,250

SP = ₹1,000

$$
Loss=1250-1000
$$

$$
\boxed{₹250}
$$

---

# 16. Finding Loss Percentage From CP and SP

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,000

SP = ₹1,500

Loss:

$$
500
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 17. Ratio Method

If loss = `l%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100-l)
$$

Example:

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 18. Example — Ratio Method

An article is sold at 20% loss.

SP = ₹1,600.

Find CP.

Ratio:

$$
CP:SP=5:4
$$

4 parts = ₹1,600.

1 part:

$$
1600/4=400
$$

CP:

$$
5(400)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 19. Loss as a Fraction of CP

Common conversions:

| Loss % | Loss / CP | SP / CP |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 1/10 | 9/10 |
| 20% | 1/5 | 4/5 |
| 25% | 1/4 | 3/4 |
| 30% | 3/10 | 7/10 |
| 40% | 2/5 | 3/5 |
| 50% | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| 75% | 3/4 | 1/4 |

Example:

Loss = 25%.

$$
SP=\frac34CP
$$

---

# 20. Loss and Selling Price

If loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
SP=(100-l)\%\ of\ CP
}
$$

Example:

Loss = 30%.

$$
SP=70\%\ of\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=0.7CP
}
$$

---

# 21. Loss and Cost Price Ratio

If:

$$
CP:SP=a:b
$$

where:

$$
a>b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{a-b}{a}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
CP:SP=5:4
$$

Loss:

$$
5-4=1
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac15\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 22. Finding Loss From Ratio

If:

$$
CP:SP=8:7
$$

Loss:

$$
8-7=1
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac18\times100
=
\boxed{12.5\%}
$$

---

# 23. Loss With Marked Price

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 40%.

SP:

$$
1500\times\frac{60}{100}
=
₹900
$$

Since:

$$
SP<CP
$$

Loss:

$$
1000-900
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{10\%}
$$

> [!important]
> Always calculate the final SP before determining profit or loss when MP and discount are involved.

---

# 24. Loss After Discount

CP = ₹2,000

MP = ₹2,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
2500(0.8)
=
₹2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

---

# 25. Required SP for a Desired Loss

CP = ₹3,000.

Required loss = 15%.

$$
SP=
3000\times\frac{85}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2550}
$$

---

# 26. Required Loss Amount

Required loss = 30%.

Loss=

$$

$$
\boxed{₹750}
$$

SP:

$$
2500-750
=
\boxed{₹1750}
$$

---

# 27. Finding Required Loss Percentage

CP = ₹2,000.

SP = ₹1,500.

Loss:

=

$$

# 28. Multiple Articles — Same CP

A trader buys 50 articles at ₹100 each.

Total CP:

$$
50(100)=₹5000
$$

If all are sold at 20% loss:

$$
Total\ SP=
5000\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹4000}
$$

Total loss:

$$
5000-4000
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 29. Loss Per Article

If CP per article = `C` and loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\ per\ article=
C\frac{l}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹80

Loss = 25%.

$$
Loss=80(0.25)
=
\boxed{₹20}
$$

SP:

$$
80-20
=
\boxed{₹60}
$$

---

# 30. Total Loss

If:

- number of articles = `n`
- loss per article = `L`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ Loss=nL
}
$$

Example:

100 articles have loss ₹15 each.

$$
100(15)
=
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

---

# 31. Different CPs

Suppose:

- 10 items → CP ₹100 → 20% loss
- 20 items → CP ₹200 → 10% loss

First group loss:

$$
10(100)(0.2)
=
₹200
$$

Second group loss:

$$
20(200)(0.1)
=
₹400
$$

Total loss:

$$
200+400
=
₹600
$$

Total CP:

$$
1000+4000
=
₹5000
$$

Overall loss percentage:

$$
\frac{600}{5000}\times100
=
\boxed{12\%}
$$

---

# 32. Overall Loss Percentage

For multiple articles:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{Total\ Loss}
{Total\ CP}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> Do not simply average individual loss percentages unless the relevant Cost Prices are equal.

---

# 33. Equal CP — Average Loss %

If two groups have equal Cost Prices and are sold at 10% and 20% loss:

$$
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{10+20}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{15\%}
$$

This shortcut works because the CP weights are equal.

---

# 34. Equal SP — Profit and Loss

A special aptitude pattern:

Two articles are sold for the same SP.

One is sold at `p%` profit.

The other is sold at `p%` loss.

The overall result is always a loss.

For `20%`:

Assume SP = ₹120 each.

First CP:

$$
120/1.2=100
$$

Second CP:

$$
120/0.8=150
$$

Total CP:

$$
250
$$

Total SP:

$$
240
$$

Loss:

$$
10
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{10}{250}\times100
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

---

# 35. Equal SP Shortcut

For equal SP, one article at `p%` profit and another at `p%` loss:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{p^2}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% profit and 20% loss:

$$
\frac{20^2}{100}
=
\boxed{4\% Loss}
$$

Example:

10% profit and 10% loss:

$$
\frac{10^2}{100}
=
\boxed{1\% Loss}
$$

---

# 36. Important Difference

### Equal CP

20% profit + 20% loss:

$$
\boxed{No\ Overall\ Gain/Loss}
$$

### Equal SP

20% profit + 20% loss:

$$
\boxed{4\%\ Overall\ Loss}
$$

This is a very common aptitude trap.

---

# 37. Loss and Markup

A seller may mark the article above CP but still make a loss after a large discount.

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 40%

SP:

$$
1500(0.6)=900
$$

Loss:

$$
1000-900
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\boxed{10\%}
$$

---

# 38. Markup + Discount Leading to Loss

If markup = `m%` and discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=
CP
\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

If:

$$
SP<CP
$$

there is a loss.

Net percentage:

$$
\boxed{
Net\%=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

If this value is negative, it represents a loss.

---

# 39. Example — Loss After Markup

Markup = 20%

Discount = 30%

Net:

$$
20-30-\frac{20(30)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-30-6
$$

$$
=-16\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16\%\ Loss}
$$

---

# 40. No Profit No Loss Condition

For:

- markup = `m%`
- discount = `d%`

No profit/no loss means:

$$
SP=CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
m-d-\frac{md}{100}=0
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%.

$$
d=
\frac{100(50)}{150}
$$

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

So a 50% markup followed by a 33⅓% discount results in no profit/no loss.

---

# 41. Maximum Discount Before Loss

If:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

To avoid loss:

$$
SP\ge CP
$$

Therefore minimum acceptable SP = ₹1,000.

Maximum discount:

$$
1500-1000
=
₹500
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{1500}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 42. Loss and Tax

Suppose the selling price before tax is ₹900 and tax is 10%.

Customer pays:

$$
900(1.1)=₹990
$$

Whether the seller's profit/loss is calculated using ₹900 or ₹990 depends on the question's definition of selling price and tax.

> [!warning]
> Do not automatically treat tax collected from the customer as the seller's profit.

# 43. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Wrong Formula

Loss:

$$
\boxed{CP-SP}
$$

not:

$$
SP-CP
$$

### Mistake 2 — Wrong Percentage Base

Loss percentage uses CP:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 3 — Ignoring Discount

If MP and discount are given, calculate SP first.

### Mistake 4 — Averaging Unequal Loss %

Use total CP.

### Mistake 5 — Confusing Equal CP and Equal SP

Equal CP:

20% profit and 20% loss can cancel.

Equal SP:

20% profit and 20% loss produce 4% loss.

### Mistake 6 — Assuming MP Determines Loss

The final SP determines profit/loss.

# 46. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Loss Condition

$$
\boxed{SP<CP}
$$

### Loss

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

### Loss %

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

### Reverse

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

### Percentage Base

> **Loss % → CP**

### Discount Base

> **Discount % → MP**

### Transaction Flow

```text
CP
 ↓
Markup
 ↓
MP
 ↓
Discount
 ↓
SP
 ↓
Compare SP with CP
 ↓
Loss
```

### Golden Memory Trick

> **Loss = What you paid − What you got**
>
> $$\boxed{Loss=CP-SP}$$
>
> **Loss percentage always uses CP:**
>
> $$\boxed{Loss\%=\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100}$$
