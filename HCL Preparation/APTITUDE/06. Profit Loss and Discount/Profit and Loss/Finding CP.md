# Finding Cost Price (CP)

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Finding Cost Price** means determining the original buying price when the Selling Price, profit/loss amount, or profit/loss percentage is given.

This is a **reverse calculation** problem.

The most important formulas are:

### If profit = `p%`

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\times\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

### If loss = `l%`

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\times\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

---

# 2. Why Finding CP Is Important

Many aptitude questions do not directly provide CP.

Instead, they give:

- Selling Price
- Profit percentage
- Loss percentage
- Profit amount
- Loss amount
- Ratio of CP and SP
- Discount and Marked Price
- Multiple transaction conditions

Your job is to work **backward** to find CP.

---

# 3. Basic Relationship

The fundamental relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP+Profit
}
$$

For loss:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

Therefore:

### Profit

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP-Profit
}
$$

### Loss

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP+Loss
}
$$

---

# 4. Finding CP When Profit Amount Is Given

Suppose:

SP = ₹1,500

Profit = ₹300

Then:

$$
CP=SP-Profit
$$

$$
=1500-300
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 5. Finding CP When Loss Amount Is Given

Suppose:

SP = ₹1,200

Loss = ₹300

Then:

$$
CP=SP+Loss
$$

$$
=1200+300
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

# 6. Finding CP From Profit Percentage

Suppose:

SP = ₹1,200

Profit = 20%.

Since profit is calculated on CP:

$$
SP=120\%\ of\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
1200=1.2CP
$$

$$
CP=\frac{1200}{1.2}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 7. Standard Formula — Profit

If profit = `p%`:

$$
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Rearranging:

$$
SP\times100=CP(100+p)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
\frac{SP\times100}{100+p}
}
$$

---

# 8. Example — Profit Percentage

SP = ₹1,800

Profit = 20%.

$$
CP=
\frac{1800\times100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

Check:

Profit:

$$
1800-1500=300
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{300}{1500}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Correct.

---

# 9. Finding CP From Loss Percentage

Suppose:

SP = ₹1,600

Loss = 20%.

At 20% loss:

$$
SP=80\%\ of\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
1600=0.8CP
$$

$$
CP=\frac{1600}{0.8}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 10. Standard Formula — Loss

If loss = `l%`:

$$
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
$$

Rearranging:

$$
SP\times100=CP(100-l)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
\frac{SP\times100}{100-l}
}
$$

---

# 11. Example — Loss Percentage

SP = ₹1,400

Loss = 30%.

$$
CP=
\frac{1400\times100}{70}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Loss:

$$
2000-1400
=

$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{600}{2000}\times100
=
30\%
$$

Correct.

# 12. The Most Important Pattern

> [!important]
> **Profit → denominator becomes `100 + profit%`**
>
> **Loss → denominator becomes `100 − loss%`**

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100+p}
}
$$

for profit.

And:

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100-l}
}
$$

for loss.

$$

$$
\boxed{

$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

# 48. Finding CP From Percentage Difference

If SP is `x%` higher than CP:

$$
SP=(100+x)\%\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{100}{100+x}SP
}
$$

If SP is `x%` lower than CP:

$$
SP=(100-x)\%\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{100}{100-x}SP
}
$$

# 49. Important Reverse Percentage Trap

> [!warning]
> If SP is 20% greater than CP, CP is **not** 20% less than SP.

Example:

Let:

$$
CP=100
$$

SP:

$$
120
$$

SP is 20% greater than CP.

But CP relative to SP:

$$
\frac{120-100}{120}\times100
=
16.67\%
$$

So CP is only:

$$
\boxed{16.67\%\ lower\ than\ SP}
$$

---

# 50. Reverse Loss Trap

If an article is sold at 20% loss:

$$
SP=80\%\ CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
CP=125\%\ SP
$$

So CP is 25% greater than SP.

> [!important]
> **20% loss from CP does not mean SP is 20% below CP and CP is 20% above SP.**

The reverse percentage changes the base.

# 54. Three Methods for Finding CP

### Method 1 — Formula

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100+p}
}
$$

### Method 2 — Ratio

$$
CP:SP=100:(100+p)
$$

### Method 3 — Percentage Assumption

Assume:

$$
CP=100
$$

Then calculate SP and scale.

> [!tip]
> Choose the method that gives the shortest calculation.

# 55. Which Method Should You Use?

| Situation | Best Method |
|---|---|
| Simple percentage | Formula |
| Clean ratios | Ratio |
| Mental calculation | Percentage assumption |
| Multiple conditions | Formula |
| MCQ with simple numbers | Ratio |
| Large numbers | Formula |
| Fractions | Fraction method |

# 56. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Adding Profit Percentage to SP

Profit percentage is based on CP.

### Mistake 2 — Subtracting Profit % From SP

Reverse calculation requires division.

Wrong:

$$
SP-20\%
$$

Correct:

$$
CP=\frac{SP}{1.20}
$$

### Mistake 3 — For Loss, Using `100 + loss`

Wrong:

$$
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100+l}
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100-l}
}
$$

### Mistake 4 — Confusing Markup and Profit

Markup uses:

$$
MP-CP
$$

Profit uses:

$$
SP-CP
$$

### Mistake 5 — Ignoring Discount

If MP and discount are given, calculate SP first.

```text
│   │   └── CP = SP - Profit
│   │
│   └── Loss Amount
│       └── CP = SP + Loss
│
├── Given Profit/Loss Amount + %
│   ├── CP = 100P/p
│   └── CP = 100L/l
│
├── Given Ratio
│   ├── CP:SP
│   └── SP:CP
│
├── Given MP + Discount
│   ├── Find SP first
│   └── Then find CP
│
├── Given Markup
│   ├── Find MP
│   └── Reverse to CP
│
└── Advanced
    ├── Multiple Articles
    ├── Tax
    ├── Equal SP
    ├── Equal CP
    └── Mixed Profit-Loss
```

> [!success]
> **Core skill:** Finding CP is a reverse-percentage problem.
>
> If there is **profit**:
>
> $$\boxed{
> CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100+Profit\%}
> }$$
>
> If there is **loss**:
>
> $$\boxed{
> CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100-Loss\%}
> }$$
>
> **Never subtract the percentage directly from SP. Divide by the appropriate percentage multiplier.**
