# Profit Percentage

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Profit Percentage** tells us the profit earned as a percentage of the Cost Price.
>
> The most important rule is:
>
> $$\boxed{\text{Profit Percentage is calculated on CP}}$$

Basic formula:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Profit=SP-CP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. Why CP Is the Base

Suppose:

CP = ₹500

SP = ₹600

Profit:

$$
600-500=₹100
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

The denominator is CP because the profit is measured relative to the original investment.

---

# 3. Golden Formula

> [!important]
> Memorize this first:
>
> $$\boxed{
> Profit\%=
> \frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
> }$$

Everything else can be derived from this formula.

---

# 4. Finding Profit

If CP and SP are known:

$$
\boxed{
Profit=SP-CP
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹800

SP = ₹1,000

$$
Profit=1000-800
$$

$$
\boxed{₹200}
$$

---

# 5. Finding Profit Percentage

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{200}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

> An article bought for ₹800 and sold for ₹1,000 gives a **25% profit**.

---

# 6. CP as 100%

> [!important]
> For percentage-based profit problems, assume:
>
> $$\boxed{CP=100}$$

If profit = 20%:

$$
Profit=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100+20
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=120}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{CP:SP=100:120=5:6}
$$

---

# 7. Profit Percentage to SP

If profit = `p%`:

$$
SP=CP+\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 8. Example

CP = ₹1,000

Profit = 20%.

$$
SP=
1000\times\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000=₹200
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 9. Finding CP From SP and Profit Percentage

If profit = `p%`:

$$
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

---

# 10. Example

SP = ₹1,500

Profit = 20%.

$$
CP=
1500\times\frac{100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1250}
$$

Check:

$$
1250(1.2)=1500
$$

Correct.

---

# 11. Finding Profit Amount From CP

If CP = `C` and profit = `p%`:

$$
\boxed{
Profit=
C\frac{p}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,000

Profit = 15%.

$$
Profit=
2000\times\frac{15}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹300}
$$

SP:

$$
2000+300
=
\boxed{₹2300}
$$

---

# 12. Finding Profit Percentage From Profit and CP

If:

- Profit = `P`
- CP = `C`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=\frac{P}{C}\times100
}
$$

Example:

Profit = ₹450

CP = ₹1,500

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{450}{1500}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{30\%}
$$

---

# 13. Finding Profit Percentage From CP and SP

Use directly:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,500

SP = ₹3,000

$$
Profit=500
$$

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{500}{2500}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 14. Ratio Method

If profit = `p%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100+p)
$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

---

# 15. Example — Find CP Using Ratio

An article is sold at 25% profit for ₹2,000.

Since:

$$
CP:SP=4:5
$$

5 parts = ₹2,000.

1 part:

$$
2000/5=400
$$

CP:

$$
4(400)
=
\boxed{₹1600}
$$

---

# 16. Example — Find SP Using Ratio

CP = ₹1,200.

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=4:5
$$

4 parts = ₹1,200.

1 part:

$$
1200/4=300
$$

SP:

$$
5(300)
=
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

---

| 20% | 1/5 | 6/5 |
| 25% | 1/4 | 5/4 |
| 30% | 3/10 | 13/10 |
| 40% | 2/5 | 7/5 |
| 50% | 1/2 | 3/2 |
| 75% | 3/4 | 7/4 |
| 100% | 1 | 2 |

# 19. Profit Percentage From SP:CP Ratio

If:

$$
SP:CP=a:b
$$

and:

$$
a>b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{a-b}{b}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
SP:CP=5:4
$$

Profit:

$$
5-4=1
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac14\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 20. CP:SP Ratio Method

If:

$$
CP:SP=a:b
$$

where:

$$
b>a
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
\frac{b-a}{a}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
CP:SP=4:5
$$

$$
Profit\%=
\frac{5-4}{4}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 21. Profit Percentage and Selling Price

If an article is sold at `p%` profit:

$$
\boxed{
SP=(100+p)\%\text{ of CP}
}
$$

Example:

Profit = 40%.

$$
SP=140\%\text{ of CP}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=1.4CP
$$

---

# 22. Reverse Percentage Problem

An article is sold for ₹2,400 at 20% profit.

Find CP.

$$
CP=
2400\times\frac{100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Profit:

$$
2400-2000
=
₹400
$$

---

# 23. Finding SP When Profit Amount Is Given

CP = ₹2,000.

Profit = ₹500.

$$
SP=CP+Profit
$$

$$
=2000+500
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 24. Finding CP When Profit Amount Is Given

SP = ₹2,500.

Profit = ₹500.

$$
CP=SP-Profit
$$

$$
2500-500
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 25. Profit Percentage and Marked Price

Profit percentage is based on CP, not MP.

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
1500(0.8)=1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000=200
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 26. Markup + Discount

Suppose markup = `m%`.

Then:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\left(\frac{100+m}{100}\right)
\left(\frac{100-d}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 27. Net Profit Percentage

From the markup-discount formula:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Profit\%=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 40%

Discount = 10%.

$$
40-10-\frac{40(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=40-10-4
$$

$$
\boxed{26\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 28. Example — Profit After Discount

CP = ₹1,000.

MP = ₹1,500.

Discount = 10%.

SP:

$$
1500(0.9)
=
₹1350
$$

Profit:

$$
1350-1000
=
₹350
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{350}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{35\%}
$$

---

# 29. Required SP for Desired Profit

$$
\boxed{₹1950}

A shopkeeper buys 100 pens for ₹20 each.

Total CP:

$$
100(20)
=
₹2000
$$

If sold at 25% profit:

$$
Total\ SP=
2000(1.25)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}

# 32. Profit Per Article

If:

- CP per article = `C`
- profit = `p%`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\ per\ article=
C\frac{p}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹80.

Profit = 25%.

$$
Profit=80(0.25)
=
\boxed{₹20}
$$

SP:

$$
80+20
=
\boxed{₹100}
$$

---

# 33. Overall Profit Percentage

For multiple products:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Profit\%=
\frac{Total\ Profit}{Total\ CP}\times100
}
$$

Do not simply average profit percentages unless their CP values are equal.

---

# 34. Example — Different CPs

Product A:

CP = ₹100

Profit = 20%

Product B:

CP = ₹300

Profit = 30%

Profit from A:

$$
100(0.2)=₹20
$$

Profit from B:

$$
300(0.3)=₹90
$$

Total profit:

$$
20+90
=
₹110
$$

Total CP:

$$
100+300
=
₹400
$$

Overall profit:

$$
\frac{110}{400}\times100
=
\boxed{27.5\%}
$$

---

# 35. Equal CP Shortcut

If all articles have the same CP, overall profit percentage is the average of their profit percentages.

Example:

Two equal-CP articles are sold at:

- 20% profit
- 30% profit

Overall:

$$
\frac{20+30}{2}
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 36. Equal Quantity but Different CP

Equal quantity does **not** automatically mean equal weighting.

Example:

10 products have CP ₹100.

10 products have CP ₹200.

Although quantities are equal, total CPs are different.

Therefore, use total profit / total CP.

---

# 37. Equal Selling Price Trap

Two articles are sold at the same SP.

One gives 20% profit.

The other gives 20% loss.

Assume SP = ₹120 each.

First CP:

$$
120/1.2=100
$$

Second CP:

$$
120/0.8=150
$$

Total CP:

$$
250
$$

Total SP:

$$
240
$$

Loss:

$$
10
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{10}{250}\times100
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

> [!important]
> Equal SP + equal profit/loss percentage produces an overall loss.

Shortcut:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{p^2}{100}
}
$$

For 20%:

$$
\frac{20^2}{100}=4\%
$$

---

# 38. Equal CP Profit-Loss Combination

If two articles have equal CP:

- one sold at `p%` profit
- another sold at `p%` loss

then:

$$
\boxed{No\ Overall\ Profit\ or\ Loss}
$$

Example:

CP of each = ₹1,000.

First SP:

$$
1000(1.2)=1200
$$

Second SP:

$$
1000(0.8)=800
$$

Total CP:

$$
2000
$$

Total SP:

$$
2000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

---

# 39. Profit Percentage and Successive Changes

Suppose a value increases by 20% and then by 10%.

Net increase:

$$
20+10+\frac{20(10)}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{32\%}
$$

The same principle is useful when profit-related percentages are applied successively.

---

# 40. Successive Profit Percentages

If an amount is increased by `a%` and then `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Increase=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% and 30%:

$$
20+30+\frac{20(30)}{100}
$$

$$
=50+6
$$

$$
\boxed{56\%}
$$

---

# 41. Profit Percentage and Reverse Change

If a value after a 20% profit becomes ₹1,200:

$$
CP=
1200\times\frac{100}{120}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

> [!tip]
> When going **forward**, multiply.
>
> When going **backward**, divide.

---

# 42. Forward vs Reverse

### Forward

Given CP and profit:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

### Reverse

Given SP and profit:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\frac{100}{100+p}
}
$$

---

# 43. Profit Percentage From SP as a Multiple of CP

If:

$$
SP=1.5CP
$$

then:

$$
Profit=1.5CP-CP
$$

$$
=0.5CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{50\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 44. Profit Percentage From SP Fraction

If:

$$
SP=\frac65CP
$$

then:

$$
Profit=
\frac65CP-CP
$$

$$
=\frac15CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 45. Finding SP When Profit Is a Fraction

If profit is `1/4` of CP:

$$
Profit=\frac14CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP+\frac14CP
$$

$$
=\frac54CP
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{SP=125\%\ of\ CP}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{Profit=25\%}
$$

# 46. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using SP as Denominator

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Profit}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Profit}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Using MP as Denominator

Profit percentage is not normally based on MP.

### Mistake 3 — Adding Successive Percentages

20% followed by 10% is not 30%.

### Mistake 4 — Averaging Unequal Profit Percentages

Use weighted totals.

### Mistake 5 — Confusing Profit and Markup

Markup:

$$
MP-CP
$$

Profit:

$$
SP-CP
$$

### Mistake 6 — Forgetting Reverse Formula

When SP is known:

$$
CP=SP\frac{100}{100+p}
$$
