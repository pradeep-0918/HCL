# Loss Percentage

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Loss Percentage** represents the loss as a percentage of the Cost Price.

The golden rule:

$$
\boxed{\text{Loss Percentage is calculated on CP}}
$$

Basic formula:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Loss=CP-SP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Example

An article is bought for ₹800 and sold for ₹600.

$$
CP=₹800
$$

$$
SP=₹600
$$

Loss:

$$
800-600=₹200
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 3. Why CP Is the Base

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

Loss = ₹200

Then:

$$
Loss\%=
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

The loss is compared with the **original investment**, which is CP.

> [!important]
> Never use SP as the denominator for the standard loss percentage.

---

# 4. Golden Formula

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Memorize this formula first.

Everything else can be derived from it.

---

# 5. CP as 100%

> [!important]
> For percentage-based loss problems, assume:
>
> $$\boxed{CP=100}$$

If loss = 20%:

$$
Loss=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100-20
$$

$$
\boxed{SP=80}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{SP=80\%\text{ of CP}}
$$

---

# 6. Finding SP From Loss %

If loss = `l%`:

$$
Loss=\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP-\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 7. Example

CP = ₹2,000

Loss = 15%

$$
SP=
2000\times\frac{85}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

Loss:

$$
2000-1700
=
₹300
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{300}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{15\%}
$$

---

# 8. Finding Loss Amount From CP

If CP = `C` and loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
Loss=
C\frac{l}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,500

Loss = 20%.

$$
Loss=
2500\times\frac{20}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹500}
$$

SP:

$$
2500-500
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 9. Finding SP From Loss Amount

If loss amount is known:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,500

Loss = ₹300

$$
SP=1500-300
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 10. Finding CP From Loss Amount

If:

$$
Loss=CP-SP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP+Loss
}
$$

Example:

SP = ₹700

Loss = ₹100

$$
CP=700+100
$$

$$
\boxed{₹800}
$$

---

# 11. Finding CP From SP and Loss %

If loss = `l%`:

$$
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
SP\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

---

# 12. Example

SP = ₹1,600

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP=
1600\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Check:

$$
2000-1600=400
$$

$$
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Correct.

# 13. Finding Loss Percentage From CP and SP

Use:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,000

SP = ₹1,500

Loss:

$$
2000-1500=500
$$

Therefore:

$$
Loss\%=
\frac{500}{2000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 14. Ratio Method

If loss = `l%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100-l)
$$

Example:

Loss = 20%.

$$
CP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

$$

$$

$$
5(400)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

$$

1 part:

$$
2500/5=500
$$

SP:

$$
4(500)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 22. Forward vs Reverse

### Forward

Given CP and loss:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

### Reverse

Given SP and loss:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

> [!tip]
> **Forward → multiply**
>
> **Reverse → divide**

# 26. Loss Per Article

If CP per article = `C` and loss = `l%`:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\ per\ article=
C\frac{l}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹200

$$

$$

$$

=

---

# 27. Total Loss

If:

- number of articles = `n`
- loss per article = `L`

then:

$$
\boxed{
Total\ Loss=nL
}
$$

Example:

50 articles have loss ₹20 each.

$$
50(20)
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 28. Overall Loss Percentage

For multiple articles:

$$
\boxed{
Overall\ Loss\%=
\frac{Total\ Loss}{Total\ CP}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> The overall percentage must use **Total CP** as the denominator.

---

# 29. Example — Different CPs

Product A:

CP = ₹100

Loss = 20%

Loss:

$$
100(0.2)=₹20
$$

Product B:

CP = ₹300

Loss = 10%

Loss:

$$
300(0.1)=₹30
$$

Total loss:

$$
20+30=₹50
$$

Total CP:

$$
100+300=₹400
$$

Overall loss:

$$
\frac{50}{400}\times100
=
\boxed{12.5\%}
$$

---

# 30. Equal CP Shortcut

If all articles have the same CP, the overall loss percentage is the average of their loss percentages.

Example:

Two equal-CP articles are sold at:

- 10% loss
- 20% loss

Overall:

$$
\frac{10+20}{2}
=
\boxed{15\%}
$$

---

# 31. Equal Quantity Is Not Enough

> [!warning]
> Equal quantity does not necessarily mean equal weighting.

Example:

10 products → CP ₹100 → 20% loss

10 products → CP ₹200 → 10% loss

First group loss:

$$
10(100)(0.2)=₹200
$$

Second group loss:

$$
10(200)(0.1)=₹200
$$

Total loss:

$$
₹400
$$

Total CP:

$$
1000+2000=₹3000
$$

Overall loss:

$$
\frac{400}{3000}\times100
=
\boxed{13.33\%}
$$

It is not simply:

$$
\frac{20+10}{2}=15\%
$$

# 34. Markup + Discount Leading to Loss

Suppose:

Markup = 20%

Discount = 30%

Net percentage:

$$
20-30-\frac{20(30)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-30-6
$$

$$

# 35. General Markup + Discount Formula

If:

- markup = `m%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
SP=
CP
\left(\frac{100+m}{100}\right)
\left(\frac{100-d}{100}\right)
$$

Net change:

$$
\boxed{
Net\%=m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

If the result is negative, it represents loss.

---

# 36. No-Profit-No-Loss Condition

For markup `m%` and discount `d%`:

$$
m-d-\frac{md}{100}=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
d=\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%.

$$
d=
\frac{5000}{150}
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

So:

50% markup + 33⅓% discount

gives:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

---

# 37. Maximum Discount Before Loss

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

For no loss:

$$
SP=CP=₹1000
$$

Maximum discount:

$$
1500-1000=₹500
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{1500}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

Any discount greater than this causes a loss.

---

# 38. Equal SP Trap

Two articles are sold at the same SP:

- one at `p%` profit
- one at `p%` loss

The overall result is a loss.

For 20%:

Assume SP = ₹120 each.

First CP:

$$
120/1.2=100
$$

Second CP:

$$
120/0.8=150
$$

Total CP:

$$
250
$$

Total SP:

$$
240
$$

Loss:

$$
10
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{10}{250}\times100
=
\boxed{4\%}
$$

Shortcut:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{p^2}{100}
}
$$

$$

Because the gain and loss amounts are equal.

# 41. Profit vs Loss Percentage

Profit:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

Loss:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

The denominator is the same:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

---

# 42. Profit and Loss Symmetry

If CP = 100:

### 20% Profit

$$
SP=120
$$

### 20% Loss

$$
SP=80
$$

The difference between the SP values is:

$$
120-80=40
$$

But this does not mean a 40% profit/loss relationship.

Always use CP as the base.

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using SP as Denominator

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Loss}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Loss}{CP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Wrong Loss Formula

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
Loss=CP-SP
}
$$

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting Reverse Formula

At `l%` loss:

$$
\boxed{
CP=SP\frac{100}{100-l}
}
$$

### Mistake 4 — Adding Loss Percentages

Successive percentage changes cannot simply be added.

### Mistake 5 — Averaging Unequal Loss Percentages

Use:

$$
\frac{Total\ Loss}{Total\ CP}\times100
$$

### Mistake 6 — Confusing Equal CP With Equal SP

They produce different results.
