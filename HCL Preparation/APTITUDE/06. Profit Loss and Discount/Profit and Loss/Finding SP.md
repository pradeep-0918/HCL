# Finding Selling Price (SP)

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Selling Price (SP)** is the final price at which an article is sold.
>
> Finding SP usually means calculating the final selling price from:
>
> - Cost Price + Profit
> - Cost Price + Profit %
> - Cost Price + Loss
> - Cost Price + Loss %
> - Marked Price + Discount
> - Markup + Discount
> - Tax-related conditions

The most important formulas are:

### Profit

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP+Profit
}
$$

### Loss

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP-Loss
}
$$

### Profit %

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

### Loss %

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 2. Basic Relationship

There are three possible situations:

```text
SP > CP  → Profit
SP = CP  → No Profit / No Loss
SP < CP  → Loss
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{Profit=SP-CP}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{Loss=CP-SP}
$$

---

# 3. Finding SP From CP and Profit Amount

If:

CP = ₹1,000

Profit = ₹200

Then:

$$
SP=CP+Profit
$$

$$
=1000+200
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

---

# 4. Finding SP From CP and Loss Amount

If:

CP = ₹1,000

Loss = ₹200

Then:

$$
SP=CP-Loss
$$

$$
=1000-200
$$

$$
\boxed{₹800}
$$

---

# 5. Finding SP From Profit Percentage

If profit = `p%`:

$$
Profit=\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP+\frac{p}{100}CP
$$

$$
SP=CP\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100+p}{100}
}
$$

---

# 6. Example — 20% Profit

CP = ₹2,000

Profit = 20%.

$$
SP=
2000\times\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

Profit:

$$
2400-2000=₹400
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

---

# 7. Fast Method — Multiply by Percentage

For 20% profit:

$$
SP=CP(1.20)
$$

For 25% profit:

$$
SP=CP(1.25)
$$

For 50% profit:

$$
SP=CP(1.50)
$$

For 100% profit:

$$
SP=CP(2)
$$

> [!tip]
> In fast aptitude calculations, converting the percentage into a multiplier is often quicker.

---

# 8. Profit Percentage → SP Ratio

If profit = `p%`:

$$
CP:SP=100:(100+p)
$$

Example:

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=100:125
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{SP=\frac54CP}
$$

---

# 9. Example — Ratio Method

CP = ₹1,600

Profit = 25%.

$$
CP:SP=4:5
$$

4 parts = ₹1,600.

1 part:

$$
1600/4=400
$$

SP:

$$
5(400)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 10. Finding SP From Loss Percentage

If loss = `l%`:

$$
Loss=\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP-\frac{l}{100}CP
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\frac{100-l}{100}
}
$$

---

# 11. Example — 20% Loss

CP = ₹2,500

Loss = 20%.

$$
SP=
2500\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Loss:

$$
2500-2000=₹500
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{2500}\times100
=
20\%
$$

---

CP = ₹4,000

Loss = 25%.

$$
SP=4000(0.75)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 13. Finding SP From Loss Ratio

If:

$$
CP:SP=a:b
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
SP=CP\times\frac{b}{a}
}
$$

Example:

$$
CP:SP=5:4
$$

CP = ₹2,500.

$$
SP=2500\times\frac45
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 19. Fraction Method — Profit

If profit is a fraction of CP:

$$
Profit=\frac{a}{b}CP
$$

then:

$$
SP=
CP+\frac{a}{b}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac{a+b}{b}CP
}
$$

Example:

Profit = `1/4` of CP.

$$
SP=
\frac{1+4}{4}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac54CP
}
$$

---

# 20. Fraction Method — Loss

If loss is `a/b` of CP:

$$
Loss=\frac{a}{b}CP
$$

then:

$$
SP=
CP-\frac{a}{b}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac{b-a}{b}CP
}
$$

Example:

Loss = `1/5` of CP.

$$
SP=
\frac{5-1}{5}CP
$$

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac45CP
}
$$

---

# 21. Finding SP From Marked Price and Discount

This is one of the most important aptitude patterns.

If:

- MP = Marked Price
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
Discount=\frac{d}{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP-Discount
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 22. Example — Discount

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = 10%.

Discount amount:

$$
2000\times\frac{10}{100}
=
₹200
$$

SP:

$$
2000-200
=
\boxed{₹1800}
$$

---

# 23. Fast Discount Method

Instead of calculating discount separately:

For 10% discount:

$$
SP=0.9MP
$$

For 20% discount:

$$
SP=0.8MP
$$

For 25% discount:

$$
SP=0.75MP
$$

For 30% discount:

$$
SP=0.7MP
$$

---

# 24. Markup + Discount

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%.

Then:

$$
MP=1000(1.5)
=
₹1500
$$

Discount = 20%.

Therefore:

$$
SP=1500(0.8)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
₹200
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 25. Direct Formula — Markup + Discount

If:

- markup = `m%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\left(\frac{100+m}{100}\right)
\left(\frac{100-d}{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 26. Net Profit Formula

From the above:

$$
\frac{SP}{CP}
=
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 40%

Discount = 10%.

$$
40-10-\frac{40(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=40-10-4
$$

$$
\boxed{26\%\ Profit}
$$

---

# 27. Markup + Discount Leading to Loss

Markup = 20%

Discount = 30%.

$$
20-30-\frac{20(30)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-30-6
$$

$$
=-16\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16\%\ Loss}
$$

---

# 28. Finding SP When MP and Discount Are Given

$$
SP=
3000\times\frac{85}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2550}
$$

# 29. Finding SP When MP and Successive Discounts Are Given

Suppose:

MP = ₹2,000

First discount = 10%

Second discount = 20%.

First discount:

$$
2000(0.9)=1800
$$

Second discount:

$$
1800(0.8)=1440
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{SP=₹1440}
$$

---

# 30. Successive Discount Formula

For discounts `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Discount\%=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

10% and 20%:

$$
10+20-\frac{10(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=30-2
$$

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=72\%\ of\ MP
$$

---

# 31. Example — Successive Discount Shortcut

MP = ₹5,000

Discounts = 20% and 10%.

Net discount:

$$
20+10-\frac{20(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=28\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=5000(0.72)
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3600}
$$

# 37. Finding SP From MP for No Profit/Loss

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,200

MP = ₹1,500

Seller wants no profit/no loss.

Therefore:

$$
SP=CP
$$

So required SP:

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

Required discount:

$$
1500-1200=₹300
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{300}{1500}\times100
=

# 38. Finding SP After Tax

Suppose:

Price before tax = ₹1,000

Tax = 10%.

Tax:

$$
1000(0.10)=₹100
$$

Final amount:

$$
1000+100
=
\boxed{₹1100}
$$

> [!warning]
> The tax-inclusive amount is not automatically the seller's profit or SP for profit/loss calculations.

---

# 39. GST-Type Application

If a product's price before tax is ₹2,000 and GST is 18%:

$$
GST=2000\times\frac{18}{100}
$$

$$
=₹360
$$

Final customer price:

$$
2000+360
=
\boxed{₹2360}
$$

---

# 40. Finding Pre-Tax SP From Final Price

Final price = ₹2,360

Tax = 18%.

Since:

$$
Final=SP(1.18)
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=\frac{2360}{1.18}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 45. Equal CP Shortcut

If equal CP articles are sold at different profit percentages:

$$
Overall\ Profit\%=
Average\ of\ Profit\%
$$

Example:

20%, 30%, 40%.

$$
\frac{20+30+40}{3}
=
\boxed{30\%}
$$

Then:

$$
SP=CP(1.3)
$$

for each equal-CP article.

---

# 46. Equal CP — Profit and Loss

If equal-CP articles are sold at:

- 20% profit
- 20% loss

then total SP equals total CP.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{No\ Overall\ Profit/Loss}
$$

---

# 47. Equal SP — Profit and Loss Trap

Two articles are sold for equal SP.

One at 20% profit.

One at 20% loss.

Overall result:

$$
\boxed{4\%\ Loss}
$$

Shortcut:

$$
\boxed{
Loss\%=\frac{20^2}{100}=4\%
}
$$

# 48. Important Reverse Logic

### Given CP → Find SP

Multiply.

Profit:

$$
CP\times\frac{100+p}{100}
$$

Loss:

$$
CP\times\frac{100-l}{100}
$$

### Given SP → Find CP

Divide.

Profit:

$$
SP\times\frac{100}{100+p}
$$

Loss:

$$
SP\times\frac{100}{100-l}
$$

> [!tip]
> **Forward = multiply**
>
> **Reverse = divide**

---

# 49. Common Mistake — Adding Profit Percentage to SP

Suppose:

SP = ₹1,200

Profit = 20%.

Wrong:

$$
1200+20\%=1220
$$

Correct:

$$
CP=\frac{1200}{1.2}
=
₹1000
$$

If the question asks for SP, CP must be known first.

---

# 50. Common Mistake — Subtracting Loss % Directly From CP

If CP = ₹1,000 and loss = 20%:

Correct:

$$
SP=1000(0.8)
=
₹800
$$

Do not interpret 20% as ₹20.

Percentage must be applied to the base.

---

# 51. Common Mistake — Using MP Instead of CP

If:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
1500(0.8)=₹1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000=₹200
$$

The profit is based on CP.
