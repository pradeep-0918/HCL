# Selling Price After Discount

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Selling Price (SP) after discount** is the final price paid by the customer after subtracting the discount from the Marked Price (MP).
>
> The fundamental relationship is:
>
> $$\boxed{SP=MP-Discount}$$
>
> If the discount is `d%`:
>
> $$\boxed{
> SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
> }$$

---

# 2. Basic Relationship

The pricing flow is:

```text
Marked Price (MP)
        ↓
     Discount
        ↓
Selling Price (SP)
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=Discount+SP
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

---

# 3. Finding SP From MP and Discount Amount

If:

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = ₹300.

Then:

$$
SP=2000-300
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

---

# 4. Finding SP From MP and Discount Percentage

If discount = `d%`:

$$
Discount=MP\times\frac d{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP-MP\frac d{100}
$$

$$
SP=MP\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 5. Example — 10% Discount

MP = ₹5,000

Discount = 10%.

$$
SP=
5000\times\frac{90}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹4500}
$$

Discount amount:

$$
5000-4500
=
\boxed{₹500}
$$

---

# 6. Example — 20% Discount

MP = ₹3,000

Discount = 20%.

Remaining percentage:

$$
100-20=80\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=3000\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

---

# 7. Fast Multiplier Method

> [!tip]
> Convert the discount into the percentage that remains.

```text
10% discount → SP = 90% of MP → × 0.90

20% discount → SP = 80% of MP → × 0.80

25% discount → SP = 75% of MP → × 0.75

30% discount → SP = 70% of MP → × 0.70

40% discount → SP = 60% of MP → × 0.60

50% discount → SP = 50% of MP → × 0.50
```

This is usually the fastest approach in aptitude exams.

# 11. Finding SP Using Fraction

If discount = `1/5` of MP:

$$
SP=MP-\frac15MP
$$

$$
SP=\frac45MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac45MP
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹2,500.

$$
SP=
2500\times\frac45
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 13. Ratio Method

If discount = `d%`:

$$
MP:SP=100:(100-d)
$$

Example:

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=\frac45MP
}
$$

---

# 18. Reverse Formula

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
SP\frac{100}{100-d}
}
$$

This is the reverse form of the SP formula.

# 20. Finding Discount Percentage From SP and MP

Using:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹4,000

SP = ₹3,200.

$$
Discount\%=
\frac{4000-3200}{4000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 21. Important Base Concept

> [!important]
> Discount percentage is calculated using **MP as the base**.

```text
Discount % → MP is the base
Profit %   → CP is the base
Loss %     → CP is the base
```

Example:

MP = ₹5,000

SP = ₹4,000

CP = ₹3,500.

Discount:

$$
\frac{5000-4000}{5000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

Profit:

$$
\frac{4000-3500}{3500}\times100
=
14.29\%
$$

---

# 22. Discount Does Not Determine Profit by Itself

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
1500(0.8)=₹1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
₹200
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 23. Discount Can Result in Loss

Example:

CP = ₹2,000

MP = ₹2,500

Discount = 30%.

SP:

$$
2500(0.7)=₹1750
$$

Since:

$$
1750<2000
$$

there is a loss.

Loss:

$$
2000-1750
=
₹250
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{250}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{12.5\%}
$$

# 25. Direct Formula — Markup + Discount

If:

- markup = `m%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
}
$$

---

# 26. Net Profit Formula

From the previous formula:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 40%

Discount = 10%.

$$
40-10-\frac{40(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=40-10-4
$$

$$
\boxed{26\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 27. No-Profit / No-Loss Condition

For no profit and no loss:

$$
SP=CP
$$

If markup = `m%`, required discount:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 25%.

$$
d=
\frac{100(25)}{125}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 28. Desired Profit

Suppose:

CP = ₹2,000

Desired profit = 20%

Discount = 20%.

Desired SP:

$$
SP=2000(1.2)
=
₹2400
$$

Since 20% discount means:

$$
SP=80\%\ MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=
\frac{2400}{0.8}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 29. General Formula — Required MP

If:

- CP = `C`
- desired profit = `p%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
CP\frac{100+p}{100-d}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹4,000

Desired profit = 25%

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP=
4000\times\frac{125}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹6250}
$$

Check:

20% discount:

$$
6250(0.8)=5000
$$

Profit:

$$
5000-4000=1000
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# 30. Successive Discounts

If two discounts are applied:

- `a%`
- `b%`

then:

$$
SP=
MP
\left(1-\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac b{100}\right)
$$

Equivalent discount:

$$
\boxed{
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

---

MP = ₹5,000.

First discount:

$$
5000(0.8)=4000
$$

Second discount:

$$
4000(0.9)=3600
$$

Final SP:

$$
\boxed{₹3600}
$$

Equivalent discount:

$$
20+10-\frac{20(10)}{100}
=
\boxed{28\%}
$$

# 36. General Comparison Formula

For the same MP and discounts `d₁` and `d₂`:

$$
\boxed{
|SP_1-SP_2|=
MP\frac{|d_1-d_2|}{100}
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹8,000

Discounts = 15% and 25%.

Difference:

$$
8000\times\frac{10}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹800}
$$

# 45. Important Reverse Percentage Concept

Suppose an article is sold after a 20% discount.

Then:

$$
SP=80\%\ MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=\frac{100}{80}SP
$$

$$
=1.25SP
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
MP=125\%\ of\ SP
}
$$

So MP is **25% greater than SP**.

> [!warning]
> A 20% discount does not mean MP is only 20% greater than SP.

# 46. Recovery After Discount

If a price is reduced by `d%`, the percentage increase needed to return to the original price is:

$$
\boxed{
Recovery\%=
\frac{100d}{100-d}
}
$$

Example:

Discount = 20%.

$$
Recovery=
\frac{100(20)}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Check:

$$
100\rightarrow80
$$

25% of 80:

$$
20
$$

$$
80+20=100
$$

# 48. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Subtracting the Percentage Directly

Wrong:

MP = ₹5,000

20% discount:

$$
5000-20=4980
$$

Correct:

$$
5000\times\frac{20}{100}=1000
$$

Then:

$$
5000-1000
=
\boxed{₹4000}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Using CP as the Discount Base

Discount is based on MP.

### Mistake 3 — Using SP as the Discount Base

Discount percentage is not:

$$
\frac{Discount}{SP}\times100
$$

### Mistake 4 — Adding Successive Discounts

20% + 10% is not 30%.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

### Mistake 5 — Confusing Discount With Loss

Discount:

$$
MP-SP
$$

Loss:

$$
CP-SP
$$

### Mistake 6 — Forgetting the Order of Operations

For:

```text
Markup → Discount → Tax
```

follow that exact order unless the question specifies another order.

# 51. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Main Formula

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

### Memory Pattern

```text
MP = 100%
     ↓
Discount = d%
     ↓
SP = (100-d)%
```

### Example

20% discount:

$$
MP=100
$$

$$
SP=80
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=80\%\ of\ MP
}
$$

### Reverse

If:

$$
SP=80\%\ MP
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
MP=125\%\ of\ SP
}
$$

### Golden Rule

> **First identify MP.**
>
> **Apply discount to MP.**
>
> **The result is SP.**
>
> **Only after finding SP should you compare it with CP for profit/loss.**
