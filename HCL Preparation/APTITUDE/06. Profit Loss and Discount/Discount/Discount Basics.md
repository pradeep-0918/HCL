# Discount Basics

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Discount** is the reduction given by a seller from the **Marked Price (MP)** of an article.
>
> The basic relationship is:
>
> $$\boxed{Discount=MP-SP}$$
>
> Discount percentage is always calculated on **Marked Price**:
>
> $$\boxed{
> Discount\%=
> \frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
> }$$

---

# 2. Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CP | Cost Price |
| MP | Marked Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| Discount | Reduction from MP |
| Discount % | Discount as a percentage of MP |

The normal transaction flow is:

```text
Cost Price (CP)
      ↓
   Markup
      ↓
Marked Price (MP)
      ↓
  Discount
      ↓
Selling Price (SP)
```

---

# 3. Marked Price

**Marked Price** is the price written or displayed by the seller before discount.

Example:

A shirt has a price tag of ₹2,000.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{MP=₹2000}
$$

If the seller gives a discount, the customer pays less than ₹2,000.

---

# 4. Discount

If:

$$
MP=₹2000
$$

and:

$$
SP=₹1600
$$

then:

$$
Discount=2000-1600
$$

$$
\boxed{₹400}
$$

---

# 5. Discount Percentage

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

For the above example:

$$
Discount\%=
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

> [!important]
> **Discount percentage uses MP as the denominator, not CP and not SP.**

---

# 6. Golden Formula

> [!important]
> Memorize these three formulas:

### Discount Amount

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

### Discount Percentage

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

### Selling Price

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

---

# 7. Finding Discount Amount

If MP and discount percentage are given:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=
MP\frac{d}{100}
}
$$

where `d` is the discount percentage.

Example:

MP = ₹5,000

Discount = 20%.

$$
Discount=
5000\times\frac{20}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 8. Finding SP From MP and Discount

Once discount is known:

$$
SP=MP-Discount
$$

Example:

MP = ₹5,000

Discount = ₹1,000.

$$
SP=5000-1000
$$

$$
\boxed{₹4000}
$$

---

# 9. Direct Formula for SP

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP-\frac d{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 10. Example — 20% Discount

MP = ₹2,500

Discount = 20%.

$$
SP=
2500\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Discount amount:

$$
2500-2000
=
\boxed{₹500}
$$

---

| Discount | SP as % of MP | Multiplier |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 5% | 95% | 0.95 |
| 10% | 90% | 0.90 |
| 15% | 85% | 0.85 |
| 20% | 80% | 0.80 |

| 40% | 60% | 0.60 |
| 50% | 50% | 0.50 |
| 60% | 40% | 0.40 |

> [!tip]
> For fast aptitude calculations, directly multiply MP by the remaining percentage.

# 12. Example — 25% Discount

MP = ₹1,600

Discount = 25%.

Remaining percentage:

$$
100-25=75\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=1600\times\frac{75}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1200}
$$

# 13. Finding MP From SP and Discount %

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
SP\frac{100}{100-d}
}
$$

---

# 14. Example — Find MP

SP = ₹1,600

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP=
1600\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

Check:

$$
2000-1600=400
$$

$$
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

---

# 15. Finding MP From Discount Amount and Percentage

If:

- Discount = `D`
- Discount percentage = `d%`

then:

$$
D=\frac d{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=\frac{100D}{d}
}
$$

Example:

Discount = ₹300

Discount = 15%.

$$
MP=
\frac{100(300)}{15}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 16. Finding SP From Discount Amount

If:

MP = ₹3,000

Discount = ₹500.

Then:

$$
SP=3000-500
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

---

# 17. Finding Discount From MP and SP

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹4,000

SP = ₹3,200.

$$
Discount=4000-3200
$$

$$
\boxed{₹800}
$$

---

# 18. Finding Discount Percentage From MP and SP

$$
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
$$

Example:

MP = ₹4,000

SP = ₹3,200.

$$
Discount=800
$$

Therefore:

$$
Discount\%=
\frac{800}{4000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 19. MP as 100%

> [!important]
> In discount problems, assume:
>
> $$\boxed{MP=100}$$

If discount = 20%:

$$
Discount=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100-20
$$

$$
\boxed{80}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{SP=80\%\ of\ MP}
$$

This is the fastest way to understand discount questions.

---

# 20. Ratio Method

If discount = `d%`:

$$
MP:SP=100:(100-d)
$$

Example:

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 21. Example — Ratio Method

SP = ₹1,600

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP:SP=5:4
$$

4 parts = ₹1,600.

1 part:

$$
1600/4=400
$$

MP:

$$
5(400)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 23. Discount and Cost Price

> [!important]
> Discount itself is calculated from MP, but the **final profit/loss** is calculated using CP.

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%.

First calculate SP:

$$
SP=1500(0.8)
$$

$$
=₹1200
$$

Then compare SP with CP:

$$
1200-1000
=
₹200
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 24. Important Distinction

### Discount %

Based on:

$$
\boxed{MP}
$$

### Profit %

Based on:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

### Loss %

Based on:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

This distinction is extremely important.

---

# 25. Discount Does Not Automatically Mean Loss

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

Discount = 20%.

SP:

$$
1500(0.8)=1200
$$

Although a 20% discount is given:

$$
SP>CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 26. Discount Does Not Automatically Mean Profit

Example:

CP = ₹2,000

MP = ₹2,500

Discount = 30%.

SP:

$$
2500(0.7)
=
₹1750
$$

Since:

$$
SP<CP
$$

there is a loss:

$$
2000-1750
=
₹250
$$

Loss percentage:

$$
\frac{250}{2000}\times100
=
\boxed{12.5\%\ loss}
$$

---

# 27. Discount + Profit

1500(0.9)
=
₹1350
$$

Profit:

$$
1350-1000
=
₹350
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\boxed{35\%}
$$

---

# 28. Discount + Loss

Suppose:

CP = ₹1,500

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = 30%.

SP:

$$
2000(0.7)
=
₹1400
$$

1500-1400

$$

\frac{100}{1500}\times100

\boxed{6.67\%\ loss}

# 29. Markup and Discount

Usually:

$$
CP<MP
$$

The seller may first increase the price above CP:

$$
CP\rightarrow MP
$$

and then offer a discount:

$$
MP\rightarrow SP
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%.

$$
MP=1000(1.5)
=
₹1500
$$

Discount = 20%.

$$
SP=1500(0.8)
=
₹1200
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 30. General Formula — Markup + Discount

Markup = `m%`

Discount = `d%`

Then:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
}
$$

---

# 31. Net Profit From Markup and Discount

The net profit percentage is:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%

Discount = 20%.

$$
50-20-\frac{50(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=50-20-10
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 32. Net Loss From Markup and Discount

Markup = 20%

Discount = 30%.

$$
20-30-\frac{20(30)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-30-6
$$

$$
=-16\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16\%\ loss}
$$

---

# 33. No Profit — No Loss Condition

Suppose markup = `m%`.

What discount gives no profit/no loss?

We need:

$$
SP=CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
m-d-\frac{md}{100}=0
$$

Solving:

$$
\boxed{
d=\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 25%.

$$
d=
\frac{2500}{125}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

So:

25% markup + 20% discount

gives:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

---

# 34. Maximum Discount Without Loss

If:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

The lowest SP without loss is:

$$
SP=CP=₹1000
$$

Therefore:

$$
Discount=1500-1000
=
₹500
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{500}{1500}\times100
=
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 35. Successive Discounts

Sometimes multiple discounts are given.

Example:

20% discount followed by 10% discount.

These are **not** equal to a 30% discount.

First:

$$
MP(0.8)
$$

Then:

$$
MP(0.8)(0.9)
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=0.72MP
$$

Net discount:

$$
1-0.72
=
0.28
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{28\%\ discount}
$$

---

# 36. Successive Discount Formula

For discounts `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
Net\ Discount\%=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

10% and 20%:

$$
10+20-\frac{10(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=30-2
$$

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

---

# 37. Three Successive Discounts

For discounts `a%`, `b%`, and `c%`:

$$
SP=
MP
\left(1-\frac a{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac b{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac c{100}\right)
$$

Example:

10%, 20%, 30%.

$$
SP=
MP(0.9)(0.8)(0.7)
$$

$$
=0.504MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{49.6\%\ net\ discount}
$$

---

# 38. Discount Equivalence

Suppose a seller offers:

- 20% discount

Another seller offers:

- 10% followed by 10%.

First seller:

$$
SP=0.8MP
$$

Second seller:

$$
SP=0.9\times0.9MP
$$

$$
=0.81MP
$$

Therefore:

- First discount = 20%
- Second discount = 19%

So they are **not equivalent**.

---

# 39. Finding Equivalent Single Discount

For successive discounts `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
Equivalent\ Discount=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

25% and 20%.

$$
25+20-\frac{25(20)}{100}
$$

$$
=45-5
$$

$$
\boxed{40\%}
$$

---

# 40. Finding Second Discount

Suppose first discount = `a%` and equivalent total discount = `D%`.

Then:

$$
D=a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
$$

Solve for `b`:

$$
D-a=b\left(1-\frac a{100}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
b=
\frac{100(D-a)}{100-a}
}
$$

Example:

First discount = 20%.

Equivalent discount = 40%.

$$
b=
\frac{100(40-20)}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

Check:

$$
20+25-\frac{20(25)}{100}
=
45-5
=
40\%
$$

---

# 47. General Formula — Desired Profit

If:

- markup = `m%`
- desired profit = `p%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
(100+m)(100-d)=100(100+p)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100(m-p)}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 50%

Desired profit = 20%.

$$
d=
\frac{100(50-20)}{150}
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 49. Discount vs Profit Percentage

Do not confuse these:

| Quantity | Base |
|---|---|
| Discount % | MP |
| Profit % | CP |
| Loss % | CP |

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

SP = ₹1,200.

Discount:

$$
\frac{1500-1200}{1500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Profit:

$$
\frac{1200-1000}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Both happen to be 20%, but their bases are different.

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Using CP as Discount Base

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Discount}{CP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Using SP as Discount Base

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Discount}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 3 — Assuming Discount = Loss

Discount is:

$$
MP-SP
$$

Loss is:

$$
CP-SP
$$

### Mistake 4 — Adding Successive Discounts

Wrong:

$$
20\%+10\%=30\%
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

### Mistake 5 — Forgetting Markup

If MP is above CP, determine MP first when markup is given.
