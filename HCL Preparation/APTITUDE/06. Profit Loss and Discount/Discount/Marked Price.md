# Marked Price

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Marked Price (MP)** is the price initially displayed or written on an article by the seller before any discount is applied.

The normal relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
MP\rightarrow Discount\rightarrow SP
}
$$

where:

- **MP** = Marked Price
- **SP** = Selling Price

If a discount is given:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

---

# 2. Marked Price vs Cost Price

These two terms must not be confused.

### Cost Price

The price paid by the seller to purchase the article.

$$
\boxed{CP=\text{buying price}}
$$

### Marked Price

The price written/displayed by the seller before discount.

$$
\boxed{MP=\text{tag/list price}}
$$

Example:

A shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹1,000 and marks it at ₹1,500.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{CP=₹1000}
$$

$$
\boxed{MP=₹1500}
$$

---

# 3. Marked Price vs Selling Price

### Marked Price

Price before discount.

### Selling Price

Final price paid by the customer after discount.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = ₹300.

$$
SP=2000-300
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1700}
$$

---

# 4. Basic Transaction Flow

```text
Seller buys article
        ↓
      CP
        ↓
   Adds markup
        ↓
      MP
        ↓
 Gives discount
        ↓
      SP
        ↓
 Customer pays
```

The complete relationship is:

$$
\boxed{
CP\rightarrow MP\rightarrow SP
}
$$

---

# 5. Marked Price Is Not Necessarily the Selling Price

Example:

$$
MP=₹3000
$$

Discount = 20%.

Then:

$$
SP=3000\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{MP\neq SP}
$$

when a discount is given.

---

# 6. When MP Equals SP

If there is no discount:

$$
Discount=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP
$$

Example:

MP = ₹2,000

Discount = 0%.

$$
\boxed{SP=₹2000}
$$

---

# 7. Marked Price and Discount

The fundamental formula is:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=SP+Discount
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP-Discount
}
$$

These three formulas should be memorized.

---

# 8. Discount Percentage Based on MP

Discount percentage is calculated using MP as the base.

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Discount=MP-SP
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> **Discount % is always based on Marked Price, not CP or SP.**

---

# 9. Example — Find Discount Percentage

MP = ₹5,000

SP = ₹4,000.

Discount:

$$
5000-4000
=
₹1000
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{1000}{5000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 10. Finding SP From MP

If discount = `d%`:

$$
Discount=MP\frac d{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=MP-MP\frac d{100}
$$

$$
SP=MP\left(1-\frac d{100}\right)
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 11. Example — 25% Discount

MP = ₹4,000

Discount = 25%.

$$
SP=
4000\times\frac{75}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

Discount amount:

$$
4000-3000
=
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 12. Finding MP From SP

If discount = `d%`:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=
SP\frac{100}{100-d}
}
$$

Example:

SP = ₹2,400

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP=
2400\times\frac{100}{80}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 13. Finding MP From Discount Amount

If discount amount = `D`:

$$
MP=SP+D
$$

Example:

SP = ₹2,500

Discount = ₹500.

$$
MP=2500+500
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 14. Finding MP From Discount Amount and Percentage

If:

- Discount = `D`
- Discount = `d%`

then:

$$
D=\frac d{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=\frac{100D}{d}
}
$$

Example:

Discount = ₹600

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP=
\frac{100(600)}{20}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

---

# 15. MP = 100% Method

> [!tip]
> In percentage-based questions, assume:
>
> $$\boxed{MP=100}$$

If discount = 20%:

$$
Discount=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100-20
$$

$$
\boxed{80}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
SP=80\%\ of\ MP
}
$$

This is one of the fastest mental methods.

---

# 16. Marked Price Ratio

If discount = `d%`:

$$
MP:SP=100:(100-d)
$$

Example:

Discount = 20%.

$$
MP:SP=100:80
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{5:4}
$$

---

# 17. Example — Ratio Method

SP = ₹2,000

Discount = 20%.

Since:

$$
MP:SP=5:4
$$

4 parts = ₹2,000.

1 part:

$$
2000/4=500
$$

MP:

$$
5(500)
=
\boxed{₹2500}
$$

---

# 18. Finding Discount From MP and SP

Suppose:

MP = ₹3,500

SP = ₹2,800.

Then:

$$
Discount=3500-2800
$$

$$
\boxed{₹700}
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{700}{3500}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

# 19. Marked Price and Cost Price

The relationship between MP and CP is often expressed through **markup**.

If markup = `m%`:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP\left(1+\frac m{100}\right)
}
$$

---

# 20. Example — Markup

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%.

$$
MP=
1000\times\frac{150}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1500}
$$

---

# 21. Finding MP From CP and Markup

Formula:

$$
\boxed{
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹2,500

Markup = 40%.

$$
MP=
2500\times\frac{140}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹3500}
$$

---

# 22. Finding CP From MP and Markup

If markup = `m%`:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
CP=
MP\frac{100}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹3,000

Markup = 50%.

$$
CP=
3000\times\frac{100}{150}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 23. Markup Percentage

Markup is the increase from CP to MP.

$$
\boxed{
Markup=MP-CP
}
$$

Markup percentage:

$$
\boxed{
Markup\%=
\frac{MP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> Markup % is calculated on **CP**, while discount % is calculated on **MP**.

---

# 24. Markup vs Discount

| Concept | Formula | Base |
|---|---|---|
| Markup | MP − CP | CP |
| Discount | MP − SP | MP |
| Profit | SP − CP | CP |
| Loss | CP − SP | CP |

This table is extremely important.

---

# 25. Example — Markup and Discount Together

CP = ₹1,000

Markup = 50%.

$$
MP=1000(1.5)
=
₹1500
$$

Discount = 20%.

$$
SP=1500(0.8)
=
₹1200
$$

Profit:

$$
1200-1000
=
₹200
$$

Profit percentage:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 26. Complete Formula

If:

- CP = `C`
- markup = `m%`
- discount = `d%`

then:

$$
MP=C\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

and:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
C\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
}
$$

---

# 27. Net Profit From MP

The profit is:

$$
Profit=SP-CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
Profit\%=
\boxed{
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 30%

Discount = 10%.

$$
30-10-\frac{30(10)}{100}
$$

$$
=30-10-3
$$

$$
\boxed{17\%\ profit}
$$

---

# 28. Marked Price and No-Profit Condition

If the seller wants:

$$
SP=CP
$$

then the discount must reduce MP back to CP.

Suppose markup = 50%.

Take:

$$
CP=100
$$

Then:

$$
MP=150
$$

For no profit:

$$
SP=100
$$

Discount:

$$
150-100=50
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{50}{150}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

---

# 29. General No-Profit Discount

If markup = `m%`:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100m}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

Markup = 40%.

$$
d=
\frac{100(40)}{140}
$$

$$
\boxed{28\frac47\%}
$$

---

# 30. Maximum Discount Without Loss

The maximum discount that can be given without incurring a loss is the discount that makes:

$$
SP=CP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Maximum\ Discount\%=
\frac{MP-CP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

This is also the markup converted to MP as the base.

---

# 31. Example

CP = ₹800

MP = ₹1,000.

Maximum discount without loss:

$$
\frac{1000-800}{1000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

At exactly 20% discount:

$$
SP=₹800
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{No\ Profit,\ No\ Loss}
$$

---

# 40. Marked Price and Successive Discounts

$$

$$

\left(1-\frac{d_1}{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac{d_2}{100}\right)
\left(1-\frac{d_3}{100}\right)
$$

# 49. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing MP With CP

MP is the listed price.

CP is the buying price.

### Mistake 2 — Confusing MP With SP

MP is before discount.

SP is after discount.

### Mistake 3 — Using CP as the Discount Base

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Discount}{CP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 4 — Adding Successive Discounts

20% + 10% is not 30%.

It is:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

### Mistake 5 — Assuming MP Must Be the Actual Selling Price

If a discount exists:

$$
SP<MP
$$

### Mistake 6 — Assuming MP Is Always Greater Than CP

In normal markup-based problems:

$$
MP>CP
$$

but do not assume this without considering the problem statement.
