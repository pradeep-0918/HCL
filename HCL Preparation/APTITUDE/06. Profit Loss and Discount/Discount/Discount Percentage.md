# Discount Percentage

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Discount Percentage** tells us what percentage of the Marked Price has been reduced from the original marked price.

The golden formula is:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

Since:

$$
Discount=MP-SP
$$

we can also write:

$$
\boxed{
Discount\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

> [!important]
> **Discount percentage is always calculated on Marked Price (MP).**

---

# 2. Basic Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MP | Marked Price |
| SP | Selling Price |
| Discount | Reduction from MP |
| Discount % | Discount as a percentage of MP |

Basic relationship:

$$
\boxed{
MP=SP+Discount
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Discount=MP-SP
}
$$

---

# 3. Basic Example

MP = ₹2,000

SP = ₹1,600.

Discount:

$$
2000-1600=₹400
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 4. Why MP Is the Denominator

Suppose:

MP = ₹1,000

Discount = ₹200.

The discount is ₹200 out of the original ₹1,000 marked price.

Therefore:

$$
\frac{200}{1000}\times100
=
20\%
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{20\%\ discount}
$$

> [!warning]
> Do **not** calculate discount percentage using SP as the denominator.

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Discount}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 5. Formula Triangle

```text
          Discount
        ─────────────
        MP       SP

Discount = MP - SP

Discount % = Discount / MP × 100

SP = MP - Discount

MP = SP + Discount
```

For percentage problems:

$$
\boxed{
D\%= \frac{D}{MP}\times100
}
$$

---

# 6. Finding Discount Percentage From MP and SP

Use:

$$
\boxed{
D\%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹5,000

SP = ₹4,250.

Discount:

$$
5000-4250
=
₹750
$$

Discount percentage:

$$
\frac{750}{5000}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{15\%}
$$

---

# 7. Finding Discount Amount From Discount Percentage

If:

MP = `M`

Discount = `d%`

then:

$$
\boxed{
D=M\frac{d}{100}
}
$$

Example:

MP = ₹4,000

Discount = 25%.

$$
D=4000\times\frac{25}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1000}
$$

---

# 8. Finding SP From Discount Percentage

After discount:

$$
SP=MP-Discount
$$

Since:

$$
Discount=\frac d{100}MP
$$

we get:

$$
SP=MP-\frac d{100}MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
}
$$

---

# 9. Example

MP = ₹3,000

Discount = 20%.

$$
SP=
3000\times\frac{80}{100}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2400}
$$

---

# 11. Finding MP From Discount Amount and Discount Percentage

Discount = `D`

$$
\boxed{

$$
MP=
\frac{100(450)}{15}

$$
\boxed{₹3000}
$$

# 14. MP = 100% Method

> [!tip]
> For percentage questions, assume:
>
> $$\boxed{MP=100}$$

If discount = 30%:

$$
Discount=30
$$

Therefore:

$$
SP=100-30
$$

$$
\boxed{70}
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
SP=70\%\ of\ MP
}
$$

This method is extremely useful for mental calculations.

# 16. Ratio Method

If discount = `d%`:

$$
MP:SP=100:(100-d)
$$

Example:

Discount = 25%.

$$
MP:SP=100:75
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{4:3}
$$

---

Discount = 25%.

$$
MP:SP=4:3
$$

$$
1500/3=500
$$

MP:

$$
4(500)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

# 18. Finding Discount Percentage From Ratio

$$
MP:SP=a:b
$$

$$
\boxed{

\frac{a-b}{a}\times100
}
$$

Example:

$$
MP:SP=5:4
$$

Discount:

$$
5-4=1
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac15\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

---

# 19. Finding Discount Percentage From SP:MP Ratio

$$
SP:MP=3:4

4-3=1

# 23. Discount and Profit Are Different

> [!important]
> Discount percentage and profit percentage have different bases.

### Discount %

Based on:

$$
\boxed{MP}
$$

### Profit %

Based on:

$$
\boxed{CP}
$$

Example:

CP = ₹1,000

MP = ₹1,500

SP = ₹1,200.

Discount:

$$
\frac{1500-1200}{1500}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Profit:

$$
\frac{1200-1000}{1000}\times100
=
\boxed{20\%}
$$

Both happen to be 20%, but they are calculated using different bases.

$$
SP>CP

\boxed{20\%\ profit}
$$

# 27. Direct Formula for Markup + Discount

If markup = `m%` and discount = `d%`:

$$
MP=CP\frac{100+m}{100}
$$

Then:

$$
SP=MP\frac{100-d}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
SP=
CP
\frac{(100+m)(100-d)}{10000}
}
$$

Net profit:

$$
\boxed{
Profit\%=
m-d-\frac{md}{100}
}
$$

# 29. General Formula — Desired Profit

If markup = `m%` and desired profit = `p%`:

$$
\boxed{
d=
\frac{100(m-p)}{100+m}
}
$$

Example:

\frac{100(60-20)}{160}

$$
\boxed{25\%}

# 33. Successive Discount Formula

For discounts `a%` and `b%`:

$$
\boxed{
Equivalent\ Discount=
a+b-\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

Example:

20% and 15%.

$$
20+15-\frac{20(15)}{100}
$$

$$
=35-3
$$

$$
\boxed{32\%}
$$

# 36. Finding an Unknown Discount

Suppose first discount = 20%.

Equivalent discount = 40%.

Let second discount = `x%`.

$$
40=
20+x-\frac{20x}{100}

$$
20=0.8x
$$

$$
\boxed{x=25\%}

# 41. Reverse Discount Percentage

Suppose an item is sold for ₹1,800 after a 10% discount.

Find MP.

Since:

$$
SP=90\%\ MP
$$

$$
1800=0.9MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=\frac{1800}{0.9}
$$

$$
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 42. Reverse Discount — Ratio Method

10% discount means:

$$
MP:SP=100:90
$$

Simplify:

$$
10:9
$$

If SP = ₹1,800:

9 parts = 1,800.

1 part:

$$
200
$$

MP:

$$
10(200)
=
\boxed{₹2000}
$$

---

# 43. Reverse Percentage Trap

If an item is sold at a 20% discount:

$$
SP=80\%\ MP
$$

Therefore:

$$
MP=125\%\ SP
$$

So MP is 25% greater than SP.

> [!warning]
> A 20% discount does **not** mean the MP is 20% higher than SP.

---

# 44. Discount vs Price Increase

If price is reduced by 20%:

$$
100\rightarrow80
$$

To return from 80 to 100:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\times100
=
25\%
$$

Therefore:

> 20% discount requires a **25% increase** to return to the original price.

---

# 45. Recovery Formula

If price decreases by `d%`, required increase to recover is:

$$
\boxed{
Recovery\%=
\frac{100d}{100-d}
}
$$

Example:

Discount = 25%.

$$
Recovery=
\frac{100(25)}{75}
$$

$$
\boxed{33\frac13\%}
$$

# 50. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Wrong Denominator

Wrong:

$$
\frac{Discount}{SP}\times100
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
$$

### Mistake 2 — Confusing Discount With Loss

Discount:

$$
MP-SP
$$

Loss:

$$
CP-SP
$$

### Mistake 3 — Adding Successive Discounts

Wrong:

$$
20+10=30\%
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{28\%}
$$

### Mistake 4 — Directly Subtracting Percentage From Rupees

A percentage must be applied to its base.

### Mistake 5 — Confusing Profit % With Discount %

Profit % → CP base.

Discount % → MP base.
