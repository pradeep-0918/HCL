# Whole Numbers

## 1. Overview

> [!summary] Core Idea
> Whole numbers are the natural numbers together with `0`.

The set of whole numbers is:

$$
\mathbb{W} = \{0,1,2,3,4,\ldots\}
$$

### Relationship

$$
\boxed{\text{Whole Numbers} = \text{Natural Numbers} \cup \{0\}}
$$

Therefore:

- Natural numbers → `1, 2, 3, 4, ...`
- Whole numbers → `0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...`

> [!important] Key Difference
> The only difference between the standard definitions is:
>
> **Natural numbers start from `1`.**
>
> **Whole numbers start from `0`.**

---

## 2. Important Properties

### 2.1 Smallest Whole Number

The smallest whole number is:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

### 2.2 Largest Whole Number

There is **no largest whole number**.

The sequence continues indefinitely:

$$
0,1,2,3,4,\ldots
$$

### 2.3 Successor

The successor of a whole number `n` is:

$$
\boxed{n+1}
$$

Example:

$$
\text{Successor of } 0 = 1
$$

$$
\text{Successor of } 25 = 26
$$

### 2.4 Predecessor

The predecessor of a whole number `n > 0` is:

$$
\boxed{n-1}
$$

Example:

$$
\text{Predecessor of }25=24
$$

> [!warning] Important Trap
> `0` has **no predecessor in the set of whole numbers**.
>
> Its predecessor mathematically is `-1`, but `-1` is not a whole number.

---

## 3. Classification of Whole Numbers

Whole numbers include:

### Even Whole Numbers

$$
0,2,4,6,8,\ldots
$$

General form:

$$
\boxed{2n}
$$

where:

$$
n=0,1,2,3,\ldots
$$

### Odd Whole Numbers

$$
1,3,5,7,9,\ldots
$$

General form:

$$
\boxed{2n+1}
$$

where:

$$
n=0,1,2,3,\ldots
$$

> [!important] Remember
> **0 is an even number.**

Because:

$$
0=2\times0
$$

---

## 4. Arithmetic Properties

### 4.1 Closure Property

Whole numbers are closed under:

- Addition
- Multiplication

If `a` and `b` are whole numbers:

$$
a+b \in \mathbb{W}
$$

and

$$
a\times b \in \mathbb{W}
$$

Example:

$$
5+7=12
$$

$$
5\times7=35
$$

Both are whole numbers.

---

### 4.2 Subtraction

Whole numbers are **not closed under subtraction**.

Example:

$$
3-5=-2
$$

`-2` is not a whole number.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Whole numbers are not closed under subtraction}}
$$

---

### 4.3 Division

Whole numbers are **not closed under division**.

Example:

$$
5\div2=2.5
$$

`2.5` is not a whole number.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Whole numbers are not closed under division}}
$$

---

## 5. Important Properties Table

| Operation | Closed in Whole Numbers? |
|---|---|
| Addition | ✅ Yes |
| Multiplication | ✅ Yes |
| Subtraction | ❌ No |
| Division | ❌ No |

> [!tip] Exam Pattern
> If a question asks **"Which operation is closed for whole numbers?"**, the safest answers are:
>
> **Addition and multiplication.**

## 6. Identity Elements

### Additive Identity

Adding `0` does not change a number:

$$
a+0=a
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0\text{ is the additive identity}}
$$

Example:

$$
25+0=25
$$

### Multiplicative Identity

Multiplying by `1` does not change a number:

$$
a\times1=a
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1\text{ is the multiplicative identity}}
$$

Example:

$$
25\times1=25
$$

---

## 7. Zero Properties

### Addition

$$
\boxed{a+0=a}
$$

### Multiplication

$$
\boxed{a\times0=0}
$$

### Division by Zero

Division by zero is **undefined**:

$$
\boxed{\frac{a}{0}\text{ is undefined}}
$$

> [!warning] Critical Trap
> Never write:
>
> $$\frac{a}{0}=0$$
>
> This is incorrect.

## 8. Important Aptitude Patterns

### Pattern 1 — Identify Whole Numbers

**Question:**

> Which of the following is a whole number?

Examples:

- `0` → ✅
- `7` → ✅
- `25` → ✅
- `-3` → ❌
- `2.5` → ❌
- `1/2` → ❌

**Pattern:**

A whole number must be:

$$
\boxed{0\text{ or a positive integer}}
$$

---

### Pattern 2 — Natural vs Whole Number

**Question:**

> Which number is a whole number but not a natural number?

Answer:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

> [!tip] Fast Recognition
> If you see:
>
> **"Whole but not natural" → `0`**

---

### Pattern 3 — Successor and Predecessor

**Question:**

> Find the successor of `999`.

$$
999+1=1000
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{1000}
$$

**Question:**

> Find the predecessor of `500`.

$$
500-1=499
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{499}
$$

---

### Pattern 4 — Number of Whole Numbers in a Range

For whole numbers from `a` to `b`, where `a` and `b` are included:

$$
\boxed{n=b-a+1}
$$

Example:

How many whole numbers are there from `10` to `50`?

$$
n=50-10+1
$$

$$
\boxed{41}
$$

> [!warning] Common Trap
> Don't use:
>
> $$50-10=40$$
>
> because both endpoints are included.

---

### Pattern 5 — Even/Odd Identification

A whole number is:

**Even:**

$$
\boxed{2n}
$$

**Odd:**

$$
\boxed{2n+1}
$$

Examples:

| Number | Type |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Even |
| 1 | Odd |
| 2 | Even |
| 3 | Odd |
| 10 | Even |
| 11 | Odd |

## 9. Number Properties You Must Know

### Even + Even

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}}
$$

### Odd + Odd

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}}
$$

### Even + Odd

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}}
$$

### Even × Even

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}}
$$

### Even × Odd

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}}
$$

### Odd × Odd

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}}
$$

> [!tip] Memory Trick
> **Multiplication:** If at least one number is even → result is even.
>
> **Addition:** Same parity → even; different parity → odd.

> [!important] Must Remember

| Concept | Formula / Result |

| Smallest whole number | `0` |

| Predecessor of `n` | `n - 1` for `n > 0` |
| Even number | `2n` |
| Odd number | `2n + 1` |

| Multiplicative identity | `1` |
| `a × 0` | `0` |
| `a ÷ 0` | Undefined |

## 11. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

- ❌ Thinking `0` is not a whole number.
- ❌ Thinking `0` is odd.
- ❌ Saying whole numbers are closed under subtraction.
- ❌ Saying whole numbers are closed under division.
- ❌ Treating `-1, -2, ...` as whole numbers.
- ❌ Forgetting that `0` is the additive identity.
- ❌ Dividing a number by zero.
- ❌ Forgetting `+1` when counting an inclusive range.

## 12. Exam Strategy

> [!tip] Fast Approach

When you see a question involving whole numbers:

1. Check whether `0` is involved.
2. Check whether negative numbers are present.
3. Check whether fractions or decimals are present.
4. Identify whether the question tests:
   - Classification
   - Successor/predecessor
   - Even/odd
   - Closure
   - Identity
   - Counting
5. Apply the direct property or formula.
