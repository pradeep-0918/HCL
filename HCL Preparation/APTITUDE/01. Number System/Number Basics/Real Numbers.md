# Real Numbers

## 1. Overview

> [!summary] Core Idea
> Real numbers are all numbers that can be represented on the number line.

The set of real numbers is represented by:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{R}}
$$

Real numbers include both:

- Rational numbers
- Irrational numbers

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{Q}\cup\mathbb{I}}
$$

where:

- $\mathbb{Q}$ = Rational Numbers
- $\mathbb{I}$ = Irrational Numbers

---

## 2. Number System Hierarchy

The important hierarchy is:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbb{N}
\subset
\mathbb{W}
\subset
\mathbb{Z}
\subset
\mathbb{Q}
\subset
\mathbb{R}
}
$$

### Meaning

$$
\text{Natural}
\rightarrow
\text{Whole}
\rightarrow
\text{Integer}
\rightarrow
\text{Rational}
\rightarrow
\text{Real}
$$

Additionally:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{Q}\cup\mathbb{I}}
$$

So real numbers consist of:

$$
\boxed{\text{Rational Numbers + Irrational Numbers}}
$$

> [!important] Key Idea
> **Every rational number is real.**
>
> **Every irrational number is real.**
>
> But not every real number is rational.

---

## 3. Examples of Real Numbers

### Natural Numbers

$$
1,2,3,4,\ldots
$$

### Whole Numbers

$$
0,1,2,3,\ldots
$$

### Integers

$$
\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots
$$

### Rational Numbers

$$
\frac12,\frac34,-\frac57,2
$$

### Irrational Numbers

$$
\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt5,\pi
$$

All of these are real numbers.

---

## 4. Numbers That Are Not Real

Numbers involving the square root of a negative number are not real.

Example:

$$
\sqrt{-1}
$$

This is not a real number.

It belongs to the complex-number system:

$$
\sqrt{-1}=i
$$

where `i` is the imaginary unit.

> [!important] Aptitude Scope
> For basic aptitude, remember:
>
> $$\boxed{\sqrt{-1}\text{ is not a real number}}
> $$
>
> Complex numbers are generally outside the core scope of basic number-system aptitude.

---

# 5. Real Number Line

Every real number can be represented by a point on the number line.

> [!example] Real Number Line
>
> `← Negative        0        Positive →`
>
> `...  -3  -2  -1  |  0  |  1  2  3  ...`

The number line also contains fractional and irrational values.

For example:

$$
\frac12
$$

and:

$$
\sqrt2
$$

can both be located on the real number line.

> [!important] Key Property
> **Every real number corresponds to a point on the number line, and every point on the number line represents a real number.**

---

# 6. Rational and Irrational Parts

Real numbers can be divided into two categories:

$$
\boxed{\text{Real Numbers}=\text{Rational Numbers}\cup\text{Irrational Numbers}}
$$

### Rational

Can be written as:

$$
\frac pq
$$

where:

$$
p,q\in\mathbb Z,\qquad q\neq0
$$

### Irrational

Cannot be written as:

$$
\frac pq
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q\cap\mathbb I=\varnothing}
$$

A number cannot be both rational and irrational.

---

# 7. Important Properties of Real Numbers

## 7.1 Closure Property

Real numbers are closed under:

- Addition
- Subtraction
- Multiplication
- Division by a non-zero real number

Therefore:

$$
a,b\in\mathbb R
$$

implies:

$$
a+b\in\mathbb R
$$

$$
a-b\in\mathbb R
$$

$$
ab\in\mathbb R
$$

and:

$$
\frac ab\in\mathbb R
$$

provided:

$$
b\neq0
$$

> [!warning] Exception
> Division by zero is undefined.
>
> $$\frac{a}{0}$$
>
> is not a real number.

---

# 8. Commutative Property

An operation is commutative if changing the order does not change the result.

### Addition

$$
\boxed{a+b=b+a}
$$

Example:

$$
3+5=5+3
$$

### Multiplication

$$
\boxed{ab=ba}
$$

Example:

$$
3\times5=5\times3
$$

> [!warning] Not Commutative
> Subtraction and division are not commutative.

For example:

$$
5-3\neq3-5
$$

and:

$$
\frac53\neq\frac35
$$

---

# 9. Associative Property

Associative property changes the grouping, not the order.

### Addition

$$
\boxed{(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)}
$$

Example:

$$
(2+3)+4=2+(3+4)
$$

### Multiplication

$$
\boxed{(ab)c=a(bc)}
$$

Example:

$$
(2\times3)\times4
=
2\times(3\times4)
$$

> [!warning] Not Associative
> Subtraction and division are not associative.

---

# 10. Distributive Property

Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction.

### Over Addition

$$
\boxed{a(b+c)=ab+ac}
$$

Example:

$$
5(3+2)
=
5(3)+5(2)
$$

$$
=15+10
$$

$$
=25
$$

### Over Subtraction

$$
\boxed{a(b-c)=ab-ac}
$$

Example:

$$
5(7-2)
=
35-10
=
25
$$

> [!tip] Aptitude Use
> The distributive property is useful for **mental calculation and simplification**.

---

# 11. Identity Properties

## Additive Identity

The additive identity is `0`.

$$
\boxed{a+0=a}
$$

## Multiplicative Identity

The multiplicative identity is `1`.

$$
\boxed{a\times1=a}
$$

---

# 12. Inverse Properties

## Additive Inverse

The additive inverse of `a` is `-a`.

$$
\boxed{a+(-a)=0}
$$

Example:

The additive inverse of `7` is:

$$
\boxed{-7}
$$

The additive inverse of `-5` is:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

---

## Multiplicative Inverse

For a non-zero real number `a`, its multiplicative inverse is:

$$
\boxed{\frac1a}
$$

because:

$$
a\times\frac1a=1
$$

Example:

Multiplicative inverse of `5`:

$$
\boxed{\frac15}
$$

> [!warning] Important
> `0` has **no multiplicative inverse** because division by zero is undefined.

---

# 13. Important Properties Summary

| Property | Addition | Multiplication |
|---|---|---|
| Closure | ✅ | ✅ |
| Commutative | ✅ | ✅ |
| Associative | ✅ | ✅ |
| Identity | `0` | `1` |
| Inverse | `-a` | `1/a`, `a ≠ 0` |

### Subtraction and Division

| Property | Subtraction | Division |
|---|---|---|
| Closure | ✅ | ✅ if divisor ≠ `0` |
| Commutative | ❌ | ❌ |
| Associative | ❌ | ❌ |
| Identity | — | `1` as right/left multiplicative identity only through multiplication |

---

# 14. Comparing Real Numbers

Real numbers can be compared using:

$$
<,\quad>,\quad=
$$

Example:

$$
5>3
$$

$$
-2<1
$$

$$
\frac12<\frac34
$$

For irrational numbers:

$$
\sqrt2<2
$$

because:

$$
\sqrt2\approx1.414
$$

---

# 15. Important Comparison Pattern

For positive numbers:

$$
a>b>0
$$

then:

$$
a^2>b^2
$$

Example:

$$
5>3
$$

Therefore:

$$
25>9
$$

> [!warning] Important
> Squaring inequalities involving negative numbers requires care.
>
> Example:
>
> $$-5<-3$$
>
> but:
>
> $$25>9$$
>
> The direction changes when reasoning through negative values.

---

# 16. Absolute Value of Real Numbers

Absolute value represents distance from zero.

$$
\boxed{|x|\geq0}
$$

Examples:

$$
|5|=5
$$

$$
|-5|=5
$$

$$
|0|=0
$$

Important property:

$$
\boxed{|x|=|-x|}
$$

---

# 17. Distance Between Real Numbers

The distance between two real numbers `a` and `b` is:

$$
\boxed{|a-b|}
$$

Example:

Find the distance between `-3` and `7`.

$$
|-3-7|
$$

$$
|-10|
$$

$$
\boxed{10}
$$

# 18. Important Aptitude Patterns

## Pattern 1 — Classify a Number

### Question

Identify the type of:

$$
\sqrt{25}
$$

Since:

$$
\sqrt{25}=5
$$

Therefore it is:

- Natural
- Whole
- Integer
- Rational
- Real

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{25}=5}
$$

> [!tip] Pattern
> A number can belong to **multiple nested sets**.

---

## Pattern 2 — Classify `√2`

$$
\sqrt2
$$

`2` is not a perfect square.

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt2
$$

is irrational.

Since every irrational number is real:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt2\text{ is irrational and real}}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Identify a Non-Real Number

### Question

Which is not a real number?

$$
\sqrt{-9}
$$

Since the square root contains a negative number:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{-9}\text{ is not real}}
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Number Between Two Real Numbers

### Question

Find a real number between `2` and `3`.

Many answers are possible:

$$
2.5
$$

or:

$$
\frac52
$$

or:

$$
\sqrt5
$$

Since:

$$
2<\sqrt5<3
$$

all are real numbers between `2` and `3`.

---

## Pattern 5 — Rational or Irrational Result

### Question

Determine the type of:

$$
3+\sqrt5
$$

`3` is rational and $\sqrt5$ is irrational.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3+\sqrt5\text{ is irrational}}
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Product of Real Numbers

If:

$$
a,b\in\mathbb R
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{ab\in\mathbb R}
$$

Example:

$$
\sqrt2\times\sqrt3=\sqrt6
$$

Since $\sqrt6$ is real:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt6\in\mathbb R}
$$

# 20. Common Traps

> [!warning] Avoid These Mistakes

- ❌ Thinking every real number is rational.
- ❌ Forgetting irrational numbers are also real.
- ❌ Thinking $\sqrt{-1}$ is real.
- ❌ Forgetting division by zero is undefined.
- ❌ Assuming subtraction is commutative.
- ❌ Assuming division is commutative.
- ❌ Assuming subtraction is associative.
- ❌ Assuming division is associative.
- ❌ Forgetting `0` has no multiplicative inverse.
- ❌ Confusing absolute value with the original number.

---

# 21. Exam Strategy

> [!tip] Fast Approach

When a question asks about real numbers:

1. Identify whether the number is natural, whole, integer, rational, or irrational.
2. Use the hierarchy:
   $$\mathbb N\subset\mathbb W\subset\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R$$
3. For square roots, check whether the value is a perfect square.
4. For negative square roots, check whether the expression is real.
5. Use absolute value for distance.
6. Remember the basic algebraic properties:
   - Commutative
   - Associative
   - Distributive
   - Identity
   - Inverse
