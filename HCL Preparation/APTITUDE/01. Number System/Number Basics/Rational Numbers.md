# Rational Numbers

## 1. Overview

> [!summary] Core Idea
> A rational number is any number that can be expressed as a fraction `p/q`, where `p` and `q` are integers and `q ≠ 0`.

The general form is:

$$
\boxed{\frac{p}{q}}
$$

where:

$$
p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad q\neq0
$$

### Examples

$$
\frac{1}{2},\quad \frac{3}{4},\quad -\frac{5}{7},\quad 8,\quad 0
$$

All of these are rational numbers.

---

## 2. Important Conditions

For:

$$
\frac{p}{q}
$$

to be a rational number:

- `p` must be an integer.
- `q` must be an integer.
- `q ≠ 0`.

> [!warning] Critical Rule
> The denominator can **never be zero**.
>
> $$\frac{5}{0}$$
>
> is undefined.

---

## 3. Integers Are Rational Numbers

Every integer can be written in the form:

$$
\frac{p}{1}
$$

Therefore:

$$
5=\frac{5}{1}
$$

$$
-7=\frac{-7}{1}
$$

$$
0=\frac{0}{1}
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}}
$$

> [!tip] Remember
> **Every integer is rational, but every rational number is not an integer.**

Example:

$$
\frac{3}{5}
$$

is rational but not an integer.

---

## 4. Number System Relationship

The important hierarchy is:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}}
$$

Where:

- $\mathbb{N}$ → Natural Numbers
- $\mathbb{W}$ → Whole Numbers
- $\mathbb{Z}$ → Integers
- $\mathbb{Q}$ → Rational Numbers
- $\mathbb{R}$ → Real Numbers

### Visual Structure

> [!example] Number System

`Natural → Whole → Integers → Rational → Real`

Each smaller set is contained inside the next larger set.

---

## 5. Positive and Negative Rational Numbers

### Positive Rational Numbers

A rational number is positive when numerator and denominator have the same sign.

$$
\frac{+p}{+q}>0
$$

$$
\frac{-p}{-q}>0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{3}{5}>0
$$

$$
\frac{-3}{-5}>0
$$

---

### Negative Rational Numbers

A rational number is negative when numerator and denominator have opposite signs.

$$
\frac{+p}{-q}<0
$$

$$
\frac{-p}{+q}<0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{-3}{5}<0
$$

$$
\frac{3}{-5}<0
$$

---

## 6. Equivalent Rational Numbers

Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number gives an equivalent rational number.

$$
\boxed{\frac{a}{b}=\frac{ak}{bk}}
$$

where:

$$
k\neq0
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{2}{3}
=
\frac{4}{6}
=
\frac{6}{9}
=
\frac{10}{15}
$$

These are all equivalent rational numbers.

> [!tip] Pattern
> **Same multiplier on numerator and denominator → Same rational value.**

---

## 7. Standard Form

A rational number is in standard form when:

1. The denominator is positive.
2. The numerator and denominator have no common factor other than `1`.

### Example

Consider:

$$
\frac{12}{18}
$$

The HCF of `12` and `18` is `6`.

Divide both by `6`:

$$
\frac{12}{18}
=
\frac{2}{3}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{2}{3}}
$$

is the standard form.

---

## 8. Comparing Rational Numbers

There are several useful methods.

### Method 1 — Same Denominator

Compare the numerators.

Example:

$$
\frac{3}{7},\quad\frac{5}{7}
$$

Since:

$$
5>3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{5}{7}>\frac{3}{7}}
$$

---

### Method 2 — Cross Multiplication

To compare:

$$
\frac{a}{b}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{c}{d}
$$

for positive denominators:

Compare:

$$
ad
\quad\text{and}\quad
bc
$$

Example:

Compare:

$$
\frac{3}{5}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{4}{7}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
3\times7=21
$$

$$
4\times5=20
$$

Since:

$$
21>20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{5}>\frac{4}{7}}
$$

> [!tip] Exam Shortcut
> For positive denominators:
>
> **Cross multiply → Compare the products.**

---

## 9. Addition of Rational Numbers

### Same Denominator

Add the numerators.

$$
\boxed{\frac{a}{c}+\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a+b}{c}}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}
=
\frac{5}{7}
$$

---

### Different Denominators

Find the LCM of the denominators.

Example:

$$
\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{4}
$$

LCM:

$$
\operatorname{LCM}(3,4)=12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{1}{3}=\frac{4}{12}
$$

and:

$$
\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}
$$

Hence:

$$
\frac{4}{12}+\frac{3}{12}
=
\boxed{\frac{7}{12}}
$$

> [!tip] Pattern
> **Different denominators → Find LCM → Convert → Add.**

---

## 10. Subtraction of Rational Numbers

For the same denominator:

$$
\boxed{\frac{a}{c}-\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a-b}{c}}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{7}{9}-\frac{2}{9}
=
\frac{5}{9}
$$

For different denominators, use the LCM.

---

## 11. Multiplication of Rational Numbers

Multiply numerators and denominators separately.

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{ac}{bd}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{5}{7}
=
\frac{10}{21}
$$

> [!tip] Shortcut
> Before multiplying, **cancel common factors diagonally** whenever possible.

Example:

$$
\frac{4}{9}\times\frac{3}{8}
$$

Cancel:

$$
4\div4=1,\qquad8\div4=2
$$

$$
3\div3=1,\qquad9\div3=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{6}}
$$

---

## 12. Division of Rational Numbers

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c}
}
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{2}{3}\div\frac{5}{7}
$$

Change division to multiplication:

$$
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{7}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{14}{15}}
$$

> [!important] Golden Rule
> **Keep → Change → Flip**
>
> Keep the first fraction.
>
> Change `÷` to `×`.
>
> Flip the second fraction.

---

## 13. Decimal Representation

A rational number has a decimal representation that is either:

1. **Terminating**
2. **Non-terminating recurring**

### Terminating Decimal

Example:

$$
\frac{1}{2}=0.5
$$

$$
\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

### Non-Terminating Recurring Decimal

Example:

$$
\frac{1}{3}=0.333\ldots
$$

$$
\frac{2}{11}=0.181818\ldots
$$

> [!important] Key Property
> Every rational number has a decimal expansion that is either:
>
> **Terminating or recurring.**

---

## 14. Terminating Decimal Test

First reduce the fraction to its lowest form.

If the denominator contains only the prime factors:

$$
\boxed{2\text{ and/or }5}
$$

then the decimal terminates.

### Example 1

$$
\frac{3}{8}
$$

Since:

$$
8=2^3
$$

the decimal terminates.

$$
\frac{3}{8}=0.375
$$

### Example 2

$$
\frac{7}{20}
$$

Since:

$$
20=2^2\times5
$$

the decimal terminates.

### Example 3

$$
\frac{5}{6}
$$

Since:

$$
6=2\times3
$$

and `3` is present, the decimal is recurring.

$$
\frac{5}{6}=0.8333\ldots
$$

> [!tip] Exam Pattern
> **Lowest-form denominator contains only `2` and `5` → Terminating decimal.**
>
> **Any other prime factor → Non-terminating recurring decimal.**

---

## 15. Converting Terminating Decimals to Rational Numbers

Example:

$$
0.75
$$

Write over a power of `10`:

$$
0.75=\frac{75}{100}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\frac{75}{100}=\frac{3}{4}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0.75=\frac{3}{4}}
$$

---

## 16. Converting Recurring Decimals to Rational Numbers

### Example

Let:

$$
x=0.333\ldots
$$

Multiply by `10`:

$$
10x=3.333\ldots
$$

Subtract:

$$
10x-x=3.333\ldots-0.333\ldots
$$

Therefore:

$$
9x=3
$$

$$
x=\frac{1}{3}
$$

Hence:

$$
\boxed{0.333\ldots=\frac{1}{3}}
$$

---

## 17. Density of Rational Numbers

Between any two distinct rational numbers, there are infinitely many rational numbers.

Example:

Between:

$$
\frac{1}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{3}{4}
$$

we can find:

$$
\frac{5}{8}
$$

because:

$$
\frac{1}{2}<\frac{5}{8}<\frac{3}{4}
$$

> [!important] Concept
> **Between any two rational numbers, infinitely many rational numbers exist.**

# 18. Important Aptitude Patterns

## Pattern 1 — Identify a Rational Number

### Question

Which of the following is rational?

$$
\frac{3}{5},\quad\sqrt{2},\quad\pi,\quad\sqrt{7}
$$

### Recognition

A rational number can be written as:

$$
\frac{p}{q}
$$

where `p` and `q` are integers and `q ≠ 0`.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{5}}
$$

is rational.

---

## Pattern 2 — Reduce to Standard Form

### Question

Express:

$$
\frac{36}{48}
$$

in standard form.

HCF:

$$
\operatorname{HCF}(36,48)=12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{36}{48}
=
\frac{3}{4}
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Compare Two Fractions

### Question

Which is greater?

$$
\frac{7}{12}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{5}{8}
$$

Cross multiply:

$$
7\times8=56
$$

$$
5\times12=60
$$

Since:

$$
60>56
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{5}{8}>\frac{7}{12}}
$$

---

## Pattern 4 — Rational Arithmetic

### Question

Simplify:

$$
\frac{2}{3}+\frac{5}{6}
$$

LCM:

$$
6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{2}{3}=\frac{4}{6}
$$

Hence:

$$
\frac{4}{6}+\frac{5}{6}
=
\frac{9}{6}
=
\boxed{\frac{3}{2}}
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Fraction Division

### Question

Simplify:

$$
\frac{4}{5}\div\frac{2}{3}
$$

Keep → Change → Flip:

$$
\frac{4}{5}\times\frac{3}{2}
$$

$$
=\frac{12}{10}
$$

$$
=\boxed{\frac{6}{5}}
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Terminating or Recurring Decimal

### Question

Determine whether:

$$
\frac{13}{40}
$$

has a terminating decimal.

Factor denominator:

$$
40=2^3\times5
$$

Only `2` and `5` are present.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Terminating}}
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Find a Rational Number Between Two Numbers

For two rational numbers `a` and `b`, their average is also rational:

$$
\boxed{\frac{a+b}{2}}
$$

Example:

Find a rational number between:

$$
\frac{1}{3}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{2}{3}
$$

Take the average:

$$
\frac{\frac13+\frac23}{2}
=
\frac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac12}
$$

lies between them.

# 19. Important Properties

| Property | Result |
|---|---|
| Rational + Rational | Rational |
| Rational − Rational | Rational |
| Rational × Rational | Rational |
| Rational ÷ Rational | Rational, if divisor ≠ `0` |
| Rational + Irrational | Irrational |
| Non-zero Rational × Irrational | Irrational |
| Rational ÷ Non-zero Irrational | Irrational |

> [!important] Closure
> Rational numbers are closed under:
>
> **Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by a non-zero rational number.**

# 20. Formula Sheet

> [!important] Must Remember

| Concept | Formula / Rule |
|---|---|
| Rational number | `p/q`, where `p,q ∈ Z` and `q ≠ 0` |
| Equivalent fraction | `a/b = ak/bk` |
| Addition | `a/b + c/d = (ad+bc)/bd` |
| Subtraction | `a/b - c/d = (ad-bc)/bd` |
| Multiplication | `a/b × c/d = ac/bd` |
| Division | `a/b ÷ c/d = ad/bc` |
| Reciprocal of `a/b` | `b/a`, `a ≠ 0` |
| Distance | `|a-b|` |
| Decimal type | Terminating or recurring |

# 21. Common Traps

> [!warning] Avoid These Mistakes

- ❌ Allowing denominator `0`.
- ❌ Forgetting to reduce fractions before testing decimal termination.
- ❌ Assuming every non-terminating decimal is irrational.
- ❌ Forgetting that recurring decimals are rational.
- ❌ Comparing fractions by comparing only their numerators.
- ❌ Forgetting to flip the second fraction during division.
- ❌ Forgetting that every integer is rational.
- ❌ Assuming rational numbers cannot be negative.
- ❌ Confusing rational numbers with natural numbers.

---

# 22. Exam Strategy

> [!tip] Fast Approach

When you see a rational-number question:

1. Check whether the number can be written as `p/q`.
2. Make sure the denominator is non-zero.
3. Reduce fractions to standard form when necessary.
4. For comparison, use cross multiplication.
5. For addition/subtraction, use LCM.
6. For multiplication, cancel common factors before multiplying.
7. For division, **Keep → Change → Flip**.
8. For decimal questions, factor the denominator.
9. If the reduced denominator contains only `2` and `5`, the decimal terminates.
