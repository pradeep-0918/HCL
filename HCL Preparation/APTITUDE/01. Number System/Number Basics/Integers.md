# Integers

## 1. Overview

> [!summary] Core Idea
> Integers are numbers that include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero, without fractions or decimal parts.

The set of integers is:

$$
\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
$$

### Classification

$$
\boxed{\text{Integers} = \text{Negative Integers} \cup \{0\} \cup \text{Positive Integers}}
$$

### Examples

- Positive integers → `1, 2, 3, 4, ...`
- Zero → `0`
- Negative integers → `-1, -2, -3, -4, ...`

---

## 2. Relationship With Other Number Sets

> [!important] Number System Hierarchy

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{W} \subset \mathbb{Z}}
$$

### Natural Numbers

$$
\mathbb{N} = \{1,2,3,\ldots\}
$$

### Whole Numbers

$$
\mathbb{W} = \{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
$$

### Integers

$$
\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
$$

Therefore:

- Every natural number is an integer.
- Every whole number is an integer.
- Every integer is **not** a natural number.
- Every integer is **not** a whole number.

> [!tip] Fast Recognition
> **Natural → Add `0` → Whole → Add negative numbers → Integers**

---

## 3. Types of Integers

### 3.1 Positive Integers

Positive integers are greater than zero.

$$
x > 0
$$

Examples:

$$
1,2,3,4,5,\ldots
$$

---

### 3.2 Negative Integers

Negative integers are less than zero.

$$
x < 0
$$

Examples:

$$
-1,-2,-3,-4,-5,\ldots
$$

---

### 3.3 Zero

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

Zero is:

- An integer
- A whole number
- Neither positive nor negative
- An even number

> [!warning] Common Trap
> `0` is **neither positive nor negative**.

---

## 4. Number Line

Integers can be represented on a number line.

> [!example] Integer Number Line
>
> `← Negative          Zero          Positive →`
>
> `...  -3  -2  -1  |  0  |  1  2  3  4  ...`

As we move:

- **Right →** numbers increase
- **Left →** numbers decrease

Therefore:

$$
-5 < -3 < -1 < 0 < 2 < 4
$$

> [!tip] Remember
> On a number line, **the number farther to the right is always greater**.

---

## 5. Comparing Integers

### 5.1 Positive Numbers

For positive numbers, the larger numerical value is greater.

Example:

$$
7 > 3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{7 > 3}
$$

---

### 5.2 Negative Numbers

For negative numbers, the number closer to zero is greater.

Example:

$$
-3 > -7
$$

Because `-3` is closer to zero.

> [!tip] Shortcut
> When comparing negative numbers:
>
> **Smaller absolute value = Greater number**

Example:

$$
|-4| = 4
$$

$$
|-9| = 9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{-4 > -9}
$$

---

## 6. Absolute Value

The absolute value of an integer is its distance from zero.

It is represented using:

$$
|x|
$$

Examples:

$$
|5| = 5
$$

$$
|-5| = 5
$$

$$
|0| = 0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{|x| \geq 0}
$$

> [!important] Key Property
> The absolute value of a number is **never negative**.

---

## 7. Important Absolute Value Properties

### Property 1

$$
\boxed{|x| = |-x|}
$$

Example:

$$
|7| = |-7| = 7
$$

### Property 2

$$
\boxed{|xy| = |x||y|}
$$

### Property 3

$$
\boxed{\left|\frac{x}{y}\right| = \frac{|x|}{|y|}}
$$

where:

$$
y \neq 0
$$

### Property 4

$$
\boxed{|x| = 0 \iff x = 0}
$$

---

## 8. Addition of Integers

### Same Signs

Add the absolute values and keep the common sign.

Example:

$$
(+5)+(+3)=+8
$$

$$
(-5)+(-3)=-8
$$

### Different Signs

Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value.

Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

Example:

$$
(+8)+(-3)=+5
$$

$$
(-8)+(+3)=-5
$$

> [!tip] Golden Rule
> **Same signs → Add**
>
> **Different signs → Subtract**
>
> **Sign → Keep the sign of the larger absolute value**

---

## 9. Subtraction of Integers

Convert subtraction into addition of the opposite number.

$$
\boxed{a-b=a+(-b)}
$$

Examples:

$$
5-3=5+(-3)=2
$$

$$
5-(-3)=5+3=8
$$

$$
-5-3=-5+(-3)=-8
$$

$$
-5-(-3)=-5+3=-2
$$

> [!tip] Golden Rule
> **Subtracting a number = Adding its opposite**

---

## 10. Multiplication of Integers

### Sign Rules

| First Sign | Second Sign | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `+` | `+` | `+` |
| `+` | `-` | `-` |
| `-` | `+` | `-` |
| `-` | `-` | `+` |

> [!important] Memory Trick
> **Same signs → Positive**
>
> **Different signs → Negative**

Examples:

$$
(+5)(+2)=+10
$$

$$
(+5)(-2)=-10
$$

$$
(-5)(+2)=-10
$$

$$
(-5)(-2)=+10
$$

---

## 11. Division of Integers

Division follows the same sign rule as multiplication.

### Same Signs

$$
+\div+=+
$$

$$
-\div-=+
$$

### Different Signs

$$
+\div-=-
$$

$$
-\div+=-
$$

Examples:

$$
\frac{-20}{-5}=4
$$

$$
\frac{-20}{5}=-4
$$

> [!warning] Important
> Division by zero is undefined.
>
> $$\frac{a}{0}$$
>
> is undefined.

---

## 12. Even and Odd Integers

### Even Integers

General form:

$$
\boxed{2n}
$$

Examples:

$$
\ldots,-6,-4,-2,0,2,4,6,\ldots
$$

### Odd Integers

General form:

$$
\boxed{2n+1}
$$

Examples:

$$
\ldots,-5,-3,-1,1,3,5,\ldots
$$

> [!important] Remember
> Negative integers can also be even or odd.
>
> `-6` → Even
>
> `-5` → Odd

---

## 13. Even and Odd Patterns

### Addition

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}+\text{Even}=\text{Even}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}+\text{Odd}=\text{Even}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}+\text{Odd}=\text{Odd}}
$$

### Subtraction

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}-\text{Even}=\text{Even}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}-\text{Odd}=\text{Even}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}-\text{Odd}=\text{Odd}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}-\text{Even}=\text{Odd}}
$$

### Multiplication

$$
\boxed{\text{Even}\times\text{Any Integer}=\text{Even}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Odd}\times\text{Odd}=\text{Odd}}
$$

---

## 14. Closure Properties

Integers are closed under:

- Addition
- Subtraction
- Multiplication

Integers are **not closed under division**.

| Operation | Closed? |
|---|---|
| Addition | ✅ |
| Subtraction | ✅ |
| Multiplication | ✅ |
| Division | ❌ |

### Example

$$
5 \div 2 = 2.5
$$

Since `2.5` is not an integer:

$$
\boxed{\text{Integers are not closed under division}}
$$

---

## 15. Identity Elements

### Additive Identity

Adding zero does not change a number.

$$
\boxed{a+0=a}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0\text{ is the additive identity}}
$$

---

### Multiplicative Identity

Multiplying by one does not change a number.

$$
\boxed{a\times1=a}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1\text{ is the multiplicative identity}}
$$

# 16. Important Aptitude Patterns

## Pattern 1 — Comparing Negative Numbers

### Question

Which is greater?

$$
-7,\ -3
$$

### Approach

The number closer to zero is greater.

$$
\boxed{-3>-7}
$$

### Recognition

> When comparing negative integers, think about their position on the number line.

---

## Pattern 2 — Simplifying Signed Expressions

### Question

Simplify:

$$
-15+8-(-4)
$$

### Approach

Convert subtraction of a negative number:

$$
-15+8+4
$$

$$
=-3
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{-3}
$$

---

## Pattern 3 — Product of Multiple Negative Numbers

### Question

Determine the sign of:

$$
(-2)(-3)(-4)(5)
$$

There are **3 negative factors**.

Since the number of negative factors is odd:

$$
\boxed{\text{Negative}}
$$

> [!tip] Sign Pattern
> **Even number of negative factors → Positive**
>
> **Odd number of negative factors → Negative**

---

## Pattern 4 — Absolute Value

### Question

Simplify:

$$
|-8|+|3|
$$

### Solution

$$
8+3=11
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{11}
$$

---

## Pattern 5 — Distance Between Two Integers

The distance between two integers `a` and `b` is:

$$
\boxed{|a-b|}
$$

### Example

Find the distance between `-7` and `4`.

$$
|-7-4|
$$

$$
|-11|=11
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{11}
$$

---

## Pattern 6 — Consecutive Integers

Consecutive integers differ by `1`.

Examples:

$$
-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3
$$

General form:

$$
\boxed{n,n+1,n+2,\ldots}
$$

For consecutive integers:

$$
\boxed{\text{Average}=\frac{\text{First}+\text{Last}}{2}}
$$

---

## Pattern 7 — Sum of Consecutive Integers

For consecutive integers from `a` to `b`:

### Number of Terms

$$
\boxed{n=b-a+1}
$$

### Sum

$$
\boxed{S=\frac{n(a+b)}{2}}
$$

### Example

Find the sum from `-5` to `5`.

Number of terms:

$$
n=5-(-5)+1
$$

$$
n=11
$$

Sum:

$$
S=\frac{11(-5+5)}{2}
$$

$$
S=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

> [!tip] Powerful Shortcut
> For a sequence symmetric around zero:
>
> $$-n,\ldots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\ldots,n$$
>
> the sum is:
>
> $$\boxed{0}$$

# 18. Common Traps

> [!warning] Avoid These Mistakes

- ❌ Thinking `0` is positive.
- ❌ Thinking `0` is negative.
- ❌ Thinking `-8 > -3`.
- ❌ Forgetting that `|-x| = |x|`.
- ❌ Ignoring signs during addition and subtraction.
- ❌ Forgetting that subtracting a negative becomes addition.
- ❌ Thinking an odd number of negative factors gives a positive result.
- ❌ Assuming integers are closed under division.
- ❌ Forgetting that negative integers can be even or odd.
- ❌ Forgetting `+1` when counting an inclusive range.

---

# 19. Exam Strategy

> [!tip] Fast Approach

When you see an integer question:

1. Identify positive, negative, and zero values.
2. For comparison, visualize the number line.
3. For addition/subtraction, handle signs first.
4. For multiplication/division, count negative signs.
5. For absolute value, think **distance from zero**.
6. For consecutive integers, use the average and number-of-terms formulas.
7. Check whether the question is testing an even/odd pattern.
