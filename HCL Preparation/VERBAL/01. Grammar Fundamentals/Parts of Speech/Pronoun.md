# Pronoun

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **pronoun** is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

### Example

Instead of:

> Rahul said Rahul would complete Rahul's work.

We use:

> Rahul said **he** would complete **his** work.

Here:

- Rahul → noun
- he → pronoun
- his → pronoun

### Intuition

A pronoun is basically a **replacement word for a noun**.

Common pronouns:

`I, we, you, he, she, it, they, me, him, her, us, them, this, that, who, which, someone`

> [!important]
> Quick test: If a word **replaces or refers back to a noun**, think **PRONOUN**.

---

## 2. Basic Meaning

Pronouns make sentences shorter, clearer, and less repetitive.

### Without Pronoun

> Priya went to Priya's room because Priya wanted Priya's book.

### With Pronouns

> Priya went to **her** room because **she** wanted **her** book.

Pronouns must correctly match the noun they refer to.

This is called **pronoun-antecedent agreement**.

### Example

> Ravi forgot **his** phone.

- Ravi → antecedent
- his → pronoun

The noun that a pronoun refers to is called its **antecedent**.

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no mathematical formula, but remember:

$$
\text{Pronoun} = \text{Word used in place of a Noun}
$$

For agreement:

$$
\text{Pronoun} \leftrightarrow \text{Antecedent}
$$

The pronoun should generally agree with its antecedent in:

- Number
- Person
- Gender where relevant

### Example

> The boy lost **his** bag.

$$
\text{boy} \rightarrow \text{his}
$$

> The girls finished **their** work.

$$
\text{girls} \rightarrow \text{their}
$$

---

# 4. Types of Pronouns

| Type | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Refers to people/things | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| Possessive | Shows ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive | Refers back to subject | myself, yourself, himself |
| Demonstrative | Points to something | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | Used to ask questions | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| Relative | Connects a clause to a noun | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| Indefinite | Refers to non-specific persons/things | someone, anyone, everyone |
| Distributive | Refers to members individually | each, either, neither |
| Reciprocal | Shows mutual action | each other, one another |

---

# 5. Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns refer to specific people or things.

## Subject Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | I | we |
| Second | you | you |
| Third | he, she, it | they |

### Examples

> **I** am studying.

> **She** is working.

> **They** are playing.

---

## Object Pronouns

| Subject | Object |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| we | us |
| you | you |
| he | him |
| she | her |
| it | it |
| they | them |

### Example

> Rahul called **me**.

> The teacher praised **him**.

> She invited **them**.

> [!important]
> Subject pronouns generally perform the action; object pronouns receive the action or follow a preposition.

---

# 6. Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns show ownership.

Examples:

- mine
- yours
- his
- hers
- ours
- theirs

### Example

> This book is **mine**.

> That car is **hers**.

### Important Difference

| Possessive Determiner | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| my | mine |
| your | yours |
| his | his |
| her | hers |
| our | ours |
| their | theirs |

### Example

> This is **my** book.

> This book is **mine**.

> [!warning] Common Trap
> Do not confuse **its** and **it's**.
>
> `its` = possession  
> `it's` = it is / it has

Example:

> The dog hurt **its** leg.

> **It's** raining.

---

# 7. Reflexive Pronouns

Reflexive pronouns refer back to the subject.

| Person | Reflexive |
|---|---|
| I | myself |
| you | yourself |
| he | himself |
| she | herself |
| it | itself |
| we | ourselves |
| you | yourselves |
| they | themselves |

### Example

> I hurt **myself**.

> She prepared **herself**.

> They introduced **themselves**.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> If the subject and object refer to the **same person**, think of a reflexive pronoun.

$$
\text{Subject} = \text{Object} \rightarrow \text{Reflexive Pronoun}
$$

---

# 8. Demonstrative Pronouns

Demonstrative pronouns point to people or things.

| Near | Far |
|---|---|
| this | that |
| these | those |

### Singular

> **This** is my book.

> **That** is your car.

### Plural

> **These** are my shoes.

> **Those** are your bags.

> [!tip]
> `This / That` → singular  
> `These / Those` → plural

---

# 9. Interrogative Pronouns

Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions.

Common examples:

- who
- whom
- whose
- which
- what

### Examples

> **Who** called you?

> **Whom** did you meet?

> **Whose** bag is this?

> **Which** do you prefer?

> **What** happened?

---

# 10. Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns connect a dependent clause to a noun.

Common relative pronouns:

- who
- whom
- whose
- which
- that

### Example

> The boy **who** won the race is my friend.

The word **who** connects information about the boy.

### Another Example

> This is the book **that** I bought.

> [!important]
> `who` → generally used for people  
> `which` → generally used for things/animals  
> `that` → commonly used for people or things in restrictive clauses

---

# 11. Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns refer to people or things without specifying exactly who or what.

Examples:

- someone
- somebody
- anyone
- anybody
- everyone
- everybody
- nobody
- nothing
- something
- each
- either
- neither

### Examples

> **Someone** is waiting outside.

> **Everyone** enjoyed the event.

> **Nobody** knows the answer.

> [!important]
> Words such as **everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody, each, either, neither** are generally treated as **singular** for subject-verb agreement.

Example:

> Everyone **is** ready.

Not:

> Everyone **are** ready.

---

# 12. Important Properties

## 12.1 Pronoun Must Agree with Antecedent

Incorrect:

> The boy forgot **their** book.

Correct:

> The boy forgot **his** book.

For plural:

> The boys forgot **their** books.

---

## 12.2 Singular and Plural Agreement

| Antecedent | Correct Pronoun |
|---|---|
| boy | he/his |
| girl | she/her |
| book | it/its |
| boys | they/their |
| girls | they/their |
| books | they/their |

---

## 12.3 Person Agreement

### First Person

The speaker:

> I, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours

### Second Person

The person being addressed:

> you, your, yours

### Third Person

The person/thing being discussed:

> he, she, it, they, him, her, them

---

# 13. Basic Examples

## Example 1 — Identify the Pronoun

### Question

Identify the pronoun:

> Rahul said that he was tired.

### Pattern

Find the word replacing/referring to a noun.

### Calculation

- Rahul → noun
- he → refers to Rahul

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{he}}
$$

## Example 2 — Subject Pronoun

### Question

Choose the correct word:

> ___ am going to college.

A. Me  
B. I  
C. Mine

### Pattern

The blank is the subject performing the action.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. I}}
$$

Correct sentence:

> I am going to college.

## Example 3 — Object Pronoun

### Question

Choose the correct word:

> The teacher called ___.

A. I  
B. me  
C. my

### Pattern

The pronoun receives the action of "called".

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. me}}
$$

### Question

Choose the correct word:

> This laptop is ___.

A. my  
B. mine  
C. me

### Pattern

The word must show ownership and stand alone.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. mine}}
$$

### Answer

\boxed{\text{B. herself}}
$$

# 14. Advanced Examples

## Example 6 — Pronoun Agreement

### Question

Find the error:

> Every student must submit their assignment.

### Pattern

`Every` is grammatically singular.

Traditional aptitude grammar expects a singular pronoun.

### Correct

> Every student must submit **his or her** assignment.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{their} \rightarrow \text{his or her}}
$$

> [!important]
> In modern English, singular **they/their** is widely accepted, but traditional placement grammar may still expect **his or her**. Follow the convention used by the exam.

---

## Example 7 — Everyone

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Everyone have completed their work.  
B. Everyone has completed his or her work.

### Pattern

`Everyone` is treated as singular for verb agreement.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B}}
$$

---

## Example 8 — Each

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Each of the boys has submitted his assignment.  
B. Each of the boys have submitted their assignment.

### Pattern

`Each` is singular.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{A}}
$$

The phrase **of the boys** does not change the grammatical number of **each**.

---

## Example 9 — Who vs Whom

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Whom called you?  
B. Who called you?

### Pattern

Ask:

> Who performed the action?

The answer is the subject.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. Who called you?}}
$$

---

## Example 10 — Who vs Whom

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Who did you meet?  
B. Whom did you meet?

### Pattern

You performed the action; the person received it.

Therefore the pronoun is functioning as an object.

### Traditional Grammar Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. Whom did you meet?}}
$$

> [!tip]
> Quick test:
>
> `he → who`
>
> `him → whom`
>
> If **he** fits → who.  
> If **him** fits → whom.

---

## Example 11 — Reflexive Pronoun Error

### Question

Find the error:

> Myself will attend the meeting.

### Pattern

`Myself` should not normally be used as the subject simply instead of `I`.

### Correct

> **I** will attend the meeting.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{Myself} \rightarrow \text{I}}
$$

---

## Example 12 — Exam-Style Error Detection

### Question

Find the error:

> The manager asked Ravi and I to complete the task.

### Pattern

After the verb "asked", the pronoun functions as an object.

Use:

> me

not:

> I

### Correct

> The manager asked Ravi and **me** to complete the task.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{I} \rightarrow \text{me}}
$$

# 15. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1: He/Him Test**
>
> For `who` vs `whom`:
>
> Replace the word with **he/him**.
>
> `he` works → **who**
>
> `him` works → **whom**

Example:

> ___ called you?
>
> **He** called you.
>
> Therefore → **Who**

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2: Each/Every = Singular**
>
> When the subject begins with:
>
> `each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody`
>
> immediately check for **singular agreement**.

Example:

> Everyone **is** ready.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3: This/That vs These/Those**

$$
\text{This/That} = Singular
$$

$$
\text{These/Those} = Plural
$$

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4: Reflexive Test**

Ask:

> Did the subject perform the action on itself?

If yes, consider a reflexive pronoun.

Example:

> She hurt **herself**.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5: My vs Mine**

Use:

> `my + noun`

Example:

> my book

Use:

> `mine` alone

Example:

> The book is mine.

# 16. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Replacement of Noun

> [!important]
> If a word replaces a previously mentioned noun, think **Pronoun**.

Example:

> Priya is smart. **She** works hard.

`She` replaces Priya.

---

## Pattern 2 — Subject vs Object

> [!important]
> If the pronoun performs the action, use a **subject pronoun**.
>
> If the pronoun receives the action, use an **object pronoun**.

### Subject

> **He** called me.

### Object

> I called **him**.

---

## Pattern 3 — Ownership

> [!important]
> If the question asks who owns something, think **possessive pronoun/determiner**.

Examples:

- my
- your
- his
- her
- mine
- yours
- theirs

---

## Pattern 4 — Same Subject and Object

> [!important]
> If the subject performs an action on the same person, think **reflexive pronoun**.

Example:

> Rahul introduced **himself**.

---

## Pattern 5 — Pointing

> [!important]
> If the word points toward something, think **demonstrative pronoun**.

`this, that, these, those`

---

## Pattern 6 — Question

> [!important]
> If the pronoun is used to ask a question, think **interrogative pronoun**.

`who, whom, whose, which, what`

---

## Pattern 7 — Connecting Clauses

> [!important]
> If the word connects a noun with additional information, think **relative pronoun**.

Example:

> The student **who** won the prize is my friend.

# 18. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — I vs Me

Incorrect:

> She called I.

Correct:

> She called me.

Reason: `me` is the object.

---

### Mistake 2 — Myself as Subject

Incorrect:

> Myself will attend.

Correct:

> I will attend.

Reason: `myself` is reflexive, not the normal subject pronoun.

---

### Mistake 3 — Each with Plural Verb

Incorrect:

> Each of the students are ready.

Correct:

> Each of the students is ready.

Reason:

> `Each` is singular.

---

### Mistake 4 — Everyone with Plural Verb

Incorrect:

> Everyone are happy.

Correct:

> Everyone is happy.

---

### Mistake 5 — This vs These

Incorrect:

> These book is expensive.

Correct:

> This book is expensive.

Reason:

> `this` → singular  
> `these` → plural

---

### Mistake 6 — Its vs It's

Incorrect:

> The company changed it's policy.

Correct:

> The company changed its policy.

Reason:

- its → possessive
- it's → it is / it has

---

### Mistake 7 — Who vs Whom

Incorrect in formal grammar:

> Whom called you?

Correct:

> Who called you?

Because the person performed the action.

---

### Mistake 8 — Ambiguous Pronoun

Example:

> Ravi told Arun that he had won.

Who does **he** refer to?

The sentence is ambiguous.

In exams, check whether the pronoun has a clear antecedent.

# 20. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Pronoun

A word used instead of a noun.

### Main Types

- Personal
- Possessive
- Reflexive
- Demonstrative
- Interrogative
- Relative
- Indefinite
- Distributive
- Reciprocal

### High-Priority Rules

1. Pronoun must agree with its antecedent.
2. Subject → `I, he, she, we, they`
3. Object → `me, him, her, us, them`
4. Same subject/object → reflexive pronoun.
5. `this/that` → singular.
6. `these/those` → plural.
7. `each/every/everyone` → generally singular in traditional exam grammar.
8. `who` → subject.
9. `whom` → object.
10. `my` comes before a noun; `mine` stands alone.
11. `its` = possession.
12. `it's` = it is / it has.

### Fast Exam Strategy

1. Find the pronoun.
2. Find its antecedent.
3. Check singular/plural agreement.
4. Check subject/object position.
5. Check pronoun type.
6. Check special traps such as `who/whom`, `I/me`, `my/mine`, and reflexives.
