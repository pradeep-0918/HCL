# Interjection

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> An **interjection** is a word or short expression used to express a sudden or strong emotion, feeling, reaction, or response.

Common interjections:

`wow, oh, alas, hooray, bravo, ouch, ah, hey, hello, well`

### Examples

> **Wow!** That is beautiful.

> **Ouch!** That hurts.

> **Hurray!** We won the match.

> **Alas!** He failed the examination.

### Intuition

An interjection expresses the **speaker's immediate emotion or reaction**.

It can show:

- Joy
- Surprise
- Pain
- Sorrow
- Anger
- Approval
- Greeting
- Attention

> [!important]
> Quick test: If a word suddenly expresses an **emotion, reaction, or feeling**, think **INTERJECTION**.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Interjections are usually short and can stand independently from the grammatical structure of a sentence.

### Example

> **Wow!** You solved it.

The word `wow` expresses surprise or admiration.

The sentence would still be grammatically complete without it:

> You solved it.

This is an important characteristic of interjections.

### Important Point

An interjection is usually not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence.

Example:

> **Oh!** I forgot my wallet.

`Oh` expresses the speaker's reaction.

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no mathematical formula for interjections.

The basic structure is:

$$
\text{Interjection} + \text{Sentence}
$$

Example:

> **Wow!** This is amazing.

It can also appear after a sentence:

> We won the match, **hooray!**

### Core Idea

$$
\text{Interjection} = \text{Emotion / Reaction}
$$

# 11. Interjections for Attention

These are used to attract someone's attention.

- hey
- look
- listen

> **Look!** There is a rainbow.

---

# 12. Punctuation with Interjections

Interjections are commonly followed by:

- Exclamation mark `!`
- Comma `,`

### Strong Emotion

Use an exclamation mark.

> **Wow!** That is incredible.

> **Ouch!** That hurts.

### Mild Reaction

A comma may be used.

> Oh, I understand now.

> Well, let's begin.

> [!important]
> The punctuation often reflects the **strength of emotion**, but punctuation alone does not determine whether a word is an interjection.

---

# 13. Interjection vs Conjunction

This is an important distinction.

### Interjection

Expresses emotion.

> **Wow!** You won.

### Conjunction

Connects ideas.

> You studied **and** passed.

| Interjection | Conjunction |
|---|---|
| Expresses emotion | Connects words/clauses |
| wow | and |
| ouch | but |
| alas | because |
| hooray | or |

> [!important]
> Emotion → **Interjection**
>
> Connection → **Conjunction**

---

# 14. Interjection vs Adverb

An interjection expresses a reaction.

An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb.

### Interjection

> **Wow!** That is beautiful.

### Adverb

> She sings **beautifully**.

`wow` → reaction

`beautifully` → modifies `sings`

# 20. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Confusing Interjection with Conjunction

Incorrect classification:

> `and` → interjection

Correct:

> `and` → conjunction

Reason:

`and` connects ideas; it does not express emotion.

---

### Mistake 2 — Assuming Every Exclamation Is an Interjection

An exclamation mark does not automatically make a word an interjection.

Example:

> Stop!

`Stop` is a verb used as a command.

Therefore, identify the grammatical function from context.

---

### Mistake 3 — Confusing "Well"

`Well` can function differently depending on context.

> **Well!** I didn't expect that.

Here it expresses a reaction.

But:

> She performed **well**.

Here it is an adverb.

---

### Mistake 4 — Confusing "Look"

> **Look!** There is a car coming.

Here `look` is used as an attention-getting expression.

But:

> I **look** at the picture every day.

Here `look` is a verb.

---

### Mistake 5 — Confusing "Oh" with a Regular Word

`Oh` commonly expresses surprise, realization, disappointment, or other reactions.

Example:

> Oh! I understand now.

It functions as an interjection.
