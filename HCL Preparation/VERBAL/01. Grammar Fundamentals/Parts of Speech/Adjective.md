# Adjective

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> An **adjective** is a word that describes, identifies, or gives more information about a **noun or pronoun**.

An adjective can tell us:

- What kind?
- Which one?
- How many?
- How much?
- Whose?

### Examples

- a **beautiful** flower
- a **tall** boy
- **three** books
- **this** car
- **my** laptop

### Intuition

A noun gives the **name**.

An adjective gives **more information about that noun**.

Example:

> The **red** car is fast.

- car → noun
- red → adjective

> [!important]
> Quick test: If a word **describes or limits a noun/pronoun**, think **ADJECTIVE**.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Adjectives make sentences more specific.

Compare:

> I bought a car.

> I bought a **new red** car.

The words `new` and `red` give additional information about the noun `car`.

### Adjective Questions

An adjective often answers:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| What kind? | a beautiful girl |
| Which one? | this book |
| How many? | five students |
| How much? | some water |
| Whose? | my bag |

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no mathematical formula for adjectives, but the basic relationship is:

$$
\text{Adjective} \rightarrow \text{Describes/Modifies Noun or Pronoun}
$$

Common sentence pattern:

$$
\text{Adjective} + \text{Noun}
$$

Example:

> a **smart** student

Another pattern:

$$
\text{Subject} + \text{Linking Verb} + \text{Adjective}
$$

Example:

> The student **is smart**.

Here `smart` describes the subject `student`.

---

# 4. Types of Adjectives

| Type | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective of Quality | Shows quality | good, beautiful, honest |
| Adjective of Quantity | Shows amount | some, much, little |
| Adjective of Number | Shows number/order | one, two, first, second |
| Demonstrative | Points out noun | this, that, these, those |
| Possessive | Shows possession | my, your, his, her |
| Interrogative | Used in questions | which, what, whose |
| Distributive | Refers to members individually | each, every, either, neither |
| Proper Adjective | Derived from proper noun | Indian, Indian, American |

---

# 5. Adjective of Quality

Adjectives of quality describe the nature or characteristics of a noun.

Examples:

- good
- bad
- beautiful
- intelligent
- honest
- brave
- tall
- short
- expensive
- difficult

### Example

> She is an **intelligent** student.

`intelligent` describes the student.

---

# 6. Adjective of Quantity

These indicate the amount of something.

Examples:

- much
- little
- some
- enough
- sufficient
- whole
- enough

They are commonly used with **uncountable nouns**.

### Examples

> I have **some** money.

> There is **little** water left.

> We have **enough** time.

> [!important]
> Quantity usually answers **"How much?"**

---

# 7. Adjective of Number

These indicate number or order.

Examples:

- one
- two
- ten
- first
- second
- several
- many
- few

### Examples

> I have **three** books.

> He stood **first** in the class.

> [!important]
> Number usually answers **"How many?"** or **"In what order?"**

---

# 8. Demonstrative Adjectives

These point to a noun.

- this
- that
- these
- those

### Examples

> **This book** is useful.

> **That car** is expensive.

> **These students** are prepared.

> **Those houses** are old.

### Important Rule

$$
\text{This/That} \rightarrow \text{Singular}
$$

$$
\text{These/Those} \rightarrow \text{Plural}
$$

---

# 9. Possessive Adjectives

These show ownership and come before a noun.

Examples:

- my
- your
- his
- her
- its
- our
- their

### Examples

> This is **my book**.

> He lost **his phone**.

> They completed **their work**.

> [!important]
> A possessive adjective is normally followed by a noun.
>
> `my book`
>
> `your car`
>
> `their house`

Compare:

> This is **my** book.

> This book is **mine**.

`my` → possessive adjective/determiner

`mine` → possessive pronoun

---

# 10. Interrogative Adjectives

These are used with nouns to ask questions.

Common examples:

- what
- which
- whose

### Examples

> **Which book** do you want?

> **What subject** are you studying?

> **Whose bag** is this?

### Important Difference

> **Which book** do you want?

`which` modifies `book` → adjective.

> **Which** do you want?

`which` stands alone → pronoun.

---

# 11. Distributive Adjectives

These refer to members of a group individually.

Common examples:

- each
- every
- either
- neither

### Examples

> **Each student** received a certificate.

> **Every employee** must attend.

> **Either option** is acceptable.

> **Neither answer** is correct.

> [!important]
> `each`, `every`, `either`, and `neither` are commonly followed by **singular nouns**.

---

# 12. Degrees of Comparison

Adjectives can compare people or things.

There are three degrees:

1. Positive
2. Comparative
3. Superlative

| Degree | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | One person/thing | tall |
| Comparative | Two | taller |
| Superlative | Three or more | tallest |

### Example

> Rahul is **tall**.

> Rahul is **taller** than Ravi.

> Rahul is the **tallest** boy in the class.

---

## 12.1 Positive Degree

Basic form.

> Ram is tall.

---

## 12.2 Comparative Degree

Used when comparing two.

$$
\text{A} + \text{is} + \text{comparative} + \text{than} + \text{B}
$$

Example:

> Ram is taller than Ravi.

---

## 12.3 Superlative Degree

Used when comparing three or more.

$$
\text{the} + \text{superlative}
$$

Example:

> Ram is the tallest student in the class.

> [!important]
> Comparative → generally **than**
>
> Superlative → generally **the**

---

# 13. Formation of Comparison

### Short Adjectives

Usually add:

$$
-er,\ -est
$$

Example:

> tall → taller → tallest

> fast → faster → fastest

### Adjectives Ending in `y`

Usually:

$$
y \rightarrow i
$$

Example:

> happy → happier → happiest

> easy → easier → easiest

### Longer Adjectives

Usually use:

$$
\text{more} + \text{adjective}
$$

$$
\text{most} + \text{adjective}
$$

Example:

> beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful

> difficult → more difficult → most difficult

---

# 14. Irregular Comparison

Some adjectives have irregular forms.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| little | less | least |
| much | more | most |
| many | more | most |
| far | farther/further | farthest/furthest |

> [!warning]
> Do not use regular `-er/-est` forms for irregular adjectives.
>
> Incorrect: `gooder`
>
> Correct: `better`

---

# 15. Important Properties

## 15.1 Adjective Before Noun

The common position is:

$$
\text{Adjective} + \text{Noun}
$$

Example:

> a **beautiful** garden

---

## 15.2 Adjective After Linking Verb

An adjective can come after a linking verb.

Example:

> The garden is **beautiful**.

Common linking verbs:

- is
- am
- are
- was
- were
- seem
- become
- feel
- look
- appear

---

## 15.3 Adjective vs Adverb

This is a very common exam trap.

### Adjective

Describes a noun/pronoun.

> He is a **quick** runner.

### Adverb

Describes a verb/adjective/adverb.

> He runs **quickly**.

> [!important]
> Ask:
>
> **Does it describe a noun?** → Adjective
>
> **Does it describe an action/verb?** → Adverb

# 17. Advanced Examples

## Example 6 — Adjective vs Adverb

### Question

Choose the correct word:

> He is a ___ driver.

A. careful  
B. carefully

### Pattern

The blank describes the noun `driver`.

Therefore, use an adjective.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{A. careful}}
$$

Correct sentence:

> He is a careful driver.

---

## Example 7 — Adjective After Linking Verb

### Question

Choose the correct word:

> The soup tastes ___.

A. delicious  
B. deliciously

### Pattern

`tastes` is functioning as a linking verb.

The word describes the subject `soup`.

Therefore, use an adjective.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{A. delicious}}
$$

---

## Example 8 — Comparative Degree

### Question

Complete:

> Rahul is ___ than Ravi.

A. tall  
B. taller  
C. tallest

### Pattern

The word `than` indicates a comparison between two.

Therefore, use comparative degree.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. taller}}
$$

---

## Example 9 — Superlative Degree

### Question

Complete:

> Mount Everest is the ___ mountain in the world.

A. high  
B. higher  
C. highest

### Pattern

The sentence compares one item with all others.

Use superlative.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{C. highest}}
$$

---

## Example 10 — Every + Singular Noun

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Every students must attend.  
B. Every student must attend.

### Pattern

`Every` is followed by a singular countable noun.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. Every student must attend.}}
$$

---

## Example 11 — More vs -er

### Question

Choose the correct form:

> This problem is ___ than the previous one.

A. difficulter  
B. more difficult  
C. most difficult

### Pattern

`difficult` is a longer adjective.

Use:

$$
\text{more} + \text{adjective}
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. more difficult}}
$$

---

## Example 12 — Double Comparison Trap

### Question

Find the error:

> She is more smarter than her sister.

### Pattern

Do not normally use `more` with the comparative `smarter`.

Incorrect:

$$
\text{more} + \text{smarter}
$$

Correct:

> She is smarter than her sister.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{more smarter} \rightarrow \text{smarter}}
$$

---

## Example 13 — Double Superlative Trap

### Question

Find the error:

> He is the most tallest boy in the class.

### Pattern

`tallest` is already superlative.

Do not use `most` before it.

### Correct

> He is the tallest boy in the class.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{most tallest} \rightarrow \text{tallest}}
$$

---

## Example 14 — Exam-Style Comparison

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Of the two brothers, Ravi is the tallest.  
B. Of the two brothers, Ravi is taller.  
C. Of the two brothers, Ravi is most tall.

### Pattern

There are **two** people.

Use comparative degree.

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. Ravi is taller.}}
$$

> [!important]
> In standard aptitude grammar, use the **comparative degree for two** and the **superlative degree for three or more**.

# 18. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1: Noun Test**
>
> If the word describes a noun, think **adjective**.
>
> `a beautiful flower`
>
> beautiful → adjective

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2: Than = Comparative**
>
> When you see:

$$
\text{than}
$$

immediately check for a comparative form.

Example:

> faster than

> better than

> more useful than

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3: The + Highest = Superlative**

If a sentence compares one item against a group, think:

$$
\text{the} + \text{superlative}
$$

Example:

> the fastest

> the best

> the most useful

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4: Two vs Three+**

$$
2\ items \rightarrow Comparative
$$

$$
3+\ items \rightarrow Superlative
$$

Example:

> Ravi is taller than Arun.

> Ravi is the tallest of the three.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5: Every/Each**

After `every` and `each`, use a singular noun.

> every student

> each employee

Not:

> every students

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 6: Avoid Double Comparison**

Do not normally combine:

> more + smarter

> most + tallest

Use:

> smarter

> tallest

---

# 19. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Description

> [!important]
> If the word describes **what a noun is like**, think **Adjective of Quality**.

Example:

> an **honest** man

---

## Pattern 2 — How Many?

> [!important]
> If the word answers **"How many?"**, think **Adjective of Number**.

Example:

> five students

---

## Pattern 3 — How Much?

> [!important]
> If the word answers **"How much?"**, think **Adjective of Quantity**.

Example:

> little water

---

## Pattern 4 — Which One?

> [!important]
> If the word points to a particular noun, think **Demonstrative Adjective**.

Example:

> this book

---

## Pattern 5 — Whose?

> [!important]
> If the word shows ownership before a noun, think **Possessive Adjective**.

Example:

> my phone

---

## Pattern 6 — Comparison of Two

> [!important]
> If the sentence compares **two**, think **Comparative**.

Look for:

> than

Example:

> Ram is taller than Ravi.

---

## Pattern 7 — Comparison of Group

> [!important]
> If the sentence compares one person/thing with **three or more**, think **Superlative**.

Example:

> Ram is the tallest student in the class.

---

## Pattern 8 — Adjective vs Adverb

> [!important]
> If the word describes a **noun**, use adjective.
>
> If it describes a **verb/action**, use adverb.

Example:

> He is a **slow** runner.

> He runs **slowly**.

# 21. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Adjective vs Adverb

Incorrect:

> He is a carefully driver.

Correct:

> He is a careful driver.

Reason:

`careful` describes the noun `driver`.

---

### Mistake 2 — Using Superlative for Two

Incorrect:

> Of the two, Ravi is the tallest.

Preferred in standard aptitude grammar:

> Of the two, Ravi is taller.

---

### Mistake 3 — Double Comparison

Incorrect:

> more better

Correct:

> better

---

### Mistake 4 — Double Superlative

Incorrect:

> most fastest

Correct:

> fastest

---

### Mistake 5 — Every + Plural Noun

Incorrect:

> Every students

Correct:

> Every student

---

### Mistake 6 — Confusing Quantity and Number

Use number with countable nouns:

> many books

Use quantity with uncountable nouns:

> much water

---

### Mistake 7 — Good vs Well

`good` is generally an adjective.

`well` is generally an adverb when describing how an action is performed.

Example:

> She is a **good** singer.

> She sings **well**.

---

### Mistake 8 — Less vs Fewer

Use **fewer** with countable nouns.

> fewer students

Use **less** with uncountable nouns.

> less water

# 23. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Adjective

A word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

### Main Types

- Quality
- Quantity
- Number
- Demonstrative
- Possessive
- Interrogative
- Distributive
- Proper

### Comparison

| Degree | Use |
|---|---|
| Positive | One |
| Comparative | Two |
| Superlative | Three or more |

### Fast Recognition

- Describes noun → **Adjective**
- How many? → **Number**
- How much? → **Quantity**
- Which one? → **Demonstrative**
- Whose? → **Possessive**
- Two → **Comparative**
- Three or more → **Superlative**

### High-Priority Traps

- more smarter ❌
- most tallest ❌
- every students ❌
- less students ❌
- much books ❌

Correct:

- smarter
- tallest
- every student
- fewer students
- many books

### Exam Strategy

1. Find the noun.
2. Check whether another word describes it.
3. Identify the adjective type.
4. If comparison exists, determine two vs three+.
5. Check comparative/superlative formation.
6. Check countable vs uncountable usage.
