# Verb

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **verb** is a word that shows an **action, state, occurrence, or condition**.

Examples:

- run
- eat
- write
- study
- sleep
- is
- are
- seem
- become

### Intuition

A verb tells us **what the subject does** or **what state the subject is in**.

Example:

> Rahul **runs** every morning.

`runs` tells us what Rahul does.

> Rahul **is** tired.

`is` tells us Rahul's state.

> [!important]
> Quick test: Ask **"What is the subject doing?"** or **"What is happening?"** The answer often contains the verb.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

A sentence usually needs a verb to express a complete action or state.

### Examples

> She **writes**.

> They **play** football.

> The child **is** happy.

> He **became** a doctor.

The verb may show:

1. Action
2. State
3. Existence
4. Occurrence

---

# 3. Main Formula

There is no mathematical formula for verbs, but the basic sentence pattern is:

$$
\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}
$$

Example:

> Ravi **reads** books.

$$
\text{Ravi} + \text{reads} + \text{books}
$$

Another common structure:

$$
\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Complement}
$$

Example:

> She **is** intelligent.

---

# 4. Important Properties

## 4.1 Action Verbs

Action verbs show physical or mental actions.

Examples:

- run
- walk
- write
- read
- think
- remember
- solve
- speak

Example:

> The student **solved** the problem.

---

## 4.2 State Verbs

State verbs describe a condition, feeling, possession, or mental state rather than a physical action.

Examples:

- know
- believe
- understand
- love
- own
- need
- want
- seem

Example:

> I **know** the answer.

---

## 4.3 Main Verb

The main verb carries the primary meaning of the action or state.

Example:

> She **writes** well.

`writes` → main verb

---

## 4.4 Helping Verb

Helping verbs support the main verb.

Common helping verbs:

- be
- am
- is
- are
- was
- were
- have
- has
- had
- do
- does
- did

Modal auxiliaries also help the main verb:

- can
- could
- may
- might
- shall
- should
- will
- would
- must

Example:

> She **is studying**.

- is → helping verb
- studying → main verb

---

## 4.5 Transitive Verb

A transitive verb normally takes a direct object.

Example:

> Ravi **bought** a laptop.

Ask:

> Ravi bought what?

Answer:

> a laptop

Therefore, `bought` is transitive.

$$
\text{Subject} + \text{Transitive Verb} + \text{Object}
$$

---

## 4.6 Intransitive Verb

An intransitive verb does not take a direct object.

Example:

> The baby **slept**.

There is no direct object.

> The birds **flew**.

Again, no direct object.

---

## 4.7 Linking Verb

A linking verb connects the subject to information describing or identifying it.

Common linking verbs:

- be
- seem
- become
- appear
- feel
- look
- remain
- stay

Example:

> She **is** happy.

`is` connects `She` with `happy`.

---

# 5. Verb Forms

A verb can appear in different forms.

| Form | Example: write | Example: go |
|---|---|---|
| Base form | write | go |
| Present singular | writes | goes |
| Past | wrote | went |
| Past participle | written | gone |
| Present participle | writing | going |

### Important

Regular verbs generally form the past tense using `-ed`.

Example:

> work → worked

Irregular verbs change differently.

Example:

> go → went → gone

> [!warning]
> Irregular verbs must often be memorized because they do not follow a single `-ed` pattern.

---

# 6. Verb and Subject Agreement

The verb must agree with the subject in number and person.

### Singular

> He **plays** cricket.

### Plural

> They **play** cricket.

Compare:

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| I | play |
| You | play |
| We | play |
| They | play |
| He | plays |
| She | plays |
| It | plays |

> [!important]
> In the simple present tense, a third-person singular subject generally takes **-s/-es**.

# 8. Advanced Examples

## Example 6 — Subject-Verb Agreement

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. He play cricket every day.  
B. He plays cricket every day.

### Pattern

`He` is third-person singular.

In simple present:

$$
\text{He} + \text{verb-s}
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. He plays cricket every day.}}
$$

---

## Example 7 — Singular Subject with "Does"

### Question

Choose the correct sentence:

A. She does not likes coffee.  
B. She does not like coffee.

### Pattern

After `does`, use the **base form** of the main verb.

Incorrect:

> does + likes

Correct:

> does + like

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. She does not like coffee.}}
$$

---

## Example 8 — Past Tense with "Did"

Choose the correct sentence:

A. Did you went there?  
B. Did you go there?

### Pattern

After `did`, use the base form.

$$
\text{did} + \text{V1}
$$

### Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{B. Did you go there?}}
$$

# 9. Shortcuts

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 1: Find the Action**
>
> Ask:
>
> **What is the subject doing?**
>
> The answer is often the main verb.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 2: Object Test**
>
> Ask:
>
> **Verb + what? / Verb + whom?**
>
> If there is a direct answer, the verb is likely **transitive**.
>
> Example:
>
> `She bought a phone.`
>
> Bought what? → phone
>
> Therefore → Transitive.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 3: Did/Does/Do Rule**

After:

`do / does / did`

use:

$$
V1
$$

Examples:

> Does he **play**?

> Did she **go**?

> They did not **finish**.

Never:

> Did she went?

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 4: Modal Rule**

After:

`can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must`

use:

$$
V1
$$

Example:

> He can **drive**.

Not:

> He can drives.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 5: Has/Have/Had Rule**

For perfect constructions:

$$
\text{has/have/had} + V3
$$

Example:

> She has **written** the letter.

---

> [!tip]
> **Shortcut 6: Be + V-ing**

For continuous constructions:

$$
\text{am/is/are/was/were} + V\text{-ing}
$$

Example:

> They are **playing**.

---

# 10. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Action

> [!important]
> If the question asks what the subject **does**, identify the action verb.

Example:

> The student **solved** the problem.

Think:

> What did the student do? → solved

---

## Pattern 2 — Helping Verb

> [!important]
> If you see `am, is, are, was, were, has, have, had, do, does, did`, check whether another verb follows.

Example:

> She **is studying**.

`is` → helping  
`studying` → main verb

---

## Pattern 3 — Transitive Verb

> [!important]
> If you can ask **verb + what/whom?** and get a direct object, think **Transitive Verb**.

Example:

> He opened the door.

Opened what?

> the door

Therefore → transitive.

---

## Pattern 4 — Intransitive Verb

> [!important]
> If the verb does not have a direct object, think **Intransitive Verb**.

Example:

> The child slept.

Slept what?

No direct object.

Therefore → intransitive.

---

## Pattern 5 — Modal

> [!important]
> If you see `can, could, may, might, should, must, will, would`, immediately check that the next verb is in **base form**.

Example:

> She can **dance**.

---

## Pattern 6 — Did/Does/Do

> [!important]
> If you see `did`, `does`, or `do`, check whether the following main verb is **V1**.

Example:

> Did he **go**?

Not:

> Did he **went**?

---

## Pattern 7 — Perfect Tense

> [!important]
> If you see `has, have, had`, check for **V3**.

Example:

> He has **finished**.

---

## Pattern 8 — Continuous Tense

> [!important]
> If you see `am/is/are/was/were`, check whether the main verb is **V-ing** when a continuous tense is intended.

Example:

> She is **reading**.

# 12. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Third-Person Singular

Incorrect:

> He play cricket.

Correct:

> He plays cricket.

---

### Mistake 2 — Does + V-s

Incorrect:

> She does not likes tea.

Correct:

> She does not like tea.

Reason:

$$
\text{does} + V1
$$

---

### Mistake 3 — Did + V2

Incorrect:

> Did you went there?

Correct:

> Did you go there?

Reason:

$$
\text{did} + V1
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Modal + V-s

Incorrect:

> He can sings.

Correct:

> He can sing.

Reason:

$$
\text{modal} + V1
$$

---

### Mistake 5 — Has + V1

Incorrect:

> She has complete the task.

Correct:

> She has completed the task.

Reason:

$$
\text{has/have/had} + V3
$$

---

### Mistake 6 — Confusing Main and Helping Verb

In:

> She is working.

`is` is not the main action. `working` carries the main action.

Correct:

- is → helping verb
- working → main verb

---

### Mistake 7 — Incorrect Past Participle

Incorrect:

> He has went home.

Correct:

> He has gone home.

Because:

$$
\text{go} \rightarrow \text{went} \rightarrow \text{gone}
$$

---

### Mistake 8 — Confusing Transitive and Intransitive

Do not assume that every verb must have an object.

Example:

> The baby slept.

`Slept` has no direct object.

# 14. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Verb

A word showing an action, state, occurrence, or condition.

### Main Categories

- Main verb
- Helping verb
- Transitive verb
- Intransitive verb
- Linking verb
- Regular verb
- Irregular verb

### High-Priority Rules

1. Third-person singular → generally `V-s/V-es` in simple present.
2. `do/does/did + V1`
3. `modal + V1`
4. `has/have/had + V3`
5. `am/is/are/was/were + V-ing` for continuous forms.
6. Verb + direct object → transitive.
7. No direct object → intransitive.
8. Linking verb connects subject with description or identity.

### Fast Exam Strategy

1. Find what the subject does or what state it is in.
2. Identify the main verb.
3. Check for helping verbs.
4. Check subject-verb agreement.
5. Look for `do/does/did`, modals, or perfect auxiliaries.
6. Verify the correct verb form.
7. Check whether a direct object exists.

## Golden Memory Trick

**The verb tells you what the subject does, what happens, or what state the subject is in.**

## One-Line Recognition

**When you see a sentence, first find the action or state of the subject — that is your verb.**
