# Preposition

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> A **preposition** is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in a sentence.

Prepositions commonly show:

- Time
- Place
- Position
- Direction
- Movement
- Relationship

Common prepositions:

`in, on, at, by, for, from, to, with, into, onto, under, over, between, among, beside, behind, before, after`

### Intuition

A preposition usually answers questions such as:

- Where?
- When?
- In which direction?
- Between whom or what?
- From where?
- With whom?

Example:

> The book is **on** the table.

`on` shows the relationship between `book` and `table`.

> [!important]
> Quick test: If a word shows the **relationship of a noun/pronoun with another word**, think **PREPOSITION**.

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

A preposition is generally followed by a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase.

### Examples

> The book is **on the table**.

> She came **from Chennai**.

> He is sitting **under the tree**.

> We will meet **at 5 PM**.

The noun or pronoun after a preposition is called its **object**.

### Example

> She sat **beside Rahul**.

- beside → preposition
- Rahul → object of the preposition

---

# 3. Main Formula

$$
\text{Preposition} + \text{Noun/Pronoun}
$$

Example:

$$
\text{on} + \text{the table}
$$

A common structure is:

$$
\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Preposition} + \text{Object}
$$

Example:

> She sat **on the chair**.

# 5. Important Prepositions of Time

## At

Use **at** for a specific clock time or certain specific points of time.

Examples:

> at 5 PM

> at noon

> at midnight

> at night

### Recognition

> [!important]
> Exact clock time → **AT**

$$
\text{at} + \text{specific time}
$$

Example:

> The train arrives **at 6:30 PM**.

---

## On

Use **on** with days and dates.

Examples:

> on Monday

> on Sunday

> on 15 August

> on my birthday

### Recognition

> [!important]
> Day or date → **ON**

---

## In

Use **in** with months, years, seasons, and longer periods.

Examples:

> in January

> in 2026

> in summer

> in the morning

### Recognition

> [!important]
> Month, year, season, or longer period → **IN**

---

# 6. At vs On vs In

| Preposition | Typical Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| At | Exact time | at 7 PM |
| On | Day/date | on Monday |
| In | Month/year/period | in January |
| At | Point/place | at the station |
| On | Surface | on the table |
| In | Enclosed area | in the room |

### Memory Rule

$$
\boxed{\text{AT} = \text{Point}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{ON} = \text{Surface/Day}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{IN} = \text{Inside/Longer Period}}
$$

---

# 7. Since vs For

This is a very common aptitude pattern.

## Since

Use **since** with the **starting point** of an action.

Examples:

> since Monday

> since 2020

> since morning

> since 5 PM

## For

Use **for** with the **duration**.

Examples:

> for two hours

> for three days

> for five years

### Formula

$$
\text{Since} = \text{Starting Point}
$$

$$
\text{For} = \text{Duration}
$$

> [!important]
> If the phrase answers **"From when?"**, think **since**.
>
> If it answers **"How long?"**, think **for**.

---

# 8. By vs Until

## By

Means **not later than a particular time**.

Example:

> Submit the assignment **by Monday**.

Meaning:

> Monday is the deadline.

## Until

Means an action or state continues **up to a particular time**.

Example:

> I will wait **until Monday**.

### Recognition

$$
\text{By} = \text{Deadline}
$$

$$
\text{Until} = \text{Continuation}
$$

---

# 9. Important Prepositions of Place

## In

Used for an enclosed area or larger geographical area.

Examples:

> in the room

> in India

> in Chennai

> in a box

---

## On

Used for a surface.

Examples:

> on the table

> on the wall

> on the floor

---

## At

Used for a specific point or location.

Examples:

> at the station

> at the bus stop

> at the door

> at school

---

# 10. Between vs Among

## Between

Traditionally used when referring to **two** people or things, and also commonly used for distinct individual relationships involving more than two.

Basic aptitude rule:

$$
\text{Between} \rightarrow \text{Two}
$$

Example:

> The agreement is between Ravi and Arun.

## Among

Used for a group or when considering people/things collectively.

$$
\text{Among} \rightarrow \text{Group}
$$

Example:

> She was sitting among her friends.

> [!important]
> For basic placement questions:
>
> `between` → two/distinct individuals
>
> `among` → group/collective setting

---

# 11. Beside vs Besides

These are frequently confused.

## Beside

Means **next to**.

Example:

> He sat beside me.

## Besides

Means **in addition to**.

Example:

> Besides English, she knows Hindi.

### Memory Rule

$$
\text{Beside} = \text{Next to}
$$

$$
\text{Besides} = \text{In addition to}
$$

---

# 12. Into vs In

## In

Shows position inside something.

> He is **in** the room.

## Into

Shows movement from outside to inside.

> He walked **into** the room.

### Memory Rule

$$
\text{In} = \text{Position}
$$

$$
\text{Into} = \text{Movement}
$$

---

# 13. On vs Onto

## On

Shows position.

> The book is on the table.

## Onto

Shows movement toward a surface.

> The cat jumped onto the table.

### Memory Rule

$$
\text{On} = \text{Position}
$$

$$
\text{Onto} = \text{Movement to Surface}
$$

---

# 14. By vs With

## By

Often shows:

- Agent
- Method
- Transport

Examples:

> The novel was written **by** Shakespeare.

> I travelled **by** train.

## With

Often shows:

- Instrument
- Accompaniment

Examples:

> He cut the paper **with** a knife.

> She went **with** her friend.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> Passive-agent → **by**
>
> Instrument/tool → **with**

Some verbs/adjectives are commonly followed by particular prepositions.

| proud | of | proud of |
| belong | to | belong to |

| wait | for | wait for |
| agree | with | agree with |

> [!warning]
> Do not always translate prepositions directly from your native language. English uses fixed combinations that must be learned through usage.

# 19. Recognition Tricks

## Pattern 1 — Exact Time

> [!important]
> If the question gives a clock time such as `5 PM`, `10:30 AM`, or `midnight`, think **AT**.

---

## Pattern 2 — Day or Date

> [!important]
> If the question gives a day or date, think **ON**.

Examples:

- on Monday
- on Sunday
- on 15 August

---

## Pattern 3 — Month or Year

> [!important]
> If the question gives a month, year, season, or longer period, think **IN**.

Examples:

- in January
- in 2026
- in summer

---

## Pattern 4 — Starting Point vs Duration

> [!important]
> If the question says a **starting point**, think **SINCE**.
>
> If it says a **duration**, think **FOR**.

---

## Pattern 5 — Deadline

> [!important]
> If something must happen **not later than** a particular time, think **BY**.

Example:

> Submit it by Friday.

---

## Pattern 6 — Continuation

> [!important]
> If an action continues up to a particular point, think **UNTIL**.

Example:

> Wait until Monday.

---

## Pattern 7 — Movement Inside

> [!important]
> If something moves from outside to inside, think **INTO**.

Example:

> He walked into the room.

---

## Pattern 8 — Tool vs Agent

> [!important]
> If a person performs an action in passive voice, think **BY**.
>
> If a tool is used to perform an action, think **WITH**.

---

## Pattern 9 — Fixed Preposition

> [!important]
> Some words naturally pair with specific prepositions.
>
> Examples:
>
> `depend on`
>
> `interested in`
>
> `good at`
>
> `afraid of`
>
> `belong to`

# 21. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Incorrect Time Preposition

Incorrect:

> The meeting starts in 5 PM.

Correct:

> The meeting starts at 5 PM.

Reason:

Exact clock time → `at`.

---

### Mistake 2 — Day with In

Incorrect:

> I will come in Monday.

Correct:

> I will come on Monday.

---

### Mistake 3 — Year with On

Incorrect:

> He joined on 2020.

Correct:

> He joined in 2020.

---

### Mistake 4 — Since + Duration

Incorrect:

> I have studied here since five years.

Correct:

> I have studied here for five years.

---

### Mistake 5 — For + Starting Point

Incorrect:

> She has lived here for 2020.

Correct:

> She has lived here since 2020.

---

### Mistake 6 — By vs Until

Incorrect:

> Submit the form until Friday.

If Friday is the deadline:

> Submit the form by Friday.

---

### Mistake 7 — Beside vs Besides

Incorrect:

> Besides me means next to me.

Correct:

> Beside me means next to me.

---

### Mistake 8 — In vs Into

Incorrect:

> He walked in the room.

When movement into the room is emphasized:

> He walked into the room.

---

### Mistake 9 — Fixed Preposition

Incorrect:

> She is good in mathematics.

Correct:

> She is good at mathematics.

---

### Mistake 10 — Senior Than

Incorrect:

> He is senior than me.

Correct:

> He is senior to me.

# 23. Quick Revision

> [!summary] One-Minute Revision

### Preposition

A word that shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another word.

### Time

- Exact time → `at`
- Day/date → `on`
- Month/year/long period → `in`
- Starting point → `since`
- Duration → `for`
- Deadline → `by`
- Continuation → `until`

### Place

- Enclosed area → `in`
- Surface → `on`
- Specific point → `at`

### Direction

- Movement inside → `into`
- Movement toward surface → `onto`
- Direction → `to/towards`
- Starting location → `from`

### Important Pairs

- beside → next to
- besides → in addition to
- between → two/distinct relationships
- among → group
- by → agent
- with → instrument

### Fixed Combinations

- depend **on**
- interested **in**
- good **at**
- afraid **of**
- proud **of**
- belong **to**
- listen **to**
- wait **for**
- agree **with**
- consist **of**

### Exam Strategy

1. Identify the relationship.
2. Decide whether it refers to time, place, direction, or another relationship.
3. Check special pairs.
4. Check fixed preposition combinations.
5. Eliminate options that violate the standard usage.

---

## Golden Memory Trick

**AT is a point, ON is a surface/day, IN is inside/a longer period.**

## One-Line Recognition

**When a word connects a noun or pronoun to another part of the sentence by showing time, place, direction, or relationship, think PREPOSITION.**
