# Past Tense

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Past Tense** is used to describe actions, events, situations, habits, or states that happened or existed before the present time.

The past tense has **four major forms**:

1. Simple Past
2. Past Continuous
3. Past Perfect
4. Past Perfect Continuous

### Core Idea

Think:

- **Simple Past** → completed past action
- **Past Continuous** → action in progress at a particular past time
- **Past Perfect** → action completed before another past action
- **Past Perfect Continuous** → action continuing for a duration before another past point/action

### The Most Important Pattern

> [!important]
> When a question contains **two past actions**, first ask:
>
> **Which action happened first?**
>
> Earlier action → often **Past Perfect**
>
> Later action → often **Simple Past**

Example:

> The train **had left** before I **reached** the station.

First action:

> had left

Second action:

> reached

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Past tense can describe:

- completed actions
- past habits
- past states
- actions happening at a particular past time
- two past actions in sequence
- an action that happened before another past action
- duration before a past event
- repeated actions in the past
- past situations that are no longer true

### Examples

> I visited Chennai yesterday.

Simple Past.

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

Past Continuous.

> I had finished the work before he arrived.

Past Perfect.

> I had been studying for two hours before he called.

Past Perfect Continuous.

---

# 3. Main Formula

| Tense | Basic Formula | Main Use |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Past | Subject + V2 | Completed past action |
| Past Continuous | Subject + was/were + V-ing | Action in progress in the past |
| Past Perfect | Subject + had + V3 | Earlier of two past actions |
| Past Perfect Continuous | Subject + had been + V-ing | Duration before a past point |

### Quick Comparison

$$
\boxed{\text{Simple Past} = V2}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Continuous} = was/were + V-ing}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect} = had + V3}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect Continuous} = had been + V-ing}
$$

# 4. Verb Forms Used in Past Tense

For regular verbs:

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| V1 | work |
| V2 | worked |
| V3 | worked |
| V-ing | working |

For irregular verbs:

| V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| write | wrote | written |
| see | saw | seen |
| take | took | taken |
| come | came | come |
| do | did | done |
| speak | spoke | spoken |

> [!important]
> **Simple Past uses V2.**
>
> **Past Perfect uses V3 after `had`.**

# 5. Simple Past Tense

## 5.1 Definition

Simple Past describes an action or situation that was completed in the past.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + V2}
$$

Examples:

> I visited Delhi.

> She completed the assignment.

> They played cricket.

> He went home.

---

# 6. Simple Past — Completed Action

Use Simple Past when an action started and ended in the past.

Examples:

> I watched a movie yesterday.

> She completed the project last week.

> They visited Mumbai in 2024.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> Completed action + finished past time → **Simple Past**

---

# 7. Simple Past — Common Time Clues

Common clues include:

- yesterday
- last night
- last week
- last month
- last year
- ago
- in 2020
- in 2019
- two days ago
- when I was young
- once
- previously

### Examples

> I met him yesterday.

> She graduated in 2024.

> He left two hours ago.

---

# 8. Simple Past — Past Habits

Simple Past can describe repeated habits that existed in the past.

Examples:

> I played cricket when I was young.

> She visited her grandmother every Sunday.

> We walked to school every day.

### Important

For a regular past habit:

$$
\boxed{\text{Simple Past}}
$$

can be used when the context clearly places the habit in the past.

---

# 9. Simple Past — Past States

Use Simple Past for situations that existed in the past.

Examples:

> I lived in Chennai in 2020.

> She worked for that company before.

> He was very shy as a child.

---

# 10. Regular Verbs in Simple Past

Most regular verbs form the past by adding `-ed`.

Examples:

> work → worked

> play → played

> clean → cleaned

> watch → watched

> visit → visited

### Formula

$$
\boxed{V1 + ed \rightarrow V2}
$$

However, spelling rules apply.

---

# 11. Spelling Rules for -ED

## Rule 1 — Usually Add ED

> work → worked

> clean → cleaned

> open → opened

---

## Rule 2 — Verb Ending in E

Add only `d`.

> live → lived

> love → loved

> move → moved

---

## Rule 3 — Consonant + Y

Change `y` to `i` and add `ed`.

> study → studied

> carry → carried

> try → tried

---

## Rule 4 — Vowel + Y

Simply add `ed`.

> play → played

> enjoy → enjoyed

---

## Rule 5 — Some Short Verbs Double the Final Consonant

Examples:

> stop → stopped

> plan → planned

> rob → robbed

> [!important]
> Spelling questions may test the difference between:
>
> `study → studied`
>
> `play → played`
>
> `stop → stopped`

---

# 13. Negative Simple Past

Use:

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + did\ not + V1}
$$

Examples:

> I did not go.

> She did not finish the work.

> They did not play.

> He did not come.

> [!warning]
> After `did` or `didn't`, always use **V1**.

Correct:

> He did not **go**.

Incorrect:

> He did not **went**.

---

# 14. Simple Past Questions

Formula:

$$
\boxed{\text{Did} + \text{Subject} + V1?}
$$

Examples:

> Did you go there?

> Did she finish the work?

> Did they play cricket?

> Did he call you?

### Important

After `did`, use V1.

Correct:

> Did he **go**?

Incorrect:

> Did he **went**?

---

# 15. Simple Past with "Be"

The verb `be` is special.

### Forms

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| am | was |
| is | was |
| are | were |

### Examples

> I was tired.

> He was late.

> She was happy.

> You were busy.

> They were ready.

---

# 16. Was vs Were

Use:

$$
\boxed{\text{I/He/She/It} \rightarrow \text{was}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{You/We/They} \rightarrow \text{were}}
$$

Examples:

> I was tired.

> He was late.

> She was happy.

> We were ready.

> They were busy.

### Important Exception

`You` takes `were`:

> You were late.

Not:

> You was late.

---

# 17. Past Continuous Tense

## 17.1 Definition

Past Continuous describes an action that was **in progress at a particular time in the past**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + was/were + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I was studying.

> She was sleeping.

> They were playing.

---

# 18. Past Continuous — Was/Were

| Subject | Helping Verb |
|---|---|
| I | was |
| He | was |
| She | was |
| It | was |
| You | were |
| We | were |
| They | were |

### Examples

> I was working.

> He was working.

> They were working.

> We were working.

---

# 19. Past Continuous — Specific Past Time

Use Past Continuous when an action was happening at a particular moment in the past.

Examples:

> At 8 p.m., I was studying.

> At midnight, she was sleeping.

> At 5 o'clock, they were playing football.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> **At + specific past time + ongoing action → Past Continuous**

---

# 20. Past Continuous — Interrupted Action

This is one of the most important patterns.

One action was in progress when another action happened.

### Structure

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Continuous} + \text{when} + \text{Simple Past}}
$$

Example:

> I was studying when my friend called.

The ongoing action:

> was studying

The interrupting action:

> called

---

# 21. When vs While

### When

`When` is commonly used to introduce the shorter/interruption action.

Example:

> I was sleeping when the phone rang.

### While

`While` commonly introduces an action that was in progress.

Example:

> While I was sleeping, the phone rang.

### Common Pattern

$$
\boxed{\text{While} + \text{Past Continuous}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{When} + \text{Simple Past}}
$$

This is a useful pattern, but context can vary.

---

# 22. Two Simultaneous Past Actions

Use Past Continuous for two actions happening at the same time.

Examples:

> While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.

> She was cooking while he was cleaning.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{While + Past Continuous},\ \text{Past Continuous}}
$$

---

# 23. Past Continuous — Background Action

Past Continuous is often used to establish the background of a story.

Example:

> It was raining when I left the house.

`was raining` gives the background.

`left` gives the main event.

> [!important]
> **Background action → Past Continuous**
>
> **Main/interruption event → Simple Past**

---

# 24. Past Continuous — Temporary Past Situation

Past Continuous can describe a temporary situation during a past period.

Example:

> I was staying with my friend that week.

> She was working in Chennai at that time.

---

# 25. Past Continuous — Negative

Formula:

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + was/were + not + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I was not sleeping.

> He was not working.

> They were not studying.

---

# 26. Past Continuous — Questions

Formula:

$$
\boxed{\text{Was/Were} + \text{Subject} + V-ing?}
$$

Examples:

> Were you sleeping?

> Was she working?

> Were they studying?

---

# 27. Past Perfect Tense

## 27.1 Definition

Past Perfect describes an action that was completed **before another action or point in the past**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + had + V3}
$$

Example:

> I had finished the work before he arrived.

First:

> had finished

Second:

> arrived

---

# 28. The Golden Rule of Past Perfect

When two actions happened in the past:

### Ask:

> Which happened first?

The earlier action can take:

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect}}
$$

The later action can take:

$$
\boxed{\text{Simple Past}}
$$

### Example

> The movie had started before we arrived.

Timeline:

$$
\text{Movie started} \rightarrow \text{We arrived}
$$

Grammar:

> had started → arrived

---

# 29. Past Perfect with Before

Common structure:

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect} + before + \text{Simple Past}}
$$

Example:

> She had left before I arrived.

Another example:

> They had completed the work before the manager came.

---

# 30. Past Perfect with After

Common structure:

$$
\boxed{\text{After} + \text{Past Perfect},\ \text{Simple Past}}
$$

Example:

> After he had finished the work, he went home.

### Timeline

$$
\text{finished} \rightarrow \text{went}
$$

---

# 31. Past Perfect with By the Time

`By the time` is a strong clue for a completed earlier action.

Example:

> By the time I arrived, the train had left.

### Pattern

$$
\boxed{\text{By the time} + \text{Simple Past},\ \text{Past Perfect}}
$$

Example:

> By the time she reached the station, the bus had departed.

---

# 32. Past Perfect with Already

Example:

> She had already finished the work when I called.

`already` emphasizes that the action was completed before another past event.

---

# 33. Past Perfect with Just

Example:

> He had just left when I arrived.

Meaning:

He left shortly before I arrived.

---

# 34. Past Perfect with Never

Example:

> I had never seen snow before I visited Kashmir.

The experience had not occurred before that past point.

---

# 35. Past Perfect with Until

Example:

> He had never travelled abroad until he got the job.

The past perfect describes the state before the later past event.

---

# 36. Past Perfect — Negative

Formula:

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + had + not + V3}
$$

Examples:

> I had not finished the work.

> She had not arrived.

> They had not eaten.

---

# 37. Past Perfect — Questions

Formula:

$$
\boxed{\text{Had} + \text{Subject} + V3?}
$$

Examples:

> Had you finished the work?

> Had she arrived?

> Had they left?

---

# 38. Past Perfect Continuous

## 38.1 Definition

Past Perfect Continuous describes an action that:

- started before a particular point in the past
- continued for some duration
- continued until another past event or point
- emphasizes duration

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Subject} + had + been + V-ing}
$$

Example:

> I had been studying for two hours before he called.

---

# 39. Past Perfect Continuous with For

Use `for` to show duration.

Examples:

> I had been studying for three hours before dinner.

> She had been working for five years before she changed jobs.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{had been + V-ing} + \text{for + duration}}
$$

---

# 40. Past Perfect Continuous with Since

Use `since` to show the starting point.

Examples:

> She had been working since morning before she took a break.

> They had been living there since 2018 before they moved.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Since} + \text{Starting Point}}
$$

---

# 41. Past Perfect vs Past Perfect Continuous

This is a high-value distinction.

### Past Perfect

Focus:

> completion/result

Example:

> I had written five pages before dinner.

Focus:

> five pages were completed.

### Past Perfect Continuous

Focus:

> duration/activity

Example:

> I had been writing for two hours before dinner.

Focus:

> how long I had been writing.

| Past Perfect | Past Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|
| had + V3 | had been + V-ing |
| Completion/result | Duration/activity |
| I had written five pages. | I had been writing for two hours. |

---

# 42. Past Tense Timeline

Understanding the timeline makes tense questions much easier.

### Simple Past

$$
\text{Past Event}
$$

Example:

> I visited Delhi.

### Past Continuous

$$
\text{Past} \quad [\text{Action in Progress}] \quad \text{Past}
$$

Example:

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

### Past Perfect

$$
\text{Earlier Past} \rightarrow \text{Later Past}
$$

Example:

> I had studied before he arrived.

### Past Perfect Continuous

$$
\text{Started Earlier} \rightarrow \text{Duration} \rightarrow \text{Past Event}
$$

Example:

> I had been studying for two hours before he arrived.

---

# 43. Simple Past vs Past Continuous

## Simple Past

Completed action:

> I watched TV.

## Past Continuous

Action in progress at a past time:

> I was watching TV at 8 p.m.

### Compare

> I watched TV yesterday.

The action is presented as a completed event.

> I was watching TV when you called.

The action was in progress when another event occurred.

> [!important]
> **Completed event → Simple Past**
>
> **Action in progress → Past Continuous**

---

# 44. Simple Past vs Past Perfect

### Simple Past

Use when there is only one completed past action.

> I visited Chennai yesterday.

### Past Perfect

Use when you need to show an earlier action relative to another past action.

> I had visited Chennai before I moved there.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> If there is only one completed past event, do not add Past Perfect unnecessarily.

---

# 45. Past Perfect vs Simple Past

Example:

> When I reached the station, the train left.

This can imply that the train left around the time I reached.

But:

> When I reached the station, the train had left.

This clearly means:

$$
\text{Train left first} \rightarrow \text{I reached later}
$$

> [!important]
> **Had + V3 creates a clear "earlier past" relationship.**

---

# 46. Past Continuous vs Past Perfect Continuous

### Past Continuous

Focus:

> action in progress at a specific past time.

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

### Past Perfect Continuous

Focus:

> duration leading up to a past point.

> I had been studying for two hours before 8 p.m.

# 51. Important Past Tense Patterns

## Pattern 1 — Finished Past Time

> [!important]
> If the question contains:
>
> `yesterday`
>
> `last week`
>
> `last year`
>
> `ago`
>
> `in 2020`
>
> and the event is finished:
>
> Think **Simple Past**.

Example:

> I visited Delhi last year.

---

## Pattern 2 — Action in Progress

> [!important]
> If an action was happening at a particular past time:
>
> Think **Past Continuous**.

Example:

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

---

## Pattern 3 — Interrupted Action

> [!important]
> If one action was already happening when another action occurred:
>
> Ongoing action → **Past Continuous**
>
> Interrupting action → **Simple Past**

Example:

> I was sleeping when the phone rang.

---

## Pattern 4 — Two Past Actions

> [!important]
> If two past actions are given, identify which happened first.
>
> Earlier → **Past Perfect**
>
> Later → **Simple Past**

Example:

> The train had left before we arrived.

---

## Pattern 5 — Before

> [!important]
> `before` often signals a sequence.
>
> Earlier completed action → **Past Perfect**
>
> Later action → **Simple Past**

Example:

> She had finished before I arrived.

---

## Pattern 6 — After

> [!important]
> `after` can introduce an earlier completed action.
>
> Example:
>
> After he had finished, he went home.

---

## Pattern 7 — By the Time

> [!important]
> `by the time` is a strong clue for an action completed before another past event.

Example:

> By the time I arrived, the train had left.

---

## Pattern 8 — Duration Before Past Event

> [!important]
> If an activity continued for a duration before another past event:
>
> Think **Past Perfect Continuous**.

Example:

> I had been waiting for an hour before the bus arrived.

---

## Pattern 9 — While

> [!important]
> `while` commonly introduces an ongoing background action.

Example:

> While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.

---

## Pattern 10 — Was/Were + V-ing

> [!important]
> If you see:
>
> `was/were + V-ing`
>
> the sentence is using **Past Continuous**.

---

## Pattern 11 — Had + V3

> [!important]
> If you see:
>
> `had + V3`
>
> think **Past Perfect**.

Example:

> had finished

> had gone

> had written

---

## Pattern 12 — Had Been + V-ing

> [!important]
> If you see:
>
> `had been + V-ing`
>
> think **Past Perfect Continuous**.

Example:

> had been studying

> had been working

> had been waiting

---

# 52. Since vs For

This is a common placement-test pattern.

## Since

Use `since` with a **starting point**.

Examples:

- since Monday
- since 2020
- since morning
- since 5 p.m.
- since childhood

$$
\boxed{\text{Since} = \text{When it started}}
$$

---

## For

Use `for` with a **duration**.

Examples:

- for two hours
- for five days
- for three years
- for a long time

$$
\boxed{\text{For} = \text{How long}}
$$

### Quick Trick

> [!tip]
> Ask:
>
> **"When did it start?" → SINCE**
>
> **"How long?" → FOR**

---

# 53. Before vs After

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| before | Earlier than |
| after | Later than |
| by the time | No later than that point |
| when | At the time that |
| while | During an ongoing action |
| until | Up to a particular time |

### Example

> I had eaten before he arrived.

Timeline:

$$
\text{ate} \rightarrow \text{arrived}
$$

> After I had eaten, I went outside.

Timeline:

$$
\text{ate} \rightarrow \text{went}
$$

# 54. Past Tense Decision Tree

> [!important] Fast Exam Method

### Step 1 — Is the action completed in the past?

Yes:

Check for a finished past time.

If yes:

$$
\boxed{\text{Simple Past}}
$$

Example:

> I visited Delhi yesterday.

---

### Step 2 — Was the action in progress at a past time?

Yes:

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Continuous}}
$$

Example:

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

---

### Step 3 — Are there two past actions?

Ask:

> Which happened first?

Earlier:

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect}}
$$

Later:

$$
\boxed{\text{Simple Past}}
$$

---

### Step 4 — Was duration important before another past event?

Yes:

$$
\boxed{\text{Past Perfect Continuous}}
$$

Example:

> I had been waiting for two hours before the bus arrived.

# 55. Timeline Recognition

> [!important]
> Learn to visualize the timeline.

### Simple Past

$$
\boxed{\text{PAST EVENT}}
$$

### Past Continuous

$$
\boxed{\text{PAST} \quad [\text{ACTION IN PROGRESS}]}
$$

### Past Perfect

$$
\boxed{\text{EARLIER PAST} \rightarrow \text{LATER PAST}}
$$

### Past Perfect Continuous

$$
\boxed{\text{START} \rightarrow \text{DURATION} \rightarrow \text{PAST EVENT}}
$$

This timeline method is often faster than memorizing isolated rules.

# 57. Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Avoid These

### Mistake 1 — Did + V2

Incorrect:

> Did he went?

Correct:

> Did he go?

Rule:

$$
\boxed{Did + V1}
$$

---

### Mistake 2 — Didn't + V2

Incorrect:

> She didn't completed the task.

Correct:

> She didn't complete the task.

---

### Mistake 3 — Wrong Past Perfect Form

Incorrect:

> He had went home.

Correct:

> He had gone home.

Rule:

$$
\boxed{Had + V3}
$$

---

### Mistake 4 — Using Had Without a Past Relationship

Incorrect in a simple one-event context:

> I had visited Delhi yesterday.

Better:

> I visited Delhi yesterday.

Past Perfect is normally used to establish an earlier relationship with another past point/action.

---

### Mistake 5 — Confusing V2 and V3

Incorrect:

> She had wrote a letter.

Correct:

> She had written a letter.

Remember:

> write → wrote → written

---

### Mistake 6 — Wrong Continuous Form

Incorrect:

> I was study.

Correct:

> I was studying.

---

### Mistake 7 — Wrong Helping Verb

Incorrect:

> They was playing.

Correct:

> They were playing.

---

### Mistake 8 — You + Was

Incorrect:

> You was late.

Correct:

> You were late.

---

### Mistake 9 — Missing Past Perfect

Incorrect:

> When I arrived, the train left.

If the train had already departed:

Correct:

> When I arrived, the train had left.

---

### Mistake 10 — Wrong Since/For

Incorrect:

> I had been waiting since two hours.

Correct:

> I had been waiting for two hours.

---

### Mistake 11 — Using Simple Past for an Ongoing Background Action

Less precise:

> I studied when he called.

If the studying was already in progress:

> I was studying when he called.

---

### Mistake 12 — Confusing Earlier Action

Incorrect:

> After he went home, he had called me.

If the intended order is:

$$
\text{went home} \rightarrow \text{called}
$$

Simple Past can describe both when the sequence is already clear.

If emphasis is on the earlier completed action:

> After he had gone home, he called me.

---

# 58. Advanced Error Detection

## Error 1

> When I reached the office, the manager has left.

### Problem

The manager left before I reached.

Both events are in the past.

### Correct

> When I reached the office, the manager had left.

---

## Error 2

> She was write a letter when I entered.

### Problem

Past Continuous requires V-ing.

### Correct

> She was writing a letter when I entered.

---

## Error 3

> They had been waited for two hours.

### Problem

Past Perfect Continuous requires V-ing.

### Correct

> They had been waiting for two hours.

---

## Error 4

> Did you saw the movie?

### Problem

`did + V1`.

### Correct

> Did you see the movie?

---

## Error 5

> He had completed the work yesterday before he left.

If `yesterday` simply identifies a finished past event, Simple Past may be more natural unless the earlier relationship is specifically relevant.

Better in many contexts:

> He completed the work yesterday before he left.

However, if establishing sequence is important:

> He had completed the work before he left yesterday.

> [!important]
> Do not choose Past Perfect just because `yesterday` appears. Analyze the timeline and intended emphasis.
