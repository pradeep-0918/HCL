# Continuous Tense

## 1. Core Concept

> [!summary]
> **Continuous Tense** is used to show that an action is **in progress or continuing around a particular time**.

The key idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Continuous Tense} = \text{Action in Progress}}
$$

There are three main Continuous forms:

1. Present Continuous
2. Past Continuous
3. Future Continuous

The basic structure always contains:

$$
\boxed{\text{BE verb} + V-ing}
$$

### Master Structure

| Tense | Formula | Main Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + V-ing | Happening now/around now |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | Happening at a past time |
| Future Continuous | will be + V-ing | Happening at a future time |

> [!important]
> The easiest recognition trick:
>
> **BE + V-ing = Continuous**
>
> `am/is/are + V-ing` → Present Continuous
>
> `was/were + V-ing` → Past Continuous
>
> `will be + V-ing` → Future Continuous

---

# 2. Basic Meaning

Continuous Tense focuses on the **activity itself**, not simply whether it is completed.

Compare:

> I study Java.

This describes a habit or general fact.

> I am studying Java.

This focuses on an activity happening around the present time.

Similarly:

> I studied Java yesterday.

Simple Past.

> I was studying Java at 8 p.m. yesterday.

Past Continuous.

> I will study Java tomorrow.

Simple Future.

> I will be studying Java at 8 p.m. tomorrow.

Future Continuous.

---

# 3. Main Formula

## Present Continuous

$$
\boxed{S + am/is/are + V-ing}
$$

## Past Continuous

$$
\boxed{S + was/were + V-ing}
$$

## Future Continuous

$$
\boxed{S + will + be + V-ing}
$$

---

# 4. Important Properties

1. Continuous Tense uses `V-ing`.
2. A form of `be` must appear before `V-ing`.
3. The `be` verb changes according to time.
4. Continuous Tense focuses on an action in progress.
5. A specific time is often mentioned.
6. Continuous Tense can describe temporary situations.
7. Present Continuous can describe definite future arrangements.
8. Past Continuous can describe an interrupted action.
9. Two simultaneous actions can both use Continuous Tense.
10. Future Continuous can describe an activity expected to be in progress at a future time.

---

# 5. Present Continuous

## 5.1 Definition

Present Continuous describes an action that is happening **now or around the present period**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + am/is/are + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I am studying.

> She is working.

> They are playing.

---

# 6. Present Continuous — Am, Is, Are

| Subject | Helping Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am studying. |
| He | is | He is studying. |
| She | is | She is studying. |
| It | is | It is raining. |
| You | are | You are studying. |
| We | are | We are studying. |
| They | are | They are studying. |

> [!important]
> Remember:
>
> **I → am**
>
> **He/She/It → is**
>
> **You/We/They → are**

---

# 7. Present Continuous — Action Happening Now

Use Present Continuous when an action is happening at the time of speaking.

Examples:

> I am writing my notes.

> She is talking to her friend.

> They are playing football.

> The teacher is explaining the topic.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> If the action is happening **right now**, think:
>
> **am/is/are + V-ing**

---

# 8. Present Continuous — Around Now

The action does not always have to happen at the exact second of speaking.

Example:

> I am learning Python this month.

This means the activity is currently in progress during the present period.

Another example:

> She is preparing for her placement exams.

The preparation is an ongoing temporary activity.

---

# 9. Present Continuous — Temporary Situation

Present Continuous is commonly used for temporary situations.

Compare:

> He works in Chennai.

This may describe a regular or permanent situation.

> He is working in Chennai this month.

This emphasizes a temporary situation.

> [!important]
> **Temporary/current situation → Present Continuous**
>
> **Permanent/general situation → often Simple Present**

---

# 10. Present Continuous — Changing Situation

Present Continuous can describe something that is gradually changing.

Examples:

> The weather is getting colder.

> Technology is changing rapidly.

> The company is growing.

> Prices are increasing.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> If the sentence describes a **change or development happening around now**, consider Present Continuous.

---

# 11. Present Continuous — Definite Future Arrangement

Present Continuous can refer to a future arrangement that has already been planned.

Examples:

> I am meeting my professor tomorrow.

> She is travelling to Delhi next week.

> We are attending the conference on Monday.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> **Future time + fixed personal arrangement → Present Continuous**

---

# 12. Present Continuous — Negative

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + am/is/are + not + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I am not studying.

> She is not working.

> They are not coming.

Short forms:

> isn't = is not

> aren't = are not

---

# 13. Present Continuous — Questions

### Formula

$$
\boxed{Am/Is/Are + S + V-ing?}
$$

Examples:

> Are you studying?

> Is she working?

> Are they coming?

> Am I disturbing you?

---

# 14. Past Continuous

## 14.1 Definition

Past Continuous describes an action that was **in progress at a particular time in the past**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + was/were + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I was studying.

> She was working.

> They were playing.

---

# 15. Past Continuous — Was vs Were

| Subject | Helping Verb |
|---|---|
| I | was |
| He | was |
| She | was |
| It | was |
| You | were |
| We | were |
| They | were |

### Examples

> I was studying.

> He was sleeping.

> She was working.

> We were studying.

> They were playing.

> [!important]
> **I/He/She/It → WAS**
>
> **You/We/They → WERE**

---

# 16. Past Continuous — Specific Past Time

Use Past Continuous when an action was happening at a particular time in the past.

Example:

> At 8 p.m. yesterday, I was studying.

Timeline:

$$
\text{Past} \rightarrow [\text{ACTION IN PROGRESS}] \rightarrow \text{Later}
$$

Other examples:

> At midnight, she was sleeping.

> At 10 a.m., they were attending the meeting.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> **At + past time + action in progress → Past Continuous**

---

# 17. Past Continuous — Interrupted Action

This is one of the most important placement patterns.

An action was in progress when another shorter action occurred.

### Pattern

$$
\boxed{Past\ Continuous + when + Simple\ Past}
$$

Example:

> I was studying when my friend called.

The longer action:

> was studying

The interrupting action:

> called

### Timeline

$$
\text{studying} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{phone call}} \rightarrow \text{studying interrupted}
$$

---

# 18. Interrupted Action — Recognition

> [!important]
> If you see:
>
> **was/were + V-ing + when + V2**
>
> think:
>
> **Past Continuous + Simple Past**

Example:

> She was sleeping when the phone rang.

---

# 19. Past Continuous with While

`While` often introduces an action that was in progress.

Example:

> While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.

Both actions were happening during the same period.

### Pattern

$$
\boxed{While + Past\ Continuous,\ Past\ Continuous}
$$

---

# 20. Two Simultaneous Past Actions

When two actions were happening at the same time, Past Continuous can be used for both.

Example:

> I was studying while my sister was watching television.

Timeline:

$$
\text{Studying} \parallel \text{Watching TV}
$$

### Recognition

> [!important]
> **Two simultaneous ongoing past actions → Past Continuous + Past Continuous**

---

# 21. Past Continuous — Background Action

Past Continuous is useful for setting the background of a story.

Example:

> It was raining, people were running, and cars were moving slowly.

The actions provide the background.

---

# 22. Past Continuous — Negative

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + was/were + not + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I was not sleeping.

> She was not studying.

> They were not working.

Short forms:

> wasn't = was not

> weren't = were not

---

# 23. Past Continuous — Questions

### Formula

$$
\boxed{Was/Were + S + V-ing?}
$$

Examples:

> Was he sleeping?

> Were they studying?

> Was she working?

---

# 24. Future Continuous

## 24.1 Definition

Future Continuous describes an action that **will be in progress at a particular future time**.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + will + be + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I will be studying.

> She will be working.

> They will be travelling.

---

# 25. Future Continuous — Specific Future Time

Example:

> At 8 p.m. tomorrow, I will be studying.

The action will be in progress at 8 p.m.

### Recognition

> [!important]
> **At + future time + action in progress → Future Continuous**

---

# 26. Future Continuous — Expected Future Activity

Example:

> This time tomorrow, I will be travelling.

> Next week, she will be attending a training program.

> At noon, they will be having lunch.

The focus is on what will be happening at that future time.

---

# 27. Future Continuous — Polite Inquiry

Future Continuous can be used to ask about someone's expected future activity politely.

Examples:

> Will you be attending the meeting tomorrow?

> Will you be using the computer tonight?

> Will you be travelling next week?

It can sound less direct than asking about a decision.

---

# 28. Future Continuous — Negative

### Formula

$$
\boxed{S + will + not + be + V-ing}
$$

Examples:

> I will not be working tomorrow.

> She won't be attending the meeting.

> They won't be travelling next week.

---

# 29. Future Continuous — Questions

### Formula

$$
\boxed{Will + S + be + V-ing?}
$$

Examples:

> Will you be studying tonight?

> Will she be working tomorrow?

> Will they be travelling next week?

---

## Past Continuous

$$
\boxed{\text{PAST TIME} \rightarrow [\text{ACTION IN PROGRESS}]}

\boxed{\text{FUTURE TIME} \rightarrow [\text{ACTION IN PROGRESS}]}

# 31. Simple vs Continuous

This is one of the most important distinctions.

| Simple Tense | Continuous Tense |
|---|---|
| Habit/general fact | Action in progress |
| Complete event | Temporary activity |
| Repeated action | Current activity |
| State | Developing/changing activity |

### Present

> I study every day.

Habit.

> I am studying now.

Current activity.

### Past

> I studied yesterday.

Completed past event.

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

Action in progress at that time.

### Future

> I will study tomorrow.

Future action.

> I will be studying at 8 p.m.

Action in progress at that future time.

---

# 32. Present Simple vs Present Continuous

### Present Simple

Use for:

- habits
- routines
- facts
- permanent situations
- general truths

Example:

> I study every day.

### Present Continuous

Use for:

- actions happening now
- temporary situations
- current trends
- changing situations
- future arrangements

Example:

> I am studying now.

> [!tip]
> **Every day → Simple Present**
>
> **Now/at the moment → Present Continuous**

---

# 33. Past Simple vs Past Continuous

### Past Simple

Focus:

> completed past action.

> I watched the movie yesterday.

### Past Continuous

Focus:

> action in progress at a past time.

> I was watching the movie at 8 p.m.

---

# 34. Future Simple vs Future Continuous

### Future Simple

> I will study tomorrow.

General future action.

### Future Continuous

> I will be studying at 8 p.m. tomorrow.

Action in progress at a specific future time.

> [!important]
> **Future action → will + V1**
>
> **Future action in progress → will be + V-ing**

---

# 35. Continuous Tense with Time Expressions

Common clues:

### Present Continuous

- now
- right now
- at the moment
- currently
- these days
- this week
- today

### Past Continuous

- at 5 p.m. yesterday
- at that time
- while
- when
- all evening
- at that moment

### Future Continuous

- this time tomorrow
- at 8 p.m. tomorrow
- tomorrow at this time
- next week at this time

> [!warning]
> Time expressions are clues, not absolute rules. Always check the meaning of the entire sentence.

# 36. Stative Verbs — Important Exception

Some verbs normally describe states rather than physical activities.

These are called **stative verbs**.

Common examples:

- know
- understand
- believe
- remember
- love
- hate
- like
- want
- need
- own
- belong
- seem
- know
- prefer

Usually:

> I know the answer.

Not normally:

> I am knowing the answer.

Correct:

> I understand the problem.

Not normally:

> I am understanding the problem.

> [!important]
> Do not mechanically convert every verb into `V-ing`.

---

# 37. Common Stative Verb Categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | know, understand, remember |
| Belief | believe, think in the sense of opinion |
| Emotion | love, hate, like, prefer |
| Possession | own, possess, belong |
| Need | need, want |
| Appearance | seem, appear |
| Perception | know, recognize |

### Important Note

Some verbs can be both stative and dynamic depending on meaning.

Example:

> I think he is correct.

Here `think` expresses an opinion.

But:

> I am thinking about the problem.

Here `think` describes an ongoing mental activity.

---

# 38. Dynamic vs Stative Meaning

This is an advanced exam pattern.

### Think

Opinion:

> I think he is right.

Ongoing mental activity:

> I am thinking about the problem.

### Have

Possession:

> I have a car.

Activity/experience:

> I am having lunch.

### See

Perception:

> I see the problem.

Arrangement/meeting:

> I am seeing the doctor tomorrow.

> [!important]
> The correct tense can depend on the **meaning of the verb**, not only the verb itself.

# 39. Present Continuous for Future Arrangements

This pattern is frequently tested.

Example:

> I am meeting my manager tomorrow.

The event is future, but the grammar is Present Continuous.

### Recognition

Look for:

- tomorrow
- tonight
- next week
- at 5 p.m.
- arranged meeting
- booked travel
- planned appointment

### Examples

> We are travelling tomorrow.

> She is meeting her professor at 4 p.m.

> They are attending the conference next week.

---

# 40. Present Continuous vs Going To

Both can describe planned future activities.

### Going To

Focus:

> intention/plan.

> I am going to study tonight.

### Present Continuous

Focus:

> definite arrangement.

> I am meeting my friend tonight.

### Quick Comparison

| Going To | Present Continuous |
|---|---|
| Intention | Arrangement |
| Plan | Fixed event |
| Decision already made | Appointment/event arranged |

---

# 41. Present Continuous vs Simple Present for Future

### Present Continuous

Personal arrangement:

> I am meeting my manager tomorrow.

### Simple Present

Fixed schedule:

> The train leaves tomorrow at 6 a.m.

> [!important]
> **Personal arrangement → Present Continuous**
>
> **Official timetable/schedule → Simple Present**

# 42. Past Continuous + Simple Past

This is a high-value pattern.

### Pattern

$$
\boxed{Past\ Continuous + when + Simple\ Past}
$$

Example:

> I was studying when he called.

### Interpretation

Long/background action:

> was studying

Short/interruption:

> called

---

# 43. While + Continuous

`While` commonly connects simultaneous ongoing actions.

Example:

> While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.

### Pattern

$$
\boxed{While + Past\ Continuous,\ Past\ Continuous}
$$

Another pattern:

> While I was studying, my brother called me.

Here:

- `was studying` → ongoing background action
- `called` → shorter event

---

# 44. When + Past Continuous

`When` can introduce an event that happened during another ongoing action.

Example:

> When I was walking home, it started raining.

The ongoing action:

> was walking

The event:

> started

---

# 45. Two Actions in Progress

Example:

> While she was cooking, he was cleaning the room.

Both actions were happening at the same time.

Timeline:

$$
\text{Cooking} \parallel \text{Cleaning}
$$

Think:

$$
\boxed{was/were + V-ing}
$$

Example:

> I was studying at 8 p.m.

---

## Pattern 6 — Interrupted Past Action

### How to Recognize

If you see:

> was/were + V-ing + when + V2

Think:

$$
\boxed{Past\ Continuous + Simple\ Past}
$$

Example:

> I was sleeping when he called.

---

## Pattern 7 — Two Simultaneous Past Actions

### How to Recognize

If you see:

> while

and both actions were happening simultaneously:

Think:

$$
\boxed{Past\ Continuous + Past\ Continuous}
$$

Example:

> While I was studying, she was watching TV.

---

## Pattern 8 — Future Action in Progress

### How to Recognize

If you see:

> at 8 p.m. tomorrow  
> this time tomorrow  
> tomorrow at this time

Think:

$$
\boxed{will + be + V-ing}
$$

Example:

> I will be studying at 8 p.m. tomorrow.

---

## Pattern 9 — Future Expected Activity

### How to Recognize

If the question asks what someone will be doing at a future point:

Think:

$$
\boxed{will + be + V-ing}
$$

Example:

> Will you be attending the meeting tomorrow?

---

## Pattern 10 — Stative Verb

### How to Recognize

If the verb describes:

- knowledge
- possession
- belief
- emotion
- need
- preference

be careful with `V-ing`.

Example:

> I know the answer.

Not normally:

> I am knowing the answer.

# 53. Will vs Will Be

This is a common placement trap.

### Will + V1

General future action.

> I will study tomorrow.

### Will Be + V-ing

Future action in progress at a specific time.

> I will be studying at 8 p.m. tomorrow.

### Quick Trick

> [!tip]
> If the sentence asks **WHAT WILL HAPPEN?**
>
> Think:
>
> `will + V1`
>
> If it asks **WHAT WILL BE HAPPENING AT THAT TIME?**
>
> Think:
>
> `will be + V-ing`

---

# 54. Simple Past vs Past Continuous — Quick Trick

### Simple Past

Completed event:

> I watched TV.

### Past Continuous

Action in progress:

> I was watching TV.

### With Interruption

> I was watching TV when he called.

> [!important]
> Long/background action → **Past Continuous**
>
> Short/interruption → **Simple Past**

# 63. V-ing Formation Basics

Most verbs:

$$
\boxed{V1 + ing}
$$

Examples:

> work → working

> study → studying

> play → playing

> read → reading

### Common spelling patterns

#### Verb ending in silent `e`

Usually remove `e` before `-ing`.

> make → making

> write → writing

> come → coming

#### Verb ending in `ie`

Often changes to `y`.

> lie → lying

> die → dying

> tie → tying

#### Short stressed consonant-vowel-consonant pattern

The final consonant may double.

> run → running

> sit → sitting

> stop → stopping

> [!warning]
> Do not apply spelling rules blindly to every word. Learn common patterns and check the verb form when necessary.

### Step 3 — Search for Strong Clues

> now

> while
